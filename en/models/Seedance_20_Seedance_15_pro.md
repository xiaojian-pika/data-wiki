## Basic Information

### Name

Seedance 2.0 and Seedance 1.5 pro (with Seedance 1.0 as the longitudinal baseline for the data pipeline)

### Releasing institution/company

ByteDance Seed Team (ByteDance Seed / Team Seedance)

### Release date (technical report/paper/open-source date)

Seedance 1.5 pro: technical report arXiv:2512.13507, v1 submitted 2025-12-15, v3 updated 2025-12-23; the product went live on Doubao/Jimeng/Volcano Engine Ark in December 2025 (model ID: Doubao-Seedance-1.5-pro). Seedance 2.0: officially released in China in early February 2026 (model ID: doubao-seedance-2-0-260128); technical report arXiv:2604.14148 submitted 2026-04-15; Volcano Engine API fully opened on 2026-04-14. Seedance 1.0, used as the longitudinal comparison baseline, has technical report arXiv:2506.09113 (2025-06).

### Type (model/dataset/toolchain/evaluation benchmark)

A closed-source commercial model (a video/joint audio-video generation foundation model) + an accompanying in-house evaluation benchmark (SeedVideoBench 1.0/1.5/2.0). Both technical reports are closer to a model card + evaluation report than a full methodology paper.

### Degree of openness (whether weights/code/data/pipeline are each open source)

Weights: not open source; code: not open source; training data: not public; data-processing pipeline: only the Seedance 1.0 report gives a reasonably complete textual description (no code, no thresholds, no open-source tooling), the 1.5 pro report gives only a brief bullet-point-style description, and the 2.0 report's data section is entirely undisclosed. Externally, only API/product access is provided (Volcano Engine Ark, Doubao, Jimeng, and third-party hosting such as Replicate).

### Whether joint audio-video generation is supported, and the implementation approach (native joint/cascade/MoE fusion) ⚠️

Supported. Seedance 1.5 pro is native joint audio-video generation: it employs a dual-branch Diffusion Transformer (the report's abstract describes it as a dual-branch DiT, while the main text describes it as a unified framework based on MMDiT) + a cross-modal joint module, with multi-task pretraining on large-scale mixed-modality data, simultaneously supporting T2VA, I2VA, as well as vision-only T2V/I2V, and therefore belongs to "native joint" generation rather than a cascaded generate-video-then-dub approach. Seedance 2.0 further upgrades to a unified multi-modal audio-video joint generation architecture (the official blog mentions a sparse architecture), supporting four input modalities — text/image/audio/video; on the audio side it adds binaural (dual-channel stereo) capability and supports multi-track parallel output of background music, ambient sound effects, and character vocal narration, precisely time-aligned with the on-screen rhythm. [Uncertain: 2.0's specific fusion approach (whether it is MoE fusion) is not stated in the report]

## Data Scale and Distribution

### Training data volume (number of videos/hours/tokens, pretraining and SFT separated) ⚠️

[Uncertain] Neither report discloses any training-data-volume figure (number of videos, hours, token count; neither pretraining nor SFT figures are given). A full-text search shows that neither the Seedance 1.5 pro report (v1/v3) nor the Seedance 2.0 report contains expressions such as million/hours/minutes for scale. The widely circulated claim of "approximately 100 million minutes of in-the-wild audio-video clips" comes from AI auto-summary sites such as emergentmind and claims to be quoted from this paper, but the original text contains no such figure — this is an unverified secondhand inference and should not be adopted. The Seedance 1.0 report likewise gives no absolute data volume.

### Data source composition (proprietary/public datasets/web scraping/licensed procurement/synthetic data) ⚠️

Seedance 1.5 pro is only described as a "large-scale mixed-modality dataset," with no breakdown of source composition. Seedance 2.0 discloses nothing. Reference can be made to the same team's Seedance 1.0 report's description: data is obtained "ethically and legally sourced," from diverse public and licensed repositories, and jointly trained with image data (image-data preparation follows the same methodology as Seedream). The respective proportions of proprietary/scraped/procured/synthetic data are not disclosed. [Uncertain: the specific source composition and mixture for 1.5 and 2.0]

### Data compliance and provenance (proportion of licensed data, rights-cleared datasets, C2PA, etc.) ⚠️

Only principle-level statements are given, with no quantitative disclosure. The Seedance 1.0 report states that data acquisition "prioritizes ethically and legally compliant content, from diverse public and licensed repositories," and deploys classifiers during the filtering stage to remove pornography, violence, child exploitation, explicit nudity, and other content to ensure "ethical compliance and dataset safety." The Seedance 2.0 report states at the end of its introduction that "safety is a core consideration of our work," implementing a structured safety-assessment framework throughout the model's iteration lifecycle and continuously assessing and mitigating potential risks to support responsible, compliant, and ethical development. [Uncertain: the proportion of licensed data, the list of rights-cleared datasets, and whether C2PA/watermark-based provenance is adopted are all undisclosed]

### Clip duration distribution and segmentation strategy ⚠️

[Uncertain] Neither 1.5 nor 2.0 discloses the training-clip duration distribution. Seedance 1.0 can be referenced: original long videos are cut via shot-aware temporal segmentation, with a maximum clip duration of 12 seconds; the first pretraining stage jointly trains on 3-12 second (12 fps) video clips together with 256px images. The duration coverage implied by the data can be inferred in reverse from generation-side capability: 1.5 pro supports roughly 10-second-class generation, Seedance 2.0 supports direct 4-15 second audio-video generation, and the official blog mentions 15-second high-quality multi-shot audio-video output, implying the training data already covers 15-second-class multi-shot clips.

### Resolution/aspect-ratio distribution and bucketing strategy ⚠️

[Uncertain] The distribution statistics and bucketing strategy for resolution/aspect ratio are not disclosed. Seedance 1.0's progressive resolution curriculum can be referenced: first thoroughly training text-to-image at 256px → 256px joint image-text-video training → resolution raised to 640px → finally raised to 24 fps. During Distribution Rebalancing, Seedance 1.0 explicitly lists "resolution" as one of the attribute dimensions whose frequency must be counted and up/down-sampled. Seedance 2.0's native output resolutions are 480p and 720p (the report specifically notes that its Arena Elo at 720p already exceeds competitors' 1080p models).

### Category/domain distribution and mixture strategy (proportional control and concept balancing across people, actions, scenes, styles, etc.) ⚠️

The category-level distribution of training data for 1.5 and 2.0 is undisclosed, but their mixture intent can be reconstructed from two pieces of circumstantial evidence: (1) Seedance 1.0's "diversity-oriented data sourcing" explicitly lists the dimensions to be maximally covered — clip duration, resolution, subject (person/animal/object), scene type (natural landscape/urban environment), subject action, genre (documentary/animation), artistic style, camera kinematics, and cinematography technique; and during "distribution rebalancing," frequencies are counted along dimensions such as subject category, scene type, dominant action, genre, visual style, clip duration, resolution, and motion characteristics, with over-represented head categories downsampled and long-tail categories given an increased sampling probability plus targeted data acquisition, with the goal of achieving a "fairer, more comprehensive representation" of the visual world; the SFT stage defines "several hundred categories" (partitioned by key attributes such as visual style and motion type) for targeted collection. (2) The Seedance 1.5 pro report states that its data pipeline prioritizes "video-audio coherence, motion expressiveness, and curriculum-based data scheduling," indicating an explicit mixture-level tilt toward samples with high motion expressiveness — echoed by the new "Video Vividness" metric in SeedVideoBench 1.5 (broken into four sub-dimensions: action, camera movement, atmosphere, emotion), and the report criticizes the industry-wide practice of trading motion for stability via slow motion. Seedance 2.0 further strengthens categories such as complex interaction and human-body motion, multi-subject interaction, multi-shot narrative, and Chinese dialects/traditional opera/singing performances. [Uncertain: all specific proportion figures]

### Audio-category distribution and mixture (proportions and control strategy for speech/sound-effect foley/music/ambient sound/silence) — a dimension unique to AV models ⚠️

[Uncertain] The training-side audio-category mixture is undisclosed. However, the evaluation-annotation systems of Seedance 1.5 pro/2.0 are highly fine-grained, and can be treated as a mirror of their data-category system: SeedVideoBench 1.5's main audio-label categories are — Human Voice Types (speech, singing, non-verbal vocalizations such as laughter, plus fine-grained sub-dimensions); Human Voice Attributes (timbre, accent, emotional tone); Non-Speech Audio (covering sound effects and music, further classified by sound source such as animal/mechanical tool, acoustic properties, music genre, and technical parameters). SeedVideoBench 2.0's fine-grained audio categories expand to 17 classes: Chinese dialects/accents, Chinese multi-person dialogue, Chinese variety-show voices, Chinese traditional opera, English, minority-ethnic languages, singing/rap, spatial scene sound, off-screen voice, non-verbal vocalizations, human-voice + action interaction sound, object-interaction sound, animal calls, ambient/background sound, special sound effects (including ASMR), instruments and audio, binaural audio. Seedance 2.0 explicitly supports multi-track parallel output of three audio-track types — background music / ambient sound effects / character voiceover. The proportion of silent samples and the specific mixture ratio of speech:sound-effect:music are not disclosed.

### Narrative-structure distribution (single-shot vs. multi-shot, average clip duration, shot-count distribution, whether native audio tracks are included) ⚠️

Seedance 1.0's segmentation strategy explicitly permits "each clip may contain one or multiple temporally coherent shots," preserving local narrative flow — this is the data foundation of its multi-shot generation capability. Seedance 1.5 pro emphasizes "narrative coherence" and the application potential of a multi-shot video generation workflow, and possesses continuous long-take, dolly-zoom (Hitchcock zoom), and other camera-movement and cinematic-transition capabilities. Seedance 2.0's headline feature is "native professional-grade multi-shot narrative capability," able to autonomously plan shot sequencing and design a visual-presentation template; SeedVideoBench 2.0 accordingly adds a narrative-quality metric, including three sub-dimensions: cinematography language (shot logic and expressiveness, checking for redundant coverage, crossing-the-line/180-degree-rule violations, shot-size mismatch, and uneven pacing), plot design, and stylized aesthetics. Specific statistics such as the proportion of native audio tracks in the training data, average clip duration, and shot-count distribution are not disclosed. [Uncertain: quantitative distribution]

### Language/accent distribution (data foundation for multilingual lip-sync capability) ⚠️

The language/accent proportions in the training data are not disclosed, but capability and evaluation coverage allow reverse inference: Seedance 1.5 pro natively supports lip sync for multiple languages and multi-region dialects, capturing each one's distinctive speech prosody and emotional intensity; Seedance 2.0 shows a marked improvement in instruction-following accuracy for Chinese dialects, traditional opera, and singing scenarios. SeedVideoBench 2.0's audio evaluation explicitly distinguishes language categories including Chinese dialects/accents, Chinese multi-person dialogue, Chinese variety-show voices, Chinese traditional opera, English, and minority-ethnic languages (in 2.0, audio-quality scores are English 4.17, minority-ethnic languages 3.82, Chinese dialects 2.82; audio-video sync scores are English 4.17, minority-ethnic languages 3.88, Chinese dialects 3.64), indicating the training corpus is predominantly Chinese and English while covering multiple Chinese dialects and minority-ethnic languages. Additionally, Seedance 1.0's captioning model is itself trained on bilingual Chinese-English data. [Uncertain: specific proportion of each language]

## Cleaning Pipeline

### Overall structure of the cleaning funnel (number of filtering tiers, order of each tier)

Seedance 1.5 pro gives only a high-level description: a holistic data framework consisting of a "multi-stage curation pipeline + an advanced captioning system + scalable infrastructure," with the pipeline prioritizing video-audio coherence, motion expressiveness, and curriculum-based data scheduling. The Seedance 2.0 report has no data section at all, disclosing nothing about the funnel structure (the report contains only an introduction, evaluation, and contributors section). The Seedance 1.0 report, usable as a longitudinal baseline, gives a six-tier sequential funnel: (1) Diversity-Oriented Data Sourcing → (2) Shot-Aware Temporal Segmentation (maximum 12 seconds) → (3) Visual Overlay Rectification (logo/watermark/subtitle/sticker detection + adaptive cropping) → (4) Quality and Safety Filtering → (5) Semantic Deduplication → (6) Distribution Rebalancing; the entire pipeline is deployed as an automated system with high throughput for massive data volumes. Note: the claim in the task background that "Seedance 2.0's data-side disclosure is the most complete" does not match the actual report content — 2.0's arXiv report is an evaluation-oriented model card, with a level of data disclosure actually lower than Seedance 1.0's.

### Quantitative funnel retention rate (input/output volume at each filtering tier and the final retention rate, e.g., Apollo's 27%) ⚠️

[Uncertain] None of the three-generation reports (1.0/1.5/2.0) give any input/output volume or retention-rate figures for any filtering tier, so no comparison can be made with disclosures such as Apollo's 27% or MOVA's 26.39%. This is the largest gap in the Seedance series' data disclosure.

### Shot-segmentation method (PySceneDetect/in-house model/shot-aware splitting) ⚠️

Not directly described for Seedance 1.5/2.0. Seedance 1.0's method: automated shot boundary detection is used, identifying natural scene transitions by analyzing inter-frame visual dissimilarities or using a pretrained detector; videos are then cut into clips of at most 12 seconds, with each clip potentially containing one or multiple temporally coherent shots, to control input length while preserving local narrative flow. It is not stated whether an open-source tool such as PySceneDetect is used, tending toward an in-house/internal solution. [Uncertain: whether 1.5 and 2.0 adopt the same approach, and whether audio-side boundary detection was introduced]

### Quality filtering (aesthetic scoring, sharpness, OCR text filtering, letterbox/watermark/logo detection) ⚠️

Seedance 1.5 pro only states that the pipeline includes filtering related to "clip quality" (a third-party summary claims the second stage is automated filtering for audio-video sync and clip quality, but this wording does not appear in the paper's original text). Seedance 1.0's specific approach: (a) Visual Overlay Rectification — a hybrid scheme of heuristic rule systems + dedicated object-detection models identifies logos, watermarks, subtitles, and on-screen graphic overlays, and frames are then adaptively cropped to maximize retention of the subject's visual content; (b) Quality filtering — a dedicated "visual quality model" systematically identifies and removes blurriness, excessive jittering, low aesthetic quality, poor cinematographic composition, and clips that are predominantly static content. In the Continue Training stage, a series of dedicated evaluation models, including an aesthetic scorer, are further used to filter out a higher-aesthetic-quality subset. All thresholds, scorer scale, and tiering are undisclosed. [Uncertain: specific thresholds and whether OCR text filtering forms its own independent tier]

### Motion filtering (optical-flow/motion-score thresholds, removal of static and jittery content) ⚠️

Seedance 1.0 removes clips with excessive jitter and predominantly static content during the pretraining filtering stage; in the Continue Training stage, "motion evaluators based on optical flow" are used together with the aesthetic scorer to jointly select a subset with higher aesthetic quality and richer motion dynamics; the report states that richer motion dynamics enable the model to generate more natural and fluid results. Seedance 1.5 pro lists "motion expressiveness" as one of the data pipeline's three priority objectives, and introduces a vividness metric on the evaluation side, explicitly opposing the practice of trading motion for stability via slow motion. The specific motion-score thresholds are not disclosed. [Uncertain: threshold values]

### Deduplication method (exact dedup and embedding-based semantic dedup recorded separately) ⚠️

Seedance 1.0 uses Semantic Deduplication: an internally developed video representation model extracts robust feature embeddings, and clips that are visually and semantically similar are clustered based on these embeddings; within each near-duplicate cluster, only the single instance with the highest overall quality score (from the previous quality-filtering tier) is retained. The report does not separately describe an exact-hash deduplication step. Deduplication schemes for 1.5 and 2.0 are undisclosed. [Uncertain: whether exact dedup is included, clustering thresholds, deduplication ratio]

### VLM/LLM as a quality-control judge (multimodal large-model quality scoring and mismatch removal, the 2026 trend from shallow scorers toward large-model semantic judgment) ⚠️

The Seedance series has substantively moved toward "model as quality-control judge," but disclosure granularity is limited: Seedance 1.0 uses a dedicated visual quality model for defect judgment, an in-house video representation model for semantic-clustering deduplication, a Tarsier2-based in-house captioning model for content understanding, and a VLM-architecture Foundational Reward Model for image-text alignment and structural-stability assessment. Seedance 1.5 pro states its captioning system can provide "rich, professional-grade descriptions" for both video and audio modalities, belonging to the large-model semantic-judgment route. The Seedance 2.0 report discloses no quality-control-model details, but the same team has the Seed-VL series of multimodal understanding models (listed in the introduction as used for cross-modal semantic understanding), presumably already applied in the data-judgment step. At the evaluation level, SeedVideoBench 2.0 explicitly distinguishes: objective metrics go through an automated pipeline, subjective metrics go through blind expert review. [Uncertain: whether 1.5/2.0 introduce a VLM scorer and its scale, scoring dimensions, and removal threshold]

### Safety and compliance filtering (NSFW, copyright, face/privacy) ⚠️

Seedance 1.0: deploys advanced classifiers to detect and remove pornography, explicit violence, child exploitation, explicit nudity, and other harmful or inappropriate content, to ensure ethical compliance and dataset safety. Seedance 2.0 report states that a structured safety-assessment framework is implemented throughout the model's iteration lifecycle, with continuous assessment and mitigation of potential risks. The specific mechanisms for face/privacy protection and copyright fingerprinting are undisclosed. [Uncertain: specific methods for face/privacy filtering and copyright detection]

## Captioning Approach

### Captioning model used (in-house VLM/open-source model, model scale) ⚠️

Seedance 1.0: the captioning model uses Tarsier2 (an open-source model with strong video-understanding capability) as its base, trained on manually annotated high-quality data, with the visual encoder frozen during training and the language model fully fine-tuned, trained on bilingual Chinese-English data to obtain bilingual capability; at inference time, a Prompt Engineering (PE) model from the same lineage rewrites the user prompt into a detailed video description aligned in content and structure with the training captions. Seedance 1.5 pro states that its "advanced captioning system" provides rich, professional-grade descriptions for both the video and audio modalities, but does not specify the base model or parameter scale. Seedance 2.0 discloses nothing. [Uncertain: the identity and scale of the captioning model for 1.5/2.0; presumably switched to the in-house Seed-VL series]

### Caption density and degree of structuring (short/long/dense description, structured fields such as camera movement, style tags) ⚠️

Seedance 1.0 adopts a "dense caption" style, fusing two categories of features — dynamic and static: dynamic features finely describe the actions and camera movements within the clip, highlighting elements of change, covering three categories — motion, changes to subject or scene, and camera movement; static features describe the characteristics of the core character or scene, covering categories such as appearance, aesthetics, and style. The team collected diverse data for these categories and performed high-quality manual annotation for training the captioning model. In the Continue Training stage, two types of captions were designed for the I2V task: (1) the original long caption containing both dynamic and static details; (2) a short caption with the static description corresponding to the first frame removed, focusing only on motion dynamics, to strengthen semantic alignment with the training objective. Seedance 1.5 pro is described as "significantly improving the richness and professionalism of video descriptions, and introducing audio descriptions." [Uncertain: whether 1.5/2.0 add new structured field schemas and their field list]

### Joint audio-video caption structure (whether visual + auditory tracks are covered simultaneously, whether it is factorized into independent fields, e.g., LTX-2's full soundscape description, Script-a-Video's factorized streams, Foley-Omni's three fields) ⚠️

Seedance 1.5 pro is the first version in this series to introduce audio-side description: its captioning system "provides rich, professional-grade descriptions for both the video and audio modalities," i.e., beyond traditional "video-caption" data, it systematically introduces structured audio descriptions, allowing the model to internalize a richer joint audio-video semantic space. However, the report does not disclose the specific schema — it does not state whether it is a single full-soundscape description (LTX-2-style) or factorized into independent stream fields (such as Script-a-Video's factorized streams or Foley-Omni's three fields). Judging from SeedVideoBench 1.5/2.0's audio-label system (three main categories: human voice types / human voice attributes / non-speech audio, refined to 17 categories in 2.0) and Seedance 2.0's support for parallel three-track output of background music, ambient sound effects, and character vocals, the training side very likely adopts a multi-field annotation structure factorized by audio track, but this is an inference, not something the paper explicitly states. [Uncertain: the exact form of the schema and its field names]

### Dialogue transcription and speaker-attribute annotation (ASR transcription + speaker identity/language/accent/emotion) ⚠️

[Uncertain] The report does not explicitly describe an ASR transcription and speaker-attribute annotation process. Indirect evidence strongly supports its existence: Seedance 1.5 pro has precise multilingual and dialectal lip sync, able to capture each language's distinctive speech prosody and emotional intensity; SeedVideoBench 1.5's "human voice attributes" label system explicitly includes timbre, accent, and emotional tone; SeedVideoBench 2.0 covers 17 categories including Chinese dialects/accents, multi-person dialogue, minority-ethnic languages, and off-screen voice. These capabilities must be built on a data foundation of dialogue transcription plus speaker identity/language/accent/emotion annotation, but the paper does not describe the specific method (whether an in-house ASR is used, whether speaker diarization is applied, etc.).

### Geometric and structured annotation (camera parameters, depth, 3D point tracks, action labels, explicit state) ⚠️

[Uncertain] Camera parameters, depth, 3D point tracks, and other geometric annotations are not disclosed. The known structured annotation remains only at the text-caption level: Seedance 1.0's captions explicitly include camera movements as one of the dynamic-feature categories; Seedance 1.5 pro has autonomous camera-movement scheduling capability (continuous long-takes, dolly zoom/Hitchcock zoom, cinematic-grade transitions, and professional color grading); Seedance 2.0 has basic directorial and cinematographic reasoning capability, able to autonomously plan shot sequencing. These suggest camera movement enters training in the form of language labels rather than numerical camera parameters.

### Synthetic data construction (controlled perturbation/edited construction of training pairs, e.g., InstructAV2AV) ⚠️

[Uncertain] The synthetic-data construction process is undisclosed. The only relevant piece is the RLHF stage: Seedance 1.0 explicitly includes "synthetic videos generated by different stages of our model" as a source of data pairs for human-preference annotation, with experiments showing that multi-source visual material improves the RM's domain capacity, expands the preference upper bound, and enhances generalization. This is preference-data synthesis rather than controlled-perturbation-style construction of editing training pairs. Seedance 2.0 has video editing and continuation capability (targeted modification of a specified clip/character/action/plot, forward/backward timeline continuation), which by inference requires paired before/after editing data for training, but the construction method is undisclosed.

### Degree of human involvement (manual annotation, manual quality control, model pre-screening + manual review) ⚠️

Human involvement runs through multiple stages and is explicitly mentioned: (1) Seedance 1.0 collected diverse category data and performed "high-quality manual annotations" to train the captioning model; (2) the SFT stage uses a high-quality video-text pair set with "manually verified captions," collected in a targeted manner across several hundred categories, constituting a Human-Curated Dataset; (3) the RLHF stage adopts a multi-dimensional manual-annotation protocol — selecting the best and worst videos under a specific dimension while ensuring the best is not inferior to the worst on other dimensions; (4) the unified platform layer of the data-processing infrastructure explicitly supports "automating human-in-the-loop workflows," task management, data visualization, and pipeline monitoring; (5) Seedance 2.0 introduces expert reviewers from advertising and game-production domains for subjective scoring and blind review at the evaluation stage, and conducts an authenticity-discrimination study (reviewers distinguish generated video from real footage), whose results feed back into the aesthetic-tuning process. The overall pattern is "model pre-screening + manual review/fine annotation." [Uncertain: the scale of manual annotation and the amount of human labor invested]

## Audio-Video Alignment

### Audio-video synchronization detection method (lip sync, event alignment) ⚠️

[Uncertain] The data-side audio-video sync detection method is not disclosed in the reports. Seedance 1.5 pro only states at the principle level that its data pipeline "prioritizes video-audio coherence"; a third-party summary claims the second stage is "automated filtering for audio-video sync and clip quality," but this wording does not appear in the paper's original text and is an unverified inference. The evaluation side has a complete definition: SeedVideoBench 1.5's "Audio-Visual Sync" metric measures the temporal alignment between the auditory and visual streams, evaluating speech-lip sync (mitigating the "ventriloquism effect" perception), the alignment of sound effects with visual events, and whether prominent on-screen actions have corresponding auditory cues; SeedVideoBench 2.0 carries this forward and scores it across 17 fine-grained categories (Seedance 2.0's T2V audio-video sync total score is 3.75, ranking first in 16 of the 17 categories, with a usability rate of 93.30% and a satisfaction rate of 68.30%, far exceeding the best competitor's 25.45%).

### Specific sync-detection metrics and thresholds (SyncNet/Synchformer/LSE/in-house, threshold values, e.g., MOVA's LSE-D≤9.5 and LSE-C≥4.5, SkyReels-V4's SyncNet |offset|≤3∧conf>1.5) ⚠️

[Uncertain] Entirely undisclosed. Neither report contains any automated sync-metric name such as SyncNet, AV-align, or LSE-C/LSE-D, nor is any filtering threshold value given. The Seedance series' audio-video sync evaluation entirely uses 1-5 point manual/expert subjective scoring (SeedVideoBench), rather than automated objective metrics — the report even specifically notes in its Arena section that its evaluation route does not rely on automated metrics such as FVD or CLIPScore. It is therefore impossible to compare against disclosures such as UniTalking's "SyncNet conf > 0.9."

### Separation of temporal sync vs. semantic sync (temporal alignment and content-semantic matching as two independent filtering conditions) ⚠️

The reports do not explicitly separate these two conditions at the data-filtering level, but the evaluation system has already completed this separation, which can be viewed as a reflection of its data-quality philosophy: SeedVideoBench 1.5/2.0 defines "Audio-Visual Sync" as a purely temporal-alignment dimension (temporal alignment of lip motion-speech, sound effect-visual event), while setting content-semantic matching as a separate "Audio Prompt Following" dimension (assessing whether human voice, dialogue, and sound effects faithfully follow the user's instructions and intended semantics, and whether there is semantic drift, with typical failure modes including a missing specified sound effect, inaccurate language or dialect, and "speech present but with no corresponding lip motion" audio-visual mismatch); in addition, "Audio Quality" and "Audio Expressiveness" are set as two further independent dimensions. This four-way split strongly suggests that the data side likewise gates on temporal alignment and semantic matching as two independent conditions. [Uncertain: whether data filtering actually follows this split]

### Audio quality filtering (SNR, silence detection and silence-ratio thresholds, no-audio-track exclusion, off-screen source exclusion, background-music separation) ⚠️

[Uncertain] No SNR threshold, silence-ratio threshold, no-audio-track sample exclusion strategy, off-screen-source exclusion, or background-music separation (source separation) method of any kind is disclosed. Only the evaluation-side definition can be referenced: SeedVideoBench 1.5's "Audio Quality" metric measures the intrinsic acoustic quality of the output (covering both vocal and non-vocal components), with key criteria including whether artifacts such as clipping or truncation are present, spatial sound-field rendering, timbre realism, and overall signal clarity — these criteria are very likely also used as the training data's audio-quality admission standard. The Seedance 2.0 report acknowledges in its limitations that "occasional audio distortion" still occurs.

### Classification and differentiated handling of speech/sound-effects/music ⚠️

The training-side classification and differentiated-handling strategy is undisclosed, but both the product and evaluation sides show a clear three-way division: Seedance 2.0 supports parallel multi-track output of background music (BGM), ambient sound effects, and character narration/voiceover, each precisely aligned with the on-screen rhythm, which architecturally requires the training data to have the capability to be separated or separately annotated by audio-track type. The evaluation label system reflects the same division: SeedVideoBench 1.5 divides audio into human voice types, human voice attributes, and non-speech audio (sound effects + music, further subdivided by sound source, acoustic properties, music genre, and technical parameters); SeedVideoBench 2.0's 17 categories draw clear boundaries between speech categories (dialects/multi-person dialogue/variety show/traditional opera/English/minority languages/singing-rap/off-screen voice/non-verbal vocalizations), sound-effect categories (voice + action interaction sound/object-interaction sound/animal calls/ambient-background sound/special effects ASMR), music categories (instruments and audio), and spatial categories (spatial scene sound/binaural audio). [Uncertain: the actual classifier and processing branch on the training-data side]

## Training Coordination

### Multi-stage training curriculum and data-curriculum scheduling (basis for stage division: resolution/duration/quality score/modality; low-res→high-res, image→video, short→long) ⚠️

Seedance 1.5 pro explicitly lists "curriculum-based data scheduling" as one of the data pipeline's three priority objectives, and adopts multi-task pretraining on a large-scale mixed-modality dataset (covering T2VA, I2VA, T2V, I2V), but the basis for stage division and the switching points are not disclosed. The Seedance 2.0 report discloses no training curriculum. Seedance 1.0's Progressive Training curriculum can be referenced: the model is first initialized with thorough 256px low-resolution text-to-image training → Stage 1 performs joint image-text-video training at 256px with 3-12 second (12 fps) video clips → Stage 2 raises the resolution to 640px with duration unchanged → Stage 3 raises the frame rate to 24 fps to improve video fluidity; overall following a low-res→high-res, image→video, low-frame-rate→high-frame-rate progression. On the diffusion-scheduling side, a "resolution-aware shift" of the timestep is also adopted, applying stronger noise perturbation to higher-resolution, longer-duration videos. The overall training is divided into four stages: pretraining → Continue Training (CT) → SFT → RLHF. [Uncertain: the number of stages for 1.5/2.0 and the basis for each stage (whether tiered by modality or quality score)]

### Data mixture changes across training stages (pretraining/annealing/SFT high-quality subset) ⚠️

Seedance 1.0 gives a few checkable mixture figures: during video pretraining, a small proportion of text-to-image tasks is retained to maintain semantic alignment, and the image-to-video (I2V) task proportion is set to 20% to activate visual-prompt-following ability; in the Continue Training stage, the I2V proportion is raised from 20% to 40%, while the training set is further refined using the aesthetic scorer and the optical-flow-based motion evaluator (higher aesthetic quality + richer motion dynamics), using a somewhat smaller number of GPUs than pretraining and an annealed learning-rate schedule; the SFT stage switches to a curated set of manually verified high-quality video-text pairs. Seedance 1.5 pro and 2.0 disclose no stage-mixture figures of any kind. [Uncertain: the per-stage mixture ratios and annealing-stage setup for 1.5/2.0]

### Post-training data (SFT curated-set scale and filtering criteria, number of preference pairs and annotation method, reward-model training data) ⚠️

Seedance 1.5 pro: uses a high-quality audio-video dataset for SFT, then adopts an RLHF algorithm customized for audio-video scenarios, and uses a multi-dimensional reward model to improve motion quality, visual aesthetics, and audio fidelity for T2V/I2V; infrastructure optimizations targeting the RLHF pipeline bring nearly a 3x training speedup. Scale and filtering criteria are not given. Seedance 1.0 discloses more detail: SFT uses a curated set of high-quality video-text pairs with manually verified captions, collected in a targeted manner across several hundred categories defined by attributes such as visual style/motion type, and trains multiple sub-models covering different styles/motions/scenes before performing model merging, using a smaller learning rate and limited GPU count than pretraining, with early stopping to prevent overfitting and preserve text controllability; on the RLHF side, prompts are collected from the training set and from online users, with data balancing and information filtering to remove duplicate and ambiguous prompts; preference data spans multiple sources of visual material including synthetic videos generated by different stages of the model, using a multi-dimensional annotation protocol (selecting the best/worst on a specified dimension while ensuring the best is not inferior to the worst on other dimensions); the reward system contains three dedicated models — Foundational RM (VLM architecture, image-text alignment and structural stability), Motion RM (suppressing artifacts, enhancing motion amplitude and vividness), and Aesthetic RM (image-space input, with the data source switched to video keyframes) — and performs multi-round iterative learning. [Uncertain: SFT curated-set scale, number of preference pairs]

### Data-processing infrastructure and throughput (NeMo Curator/Data-Juicer/in-house, GPU speedup, processing scale, cost) ⚠️

Seedance 1.5 pro states that its data framework is supported by "efficient engineering infrastructure optimized for processing massive multi-modal data," but gives no details. Seedance 1.0 gives a complete three-layer architecture: the top layer is a Unified Platform / Data Platform layer, responsible for automating human-in-the-loop workflows, task management, data visualization, and pipeline monitoring; the middle layer is the computation-framework layer, using BMF (Babit Multimedia Framework, ByteDance in-house) and Ray (open source) to achieve heterogeneous computation across CPU/GPU/NPU, with separate resource-allocation optimization for stable compute and elastic compute; the bottom layer is the resource layer, relying on ByteCloud (internal) and Volcano Engine (external) cloud infrastructure, with storage as object storage + HDFS. The data task flow is data ingestion → multi-stage data curation → data encoding and packaging. On the heterogeneous-computation side, operators are assigned to the optimal hardware (CPU decoding, GPU deep-model inference), with asynchronous communication between compute units to mitigate bottlenecks caused by hardware performance gaps; adaptive elastic scaling and preemptible-task failure retry are introduced to address the instability of elastic resources, and a customized version of BMF and Ray achieves "near-linear scaling and extremely high throughput." No external solution such as NeMo Curator or Data-Juicer is used. [Uncertain: GPU speedup ratio, processing scale, cost, and other quantitative metrics are all undisclosed]

## Effectiveness Comparison

### Quantified impact of data-strategy ablations (distinguish: filtering-strictness ablation / caption-density-style ablation / data-mixture ablation, and corresponding evaluation metrics) ⚠️

[Uncertain] None of the three reports provide any data-strategy ablation experiment (no filtering-strictness ablation, no caption-density/style ablation, no quantitative data-mixture ablation comparison). Only qualitative attribution statements exist: Seedance 1.0 states that the CT stage's richer motion dynamics and more diverse captions, and the resulting more natural, fluid, higher-aesthetic-quality training data, bring a significant improvement in T2V visual fidelity; the SFT model-merging step significantly improves visual fidelity and motion quality; on the RLHF side, the only algorithm-level comparison is that the reward-maximization method is called the most efficient and effective compared to DPO/PPO/GRPO. Cross-version end-to-end improvements can serve as a coarse-grained substitute for evidence: on SeedVideoBench 2.0, Seedance 2.0 improves by an average of 0.86 points across six dimensions relative to Seedance 1.5, with motion quality showing the largest improvement (+1.36, 2.39→3.75), audio-video sync 2.91→3.75, audio quality 2.88→3.63, and audio prompt following 2.69→3.56; T2V usability rate (≥3 points) for motion quality goes from 46.93% to 97.55%, and for audio-video sync from 69.64% to 93.30%. But these are overall model-iteration results and cannot be attributed to any single data strategy.

### Evidence for quality vs. quantity (cases where small, refined data outperforms large, mixed data) ⚠️

There is no direct "small, refined data beats large, mixed data" controlled experiment. It is indirectly reflected in the overall methodological orientation: Seedance 1.0's funnel centers on strict quality-and-safety filtering + semantic deduplication (retaining only the single instance with the highest quality score within each near-duplicate cluster) + distribution rebalancing; the CT stage actively contracts to a higher-aesthetic, richer-motion subset; the SFT stage further converges to a manually verified, several-hundred-category curated set, with an emphasis on early stopping to prevent overfitting and preserve text controllability — the whole chain shows a design of progressively "trading quantity for quality" at each level. Seedance 1.5 pro sets the data pipeline's priority objectives as coherence, motion expressiveness, and curriculum scheduling rather than scale. [Uncertain: lacking quantitative evidence]

### Alignment relationship between training-data domain distribution and the evaluation-benchmark category system (e.g., VABench's seven major categories)

A clear alignment relationship exists, but it is a one-way inference (the data category system is not public, so it can only be reverse-inferred from the evaluation category system). SeedVideoBench expands in step with model iteration: version 1.0 covers motion dynamics, prompt following, aesthetic quality, and subject consistency; version 1.5 gives fine-grained case classification and attribute labels for core video-dimension aspects such as subject, motion dynamics, interaction, and camera movement, and adds application-scenario labels such as advertising, social-media content, and short-form narrative; since 1.5 supports joint audio-video generation, a complete audio dimension is added (three main categories: human voice types / human voice attributes / non-speech audio), the video metrics add "Video Vividness" (four dimensions: action, camera movement, atmosphere, emotion), and the audio metrics include audio prompt following, audio quality, audio-video sync, and audio expressiveness; version 2.0 adds a multi-modal task evaluation system (reference category: subject/motion/effects/style reference; editing category: subject/style/scene/audio content editing; extension category: plot continuation and seamless forward/backward extension; composite category: real-workflow-adjacent composite tasks such as "swap the video subject using a reference image") and consistency metrics (reference alignment, editing consistency), adds three narrative-quality sub-dimensions (cinematography language, plot design, stylized aesthetics), refines audio down to 17 categories, and splits evaluation into an objective automated pipeline and a subjective expert-blind-review track. The report explicitly states: "we constructed dedicated datasets covering subject, motion, scene, style, and audio, with sample distributions tuned to minimize variance under a small evaluation budget" — this sentence refers to the construction of the evaluation dataset, but its category system is highly isomorphic to the attribute dimensions used in the training side's "distribution rebalancing" (subject category, scene type, dominant action, genre, visual style, duration, resolution, motion characteristics).
