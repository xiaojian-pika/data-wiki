## Basic Information

### Name

Merged entry: ① Mochi 1 (including mochi-1-preview and AsymmVAE) ② MAGI-1 (including the 24B / 4.5B variants, the Distill distilled version, and the 2026 MAGI-1.1 24B) ③ Motif-Video 2B (including T2V and I2V extensions). All three are open-source, vision-only (silent) video generation foundation models; the core value of juxtaposing them is that the granularity of data disclosure shows a clear 2024→2026 evolution curve: from "almost zero" (Mochi 1) → "method-level detail but no numbers" (MAGI-1) → "tool-level + parameter-level detail, explicitly winning with a smaller dataset" (Motif-Video 2B).

### Releasing institution/company

① Mochi 1: Genmo (Genmo AI, a San Francisco, US startup; CEO/co-founder Paras Jain, co-founder Ajay Jain, both with UC Berkeley PhD backgrounds; completed an approximately $28.4M Series A in 2024, led by NEA).
② MAGI-1: Sand AI (三体智能 / sand.ai, Beijing, China; founder Yue Cao (曹越), a Tsinghua University PhD and former Microsoft Research Asia researcher, one of the authors of Swin Transformer; the technical report is credited to 39 authors).
③ Motif-Video 2B: Motif Technologies (a Korean AI company, sharing origins with the AI chip/compiler company Moreh; previously released the Motif-2.6B / Motif-2-12.7B language model series).

### Release date (technical report/paper/open-source date)

① Mochi 1: released and open-sourced mochi-1-preview (weights + inference code, Apache 2.0) on October 22, 2024, with a teaser for a 720p HD version announced the same day (never fully open-sourced on schedule afterward); AsymmVAE and fine-tuning scripts were open-sourced progressively around November 2024. There is no formal paper or arXiv technical report — only an official blog post.
② MAGI-1: open-sourced the 24B weights and inference code on April 21, 2025 (GitHub SandAI-org/MAGI-1); published the arXiv technical report arXiv:2505.13211 "MAGI-1: Autoregressive Video Generation at Scale" (61 pages) on May 19, 2025; released the 4.5B version on April 30, 2025; released the 4.5B Distill on May 26, 2025; added ComfyUI support on May 30, 2025; open-sourced MAGI-1.1 24B on June 17, 2026.
③ Motif-Video 2B: released weights on Hugging Face on April 14, 2026 (Motif-Technologies/Motif-Video-2B, Apache 2.0); arXiv:2604.16503 "Motif-Video 2B: Technical Report," with the v2 version updated on May 19, 2026; official Diffusers integration and a community GGUF-quantized version were provided in the same period.

### Type (model/dataset/toolchain/evaluation benchmark)

All three are "model + supporting toolchain":
① Mochi 1: a 10B-parameter Asymmetric Diffusion Transformer (AsymmDiT, 48 layers, visual dimension 3072 / text dimension 1536) text-to-video model + an in-house AsymmVAE (362M parameters, 8×8 spatial + 6× temporal compression into a 12-channel latent space, total compression ratio 96×). Output: 848×480, 30 FPS, up to 5.4 seconds (84 frames).
② MAGI-1: a chunk-wise autoregressive denoising video world model, up to 24B parameters, 24 frames per chunk, context length up to 4 million tokens, supporting unified modeling of T2V / I2V / V2V video continuation and streaming generation; comes with an in-house Transformer VAE (8× spatial / 4× temporal downsampling, 16 channels), the MagiAttention distributed attention library, and Shortcut Model distillation.
③ Motif-Video 2B: a 2B-parameter rectified flow matching text-to-video model + an image-to-video extension, whose core innovations are Shared Cross-Attention and a three-stage backbone functional decomposition (early fusion / joint representation / detail decoding); the training side uses TREAD token routing and REPA representation alignment; it additionally contributes an "offline bucket-balanced sampler" data infrastructure. It is explicitly positioned as a control experiment for the "micro-budget" route.

### Degree of openness (whether weights/code/data/pipeline are each open source)

① Mochi 1 (partially open, data side fully closed): weights and inference code are open-sourced under Apache 2.0 (GitHub genmoai/mochi, HF genmo/mochi-1-preview), AsymmVAE is open-sourced alongside them, and commercial use is permitted. However there is no technical report/paper, no data-pipeline disclosure whatsoever; the training data, cleaning pipeline, and annotation method are all undisclosed, and the official stance explicitly is that data sources are "not disclosed for competitive reasons." This can be regarded as "open weights, closed method, closed data."
② MAGI-1 (highly open, data method open but numbers closed): open-sourced under Apache 2.0 are the 24B / 4.5B base and distilled weights, an FP8-quantized version, and inference code, plus the standalone MagiAttention library; the 61-page technical report fully discloses the entire methodology of Section 3 DATA (the segmentation tool, 11 categories of filter actors, the dual-model deduplication, the MLLM secondary filtering, the two caption schemas, and the three-stage data configuration table). However all filtering thresholds are phrased as "predefined threshold / lower and upper thresholds" without giving numbers, the dataset scale is given only as an order-of-magnitude phrase — "tens of petabytes of raw material" — and neither the cleaning code nor the data itself is released. This can be regarded as "open weights, open method, closed parameters, closed data."
③ Motif-Video 2B (the most thorough data disclosure among the three): weights open-sourced under Apache 2.0; the technical report writes the data pipeline as a reproducible engineering document — named tools (NeMo Curator, ffmpeg cropdetect, PaddleOCR-VL, SigLIP, Aesthetic Predictor V2.5, DOVER, UniMatch, SSCD sscd_disc_mixup, NVIDIA cuVS, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B), named hyperparameters (SSCD 512-dimensional descriptor / 320×320 / frame 10 / cosine≥0.9 / k=64 / nprobe=16, OCR clusters must appear in ≥50% of frames, top and bottom 20% regions excluded, caption sampling probabilities 0.5/0.3/0.2, SA optimization for 30,000 iterations, rolling-shuffle window of 4096), and a complete 10-stage curriculum. However the dataset itself and the cleaning code are likewise not open-sourced, and the Sankey diagram (Fig. 7) gives only flow directions, not absolute numbers. This can be regarded as "open weights, open method, open parameters, closed data."

### Whether joint audio-video generation is supported, and the implementation approach (native joint/cascade/MoE fusion)

None of the three supports joint audio-video generation; all are purely visual (silent) video generation models.
① Mochi 1: outputs 848×480 / 30fps / 5.4s silent video; the text encoder is a single T5-XXL, the architecture has no audio branch, and neither the official blog nor the model card mentions audio.
② MAGI-1: the full technical report (including Section 3 DATA and Section 4 Infrastructure) does not involve audio-track extraction, audio filtering, or audio-visual alignment; the data pipeline retains only the visual frame sequence after PySceneDetect segmentation. MAGI-1.1 likewise does not add audio capability.
③ Motif-Video 2B: the data pipeline explicitly processes only the four visual branches Image Real / Image Synthetic / Video Real / Video Synthetic; the caption's JSON schema contains no auditory field whatsoever, and the full text has no audio/speech/music-related processing.
Therefore, in this entry, all audio-related dimensions (audio_category_distribution, joint_av_caption_schema, dialogue_transcription_attributes, av_sync_detection, sync_metric_and_threshold, audio_quality_filtering, audio_type_handling) are "not applicable."

## Data Scale and Distribution

### Training data scale (video count/hours/token count, pretraining vs SFT split separately) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: completely undisclosed. The official side never gave video count, hour count, or token count, only stating that the model was "trained entirely from scratch" and was "the largest video generation model publicly released at the time." [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: only gives the order of magnitude of the raw material — "from tens of petabytes of raw videos and images collected from a wide range of sources." The number of clips, hours, or per-stage sample counts in the cleaned training set are all undisclosed; Table 5 only states that "the data volume decreases across stages" without giving numbers. Pretraining and SFT are also not measured separately (the report has no independent SFT stage; see post_training_data). [Uncertain]
③ Motif-Video 2B: gives an explicit, deliberately conservative upper bound — "fewer than 10M clips" and "less than 100,000 H200 GPU hours." The HF card states "approximately 10 million video clips." The paper contrasts this with contemporaneous open-source models' "hundreds of millions of curated clips" (Wan2.1, HunyuanVideo, Seedance, etc., which use hundreds of millions of clips and have 5B–14B parameters), claiming its data volume is an order of magnitude lower and its parameter count 7 times smaller. The SFT corpus scale is not given as an absolute number, only described as a "curated high-quality subset" iteratively supplemented by subject category (Fig. 8 gives distribution proportions, not absolute quantities). [Partially uncertain: the absolute scale of the curated SFT set]

### Data source composition (proprietary/public datasets/web scraping/licensed procurement/synthetic data) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: the official side has only a single statement — Paras Jain told VentureBeat, "Generally, we use publicly available data and sometimes work with a variety of data partners," declining to elaborate for competitive reasons. Neither any named public dataset nor the scraping/licensing ratio was specified. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: described only as "collected from a wide range of sources," without distinguishing proprietary crawling, public datasets, licensed procurement, or synthetic data, and without naming any source dataset. From "tens of petabytes of raw material + a self-built scalable data-processing system + PySceneDetect segmenting long videos," one can infer that large-scale proprietary web scraping is the primary source, but the paper does not state this explicitly. [Uncertain]
③ Motif-Video 2B: explicitly two-pronged — "an internal web-scale video collection" and "a set of publicly available image and video datasets," both routed through the same downstream sanitation / filtering / deduplication / staged quality-control pipeline to ensure the final corpus is governed by a single standard. In addition, the raw pool is explicitly split into four branches — Image Real / Image Synthetic / Video Real / Video Synthetic — i.e., acknowledging the use of synthetic data, and it stipulates that "synthetic video is injected only at 720p," on the grounds that its controllable quality best matches that stage's admission criteria. However, no specific public dataset names or per-source ratios are given. [Partially uncertain: the specific list of public datasets and their proportions]

### Data compliance and provenance (share of licensed data, rights-cleared datasets, C2PA, etc.) ⚠️

None of the three provides substantive disclosure on data compliance and provenance — this is the single biggest common gap across the three models in this group:
① Mochi 1: the data source is deliberately vague, which itself became the focal controversy at launch — VentureBeat's report directly states that "training datasets are one of the most contentious aspects of AI creative tools, as evidence has emerged that many tools have used large amounts of human work scraped from the internet without permission or payment, some of which is copyrighted," while Jain "was coy" about it. No disclosure of the share of licensed data, rights-cleared datasets, C2PA content credentials, or watermark provenance. The model card only notes, at the downstream level, that "Genmo video models may reflect biases and stereotypes present in the training data," recommending that organizations add safety protocols before commercial deployment. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: the technical report has no data-compliance section; it does not discuss copyright, licensing ratio, C2PA, or a source list, providing only Apache 2.0 at the weights level. [Uncertain]
③ Motif-Video 2B: likewise no compliance section; the licensed-data share, rights-cleared data, or content credentials are not disclosed. The relative indirect compliance action is front-loading NSFW and watermark filtering into the first stage of sanitation (a watermark/on-screen logo is itself a proxy signal for copyright), and using a VLM at the captioning stage to recheck the watermark label for secondary removal — which can be understood as "using engineering means to avoid material bearing rights markers," though the paper does not frame it as a compliance measure. [Uncertain]

### Clip duration distribution and segmentation strategy ⚠️

① Mochi 1: the training-side duration distribution and segmentation strategy are entirely undisclosed; only the generation-side cap is known — 84 frames / 30 FPS / 5.4 seconds. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: gives per-stage duration upper bounds (Table 5) — stage-1 and stage-2 are both "≤ 8 seconds," stage-3 relaxes to "≤ 16 seconds"; the paper explains that extending the duration in the last stage lets the model capture richer temporal dynamics. The segmentation strategy uses PySceneDetect to cut single-shot clips (see shot_segmentation). No lower bound and no distribution histogram are given. [Partially uncertain: the duration lower bound and the actual distribution]
③ Motif-Video 2B: sets an explicit lower bound after segmentation — "clips shorter than two seconds after merging are discarded" — to ensure that every training clip covers a meaningful temporal span. The training-side duration is actually determined by frame-count buckets: four tiers of single-frame image, 33 frames, 65 frames, and 121 frames, with clip-length filtering re-applied at every stage transition (the threshold tightens as resolution increases). No duration histogram is given. [Partially uncertain: the specific distribution]

### Resolution/aspect-ratio distribution and bucketing strategy ⚠️

① Mochi 1: the resolution and aspect-ratio distribution of the training data and the bucketing strategy are undisclosed; the output is fixed at 848×480 (approximately 16:9). [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: training resolution advances across three stages — stage-1 is 256p/360p, stage-2 is 480p, stage-3 is 720p (Table 5). At the VAE inference side, a sliding window supports arbitrary resolution (spatial window 256×256, stride 192, 25% overlap; no overlap in the temporal dimension); the model claims to support arbitrary resolution, and the 4.5B version defaults to 720×720. However, the aspect-ratio distribution and bucket weighting of the training data are undisclosed. [Partially uncertain: aspect-ratio bucketing]
③ Motif-Video 2B: resolution is the primary stratification axis of the entire pipeline — the curriculum progresses through four tiers, 144p → 360p → 480p → 720p, across ten stages, and every stage transition re-applies the four filter categories of resolution / clip-length / motion / aesthetics with stricter thresholds. Two specific engineering measures handle aspect ratio: first, ffmpeg cropdetect estimates the maximal content rectangle based on luminance statistics to remove letterbox/pillarbox black-bar padding; second, the OCR-detected regions are merged with the black-bar crop box into a single final rectangle (excluding the top 20% for on-screen logos and bottom 20% for subtitles above the content area), applied together with resolution scaling and frame-rate limiting in a single ffmpeg re-encode. During training, samples are jointly bucketed by "frame-count bucket × resolution bucket" (frame-count buckets: 1 / 33 / 65 / 121, each further subdivided into multiple spatial resolutions), with the offline bucket-balanced sampler ensuring the coefficient of variation in per-bucket sample counts across ranks is minimized. No quantitative aspect-ratio distribution is given. [Partially uncertain: quantitative aspect-ratio distribution]

### Category/domain distribution and mixture strategy (proportion control and concept balancing for people, actions, scenes, styles, etc.) ⚠️

This is the dimension with the greatest divergence among the three, forming an evolution line from "none → dynamic adaptive → explicit category balancing":
① Mochi 1: no disclosure whatsoever. Neither category statistics nor any mention of concept balancing or mixture control is given. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: does not use a static mixture table; instead it proposes "Dynamic Distribution Adjustment" — the paper explicitly states that "an appropriate data distribution is crucial for training high-performance models, but determining the optimal distribution in advance is extremely difficult," and gives a real observation: "during training we found that landscape scenes are relatively easy for the model to learn, whereas human facial expressions are significantly harder" — an insight that could not have been predicted beforehand. Its approach is therefore to continuously monitor the model's performance across semantic concepts throughout training and adaptively raise the sampling ratio of underfit subsets based on evaluation results, targeting the model's weaknesses. This is "online data-mixture adjustment driven by an evaluation-feedback loop," closer to the true spirit of curriculum learning than a static mixture table, but the paper discloses no category list, initial mixture, or adjustment magnitude. MAGI-1 also mentions a task-level mixture: because the product scenario is dominated by image-to-video, "a larger proportion of I2V tasks was allocated during training." [Partially uncertain: the category list and mixture values]
③ Motif-Video 2B: performs explicit category balancing and closes the loop by attaching it to caption metadata. The VLM produces subject and style structured labels in the same forward pass, and these two labels "drive domain balancing for the 720p stage and SFT." More critically, the SFT corpus is assembled by iteratively patching weaknesses: "we run intermediate evaluations on the latest checkpoint, identify the subject categories with the weakest generation quality, and then specifically supplement clips from those categories." Fig. 8 gives the end result — on the image side, People dominates, reflecting character-centric use cases; on the video side, the distribution tilts toward Transportation, Sports, and Animals, because these three categories involve intense dynamics and were identified as weaknesses in intermediate evaluations. This is structurally isomorphic to MAGI-1's dynamic adjustment idea, but Motif renders it into a readable category-distribution chart, and additionally uses the action=Dynamic label as the dynamic-motion admission condition for 720p SFT.

### Audio category distribution and mixture (proportions and control strategy for speech/foley/music/ambient sound/silence) — a dimension unique to AV models

Not applicable. All three models are purely visual video generation models; the training data retains no audio track, and neither the technical reports nor the blog posts touch on the categorization or mixture control of speech, foley, music, ambient sound, or silence.

### Narrative structure distribution (single-shot vs. multi-shot, average clip duration, shot-count distribution, whether a native audio track is present) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: undisclosed. The generation side outputs a single fixed 5.4-second video, suggesting the training material is single-shot short clips, but there is no official statement to this effect. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: the training clips are strictly single-shot — PySceneDetect segmentation "ensures that each clip contains only a single shot," with an additional Transition Detection actor as a backstop (PySceneDetect handles complex transitions poorly, so clips it produces may still contain multiple shots; hence it sparsely samples keyframes, computes CLIP semantic similarity between adjacent keyframes, and judges a clip as multi-shot and discards it entirely if the similarity falls below a threshold). Average clip duration by stage is ≤8s / ≤8s / ≤16s. No native audio track.
Worth singling out: although MAGI-1's training material is single-shot, it obtains multi-shot narrative capability at inference time through autoregressive captioning (see caption_structure) and chunk-wise text conditioning — the technical report's Fig. 10 shows an example of nearly 30 seconds, driven segment by segment by seven different prompts (a)–(g), generating complex action and narrative structure; Fig. 12 further shows two types of shot transition achieved by modulating the KV range across different denoising stages — "identity-preserving shot switching" and "transitions that keep the scene layout but change object details." In other words, multi-shot narrative in MAGI-1 is realized through "architecture + caption schema" rather than "multi-shot training data," which is an interesting point of contrast on the data side.
③ Motif-Video 2B: likewise strictly single-shot, with a more refined segmentation strategy: first, scene boundaries are detected with a conservative threshold biased toward "over-segmentation (better a false positive than a missed transition)"; then SigLIP embedding similarity is used for stitch detection, re-merging continuous shots that were mistakenly cut apart by momentary motion or exposure changes; finally, clips shorter than 2 seconds after merging are discarded. In addition, the VLM-produced multi_scene label is used as a "secondary check on scene segmentation," discarding the clip if triggered. No native audio track. Shot count is always 1; there is no construction of multi-shot narrative data.

### Language/accent distribution (the data basis for multilingual lip-sync capability) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: the text encoder is a single T5-XXL (predominantly English); the language composition of the captions is undisclosed. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: captions are generated by an MLLM; the examples (Table 4) are all in English, and the language distribution is undisclosed. There is one indirect clue: to mitigate the distribution mismatch between training captions and real user input, MAGI-1 designed a Prompt Enhancement (PE) strategy at inference time and distilled the large MLLM's rewriting capability into a roughly 7B small model, building a training corpus of about 2 million entries — but the language composition of this corpus is not stated. [Uncertain]
③ Motif-Video 2B: captions are generated by Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B; the schema and examples are all in English, with caption_long defined as 150–250 words and caption_short as 15–25 words, explicitly measured in words — effectively English-only. The text encoder is T5Gemma2 (a sentence-level embedding model in the earlier stages). No multilingual captions or language-distribution statistics are disclosed.
The lip-sync/accent dimension is not applicable to any of the three (no audio). [Uncertain]

## Cleaning Pipeline

### Cleaning funnel overall structure (how many filtering tiers, order of each tier) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: no pipeline disclosure whatsoever. The official blog covers only the architecture (AsymmDiT, AsymmVAE, 44,520-token full 3D attention, extended 3D RoPE, SwiGLU, QK-Norm) and the evaluation protocol (following OpenAI DALL-E 3's prompt-adherence automated evaluation paradigm, using Gemini-1.5-Pro-002 as the judge model); the data section is entirely absent. The only data-processing-related statement in the model card is that "NSFW content filtering has been applied." [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1 (a four-tier funnel, complete at the method level but blank at the parameter level, Fig. 13):
[Tier 1] Shot segmentation: PySceneDetect cuts long videos into single-shot short clips (applied only to video data; images skip this module).
[Tier 2] Parallel filtering by Filter Actors: 11 categories of dedicated filters act on the segmented clips in one pass (see quality_filtering / motion_filtering) — Video Quality Assessment (DOVER), Aesthetics (the LAION aesthetics model), Overexposed/Underexposed (mean brightness in HSI color space), Motion Strength (RAFT optical flow + saliency detection split into foreground/background), Camera Movement Stability (optical-flow consistency between adjacent frames), Slides Movement (optical-flow divergence), Border Detection (edge detection + Hough transform), Text Detection (a dedicated spatiotemporal pattern-based identification for subtitles), Logo Detection (open-vocabulary detection with Florence-2), Corner Face Detection (face detection + confidence for corner position), and Transition Detection (CLIP keyframe semantic similarity).
[Tier 3] Deduplication: dual-feature pairwise similarity from CLIP and DINOv2; a clip is deleted as a duplicate if either exceeds threshold.
[Tier 4] MLLM advanced filtering: the first three tiers have already removed most low-quality data, and the remaining volume is already greatly reduced; at this point a multimodal large model is introduced for a round of semantic-level re-screening to catch complex bad cases; the key engineering design is that "this step can be seamlessly integrated into the subsequent caption procedure, thereby reducing overall cost and improving efficiency" — i.e., the "judge" call and the "annotator" call reuse the same MLLM invocation.
[Tier 5] Captioning: data that passes filtering is captioned by the same MLLM, producing a highly descriptive caption and an auto-regressive caption.
[Tier 6] Distribution adjustment: multi-stage adjustment (quality tightened stage by stage) + dynamic distribution adjustment (mixture changed online based on evaluation feedback).
Design point: cheap rule-based/small-model filters are placed first, and the expensive MLLM is placed after the data volume has already shrunk substantially, letting the MLLM's "judge" and "annotator" roles reuse the same forward pass.
③ Motif-Video 2B (a branched funnel complete at the tool level and parameter level, Fig. 7 Sankey diagram):
[Tier 0] Branch decomposition: the raw pool is explicitly split into four branches — Image Real / Image Synthetic / Video Real / Video Synthetic — sharing the same downstream controls.
[Tier 1] Sanitation (front-loaded "disinfection"): removes corrupted/undecodable files, abnormally small files (thumbnails or download-corrupted files), SSCD near-duplicates, NSFW content, and watermarked content. NSFW and watermarks use dual signals — first an OCR pre-screen inherited from the legacy crawling pipeline (using on-frame text detection to flag high-confidence watermarks, on-screen logos, burned-in subtitles, etc.), and surviving clips are then rechecked by a VLM against structured labels (watermark / nsfw / padded / multi_scene / timelapse / quality), discarded if any is triggered; the paper calls this second pass "a semantically aware safety net on top of OCR."
[Tier 2] Black-bar detection: ffmpeg cropdetect estimates the maximal content rectangle based on luminance statistics.
[Tier 3] OCR detection: PaddleOCR-VL (served via vLLM) runs on N uniformly sampled frames per clip, clustering detection boxes across frames by spatial IoU, keeping only clusters that appear in ≥50% of frames (to distinguish fixed overlays from momentary on-scene text); the surviving OCR regions and the black-bar crop box are merged into a single final rectangle (excluding the top 20% for on-screen logos and bottom 20% for subtitles above the content area), applied in a single ffmpeg re-encode together with resolution scaling and frame-rate limiting.
[Tier 4] Scene segmentation and length control: over-segmentation with a conservative threshold + SigLIP-similarity stitch merging + discarding clips <2s.
[Tier 5] Multi-path visual-quality filtering: Aesthetic Predictor V2.5 (aesthetics), brightness (the OpenHumanVid formula), a Koala-36M-style modeled suitability score (conservative rejection), the DOVER technical score (technical quality), and UniMatch optical flow (motion quality, two-sided truncation). The paper emphasizes that these signals are "not combined into a single learned ranking; rather, each filter specifically targets one failure mode."
[Tier 6] Three-stage SSCD deduplication (embedding / grouping / representative selection, see deduplication).
[Tier 7] VLM captioning-as-metadata: Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B produces the caption and structured labels simultaneously in a single forward pass, and the labels are fed directly back into sanitation and staged filtering.
[Tier 8] Staged progressive admission: 144p → 360p → 480p → 720p, with resolution/duration/motion/aesthetic filtering re-applied at every transition with a stricter threshold; 720p SFT additionally layers on domain-balancing and dynamic-motion admission; synthetic video is injected only at 720p.
Design point, isomorphic to MAGI-1 (VLM captioning and filtering reuse one forward pass), but Motif goes further — "because these labels come from the same forward pass as the training caption, filtering and conditioning remain synchronized by construction" — i.e., structurally eliminating any drift between "the semantic judgment used for filtering" and "the text condition used for training."

### Shot-segmentation method (PySceneDetect/in-house model/shot-aware splitting) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: undisclosed. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: uses the open-source PySceneDetect to cut long videos into short clips, explicitly requiring that "each clip contains only a single shot." Because PySceneDetect handles complex transitions (fades, dissolves, etc.) poorly and the resulting clips may still retain multiple shots, the team added an independent Transition Detection filter as a backstop: it sparsely samples keyframes, computes CLIP semantic similarity between adjacent keyframes, and if it falls below a predetermined threshold, judges the clip to contain multiple shots and discards it entirely. Note that its backstop strategy is "deletion," not "re-segmentation."
③ Motif-Video 2B: adopts a bidirectional "over-segment then semantically re-merge" strategy, the most elaborate of the three. Step one detects scene boundaries with a "conservative threshold biased toward over-segmentation," i.e., deliberately allowing more false positives (over-cutting) than false negatives (missed transitions); step two uses stitch detection based on SigLIP embedding similarity to re-merge adjacent clips, specifically to recover continuous shots that were mistakenly cut apart by momentary intense motion or exposure changes; step three discards clips shorter than 2 seconds after merging. A third backstop comes from the VLM's multi_scene label — discarded if triggered, which the paper explicitly calls "a secondary check on scene segmentation." Compared with MAGI-1, Motif's difference is that it acknowledges the segmenter can err in both directions and provides separate remedies for each (merging fixes over-cutting, the VLM label fixes missed cuts), whereas MAGI-1 addresses only the missed-cut side.

### Quality filtering (aesthetic score, sharpness, OCR text filtering, black-bar/watermark/logo detection) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: only NSFW filtering is known to have been applied; all other quality-filtering strategies, tools, and thresholds are undisclosed. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1 (11 categories of Filter Actor, thoroughly detailed at the method level, all thresholds left blank):
· Video quality assessment: uses DOVER, whose output has three scores — overall / aesthetic / technical; the team empirically found that "the technical score alone is the most effective indicator," so only the technical score is used.
· Aesthetics: the LAION aesthetics predictor; because this model was designed for images, the team uses "the first frame's aesthetic score" to represent the quality of the whole clip (note this differs from Allegro's choice of the middle frame).
· Overexposure/underexposure: converts every frame to HSI color space and computes the whole-clip average brightness, filtering out over- or under-exposed samples; the paper states that such data "adversely affect training stability" — attributing the motivation for exposure filtering to training stability rather than visual perception, a notable phrasing.
· Border detection: frame-by-frame edge detection + Hough transform to identify horizontal/vertical lines persisting across frames, treated as candidate borders, using the "proportion of frames containing a border" as the filtering confidence.
· Text detection: a clip is discarded entirely if any frame has too many characters or the text region occupies too large a proportion of the frame. Subtitles are handled as a special case — subtitle text has few characters and a small area, not easily caught by the above rules, but it has a distinctive spatiotemporal pattern (fixed at the top or bottom of the frame, persisting across many consecutive frames), which is used to specifically identify and remove it.
· Logo detection: uses Florence-2 (which supports open-vocabulary object detection), given a preset keyword set, to detect and localize logos in the frame and output a confidence used for filtering.
· Corner face detection: in commentary-style videos, a host often appears fixed in a corner of the frame; the team uses a face-detection model combined with face position and detection confidence, averaged across frames for the fixed-corner face, to estimate the likelihood that "a narrator is present" and remove it.
All the above filters' thresholds are phrased in the report as "predefined threshold" without giving numbers.
③ Motif-Video 2B (5 orthogonal quality signals, tools and some parameters named):
· Aesthetics: Aesthetic Predictor V2.5 (a SigLIP-based image-level aesthetics predictor); for each video, frames are uniformly sampled over time, a frame-level score is computed, then averaged across sampled frames to get a video-level score; used as a staged filter — the low-aesthetics tail is removed, and "cutoff becomes stricter at higher-resolution stages."
· Brightness: uses the OpenHumanVid formula L = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B (ITU-R BT.709 luminance weighting), computed on sampled frames to remove the extreme low/high brightness tails for the target stage, filtering out severe under-/over-exposure and improving the visibility of subjects and scene content in the retained data.
· Model-based Suitability Score: inspired by Koala-36M, using a single estimate that aggregates multiple quality factors into "whether this video is suitable for training a video generation model"; used conservatively in practice, only as a rejection filter that removes the lowest-suitability tail, with remaining samples still needing to pass subsequent specialized filters.
· Technical quality: DOVER, taking its technical-quality-related output, filtering compression artifacts, noise, distortion, low sharpness, and other degradations.
· Text/watermark/black bars: see pipeline_overview Tiers 1–3 (OCR pre-screen + VLM watermark labels + cropdetect + PaddleOCR-VL persistent-region clustering).
· VLM quality label: clips with quality=low are excluded starting from the 480p stage.
The paper specifically emphasizes that these signals are "not used as a single learned ranking," but rather each filter targets one specific failure mode (poor exposure, compression artifacts, staticness, temporal instability) — a clear methodological contrast with approaches that fold multiple indicators into one composite score.

### Motion filtering (optical-flow/motion-score thresholds, removal of static and shaky footage) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: undisclosed. The official side emphasizes only, at the capability level, Mochi 1's advantage in motion quality (30 FPS, high temporal consistency, realistic motion dynamics); the CEO also stated the team was "focusing heavily on improving motion quality," but there is no statement of a corresponding data-side motion-filtering strategy. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1 (the most detailed of the three, and the only one to separate foreground/background motion):
· Motion strength: uses the RAFT optical-flow model; to reduce computational cost, all videos are first downsampled to 8 FPS before computing optical flow between adjacent frames; optical flow is computed at the pixel level, and the flow-magnitude average over all pixels in the clip gives the overall motion strength.
· Foreground/background separation (a key improvement): the paper notes that the above approach underestimates motion when "the background is static but the foreground moves substantially"; to address this, a saliency-detection model (Zhao & Wu, 2019) is additionally run on each frame to distinguish foreground and background regions via the saliency map, and the average optical flow is computed separately for each. This yields three motion statistics — overall motion strength, foreground motion strength, and background motion strength — each with its own upper and lower thresholds, prioritizing clips of moderate motion strength while avoiding both extremes of too static and too dynamic, to balance data quality and training difficulty.
· Camera-motion stability: because a large amount of material is handheld footage with severe shake that is hard to catch with motion strength alone, the team estimates camera stability by evaluating the consistency of optical flow between adjacent frames, removing clips with unstable camera motion.
· Slideshow-style motion: targeting floating photos/banners common in screen recordings or slide presentations, the divergence of the whole-frame pixel optical flow is analyzed per frame; if the divergence stays low for a sustained period, it is judged as slide movement and removed.
· Transitions: see shot_segmentation.
No thresholds are given numerically.
③ Motif-Video 2B: uses UniMatch to estimate optical flow between sampled frame pairs and compute a motion score per video, with two-sided truncation — removing both extremely low motion (usually static or near-static) and extremely high motion (often involving cuts, shake, or unstable camera motion), retaining the middle band to match the smooth temporal dynamics required by the main training stages. Motion filtering is re-applied with a stricter threshold at every resolution-stage transition. In addition, the 720p SFT stage introduces a semantic motion-admission condition: the VLM label action=Dynamic is used as the "dynamic-motion criterion" — i.e., on top of the low-level optical-flow statistics, an additional semantic threshold of "whether the large model judges this video's action to count as dynamic" is layered on. This is a concrete case in this group where motion filtering moves from "pixel statistics" to "semantic judgment."

### Deduplication method (exact deduplication and embedding-based semantic deduplication recorded separately) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: undisclosed. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: the paper traces the importance of deduplication back to the LLM literature — citing Lee et al. (2021) and Hernandez et al. (2022) to note that "even a small amount of duplicate data can significantly harm performance," and accordingly "conducts rigorous de-duplication." The method is a dual-feature cross-determination: computing pairwise similarity between clips using both CLIP and DINOv2, and judging a clip as a duplicate and deleting it if either similarity exceeds the threshold (a logical OR, biased toward strictness). The intent of using the CLIP + DINOv2 dual-tower approach is to cover both semantic-similarity and visual/self-supervised structural-similarity types of duplication. The threshold value and deduplication scale are not given, nor is it explained how the O(N²) pairwise computation is avoided at large scale. [Partially uncertain: threshold, indexing scheme, and deduplication volume]
③ Motif-Video 2B (the only one of the three to give reproducible engineering detail): a three-stage SSCD pipeline —
· Embedding: encodes every image/video using the publicly released sscd_disc_mixup TorchScript model; frames are first resized to 320×320 and ImageNet-normalized, producing a 512-dimensional descriptor. For video, the 10th frame is taken as the representative frame — the paper explicitly explains two reasons for this choice: avoiding the opening-credit and logo bias of the very first frames, and avoiding pairwise comparison of all frames to keep matching computationally tractable. The reason for choosing SSCD is that it is designed for copy detection and is robust to re-encoding, cropping, and light editing — precisely the most common forms of duplication in web-scraped video.
· Grouping: uses NVIDIA cuVS's multi-GPU IVF-PQ index to retrieve under cosine distance, taking k=64 nearest neighbors per query with nprobe=16, keeping only pairs with cosine similarity above 0.9, then using Union-Find to merge pairs into duplicate groups.
· Representative selection: within each duplicate group, a single sample is kept according to the weighted score s = 0.5·res^ + 0.3·fps^ + 0.2·filesize^ (all three terms min-max normalized within the group), with the remaining group members discarded. This rule favors copies with higher resolution, higher frame rate, and less re-compression.
Deduplication is placed at the very first stage of sanitation (rather than at the end of the funnel), meaning that all subsequent expensive model inference is never wasted on duplicate samples.

### VLM/LLM as quality inspector (multimodal-large-model quality scoring and mismatch removal — the 2026 trend from shallow scorers toward large-model semantic judgment) ⚠️

The three models in this group happen to fully illustrate the evolution of "VLM as quality inspector" from absent to becoming the hub of the pipeline — the dimension of highest comparative value in this entry:
① Mochi 1 (2024.10): no disclosure whatsoever, and no evidence of large-model scoring being used. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1 (2025.05, MLLM as an "advanced filter," positioned near the end of the funnel): the report has a dedicated subsection 3.3 "MLLM as Advanced Filter," with very clear logic — the preceding Filter Actors and deduplication have already removed most bad data, but limited by these filters' capabilities, a small amount of low-quality data remains; by this point the data volume has already shrunk significantly, so a multimodal large model is introduced for another round of filtering, "enables us to detect more complex bad cases." The key cost design is: "Notably, this step can be seamlessly integrated into the subsequent caption procedure, thereby reducing overall costs and improving efficiency" — i.e., scoring and captioning reuse the same MLLM call. However the paper does not name the specific MLLM model or scale used, nor does it state which dimensions it judges or what structure it outputs. [Partially uncertain: MLLM model and judgment dimensions]
③ Motif-Video 2B (2026.04, VLM upgraded from "end-of-funnel re-screening" to "a metadata source spanning the entire pipeline"): its caption-as-metadata design pushes VLM scoring into a new form — a single forward pass of Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B outputs, per a fixed JSON schema, both a free-text caption and structured labels (subject, style, action, camera_move, quality, watermark, nsfw, plus padded, multi_scene, timelapse), which are then reused in four ways: (i) as the text condition during training; (ii) for sanitation (nsfw / watermark / padded / multi_scene / timelapse / quality directly triggering hard deletion); (iii) for domain and subject-balanced sampling (style / subject driving domain balancing for 720p and SFT); (iv) for the dynamic-motion filter at 720p SFT (action=Dynamic). The paper also explicitly states, "we do not apply a separate post-processing model to reinterpret them" — the labels are consumed directly by the VLM. Its key methodological claim is: "because these labels come from the same forward pass as the training caption, filtering and conditioning remain synchronized by construction" — a clear design assertion that fully merges "model quality inspection" with "model captioning," eliminating semantic drift between the two, representing the mature form of this paradigm in 2026.
Viewed together: Mochi 1 (none) → MAGI-1 (MLLM end-of-funnel re-screening, call reused with captioning to save cost) → Motif (VLM forward pass as the metadata source itself, filtering and conditioning synchronized by construction) — the evolution direction is the VLM's position in the pipeline continually moving forward and its role continually expanding.

## Captioning Approach

### Captioning model used (in-house VLM/open-source model, model scale) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: the captioning model is entirely undisclosed. The only known item is the text encoder used at training time — a single T5-XXL (the official side emphasizes "a single T5-XXL language model rather than multiple pretrained models," i.e., deliberately avoiding multi-encoder fusion), with text dimension 1536 and visual dimension 3072, the visual stream having roughly 4× the parameters of the text stream. Whether a VLM was used for recaptioning, and the caption length and structure, are entirely unknown. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: uses "an MLLM" to caption the data, but the technical report never names the specific model or scale (nor states whether it is in-house or an open-source fine-tune). There is one explicit engineering parameter: since mainstream MLLMs are mostly designed for images, the team extracts a set of keyframes from each video to form an image sequence for input; through empirical analysis, "using 4 to 12 frames per video clip (depending on duration) achieves the best trade-off between description accuracy and computational efficiency." There is one further scale figure that belongs to the inference side rather than the captioning side: for lightweight deployment, the team distilled the large MLLM's generated enhanced prompts into a roughly 7B small model, with a training corpus of about 2 million entries (filtering out samples whose target text is excessively long to control output length); human evaluation shows the distilled model's generation quality is comparable to the large model, with substantially reduced latency and compute cost. [Partially uncertain: the captioning MLLM's model and scale]
③ Motif-Video 2B: explicitly named — all captions and labels come from Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B (a 30B-total-parameter / 3B-active MoE vision-language model). Video input is N uniformly sampled frames from the clip, while images are fed in directly. There is also an independent OCR-specific model, PaddleOCR-VL (served via vLLM), for on-frame text detection. For the text encoder: stages 1–3 use a sentence-level embedding model, switching to T5Gemma2 from stage 4 onward — the paper borrows PixArt-α's "class-conditional to text-conditional" curriculum idea, assuming that a lower-dimensional conditioning space can accelerate early convergence, switching to a stronger encoder only when fine-grained compositional control is needed.

### Caption density and structuredness (short/long/dense description, structured fields such as camera movement, style tags) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: undisclosed. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1 (two coexisting caption types, its most distinctive data design):
[Highly Descriptive Caption] Follows DALL-E 3's (Betker et al., 2023) conclusion, using an MLLM to generate high-density descriptions. To handle the temporal information that video has beyond images (actions, camera movement, scene changes), a two-stage prompt structure is used: the first stage guides the model to perform a structured analysis of predefined attributes with a set of directed questions; the attribute list (Table 3) has 8 items — Scene Count (identifying the number of distinct scenes in the video), Camera Transitions (recording noticeable transitions between shots), Camera Shot Type (specifying the shot type used), Camera Movement (describing camera motion), Main Subject Identification (determining who/what is the video's core focus), Subject Attributes (describing the subject's appearance), Subject Position (specifying the subject's position within the frame), Subject Action (describing what the subject is doing); the second stage then generates the final descriptive caption, which can incorporate salient observations identified during the first-stage analysis. For image data, no attribute pre-analysis is performed; the caption is generated directly. Note that these attributes are "intermediate products for guiding the analysis," not independently retained JSON fields — what ultimately reaches training is still natural-language prose.
[Auto-Regressive Caption] This is unique to MAGI-1, tightly coupled to its chunk-wise autoregressive architecture. Because the model generates chunk by chunk and can provide different text conditions for different parts of the video, the team provides a "second-by-second" fine-grained description for each clip: the caption for the 1st second is required to be a detailed description, while the caption for every subsequent second focuses on "the change relative to the previous second." Table 4's example clearly shows this incremental structure (e.g., "2nd second: the woman's head tilts slightly, her expression shifts from neutral to a faint smile, the lipstick remains in her hand, the camera stays static"). This caption design gives chunk-wise text control an evidentiary basis at training time, and is also the data foundation that lets MAGI-1 achieve multi-shot narrative at inference time using single-shot material. AR captions are used at a proportion that varies by stage: 0% in stage-1, 10% each in stage-2 and stage-3 (Table 5).
[Inference-side patch] Because the model is trained with structured, highly descriptive captions while real user input ranges from extremely minimal to verbose, the team adds a Prompt Enhancement strategy at inference time, distilled into a roughly 7B small model (a corpus of about 2 million entries), to bridge the mismatch between the training distribution and the user-input distribution.
③ Motif-Video 2B (three caption variants + a fixed JSON schema, directly targeting the training/inference distribution mismatch):
· Schema: requires every response to follow a fixed JSON format, containing both a free-text caption field and structured fields (style, subject, action, camera_move, quality, etc.); responses with invalid format are resampled.
· Dual prompts: the video prompt and image prompt share the same JSON schema, differing only in the temporal fields. The video prompt treats the sampled frames as a single description object and queries in a fixed order — camera attributes (shot type, angle, movement), subject, action, environment, lighting and color, and any on-screen text. The image prompt drops the temporal fields, instead querying composition, framing, and a verbatim transcription of text.
· Anti-hallucination constraints: both prompts explicitly forbid three things — making assertions without visual basis, frame-by-frame narration, and subjective commentary on quality/fluidity/mood; the goal is to reduce hallucinated labels and description drift.
· Three variants and sampling probability (the core design targeting the training/inference mismatch): each clip retains three caption variants derived from the same VLM response — caption_long (a detailed description of 150–250 words), caption_short (a single sentence of 15–25 words), caption_truncated (only the first sentence of caption_long); during training, they are sampled with fixed probabilities (p_long, p_short, p_truncated) = (0.5, 0.3, 0.2). The paper states its intent is to narrow the training–test mismatch between "long synthetic captions" and "short prompts users actually give," and notes that the short/truncated variants also act as a mild form of caption dropout, reducing overfitting to VLM-specific phrasing. The paper honestly notes that this is "a pragmatic recipe choice rather than an isolated claim of optimality."
Point of contrast: MAGI-1 and Motif face the same problem (training with long dense captions vs. users giving short prompts), but their solutions run in opposite directions — MAGI-1 adds Prompt Enhancement at inference time to expand short prompts, while Motif mixes short captions into training so the model directly adapts to short input. This is a very clean methodological contrast within this entry.

### Geometric and structured annotation (camera parameters, depth, 3D point tracks, action annotation, explicit state) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: no disclosure. The architecture side uses learnable RoPE positional encoding extended to three dimensions, but this is a model-design choice, not data annotation. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: only semantic-level camera structured annotation, no numerical geometric annotation. ③ Motif-Video 2B: likewise semantic-level rather than numerical-level. Geometry-related numerical processing appears only in preprocessing (cropdetect's content rectangle, IoU clustering of OCR detection boxes, the 512-dimensional SSCD descriptor) and filtering (the UniMatch optical-flow score).

① Mochi 1: no disclosure. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: all data is real material obtained through segmentation and filtering; no controlled perturbation/editing training pairs are constructed. ### Human-in-the-loop degree (manual annotation, manual QC, model pre-screen + manual review) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: no disclosure of any human involvement on the data side. Automated metrics are used on the evaluation side — the official side follows OpenAI DALL-E 3's prompt-adherence evaluation paradigm, using Gemini-1.5-Pro-002 as the judge model (i.e., substituting a large model for human evaluation). [Partially uncertain: whether there is manual QC on the data side]
② MAGI-1: the data pipeline is fully automated (PySceneDetect + 11 categories of Filter Actor + dual-model deduplication + MLLM filtering and captioning), with no mention of manual annotation or manual QC. Human involvement is concentrated, and rather substantial, at the evaluation stage: the team built an internal human-evaluation benchmark — designing a layered metric system (emphasizing completeness over conciseness, and requiring metrics to be orthogonal to avoid redundancy), organized into four main dimensions — Overall / Motion Quality / Instruction Following / Visual Quality — each further broken into sub-metrics; the benchmark dataset consists of 100 curated image–prompt pairs, deliberately diversified in source (real user input from existing video-generation platforms, synthetic images generated by FLUX, real photos from public image libraries, and professional film material), with each sample annotated with the specific evaluation target per the metric framework; evaluation uses strict double-blind pairwise comparison (Win/Tie/Lose), with judges being "experts with good aesthetic training." ③ Motif-Video 2B: the data pipeline is fully automated, with no manual annotation/QC. But there is one instance of "human in the loop" reflected in the training schedule rather than data annotation — the SFT corpus is assembled iteratively: run intermediate evaluation → have a human judge which subject categories are weakest → supplement data for them specifically → re-evaluate. The paper describes the whole training process as "a diagnostic loop rather than a single forward pass through a predefined schedule"; Stage 9's mid-course insertion of Shared Cross-Attention is precisely because a human observed a semantic-alignment regression at the 720p stage. Evaluation relies mainly on the automated VBench metric (Table 3, 16 dimensions), with no human evaluation reported.

### Separate handling of temporal vs. semantic synchronization (temporal alignment and content-semantic matching as two independent filtering conditions)

Not applicable (no audio). If this methodology is mapped onto the pure-visual side, this group does show a clear separation of "temporal quality" and "semantic matching":
· MAGI-1 splits temporal-side signals (RAFT optical flow's overall/foreground/background three-tier motion-strength upper and lower thresholds, camera stability's optical-flow consistency, slide-motion's optical-flow divergence) from semantic-side signals (Transition Detection using CLIP keyframe semantic similarity to judge multi-shot, MLLM advanced filtering doing semantic-level re-screening) into independent conditions at different tiers of the pipeline.
· Motif-Video 2B's separation is more thorough and mutually complementary: the low-level temporal signal is UniMatch optical flow's two-sided truncation, and the semantic signal is the VLM's action=Dynamic label; both are applied simultaneously at the 720p SFT stage — the former handling "whether it moved at the pixel level," the latter handling "whether it counts as dynamic action at the semantic level." Similarly, SigLIP similarity is used for temporal-continuity determination (stitch merging), while the VLM's multi_scene label is used for semantic-level multi-shot determination — these two also form a "temporal vs. semantic" dual-track backstop.
This structure of "low-level statistics + large-model semantics" dual-track filtering is methodologically isomorphic to the separation of temporal and semantic synchronization in AV models.

## Training Coordination

### Multi-stage training curriculum and data curriculum scheduling (basis for stage division: resolution/duration/quality score/modality; low-res→high-res, image→video, short→long) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: no training curriculum disclosed. Only known: the model was "trained from scratch," with full 3D attention covering a context window of 44,520 video tokens, outputting 480p; the official side stated a 720p HD version would follow (in practice it was never fully open-sourced on schedule). [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1 (three stages, scheduling axes are resolution + duration + data quality + caption type, Table 5):
· stage-1: resolution 256p/360p, video duration ≤8 seconds, image:video = 4:1, AR caption proportion 0%.
· stage-2: resolution 480p, duration ≤8 seconds, image:video = 4:1, AR caption proportion 10%.
· stage-3: resolution 720p, duration ≤16 seconds, image:video = 4:1, AR caption proportion 10%.
The paper explicitly states, "as resolution increases, the data volume gradually decreases, and a stricter filtering strategy is adopted to ensure higher data quality"; the last stage relaxes the duration cap from 8 to 16 seconds, letting the model capture richer temporal dynamics. Notably, MAGI-1's image:video ratio stays constant at 4:1 across all three stages (not adjusted by stage), while AR captions are introduced starting from the second stage and fixed at 10% — i.e., "autoregressive controllability" runs through the middle-to-late stages as a constant small-proportion auxiliary task, rather than being progressively ramped up.
③ Motif-Video 2B (ten stages, scheduling axes are modality + resolution + frame count + text encoder + REPA teacher + architecture changes, Table 1 — the most complex and "nonlinear" curriculum in this group):
· Stage 1: T2I, 144p, 1 frame, sentence-embedding encoder, REPA teacher DINOv2 — a spatial bootstrap, initializing the spatial generation pathway before introducing temporal complexity.
· Stage 2: T2IV, 144p, 33 frames, sentence embedding, REPA teacher switched to V-JEPA.
· Stage 3: T2IV, 144p, 65 frames, sentence embedding, V-JEPA.
· Stage 4: T2IV, 360p, 65 frames, text encoder switched to T5Gemma2, REPA turned off (on the grounds that after early convergence, representation alignment becomes counterproductive).
· Stage 5: T2IV, 480p, 65 frames — first takes a "resolution bridge": before introducing 480p video, the model is jointly trained on 360p video and 480p images, letting the model acquire high-resolution spatial features from the cheaper images before letting the temporal pathway adapt to the increased token count.
· Stage 6: T2IV, 480p, 121 frames.
· Stage 7: T2IV, 480p, 121 frames — the first SFT.
· Stage 8: T2IV, 720p, 121 frames — a key unconventional decision: starting from the 480p SFT checkpoint (rather than the 480p pretraining checkpoint).
· Stage 9: T2IV, 360p, 121 frames — resolution rolled back, introducing Shared Cross-Attention for refinement (because a semantic-alignment regression was observed at the 720p stage).
· Stage 10: T2IV, 720p, 121 frames — final pretraining + second SFT.
Two further design points run throughout: all video stages are trained jointly on image and text (image samples stabilize per-frame visual quality and reinforce semantic grounding, while video samples drive temporal modeling); classifier-free-guidance training's prompt-dropout probability is fixed at p=0.1, and neither the noise schedule nor the loss weighting is modified.
What's most notable about the Motif curriculum is that it acknowledges it is not run straight through in one pass — "treating training as a diagnostic loop rather than a single forward pass through a predefined schedule" — Stage 9's resolution rollback and mid-course architecture insertion being evidence of this; and the hypothesis behind Stage 8 starting from the SFT checkpoint (that SFT has already concentrated density onto the high-quality manifold, so the 720p stage inherits a cleaner starting distribution and can allocate capacity to resolution adaptation rather than simultaneously making up for pretraining quality deficits) — the paper honestly notes that this decision "was not validated with an ablation against starting from the pretraining checkpoint," and is reported only as a pragmatic recipe decision.

### Data mixture change across training stages (pretraining/annealing/SFT high-quality subset) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: undisclosed. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: the explicit part of the mixture is given in Table 5 — image:video constant at 4:1 (unchanged across three stages), AR caption proportion 0%/10%/10%, duration cap 8s/8s/16s, resolution 256p·360p/480p/720p; data volume decreases by stage and filtering thresholds tighten by stage (no numbers). The implicit part of the mixture is the "dynamic distribution adjustment": throughout training, the sampling ratio of each semantic subset is adaptively changed based on evaluation feedback to reinforce weaknesses (e.g., adding weight if human facial expressions are hard to learn) — this makes MAGI-1's actual data mixture a curve that evolves with training rather than a static table. There is also a task-level mixture: because the product is I2V-focused, a larger proportion is allocated to the I2V task during training. There is no independent annealing/SFT stage division. [Partially uncertain: the specific magnitude of dynamic adjustment and the semantic-subset division]
③ Motif-Video 2B: the mixture strategy manifests at three levels simultaneously —
· Modality level: all video stages are jointly trained on image and text; training uses "a fixed image–video interleaving schedule" (the example pattern being I-V-V, with the full schedule determined by the planned per-bucket step counts), ensuring the image/video mixture ratio stays stable throughout training.
· Quality level: resolution/duration/motion/aesthetic filtering is re-applied with a stricter threshold at every stage transition; quality=low is excluded starting from 480p; 720p SFT additionally layers on domain-balancing and action=Dynamic dynamic-motion admission.
· Source level: synthetic video is injected only at the 720p stage.
There is also an engineering-level "mixture": the joint bucketing of frame-count bucket (1/33/65/121) × resolution bucket must be balanced across all ranks, otherwise under FSDP2 synchronous training, updates for a given bucket get held back by the slowest rank; the offline bucket-balanced sampler solves this by using simulated annealing to minimize the coefficient of variation of per-bucket clip counts across ranks (see data_infra_throughput). The category mixture of the SFT corpus is instead decided iteratively by intermediate-evaluation feedback (Fig. 8).

### Post-training data (scale and selection criteria of the curated SFT set, number of preference pairs and annotation method, reward-model training data) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: no post-training data disclosed at all. Neither an SFT curated-set description nor any DPO/RLHF, preference-pair, or reward-model information is given. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: the technical report has no independent SFT section, nor does it describe preference-alignment post-training (no DPO/RLHF, no preference-pair count, no reward-model training data). Its "post-training" is in effect handled by two components: (i) the final stage of multi-stage training itself, which serves a refinement role via more strictly filtered high-quality data and longer duration (≤16s); (ii) Shortcut Model distillation — using a shortcut model as the distillation target, with a minimum step size of 1/64 (corresponding to 64 function evaluations for standard flow matching); distillation step sizes are cyclically sampled from the set [1/64]×8 ∪ [1/32, 1/16, 1/8], allowing a single distilled model to run inference at different compute budgets such as 64/32/16/8 steps; classifier-free-guidance distillation is folded in during minimum-step-size training. In addition, the inference-side Prompt Enhancement small model (roughly 7B) is distilled from a corpus of about 2 million MLLM-generated enhanced prompts, with samples whose target text is too long filtered out to control output length. [Partially uncertain: whether there is undisclosed preference data]
③ Motif-Video 2B: explicitly has two SFT rounds — Stage 7 (480p) and Stage 10 (720p), each conducted on "the curated high-quality subset described in Section 5." The paper states the purpose of SFT directly: to push the model's output distribution toward the high-quality tail of the training data, improving aesthetic quality, motion smoothness, and prompt adherence — something "broad pretraining on loosely filtered data cannot achieve"; it notes this is already standard practice for video generation, citing HunyuanVideo 1.5, Wan2.1, SANA-Video, Seedance 1.5, and SkyReels-V2 as all reporting dedicated SFT stages. The SFT data's selection criteria layer stricter aesthetic cutoffs, domain-balancing (driven by style/subject labels), and the video-side action=Dynamic dynamic-motion admission on top of the regular filtering; the corpus assembly is iterative weakness-patching (see domain_distribution). An unconventional aspect is that 720p pretraining starts from the 480p SFT checkpoint rather than the pretraining checkpoint (see multi_stage_curriculum). Likewise, no preference-alignment post-training is used (no DPO/RLHF, no reward model). The absolute scale of the SFT curated set is not given. [Partially uncertain: the SFT curated set's scale and aesthetic-threshold values]

### Data-processing infrastructure and throughput (NeMo Curator/Data-Juicer/in-house, GPU speedup ratio, processing scale, cost) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: no disclosure of data-processing infrastructure. The training-side scale is likewise undisclosed; only known is that inference requires about 60GB of VRAM per GPU. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: the report states that "a scalable data processing system" was built to construct the training set from tens of petabytes of raw material, but names no framework (no mention of NeMo Curator / Data-Juicer, etc.), gives no GPU speedup ratio, processing machine-hours, or cost. A clue to the inferable cost order of magnitude: over 10 categories of model inference — PySceneDetect, DOVER, LAION aesthetics, RAFT (already downsampled to 8 FPS to save compute), saliency detection, Hough border detection, text detection, Florence-2, face detection, CLIP, DINOv2 — must be run over petabyte-scale material, followed by MLLM inference on the survivors; merging MLLM filtering and captioning into a single call is precisely to control this cost.
Training-side infrastructure disclosure is very thorough, however (occupying a large portion of Section 4 of the report): DP + CP + TP three-dimensional parallelism is used; to address DP load imbalance and low GPU utilization on short sequences caused by variable-length video sequences, an online distributed Packing and Padding (PnP) scheme was designed — reformulating the bin-packing problem as "packing M candidate samples into N bins of capacity max_length, with M ≫ N, N required to be divisible by DP_SIZE, and max_length required to be divisible by TP_SIZE × CP_SIZE," solved online with an extended First-Fit-Decreasing greedy plus custom heuristics, achieving 99% capacity utilization with negligible variance across DP groups. The reason it must be online rather than offline packing is precisely because "the data composition is adjusted frequently during training" (echoing the dynamic distribution adjustment of Section 3.5) — a typical case of the data strategy in turn constraining the infrastructure design. It also contributes MagiAttention, a distributed attention library (Flex-Flash-Attention + a dispatch solver + Group-Cast/Group-Reduce communication primitives + multi-level overlap), aimed at linearly scalable support for ultra-long sequences and heterogeneous masks. [Partially uncertain: the framework, throughput, and cost on the data-cleaning side]
③ Motif-Video 2B (the most complete data-infrastructure disclosure in this group, and the only one to give quantified gains):
· Cleaning framework: "We made extensive use of NeMo Curator," stating that its scalable data-governance toolkit and support for large-scale video processing pipelines significantly simplified the preprocessing workflow.
· Deduplication acceleration: NVIDIA cuVS's multi-GPU IVF-PQ index (cosine distance, k=64, nprobe=16).
· OCR servicing: PaddleOCR-VL deployed via vLLM.
· Preprocessing landing: black-bar cropping, OCR-region exclusion, resolution scaling, and frame-rate limiting are all completed in a single ffmpeg re-encode, avoiding multiple transcodes.
· Storage and sampling (an independent contribution, Section 5.3 + Table 2): the corpus is stored as WebDataset tar shards. The baseline approach (a global clip index + shuffle_block_size=1 random shuffling + round-robin assignment by index mod W), while preserving randomness, breaks archival locality, sacrifices WebDataset's core advantage of sequential reads, and causes severe cross-rank bucket imbalance — under FSDP2/HSDP synchronous training, the global step count for a given bucket is determined by the slowest rank on that bucket, causing the baseline to achieve only N steps per epoch and roughly 20% utilization, with dataloader latency of 0.05 s/step. The team's offline bucket-balanced sampler moves filtering, bucketing, and rank assignment entirely to an offline planning stage: filtering rules are first applied from clip-level Parquet metadata and samples are assigned to joint "frame-count × resolution" buckets; each tar shard is then greedily assigned to the rank that most reduces the current cross-rank bucket imbalance; simulated annealing then runs for 30,000 iterations (each proposing a swap of two tar shards) to optimize the shard-to-rank mapping, with the objective of minimizing the coefficient of variation of clip counts across ranks for each of the 1f/33f/65f/121f buckets; the final plan is serialized into a per-rank rank{r}.npz plan file. At runtime, each rank reads only its assigned tar shards in shard order, combined with a rolling shuffle over a 4096-sample window to preserve intra-bucket randomness without breaking read locality.
· Quantified gains (Table 2): baseline — N steps/epoch, about 20% utilization, 0.05 s/step; offline sampler + greedy — about 4.6N steps, about 76% utilization, <0.001 s/step; offline sampler + simulated annealing (the final scheme) — about 5.4N steps, utilization near 90%, <0.001 s/step. I.e., roughly a 5.4× throughput improvement relative to baseline, utilization rising from 20% to about 90%, and dataloader latency reduced by more than 50×; SA improves a further ~18% over greedy.
· Training hardware: 8 Azure nodes × 8 H200 GPUs = 64 GPUs total, orchestrated with Kubernetes, launched via SkyPilot (handling scheduling, failure recovery, and cloud-resource provisioning), using FSDP2 via Accelerate; at the 2B scale, a single node's sharding group is sufficient to hold the full model state for the 720p/121-frame configuration, so tensor parallelism or sequence parallelism is unnecessary, simplifying the communication pattern and reducing synchronization overhead. Mixed precision uses bfloat16 for compute and activations, with float32 retained for reduction-sensitive communication and optimizer state.
This section is the only one in this group of three models to fully connect "data-infrastructure improvement → quantifiable training-throughput gain," and is of the highest practical reference value for "building video models on a small budget."

## Effectiveness Comparison

### Quantified impact of data-strategy ablations (distinguishing: filtering-strictness ablation / caption-density-style ablation / data-mixture ablation, and corresponding evaluation metrics) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: no data-strategy ablation, and no data disclosure. Evaluation consists only of prompt adherence (following the DALL-E 3 protocol, judged by Gemini-1.5-Pro-002) and qualitative/automated motion-quality indicators. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: data-side ablations are essentially absent — no filtering-strictness ablation, no caption density/style ablation, no data-mixture ablation with quantified results. The empirical statements related to data decisions in the report are all "empirical findings" rather than controlled experiments, e.g., "through empirical evaluation we found the technical score of DOVER alone to be the most effective indicator," "through empirical analysis, using 4–12 frames per clip achieves the best trade-off between description accuracy and computational efficiency," "observed during training that landscapes are easy to learn while human facial expressions are hard" — these are the basis for design choices, but no metric values for the compared alternatives are given. End-to-end evaluation, on the other hand, is fairly thorough: a self-built double-blind expert human evaluation on 100 image–prompt pairs (four main dimensions + sub-metrics), VBench-I2V (including two variants: a 1× decoder at 1280×720 and 2× VAE upsampling at 2560×1440), and the Physics-IQ physical-understanding benchmark (8-second real physical-phenomenon videos, predicting the last 5 seconds from the first 3; MAGI's V2V variant uses the full 96-frame input at 24 FPS). Its data-design effectiveness is therefore likewise backed only indirectly, through the final ranking.
③ Motif-Video 2B: the data-filtering components themselves are "not ablated in isolation, evaluated only via end-to-end model performance," a stance the paper is candid about, flagging its own limitations in several places — the caption three-variant sampling probabilities of 0.5/0.3/0.2 are explicitly stated to be "a pragmatic recipe choice rather than an isolated claim of optimality"; the decision to start 720p from the 480p SFT checkpoint "was not compared against starting from the pretraining checkpoint," reported only for reproducibility; the early-training-efficiency check (at 6.5×10²⁰ FLOPs this model achieves FID 15.5 vs. that scaling law's prediction of FID ≈ 30 for vanilla DiT) is likewise explicitly flagged as "confounded with the parallel REPA and architectural differences, serving only as a consistency check rather than an independent validation of the early curriculum design."
What genuinely receives quantified comparison is the data-infrastructure side (Table 2, see data_infra_throughput): baseline vs. offline sampler+greedy vs. offline sampler+SA, giving a complete comparison of steps/epoch (N / 4.6N / 5.4N), utilization (20% / 76% / 90%), and dataloader latency (0.05 / <0.001 / <0.001 s/step) — the only rigorous, quantified data-side ablation in this group, though it measures throughput rather than generation quality.
End-to-end results (Table 3, full VBench 16 dimensions): Total 83.76%, exceeding Wan2.1-T2V-14B (83.69%), HunyuanVideo (83.24%), Step-Video-T2V-30B (81.83%); Wan2.2-T2V's 84.23% is separately categorized because it uses prompt optimization and is not compared on the same basis. Strengths are on the semantic side — Spatial Relationship 83.02% (leading among open-source models with complete per-dimension results), Object Class 92.93%, Multiple Objects 77.29%, Semantic Score 80.44%; weaknesses are on the quality side — Subject Consistency 95.38%, Background Consistency 95.74%, both lower than the strongest Wan series, and Temporal Flickering 98.16% also trails the Wan2.1 family (highest 99.55%). The paper interprets this as "a specific trade-off rather than an across-the-board win."

### Evidence for quality vs. quantity (cases where small, curated data outperforms large, messy data) ⚠️

This is precisely the core focal point of this merged entry's research, and the three provide a chain of evidence of increasing strength:
① Mochi 1: no evidence (no data disclosure). [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: provides structural evidence — starting from tens of petabytes of raw material, contracting through the layered shrinkage of 11 categories of Filter Actor + strict CLIP/DINOv2 dual-model deduplication + MLLM semantic re-screening, explicitly stating that "as resolution increases, the data volume gradually decreases, and a stricter filtering strategy is adopted to ensure higher data quality"; it cites LLM literature to argue that "even a small amount of duplicate data can significantly harm performance" as the motivation for strict deduplication. But it gives neither retention-rate numbers nor a controlled "small-and-curated vs. large-and-messy" experiment, remaining indirect evidence at the level of design philosophy. [Partially uncertain]
③ Motif-Video 2B: the strongest evidence in this group, and one of the rarer instances in this entire research — it turns "quality beats quantity" directly into the paper's central thesis, with comparable results. The paper opens by asking: "Training strong video-generation models typically requires massive data, huge parameter counts, and vast compute. This work asks: under a much smaller budget — fewer than 10 million clips, less than 100,000 H200 GPU hours — can strong text-to-video quality also be achieved?", and notes that the current strongest open-source models (Wan2.1, HunyuanVideo, Seedance) are "trained on hundreds of millions of curated clips, with parameter counts of 5B–14B," and that "this concentration of resources, while producing impressive results, has also narrowed participation: in practice, very few teams can train a competitive video generation model." The conclusion: trained with 2B parameters, fewer than 10 million clips, within 100,000 H200 hours, it reaches 83.76% on VBench, "surpassing Wan2.1-14B, with 7× fewer parameters and an order of magnitude less training data."
The core methodological statement backing this conclusion is: "Rather than maximizing raw scale, we prioritize curation quality to support resource-efficient training."
Caveats to maintain: Motif's edge comes simultaneously from architecture (Shared Cross-Attention, the three-stage backbone functional decomposition) and training techniques (TREAD token routing, REPA/V-JEPA early representation alignment, two rounds of SFT, and starting 720p from an SFT checkpoint); the paper does not isolate the contribution of "data curation quality" from these factors via ablation; and its advantage is concentrated on the semantic dimension while quality dimensions (subject/background consistency, temporal flickering) still trail the Wan series — the paper itself reads this as "a specific trade-off rather than an across-the-board win," explicitly stating that "long-horizon temporal stability and appearance consistency remain the primary targets for further scaling and data improvement." This is therefore strong but non-isolated evidence.

### Alignment between training-data domain distribution and evaluation-benchmark category taxonomy (e.g., VABench's seven major categories) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: no alignment relationship to describe (data distribution undisclosed). Evaluation uses the automated prompt-adherence protocol (the DALL-E 3 paradigm + Gemini-1.5-Pro-002 as judge) and qualitative motion-quality demonstrations. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: does not perform static category alignment, but establishes an evaluation→data feedback channel via "dynamic distribution adjustment": continuously monitoring evaluation performance during training, raising the sampling ratio for semantic-concept subsets where the model performs weakly. This effectively treats "evaluation categories" as the objective function for data mixture — stronger than a static mapping — but the paper discloses neither the semantic-concept taxonomy nor how the evaluation used corresponds to VBench's sixteen dimensions or the self-built four human-evaluation dimensions (Overall / Motion Quality / Instruction Following / Visual Quality). There is also one task-level (rather than category-level) alignment: because both the product and the evaluation are I2V-dominated (the team explicitly states "I2V more closely matches real usage patterns: users typically generate video starting from an image rather than text"), a larger proportion was allocated to I2V during training, and evaluation likewise primarily uses the VBench-I2V rather than the T2V track — a clear case of "training mixture aligned to the evaluation/product form." [Partially uncertain: the specific mapping between the semantic taxonomy and evaluation dimensions]
③ Motif-Video 2B: establishes the most explicit alignment loop in this group — the subject/style labels produced by the VLM are used both for domain balancing at 720p and SFT, and as the statistical basis for the Fig. 8 category-distribution chart; and the direction in which the SFT corpus is supplemented is directly determined by "the subject categories with the weakest generation quality in intermediate evaluations," with the resulting video-side distribution tilting toward Transportation / Sports / Animals precisely because these three categories involve intense dynamics and were identified as weaknesses in evaluation. This loop — "evaluation finds a weakness → supplement data by subject label → re-evaluate" — effectively shapes the training-data category distribution with evaluation results.
However there is no formal mapping to the VBench sixteen-dimension taxonomy: Motif's subject categories (People, Transportation, Sports, Animals, etc.) are content-subject classifications, while VBench's dimensions are capability classifications (Object Class, Multiple Objects, Spatial Relationship, Human Action, Subject Consistency, Temporal Flickering, etc.), and the two correspond only naively on a few dimensions such as Human Action. Nor does the paper supplement data in reverse based on VBench weak dimensions (e.g., Subject Consistency, Temporal Flickering); instead it acknowledges these as "the primary targets for further scaling and data improvement."
