Geometric/Structured Annotation Dataset Collection (SceneScribe-1M / SpatialVID / WildWorld / Action100M)

All four are open academic datasets, with weights not being a primary output. ### Whether Simultaneous Audio-Video Generation Is Supported, and Implementation Approach (Native Joint/Cascaded/MoE Fusion)

None of the four support simultaneous audio-video generation, nor do they involve the audio modality at all. All four represent a purely visual geometric/structured annotation paradigm: SceneScribe-1M and SpatialVID target camera-controllable video generation and 3D/4D perception, WildWorld targets action-conditioned world models, and Action100M targets action understanding and video-text representation learning. Action100M is derived from HowTo100M (which includes narration ASR), but the ASR is used only to aid text supervision, not for audio generation.

## Data Scale and Distribution

### Training Data Scale (Video Count/Hours/Token Count, Pretraining and SFT Listed Separately)

SceneScribe-1M: about 1 million video clips, 156.7M frames, over 4,000 hours total; SpatialVID: 33,443 original YouTube long-form videos (21,789 hours), producing 2.71 million clips, 127.60M frames, and 7,089 hours of dynamic content, with the high-quality subset SpatialVID-HQ at 0.37M clips and 20.63M frames; WildWorld: over 108 million frames, about 1,800 hours (converted at 30FPS), with 119 columns of annotation per frame; Action100M: derived from 1.2 million (1,199,096) instructional videos totaling about 14.6 years of duration (72% of which, i.e., 10.6 years, has usable ASR), producing 147 million temporally localized segments and about 21.3 billion English words of annotation. All four are single datasets with no split between pretraining and SFT (Action100M provides a semantically resampled subset to mitigate long-tail distribution).

### Data Source Composition (In-House/Public Datasets/Web Scraping/Licensed Procurement/Synthetic Data)

SceneScribe-1M: a mix of reused existing public datasets + web material — HD-VILA-100M (large-scale, multi-category video), Panda-70M (video-caption pairs), Koala-36M (precise temporal splitting), and Pexels-Video (668K high-quality Pexels videos collected via the OpenVideo toolbox); SpatialVID: self-crawled from YouTube, retrieved via motion-related keywords (walk / tour / drone, etc.) to ensure camera-trajectory diversity; WildWorld: fully synthetic/engine-captured — built on an in-house game-data capture platform, recorded in real time from the engine of the AAA photorealistic action-RPG "Monster Hunter: Wilds," neither crawled nor filmed with real people; Action100M: reuses HowTo100M (YouTube instructional videos, the face-blurred 1,199,096-clip version), a case of re-annotating an existing public dataset.

### Data Compliance and Provenance (Proportion of Licensed Data, Rights-Cleared Datasets, C2PA, etc.) ⚠️

SpatialVID has the clearest compliance stance: the entire library uses the CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0 non-commercial license, and the manual pre-screening stage removes videos whose titles contain inappropriate terms, but the copyright/licensing status of the underlying YouTube material itself is not individually cleared [uncertain]; SceneScribe-1M inherits licensing from its upstream datasets (HD-VILA/Panda-70M/Koala-36M) respectively, with the Pexels portion being freely commercially usable material, but the overall proportion of licensed content is undisclosed [uncertain]; WildWorld's data is generated by a game engine, involving no real-person likenesses or web copyright, but the copyright of the game content itself belongs to the publisher, and the paper does not clarify the terms for academic redistribution [uncertain]; Action100M releases only annotations (about 205GB), requiring users to obtain the videos themselves from HowTo100M, sidestepping video-redistribution risk, and the source videos have already been face-blurred for privacy protection. None of the four mention content-provenance watermarking mechanisms such as C2PA.

### Clip Duration Distribution and Splitting Strategy ⚠️

SceneScribe-1M: spec-based filtering requires a duration between 5 seconds and 1 minute; videos judged "non-continuous" are split using TransNetV2 and re-filtered, with the final clips averaging about 14.4 seconds (an approximation derived from 156.7M frames / 1 million clips at the corresponding fps) [uncertain]; SpatialVID: split into 3–15 second clips using a modified PySceneDetect, averaging about 9.4 seconds (estimated from 127.6M frames / 2.71M clips at roughly 30fps); WildWorld: training samples are fixed at 81-frame clips (recorded at 30FPS, inference at 16FPS), organized by action sequence; Action100M: hierarchical temporal segmentation, discarding segments shorter than 0.5 seconds, with the LLM aggregation stage further removing nodes shorter than 4 seconds, forming a multi-level time tree spanning from second-level to minute-level granularity.

### Resolution/Aspect Ratio Distribution and Bucketing Strategy

SceneScribe-1M: spec-based filtering requires resolution above 1080p and frame rate ≥10fps, to preserve fine-grained geometric detail; SpatialVID: all clips are standardized to 1280×720, H.265-encoded MP4; WildWorld: captured at 2K full-screen with a 720p sub-window, with model training at a resolution of 544×960 (roughly a variant of a 9:16 vertical crop ratio); Action100M: follows the original HowTo100M resolution with no unified bucketing strategy, with visual encoding using V-JEPA 2's fixed sampling window. None of the four adopt the multi-resolution/multi-aspect-ratio bucketing training strategy common in video-generation models — because they are positioned as annotation datasets rather than training recipes.

### Category/Domain Distribution and Mixture Strategy (Balance Control and Ratios for People, Actions, Scenes, Styles, etc.)

SceneScribe-1M: sourced from open-domain web videos in HD-VILA-100M/Panda-70M/Koala-36M, covering a wide range of everyday scene categories, with no explicit category-mixture control; selection preference is driven by "motion diversity" (the source pool was substantially reduced during pre-screening due to the motion-diversity requirement); SpatialVID: actively constructs domains via motion keywords (first-person walking, urban/nature touring, drone footage), and explicitly balances category distribution and motion diversity for the SpatialVID-HQ subset, with a structured tag system covering five dimensions — weather, time of day, crowd density, lighting, and scene type — enabling queryable domain control; compared with Panda-70M, SpatialVID-HQ shows a more concentrated distribution across aesthetics, brightness, and motion, with 80% of clips featuring curved or turning trajectories; WildWorld: domain is highly controlled but narrow, entirely from combat/exploration scenes in a single game world, with the WildBench test set explicitly balanced by scene type — 100 cooperative-scenario clips (players + 3 NPCs fighting a monster) and 100 solo-scenario clips, covering multiple characters, weapons, monster species, difficulty levels, and events (skill casting, knockdowns, deaths, critical hits); Action100M: an instructional-video domain (HowTo100M), with an open-vocabulary, extremely long-tailed action taxonomy; the authors use k-means semantic resampling (k=10³/10⁴/10⁵) to mitigate the long tail, and report 7.58 million duplicate groups totaling 141.8 million duplicate instances requiring deduplication before resampling.

### Audio Category Distribution and Mixture (Proportions and Control Strategy for Speech/SFX/Music/Ambient Sound/Silence) — AV-Model-Specific Dimension

Not applicable. None of the four datasets involve audio-modality annotation, so there is no category mixture for speech/sound effects/music/ambient sound. Action100M is the only one to indirectly touch on audio — its source, HowTo100M, has narration ASR transcripts (usable for 72% of videos, covering 10.6 years of content), but this serves only as a weak textual-supervision cue and is not used for any distribution statistics or mixture control by audio category.

### Narrative Structure Distribution (Single-Shot vs. Multi-Shot, Average Clip Length, Shot-Count Distribution, Whether Native Audio Track Is Present)

SceneScribe-1M: explicitly pursues single-shot continuity — first using TransNetV2 to detect hard cuts and gradual transitions, splitting "non-continuous" videos into semantically coherent single-shot clips and re-filtering; the final library consists entirely of single shots; SpatialVID: uses a modified PySceneDetect (adjusted sensitivity thresholds + an interval-based multi-frame comparison strategy) to ensure no transitions occur within a 3–15 second clip — also a single-shot library, but one that emphasizes within-shot camera-trajectory complexity (80% of clips contain curved/turning trajectories); WildWorld: continuously recorded in-game, naturally single-shot, organized at two levels — action sequence and sample — with the combat round as the narrative unit; Action100M: does not pursue single-shot content, instead building a narrative structure "above the shot level" — using Ward-linkage hierarchical clustering to organize long videos into a multi-level time tree (Tree-of-Captions), preserving frame-level, segment-level, and aggregate-level semantics simultaneously. None of the four have any narrative annotation related to a native audio track.

### Language/Accent Distribution (Data Foundation for Multilingual Lip-Sync Capability)

SceneScribe-1M / SpatialVID / WildWorld: captions are entirely single-language English, with no multilingual or accent dimension, and no speakers; Action100M: all 21.3 billion words of annotation are in English, drawn from the English-language subset of HowTo100M instructional videos (with English-language ASR), with no statistics on language or accent distribution. None of the four have a data foundation for multilingual lip-sync capability.

## Filtering Pipeline

### Overall Filtering Funnel Structure (Number of Stages, Order)

SceneScribe-1M (a three-stage funnel + annotation): (1) spec-based filtering (resolution >1080p, frame rate ≥10fps, duration 5 seconds–1 minute) → (2) six-dimensional content-quality filtering by Qwen2.5-VL-72B (removing clips with unknown motion intensity, visible watermarks, strong camera distortion, strong lighting artifacts, etc.) → (3) TransNetV2 temporal splitting (only for non-continuous videos), with re-filtering of the resulting clips → (4) geometric annotation (MegaSaM for camera + depth, TAPIP3D for 3D point tracks) + semantic annotation (Qwen2.5-VL-72B captioning); SpatialVID (manual pre-screening → splitting → four-dimensional quality filtering → geometric reconstruction → two-stage captioning): (1) manual screening (removing corrupted content, titles containing inappropriate terms or the phrase "panoramic camera," and content incompatible with MegaSaM reconstruction) → (2) splitting into 3–15 second clips with a modified PySceneDetect and unified transcoding to 1280×720 H.265 → (3) four quality filters (aesthetics, brightness, OCR text ratio, motion/VMAF) → (4) MegaSaM camera-pose and depth reconstruction (with the depth module replaced by UniDepth v2 and Depth Anything v2) → (5) SAM2 dynamic masking and per-frame dynamic-ratio computation → (6) trajectory-motion metrics (MoveDist/RotAngle/TrajTurns) and acceleration-based anomaly detection → (7) two-stage structured captioning combining Gemini-2.0-Flash visual parsing with Qwen3-30B-A3B language refinement → (8) balanced sampling for the HQ subset; WildWorld (direct engine capture, no traditional filtering funnel): based on an OBS Studio + Reshade capture platform that partitions the display into sub-windows, simultaneously recording RGB (720p) and depth with synchronized timestamps, while exporting skeleton, world state, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics from the game's memory/engine, producing 119 columns of structured per-frame annotation, followed by VLM-generated two-tier captions; Action100M (an annotation-focused pipeline that performs almost no video filtering): (1) the V-JEPA 2 encoder converts video into dense temporal visual embeddings (sampling 1 frame every 4, with 64-frame overlapping windows and a stride of 8) → (2) temporally constrained hierarchical agglomerative clustering (Ward linkage) generates a time tree → (3) Llama-3.2-Vision-11B for frame-level captioning + Perception-LM-3B for segment-level captioning → (4) GPT-OSS-120B performs evidence aggregation and structured extraction, with three rounds of Self-Refine → (5) deduplication and semantic resampling.

### Quantitative Funnel Retention Rates (Input/Output Volume and Final Retention Rate at Each Filtering Stage, e.g., Apollo's 27%) ⚠️

SpatialVID provides the clearest quantitative funnel: 21,789 hours of original YouTube video (33,443 clips) → a final 7,089 hours of dynamic content (2.71 million clips), a duration-based retention rate of about 32.5%; the high-quality subset SpatialVID-HQ is 0.37M of 2.71M clips, i.e., a further retention of about 13.7% on the already-filtered library (about 4.5% end-to-end relative to the original material). A key quantitative finding: over 80% of videos in Panda-70M cannot be successfully reconstructed by MegaSaM due to insufficient motion, constituting the primary elimination source for motion filtering. SceneScribe-1M does not disclose input/output volumes stage by stage — we only know it converges from a hundred-million-scale source pool (HD-VILA-100M/Panda-70M/Koala-36M/Pexels) down to 1 million clips, 156.7M frames, and over 4,000 hours; the paper states that "the motion-diversity requirement substantially reduced the source pool during pre-screening," but stage-by-stage retention figures are missing [uncertain]; Action100M's quantification is on the deduplication side — it identifies and removes 7.58 million duplicate groups totaling 141.8 million duplicate instances, with 72% of the 1.2 million source videos having usable ASR, producing a final 147 million segments; WildWorld is direct engine capture with no filtering-funnel concept, only mentioning that filtering yields "several thousand" clips (the exact number is not given) [uncertain].

### Shot Splitting Method (PySceneDetect/In-House Model/Shot-Aware Splitting)

SceneScribe-1M: TransNetV2, a deep model, detects hard cuts and gradual transitions, splitting only videos judged "non-continuous," with the resulting clips re-filtered to ensure semantic coherence; SpatialVID: a modified version of PySceneDetect — adjusting sensitivity thresholds and introducing an interval-based multi-frame comparison strategy to fix the original tool's missed detection of gradual transitions, outputting 3–15 second clips; WildWorld: requires no shot splitting at all, as the game engine records continuously, with samples organized by action-sequence boundaries (training uses 81-frame windows); Action100M: forgoes traditional shot detection, instead using V-JEPA 2 semantic embeddings + temporally constrained Ward-linkage hierarchical agglomerative clustering for semantic-level temporal segmentation, producing a hierarchical tree of segments that can span multiple shots, discarding segments shorter than 0.5 seconds.

### Quality Filtering (Aesthetic Score, Sharpness, OCR Text Filtering, Black-Bar/Watermark/Logo Detection)

SceneScribe-1M: the core is a six-dimensional content-quality assessment by Qwen2.5-VL-72B, explicitly removing clips with unknown motion intensity, visible watermarks, strong camera distortion, or strong lighting artifacts; layered on top of hard spec thresholds (>1080p, ≥10fps, 5s–60s). SpatialVID: a quality layer of four independent scorers — (1) aesthetics: a CLIP+MLP aesthetic predictor, removing scores <4.0; (2) brightness: keeping only clips whose mean falls within [20, 140], removing overly dark or overexposed clips; (3) OCR text: PaddleOCR detection, flagging clips for removal where text covers >30% of the frame area; (4) motion: a VMAF metric + subsequent reconstruction-feasibility judgment. WildWorld: engine-rendered footage naturally has no watermarks, no compression artifacts, and no black bars, so quality filtering degenerates into scene-validity screening, mainly ensuring recording synchronization and lossless depth encoding. Action100M: performs almost no image-quality filtering (retaining HowTo100M's original quality), with quality control instead shifted to the annotation side — via multiple rounds of LLM Self-Refine to ensure annotation quality, and removing meaningless fragments via minimum-duration thresholds (segments >0.5 seconds, tree nodes >4 seconds).

### Motion Filtering (Optical-Flow/Motion-Score Thresholds, Removing Static and Shaky Footage)

Motion filtering is a key point distinguishing these four datasets from conventional video-generation datasets — conventional pipelines remove excessive motion, whereas geometric annotation datasets instead remove "insufficient motion." SpatialVID: uses VMAF combined with three camera-trajectory metrics for filtering — MoveDist (total displacement distance), RotAngle (cumulative rotation angle), and TrajTurns (number of direction changes) — plus an acceleration-based detector to identify and remove abrupt, non-physical motion jitter; the quantitative evidence is that over 80% of Panda-70M videos cannot be reconstructed by MegaSaM due to insufficient parallax/motion, necessitating active retrieval of high-motion material such as walk/tour/drone content; the final library has 80% of clips with curved or turning trajectories. SceneScribe-1M: uses "motion diversity" as the primary pre-screening axis, with Qwen2.5-VL-72B directly removing clips whose motion intensity is judged unknown, and the motion requirement substantially reduces the source pool; MegaSaM was chosen precisely for its robustness in scenarios with limited camera parallax. WildWorld: motion is directly given by game input, requiring no inference of motion from pixels, with static frames precisely identifiable via action ID. Action100M: has no optical-flow-level motion filtering; the variance-minimizing clustering in the semantic-segmentation stage naturally merges stationary passages into longer nodes.

### Deduplication Method (Exact Deduplication and Embedding-Based Semantic Deduplication Recorded Separately) ⚠️

Action100M's deduplication is the most systematic and quantified: after annotation aggregation, it identifies 7.58 million duplicate groups totaling 141.8 million duplicate instances and performs deduplication, followed by k-means (k=10³/10⁴/10⁵) semantic resampling to flatten the long tail — a combination of embedding-based semantic deduplication and rebalancing. SceneScribe-1M: merges four libraries — HD-VILA-100M/Panda-70M/Koala-36M/Pexels — with cross-library overlap risk, and the paper does not describe an explicit deduplication step [uncertain]. SpatialVID: collects clips by video ID from YouTube, with natural near-duplicates existing between clips cut from the same long video, and the paper does not describe embedding-level semantic deduplication [uncertain]. WildWorld: has a pronounced long tail in action space (the top-150 actions account for 58.49% of samples), with the paper presenting this distribution via action-triplet statistics rather than deduplication, and no explicit deduplication process is documented [uncertain].

### VLM/LLM as Quality Inspector (Multimodal-LLM Quality Scoring and Mismatch Removal; the 2026 Trend From Shallow Scorers to Large-Model Semantic Judgment)

This batch of datasets fully embodies the 2026 trend of "large models as quality inspectors." SceneScribe-1M is the most typical case: it directly uses Qwen2.5-VL-72B as the sole content-quality judge, completing six-dimensional semantic judgments (motion intensity, watermarks, camera distortion, lighting artifacts, etc.) in a single pass, replacing the traditional combination of shallow aesthetic/sharpness scorers. SpatialVID adopts a hybrid strategy: shallow scorers (CLIP+MLP aesthetics, brightness statistics, PaddleOCR, VMAF) do coarse screening, reserving large-model compute for the annotation side — Gemini-2.0-Flash performs 1fps visual parsing, and Qwen3-30B-A3B refines the result using camera-pose priors and corrects motion-direction descriptions, essentially a reverse quality check of "using geometric ground truth to validate VLM output," able to correct VLMs' common left/right or push/pull direction hallucinations. Action100M pushes LLM quality-checking to the extreme: GPT-OSS-120B performs evidence aggregation and structured extraction over multi-source captions, executing three rounds of Self-Refine self-correction, effectively using a reasoning model as an annotation-consistency auditor. WildWorld uses a VLM as judge on the evaluation side: WildBench's Action Following metric is determined by a VLM judging whether the generated clip matches the ground-truth clip (a 0/1 score), achieving 85% agreement with human evaluation — an explicit validation of "model as judge" in a geometric-dataset context.

### Safety and Compliance Filtering (NSFW, Copyright, Face/Privacy) ⚠️

SpatialVID has explicit manual safety intervention: during the pre-screening stage, humans review and remove videos with titles containing inappropriate terms, as well as removing "panoramic camera"-type incompatible content; its license, CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0, restricts commercial use. Action100M's privacy protection comes from upstream — it uses the already face-blurred 1,199,096-clip version of HowTo100M, and releases only annotations rather than videos, further reducing copyright and privacy exposure. WildWorld naturally has no privacy risk, as all footage is game-engine-rendered virtual characters and monsters with no real human faces. SceneScribe-1M does not describe any independent NSFW/face/copyright filtering module, relying on the cleaning already performed by its upstream datasets (HD-VILA, Panda-70M, Koala-36M) [uncertain].

## Captioning Approach

### Caption Models Used (In-House VLM/Open-Source Model, Model Scale) ⚠️

SceneScribe-1M: Qwen2.5-VL-72B (an open-source VLM, 72B scale), leveraging its dynamic-resolution handling and absolute time-encoding capability to process long videos, with the same model handling both quality filtering and caption generation; SpatialVID: a two-model cascade — Gemini-2.0-Flash (a closed-source commercial API) performs visual parsing on 1fps-sampled frames to produce a draft, and Qwen3-30B-A3B (an open-source MoE, 30B total/3B active) refines the language and injects camera-pose priors to correct motion-direction descriptions; WildWorld: uses a vision-language model to generate two-tier captions at the action-sequence level and the sample level, with the specific model unspecified in the paper [uncertain]; Action100M: a three-model division of labor — Llama-3.2-Vision-11B handles frame-level (middle-frame) captioning, Perception-LM-3B (PLM-3B) handles segment-level captioning, and GPT-OSS-120B (a 120B reasoning model) handles evidence aggregation and structured field extraction, with a total annotation compute cost of about 1.3 million V100 GPU-hours plus 300,000 H100/H200 GPU-hours.

### Caption Density and Structuring Level (Short/Long/Dense Descriptions, Structured Fields Such as Camera Motion, Style Tags)

SceneScribe-1M: a single comprehensive structured scene description explicitly divided into three elements — scene settings, primary subjects or characters, and significant actions — a medium-density long caption; SpatialVID: a group of structured captions in parallel styles, with each clip producing four types of text — motion-oriented description (averaging 50.3 words after refinement), scene summary (averaging 28.6 words), immersive narrative (averaging 89.7 words) — plus five categories of structured tags (weather/time of day/crowd density/lighting/scene type), achieving a "one video, multiple granularities and styles" density spectrum; Action100M: the Tree-of-Captions hierarchical structure, with each node carrying five structured fields — brief action description (averaging 3.2 words), detailed action description (averaging 27.8 words), action-subject identification (actor), brief caption (averaging 19.2 words), and detailed caption (averaging 95.3 words) — spanning a complete density gradient from 3 to 95 words across a total of 21.3 billion words; WildWorld: captions are split into an action-sequence level and a sample level, but captions are not the primary supervisory signal, with core supervision instead coming from the 119 columns of structured numerical annotation.

### Joint Audio-Video Caption Structure (Whether It Covers Both Visual and Auditory Tracks Simultaneously, Whether Split Into Separate Fields, e.g., LTX-2's Full Soundscape Description, Script-a-Video's Factorized Streams, Foley-Omni's Three Fields)

Not applicable. All four datasets are purely visual, with no audio-track description and no visual-auditory dual-stream caption schema. Compared with audio-video-joint paradigms such as LTX-2's full soundscape description, Script-a-Video's factorized streams, and Foley-Omni's three fields, this batch of datasets represents an orthogonal direction — placing the second type of supervision beyond captioning in the geometric and state dimensions (camera, depth, 3D trajectory, skeleton, world state, action ID) rather than the auditory dimension.

### Dialogue Transcription and Speaker Attribute Annotation (ASR Transcription + Speaker Identity/Language/Accent/Emotion)

Largely not applicable. SceneScribe-1M, SpatialVID, and WildWorld have no dialogue transcription or speaker-attribute annotation at all. Action100M is the only relevant one: its source, HowTo100M's narration ASR transcription, is usable for 72% of videos (covering 10.6 years of content), but the paper explicitly frames its annotation paradigm as "vision-only action supervision that does not rely on ASR," with ASR used only as a reference/auxiliary cue, and no speaker identity, language, accent, or emotion fields are included.

### Geometric and Structured Annotation (Camera Parameters, Depth, 3D Point Tracks, Action Annotation, Explicit State)

The core field of this entry. SceneScribe-1M (a geometric triad + text): (1) Camera parameters — estimated by MegaSaM, based on the DROID-SLAM camera-tracking backbone, injecting depth priors from Depth Anything and UniDepth during bundle adjustment; the paper compares DPVO, Fast3r, MonST3R, and VGGT before settling on MegaSaM, citing greater robustness when feature points are scarce and better effectiveness for dynamic scenes and low camera parallax; (2) Dense depth maps — temporally consistent, continuous video depth output by MegaSaM's bundle-adjustment layer; (3) 3D point tracks — TAPIP3D, which projects 2D video features into 3D world space to compensate for camera motion, iteratively refining to obtain long-horizon-consistent 3D point tracks that can also be projected back onto the image plane for compatibility with 2D tracking tasks; (4) text description. SpatialVID: per-frame camera pose + intrinsics, depth maps (with MegaSaM's depth module replaced by UniDepth v2 + Depth Anything v2), dynamic-object masks and per-frame dynamic ratios generated by SAM2, three trajectory metrics (MoveDist/RotAngle/TrajTurns), and serialized motion instructions — derived from the relative translation and rotation between adjacent frames, mapped onto a controlled cinematography-term vocabulary (dolly in, pan left, truck right, etc.), discretizing continuous pose into an instruction sequence consumable by text models — this is the dataset's most distinctive design. WildWorld (engine ground truth, the highest precision): 119 columns of per-frame annotation, including (1) character and monster skeletons — 3D keypoints and joint-tree structure; (2) world state — entity position, rotation, velocity, animation ID, health/stamina, and other gameplay attributes; (3) camera intrinsics and extrinsics; (4) lossless-encoded depth maps; (5) discrete action labels — on the character side, 5,960 unique triplets (weapon type, bank ID, motion ID) and 455 unique character actions; on the monster side, 2,132 unique pairs and 527 motion IDs, covering movement, attacks, dodges, defense, item use, and transition actions. Action100M: structured annotation skewed toward semantics rather than geometry — temporal localization boundaries + open-vocabulary action labels + actor identification + a hierarchical caption tree, i.e., "structured action annotation" rather than 3D geometric annotation.

### Synthetic Data Construction (Controlled Perturbation/Edit-Constructed Training Pairs, e.g., InstructAV2AV)

WildWorld is the representative case of purely synthetic data construction: all 108 million frames are rendered in real time by the game engine, with an accompanying in-house capture platform (OBS Studio + Reshade sub-window splitting + multi-source timestamp synchronization) simultaneously capturing RGB and depth; actions and state can be actively controlled to construct controlled perturbation samples — changing the action ID under the same scene alone yields paired counterfactual sequences, a form of supervision unavailable from real video. The other three are all automated annotation of real video and do not construct synthetic training pairs: SceneScribe-1M and SpatialVID generate pseudo-ground-truth annotations via geometric reconstruction models, which is "model annotation" rather than "data synthesis"; Action100M extends supervision via VLM pseudo-labeling (with a comparison ablation against direct pseudo-labeling by PLM-3B alone), likewise not involving video synthesis.

### Human Involvement Level (Human Annotation, Human Quality Checking, Model Pre-Screening + Human Review) ⚠️

Overall human involvement is very light, dominated by automation. SpatialVID has the most human involvement: manual review of all 33,443 source videos during pre-screening, removing corrupted content, titles with inappropriate terms or "panoramic camera" phrasing, and content incompatible with the MegaSaM reconstruction pipeline; subsequent annotation is fully automated. WildWorld: human involvement is used to build the evaluation set — the 200 WildBench samples were manually selected to cover multiple characters/weapons/monsters/difficulty levels/events, with human evaluation cross-validated against VLM judging (85% agreement); data capture on the collection side is automatic in-game recording. SceneScribe-1M: the paper describes no human-verification step at all, with quality control performed entirely by Qwen2.5-VL-72B and TransNetV2 automatically [uncertain]. Action100M: no explicit human verification, relying on three rounds of GPT-OSS-120B Self-Refine in place of human review.

## Audio-Video Alignment

### Audio-Video Sync Detection Method (Lip Sync, Event Alignment)

Not applicable. None of the four datasets have an audio track, so lip-sync or audio-visual event-alignment detection is not involved. The analogous concept of "alignment" in this batch of datasets manifests as cross-modal geometric alignment — WildWorld uses multi-source timestamp synchronization to ensure RGB frames, depth frames, skeleton, and world state are strictly aligned at the same instant (the core engineering challenge of the capture platform is precisely this); SpatialVID uses camera-pose priors to validate and correct the motion-direction descriptions generated by the VLM, a form of "text-geometry alignment" rather than audio-video alignment.

### Specific Sync Detection Metrics and Thresholds (SyncNet/Synchformer/LSE/In-House, Threshold Values, e.g., MOVA's LSE-D≤9.5 and LSE-C≥4.5, SkyReels-V4's SyncNet |offset|≤3∧conf>1.5)

Not applicable — none of the four use sync-and-audio metrics/thresholds such as SyncNet / Synchformer / LSE-D / LSE-C. The corresponding quantitative thresholds instead appear in the geometric and image-quality dimensions: SpatialVID's aesthetic score removes clips <4.0, brightness must fall within [20,140], OCR text-area ratio removes clips >30%, and clip duration is 3–15 seconds; SceneScribe-1M's resolution is >1080p, frame rate ≥10fps, and duration 5–60 seconds; Action100M's segments are >0.5 seconds and tree nodes >4 seconds; WildWorld's State Alignment uses skeleton-keypoint pixel-distance thresholds of 4/8/16/32 px.

### Separate Handling of Temporal Sync vs. Semantic Sync (Time Alignment and Content-Semantic Matching as Two Independent Filtering Conditions)

Not applicable to an audio-video context. In the context of geometric annotation, all four do have an analogous idea of separating "temporal" from "semantic": SpatialVID maintains two independent sets of annotations in parallel — temporal-level camera trajectory (per-frame pose, MoveDist/RotAngle/TrajTurns) and semantic-level scene description (weather/time of day/crowd/lighting/scene-type tags) — cross-validating them against each other during the refinement stage; Action100M explicitly separates temporal localization (when something happens, determined by V-JEPA 2 embedding clustering) from semantic content (what happens, determined by VLM captioning), with the two pipelines decoupled; WildWorld's WildBench likewise splits Action Following (whether the correct semantic action was executed) and State Alignment (whether the spatiotemporal state is precisely aligned) into two independent metrics.

### Audio Quality Filtering (SNR, Silence Detection and Silence-Ratio Thresholds, No-Audio-Track Removal, Off-Screen-Sound Removal, Background-Music Separation) ⚠️

Not applicable. None of the four datasets process audio tracks, so there is no SNR, silence-ratio, off-screen-sound, or background-music-separation filtering step. Whether SpatialVID's transcoding to H.265 MP4 retains the original audio track is not stated in the paper [uncertain]; Action100M releases only text annotations, with audio to be obtained by users from the original HowTo100M source.

### Classification and Separate Handling Strategy for Speech/SFX/Music

Not applicable. None of the four datasets have any classification or differentiated handling strategy for speech/sound effects/music.

## Training Integration

### Multi-Stage Training Curriculum and Data Curriculum Scheduling (Basis for Stage Division: Resolution/Duration/Quality Score/Modality; Low-Res→High-Res, Image→Video, Short→Long) ⚠️

As datasets, none of these four define a training curriculum on their own, but all provide layered structures that support curriculum scheduling. SpatialVID offers the clearest two-tier basis for a data curriculum: the full 2.71M clips can be used for large-scale pretraining, while the balanced-sampled SpatialVID-HQ (0.37M clips, 20.63M frames) serves as a high-quality annealing/SFT subset; Action100M provides a semantically resampled subset (k-means, k=10³/10⁴/10⁵ at three granularities), allowing construction of a coarse-to-fine curriculum as needed; SceneScribe-1M's downstream experiments adopt a two-stage recipe of "baseline model + continued training with SceneScribe-1M added" to validate the data's benefit; WildWorld is used to perform conditional fine-tuning on a Wan2.2-TI2V-5B baseline, with training clips fixed at 81 frames, resolution 544×960, and inference at 16FPS, with no resolution/duration progressive curriculum described [uncertain].

### Data Mixture Changes Across Training Stages (Pretraining/Annealing/High-Quality SFT Subsets) ⚠️

SpatialVID's HQ subset is an explicit product of mixture design — tightening the distribution across aesthetics, brightness, and motion, and balancing scene-category distribution and motion diversity, showing a more concentrated quality distribution relative to Panda-70M, making it directly usable as a high-quality mixture source for the annealing stage; Action100M's k-means semantic resampling is precisely a mixture tool to counter the long tail, used together with deduplication of the 7.58M duplicate groups; SceneScribe-1M's downstream experiments uniformly adopt an additive-mixture recipe of "original recipe vs. original recipe + SceneScribe-1M," with the exact mixture ratio undisclosed [uncertain]; WildWorld does not involve multi-stage mixture scheduling [uncertain].

### Post-Training Data (SFT Curated-Set Scale and Selection Criteria, Preference-Pair Count and Annotation Method, Reward-Model Training Data) ⚠️

None of the four provide preference pairs, reward-model training data, or RLHF material. The concept closest to a post-training curated set is SpatialVID-HQ (0.37M clips, selection criteria being aesthetic score, brightness range, motion-trajectory diversity, and scene-category balance) and WildBench (200 manually selected samples, though it is positioned as an evaluation set rather than a training set, mixed in a ratio of 100 cooperative / 100 solo, covering multiple characters, weapons, monsters, difficulty levels, and key events). Action100M's high-confidence subset is obtained by convergence through multiple rounds of Self-Refine, but is not separately released as an SFT set [uncertain].

### Data Processing Infrastructure and Throughput (NeMo Curator/Data-Juicer/In-House, GPU Speedup Ratios, Processing Scale, Cost) ⚠️

Action100M discloses the most complete compute bill: about 1.3 million V100 GPU-hours plus 300,000 H100/H200 GPU-hours to complete annotation of 147 million segments, with the final annotation product being only about 205GB; it does not use ready-made frameworks such as NeMo Curator / Data-Juicer, instead using an in-house pipeline (V-JEPA 2 encoding + hierarchical clustering + three-model captioning + LLM aggregation). SpatialVID has an in-house, open-sourced annotation pipeline, with a product of about 3.53TB organized into 74 groups (each about 14GB of video + 1.5GB of annotations); GPU-hours and cost are undisclosed [uncertain]; its throughput bottleneck is clearly the MegaSaM reconstruction step (the paper states this was precisely why it was chosen, for its balance of accuracy and efficiency). SceneScribe-1M's pipeline cost is undisclosed [uncertain], with the main overhead being full-library inference with Qwen2.5-VL-72B and per-clip reconstruction with MegaSaM + TAPIP3D. WildWorld's infrastructure is an in-house game-data capture platform (OBS Studio + Reshade sub-window splitting, multi-source timestamp synchronization, lossless depth encoding), capturing 108 million frames over about 1,800 hours of game time — real-time capture rather than offline GPU processing.

## Results Comparison

### Quantified Impact of Data Strategy Ablations (Distinguishing: Filter-Strictness Ablation / Caption-Density-Style Ablation / Data-Mixture Ablation, and Corresponding Evaluation Metrics)

SceneScribe-1M (a data-mixture ablation, uniformly using a "baseline vs. baseline + this dataset" comparison across six downstream tasks): (1) monocular depth estimation (MoGe, average Rel across 8 benchmarks improves modestly, by about 6.14 on that scale); (2) 3D reconstruction (VGGT, pose AUC15 rises from 83.4 to 83.8); (3) 4D reconstruction (MonST3R, camera-pose ATE drops from 0.108 to 0.099); (4) 2D point tracking (CoTracker3, δ_avg^vis rises from 76.6 to 77.4); (5) 3D point tracking (SpatialTrackerV2, AJ rises from 23.25 to 23.5); (6) text/pose-to-video generation (AC3D, FVD drops from 38.20 to 35.15, CLIP rises from 28.62 to 29.98) — the generation task's gain is markedly larger than that of perception tasks, indicating that geometric annotation has the highest marginal value for camera-controllable video generation. SpatialVID (a filter-strictness and distribution ablation): the core evidence is its distribution comparison against Panda-70M — SpatialVID-HQ has a tighter distribution across aesthetics, brightness, and motion, with 80% of clips having curved/turning trajectories, whereas Panda-70M is dominated by static content and over 80% cannot be reconstructed by MegaSaM — quantitatively demonstrating that for geometric purposes, "motion-filtering strictness" is the primary factor determining data usability. WildWorld (an annotation-type ablation, baseline Wan2.2-TI2V-5B): compares five conditioning-injection methods — CamCtrl (camera trajectory), SkelCtrl (skeleton video), StateCtrl (discrete + continuous state, with Transformer-based entity modeling), and StateCtrl-AR (autoregressive state prediction); SkelCtrl achieves nearly 100% relative improvement on both Action Following and State Alignment versus baseline, but at the cost of aesthetic quality — a classic "structured conditioning vs. image quality" trade-off; StateCtrl-AR shows autoregressive potential but suffers from error accumulation. Action100M (a caption-density and annotation-type ablation): compares the supervisory effectiveness of the four fields — brief action / detailed action / brief caption / detailed caption — individually, contrasted against direct pseudo-labeling by PLM-3B alone, validating that the hierarchical tree + LLM aggregation approach outperforms single-model pseudo-labeling; it also performs a long-tail-mitigation ablation via k-means semantic resampling (k=10³/10⁴/10⁵).

### Evidence for Quality vs. Quantity (Cases Where Small-but-Refined Data Outperforms Large-but-Messy Data)

SpatialVID provides the most direct chain of evidence: actively retrieved, motion-keyword-targeted material totaling 21,789 hours proves far more usable than the passively crawled general-purpose Panda-70M — Panda-70M is much larger in scale, yet over 80% of it cannot complete geometric reconstruction, showing that "large but messy" is nearly ineffective for geometric annotation tasks; further, the 0.37M-clip SpatialVID-HQ subset, obtained by tightening aesthetics/brightness/motion along three dimensions and balancing categories, is positioned as a superior training choice to the full 2.71M library. SceneScribe-1M's evidence lies in its scale-comparison table: its 1 million clips / 156.7M frames is smaller than Koala-36M (36 million videos) and SpatialVID (about 2 million clips / 123.6M frames), yet it delivers gains across all six downstream tasks by virtue of its complete annotation combination of "camera + depth + 3D point tracks + text," demonstrating that annotation depth can substitute for sample count. WildWorld, with only about 1,800 hours of single-game-domain data, nearly doubles SkelCtrl's action-following performance thanks to its precise frame-level 119-column ground truth — an extreme case of trading data breadth for annotation precision. Action100M conversely shows that scale still has value, but its deduplication (141.8 million duplicate instances) and semantic resampling are precisely operations that "turn big data into refined data."

### Alignment Between Training Data Domain Distribution and Benchmark Taxonomy (e.g., VABench's Seven Major Categories) ⚠️

SceneScribe-1M's alignment relationship is the most systematic: its four annotation dimensions (text, camera parameters, depth, 3D point tracks) correspond one-to-one with its six evaluation tasks — depth annotation corresponds to the monocular-depth-estimation benchmark, camera parameters correspond to the 3D/4D reconstruction (VGGT/MonST3R) benchmarks, 3D point tracks correspond to the 2D/3D point-tracking (CoTracker3/SpatialTrackerV2) benchmarks, and text+camera corresponds to the text/pose-to-video-generation (AC3D) benchmark, forming a closed-loop "annotation-is-task" design. WildWorld and WildBench's alignment is explicitly designed: the data-side action ID corresponds to the benchmark's Action Following dimension, skeleton and world state correspond to the State Alignment dimension (evaluated at multiple pixel thresholds of 4/8/16/32), and camera intrinsics/extrinsics correspond to the ATE/RPE camera-control dimension (using the ViPE tracker for SfM), while image quality is covered by VBench's four metrics (motion smoothness, dynamic degree, aesthetic quality, image quality); the authors explicitly note that general-purpose VBench-style metrics have already "saturated" on interactive tasks — a clear example of the data-annotation system driving expansion of the evaluation system. SpatialVID's structured tag system (weather/time of day/crowd density/lighting/scene type) provides a basis for evaluation split by category, but the paper does not establish a fixed evaluation taxonomy aligned with it [uncertain]. Action100M's action-hierarchy annotation corresponds to two major families among its 16 downstream benchmarks — action recognition (SSv2, EPIC-KITCHENS-100, EgoExo4D Keysteps, Kinetics-400, COIN, CrossTask, and others, 8 total) and text-video retrieval (MSR-VTT, ActivityNet, DiDeMo, MSVD, YouCook2, PVD-Bench, Dream-1K, VDC-1K, and others, 8 total), corresponding respectively to the supervisory effectiveness of its action fields and caption fields.
