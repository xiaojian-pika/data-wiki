## Basic Information

### Name

Cosmos-Predict2.5 (the latest generation in NVIDIA's Cosmos world foundation model family; paper "World Simulation with Video Foundation Models for Physical AI," arXiv:2511.00062; the same paper also releases the Cosmos-Transfer2.5 ControlNet family)

### Releasing Organization/Company

NVIDIA (the paper is authored under the NVIDIA name, a large collaborative team with 88 contributors, including authors such as Arslan Ali, Junjie Bai, Sanja Fidler, and others; affiliated with the NVIDIA Cosmos Lab / Physical AI direction)

### Release Date (technical report/paper/open-source date)

arXiv v1 submitted October 28, 2025 (arXiv:2511.00062v1), with v2 revised on February 24, 2026 (the paper header is dated 2026-2-26, with a copyright notice of ©2026 NVIDIA). Code and weights were opened concurrently on GitHub at nvidia-cosmos/cosmos-predict2.5 and on Hugging Face at nvidia/Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B / -14B.

### Type (model/dataset/toolchain/evaluation benchmark)

Model (a video World Foundation Model) + accompanying toolchain + a self-built evaluation benchmark. The core is a flow-matching-based DiT video generation model that unifies Text2World / Image2World / Video2World in a single model, released in two sizes, 2B and 14B; the paper concurrently releases the Cosmos-Transfer2.5 ControlNet series (Sim2Real / Real2Real world translation) and several domain-specialized variants (autonomous driving with 7-view multi-camera, robot action-conditioned, AgiBot with 3 views, GR00T GR1). Evaluation uses the self-built PAI-Bench (Physical AI Bench). On the data-processing side, this work is linked to NVIDIA's separately open-sourced Cosmos Curator / Cosmos-Xenna and the NeMo Curator video curation framework.

### Degree of Openness (whether weights/code/data/pipeline are each open-sourced)

The degree of openness is relatively high among closed-source major vendors of the same tier, but the training data itself is not open.
【Open-sourced】(1) Source code: GitHub nvidia-cosmos/cosmos-predict2.5 and nvidia-cosmos/cosmos-transfer2.5, under Apache 2.0 License; (2) Pretrained and post-trained checkpoints: pre-trained and post-trained versions of both 2B / 14B, as well as distilled versions and domain-specialized versions (auto/multiview, robot/action-cond, robot/multiview-agibot, robot/gr00tdream-gr1), released on Hugging Face under the NVIDIA Open Model License; (3) curated benchmarks (PAI-Bench-related evaluation sets) and "curated post-training examples"; (4) the engineering foundation of the data pipeline is indirectly open — Cosmos Curator (github.com/NVIDIA/cosmos-curator) and its GPU streaming execution framework Cosmos-Xenna have been independently open-sourced, and the video curation module of NeMo Curator is also public; both represent the productized form of the curation pipeline described in the paper.
【Not open-sourced】The 200M-clip pretraining video dataset itself; the in-house content-type classifier (a 26-class taxonomy), the weights and thresholds of the internal aesthetic/motion/OCR/perceptual-quality/semantic-artifact scorers; the 3.1M-segment 7-camera proprietary data collected from NVIDIA's internal driving platform; the specific hyperparameters of each filter.
【Positioning】The paper explicitly states its goal is to "lower the barrier to adoption," handing the pretrained model to the community for domain specialization, rather than opening the data.

### Whether Simultaneous Audio-Video Generation Is Supported, and the Implementation Approach (native joint/cascaded/MoE fusion)

Not supported. Cosmos-Predict2.5 is a purely visual video world model; its input is text / image / video conditioning, and its output is audio-free video (93 frames, 16fps, approximately 5.8 seconds). No audio/speech/sound-related data processing or generation description appears anywhere in the 44-page paper; audio is neither a condition nor an output. Its "multimodality" is reflected in the text encoder being switched to Cosmos-Reason1 (a Physical AI-specific decoder-only VLM) and the action-conditioned input of the robotics variants, rather than joint audio-video generation. Consequently, all audio-video-related dimensions in this survey (audio category mixture, joint captioning, dialogue transcription, lip sync/event alignment, audio quality filtering, audio-type handling) do not apply to this work.

## Data Scale and Distribution

### Training Data Volume (video count/hours/token count, pretraining and SFT reported separately) ⚠️

The disclosure of pretraining and post-training scale is quite complete, one of the more valuable parts of this report:
【Raw input】Over 200 million (over 200 million) raw video clips were processed, corresponding to 35 million hours (35 million hours) of raw video footage (as a comparison, Cosmos-Predict1 used 20 million hours).
【Splitting output】After removing clips shorter than 5 seconds, over 6 billion (over 6 billion) candidate clips were obtained, ranging in duration from 5–60 seconds.
【Final pretraining set】Approximately 4% pass all filters, yielding approximately 200 million (approximately 200 million) trainable clips — these 200 million clips constitute the pretraining dataset.
【Post-training SFT domain scale】Using a multi-head classifier trained on InternVideo2 embeddings, data is split into five domains and counted: object permanence 10.4M clips, high motion 1.0M clips, complex scenes 1.6M clips, driving 3.1M clips, robotic manipulation 730K clips; there is additionally 4K high-definition cooldown data at 388K clips.
【Domain-specific data】Proprietary autonomous-driving data of approximately 3.1 million (3.1M) 20-second 7-camera surround-view clips; the multi-view model Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B/auto/multiview is trained on 1.5 million (1.5M) captioned multi-view clips; Smart Spaces has approximately 40K clips; robotics datasets are detailed under data_sources.
【Training iterations】Each domain SFT model is trained for 30k iterations with batch size 256; the RL stage runs 256 steps with batch size 32. The total token count and total iteration count for pretraining are undisclosed. [Uncertain: total pretraining iteration count/token count, the proportion of each source within the 35M hours]

### Data Source Composition (proprietary/public datasets/web crawling/licensed procurement/synthetic data)

The composition falls into four categories — "proprietary datasets + public internet platforms + public robotics datasets + NVIDIA internal collection" — but the general pretraining portion is only described qualitatively, without proportions:
(1) General pretraining: "sourced from both proprietary datasets and open internet platforms," covering domains such as driving, object manipulation, spatial navigation, human interaction, and nature scenes; the proportion between the two source types is undisclosed.
(2) The robotics domain is an explicitly named collection of public datasets, with clip counts broken down by camera viewpoint (center/left/right): AgiBot-Beta (bimanual) 194k/30k/30k, Bridge (single-arm) 36k, DROID (single-arm) 39k (wrist view)/51k/51k, GR00T (bimanual) 3k, 1X Technologies (bimanual) 17k, OpenX (single-arm) 500, RoboMIND (bimanual/humanoid) 16k/6k/7k.
(3) Autonomous driving is NVIDIA's own proprietary collection: "a proprietary dataset ... collected using NVIDIA's internal driving platform," approximately 3.1M 20-second surround-view clips, with 7 synchronized camera views (front-wide, front-tele, left, right, rear, rear-left, rear-right).
(4) Smart Spaces (warehouses/factories/construction sites, etc.): candidate videos are first recalled using search keywords, then a VLM (the Qwen2.5-VL series) makes relevance judgments clip by clip, and after splitting and filtering, approximately 40K clips survive.
(5) Synthetic data: actively excluded during pretraining (game footage, synthetic visual patterns, animation, and cartoons are all removed); however, in downstream applications, Cosmos-Transfer2.5 is used to generate synthetic augmentation data for training robot policies (see synthetic_data_synthesis).

### Clip Duration Distribution and Splitting Strategy ⚠️

The duration and splitting strategy are clearly disclosed:
(1) Long videos are first split into single-shot segments via shot-aware splitting;
(2) Duration threshold: "Very short clips under 5 seconds are discarded" — clips shorter than 5 seconds are directly discarded;
(3) Retained interval: the remaining clips range in duration from 5 to 60 seconds (ranging from 5 to 60 seconds), totaling over 6 billion clips;
(4) Caption granularity: each clip is further cut into 5-second windows for window-by-window annotation (this is a "finer content granularity" improvement relative to Cosmos-Predict1);
(5) Fixed training consumption granularity: all stages uniformly generate 93 frames at 16fps, approximately 5.8 seconds (corresponding to 24 latent frames after the WAN2.1 VAE's 4×8×8 compression);
(6) The duration of domain data is fixed-length: autonomous-driving clips are uniformly 20 seconds; Human Dynamics requires at least 5 seconds.
(7) Duration is one of the four axes of the final sharding (length), supporting duration-based sampling.
No histogram or specific distribution statistics are given for the 5–60 second range. [Uncertain: specific distributional statistics for duration]

### Resolution/Aspect Ratio Distribution and Bucketing Strategy ⚠️

(1) Bucketing: the sharding stage explicitly shards along the resolution and aspect ratio axes, together with content type and length for four axes total, with the purpose of supporting "efficient sampling, curriculum-based training, and fine-grained domain balancing" — so an explicit resolution/aspect-ratio bucketing mechanism exists, but the proportion of each bucket is not published.
(2) Resolution is used as the arbitration criterion for deduplication: during semantic deduplication, the highest-resolution version within a cluster is retained (the highest-resolution version is retained), on the grounds that higher resolution preserves finer visual detail and provides a richer training signal; during online incremental deduplication, "earlier + higher resolution" is used as the tie-breaking priority.
(3) The training-side resolution curriculum is explicit: 256p (320×192) → 480p (832×480) → 720p (1280×704), progressively increasing, moving to the next level only after the model converges and visual quality plateaus at each level.
(4) The cooldown stage uses a dedicated batch of curated 4K high-definition videos (388K clips), with the learning rate linearly decayed to 0, used to improve detail precision and motion smoothness.
(5) Architecturally, absolute position encoding is removed, retaining only relative position encoding (3D RoPE), explicitly aimed at improving generalization to resolutions and sequence lengths not seen during training.
(6) Robotics-domain pre-filtering removes low-resolution video.

### Category/Domain Distribution and Mixture Strategy (proportional control and concept balancing for people, actions, scenes, styles, etc.)

The domain system is one of the core designs of this pipeline, split into two layers — a "general 26-class taxonomy" and "five major Physical AI specialized domains":
【Layer 1: General content-type classification】At the end of the filtering chain, an internally trained content type classifier assigns each clip a semantic label, drawn from a custom-built taxonomy of 26 video types. This label is the first axis of sharding, used for "fine-grained domain balancing" and curriculum-based training. The specific category names and per-category proportions of the 26 classes are not published.
【Distribution-alignment constraint】At this stage, "physically unrealistic phenomena" are explicitly removed — video games, synthetic visual patterns, animations, cartoons — on the grounds of "maintaining alignment with the physical world distributions." This is a distribution-pruning principle grounded in physical realism, distinct from the pursuit of stylistic diversity common in general-purpose video generation models.
【Layer 2: Five major Physical AI specialized domains】To strengthen Physical AI capability, five additional dedicated domain pipelines are set up, whose output is merged into the general pretraining data: Robotics, Autonomous Driving, Smart Spaces (warehouses/factories/construction sites), Human Dynamics, and Physics. These pipelines share the same structure as the general process, but differ in two key ways: on filtering, the expensive VLM filter is omitted, replaced by a domain-specific subset of filters with adjusted hyperparameters; on captioning, a larger-parameter VLM is used with domain-customized prompts.
【Target-distribution sampling for autonomous driving】Driving data is not randomly sampled but "sampled from a large-scale corpus to align with a target distribution of diverse driving attributes" — sampled to a preset target distribution balanced across nine attribute axes: geographic region (US, Europe), traffic density (sparse/congested), ego-vehicle speed (city roads/highway), ego-vehicle acceleration (constant speed/rapid acceleration), ego-vehicle maneuvers (gentle turns/sharp turns), road type (urban/rural), rare road structures (tunnels, toll booths), visibility (clear/foggy), weather (dry/snowy), and lighting (day/night). This is the most concrete mixture-control strategy in the entire paper.
【Physics-domain taxonomy】A taxonomy of "visually observable physical phenomena" is first defined, covering core areas such as classical mechanics and fluid dynamics (e.g., shattering glass, colliding rolling balls, flowing water), and videos that highlight these dynamic properties are then specifically collected.
【Human Dynamics' quantitative admission criteria】The human domain uses hard numerical rules to control frame composition: a person must appear in more than 40% of frames; the number of visible people in any frame must not exceed 8; at least one person must occupy more than 3% of the frame area.
【Five post-training domains】SFT uses a separate division (object permanence / high motion / complex scenes / driving / robotic manipulation), produced by a multi-head classifier on InternVideo2 embeddings, see post_training_data.
[Uncertain: the specific categories and per-category proportion values of the 26-class taxonomy]

### Narrative Structure Distribution (single-shot vs. multi-shot, average clip duration, shot-count distribution, whether native audio tracks are included)

All training clips are single-shot: the first stage of the pipeline uses a high-accuracy shot-boundary-detection model to cut long videos into shots, explicitly "ensuring that raw shot transitions are excluded"; a subsequent "semantic artifacts filter" (VTSS-like) further removes structurally anomalous samples such as poor transitions and video-in-video. Consequently, the dataset contains no multi-shot narrative samples, and there is no concept of shot-count distribution.
Single-clip duration is 5–60 seconds, with captions annotated in 5-second windows; the model generates 93 frames / approximately 5.8 seconds per pass. Long-horizon capability is achieved through Video2World conditional continuation and Cosmos-Transfer2.5's long-horizon video translation, rather than by training on long clips directly.
Multi-view is a "non-narrative structural dimension" unique to this work: autonomous-driving data consists of surround-view clips from 7 synchronized cameras (30 FPS, 20 seconds); the multi-view model concatenates the 7 views along the latent time dimension (the latent time dimension is compressed to 8 to accommodate 7 views); the AgiBot robotics variant uses 3 camera views.
Native audio track: not applicable (no audio processing).

## Cleaning Pipeline

### Overall Structure of the Cleaning Funnel (number of filtering tiers, order of each tier)

The paper provides one of the most standardized and complete video curation funnel descriptions in the currently public literature, explicitly a seven-stage serial pipeline (Fig. 1):
【Stage 1: shot-aware video splitting】A high-accuracy shot-boundary-detection model splits long videos into shots, ensuring raw shot transitions are excluded;
【Stage 2: GPU-based transcoding】Each clip is GPU-accelerated transcoded (leveraging NVIDIA hardware decoders/encoders);
【Stage 3: video cropping】Black borders and spatial padding are cropped out; after this step, clips shorter than 5 seconds are discarded, leaving over 6 billion clips of 5–60 seconds;
【Stage 4: filtering (multi-tier)】Internally further split into seven serial sub-tiers, strictly ordered "cheap first, expensive last":
  4.1 aesthetic scoring filter
  4.2 motion filter (quantifies degree of motion and removes accordingly)
  4.3 OCR filter (removes clips with excessive text overlay)
  4.4 perceptual quality filter (DOVER-like, removes technical distortions and perceptual artifacts)
  4.5 semantic artifacts filter (VTSS-like, removes video-in-video, poor transitions, etc.)
  4.6 VLM filter (Qwen2.5-VL vision-language model final high-precision review; the paper explicitly states this is placed "at the very end of filtering because it is computationally more expensive")
  4.7 content type classifier (26-class taxonomy) + physical-realism pruning (removes games, synthetic patterns, animation, cartoons)
【Stage 5: captioning】Each clip is cut into 5-second windows, with Qwen2.5-VL-7B generating short/medium/long three-tier captions window by window;
【Stage 6: semantic deduplication】Embedding clustering + pairwise within-cluster comparison + retaining the highest-resolution version + online incremental deduplication;
【Stage 7: sharding】Sharded along four axes — content type / resolution / aspect ratio / length — supporting efficient sampling, curriculum-based training, and fine-grained domain balancing.
【Domain-specific side branches】Each of the five major Physical AI domains (Robotics / Autonomous Driving / Smart Spaces / Human Dynamics / Physics) follows an isomorphic side branch with two differences: the VLM filter is omitted, replaced by a domain-specific filter subset and tuning; a larger VLM with domain-customized prompts is used for captioning. Output is merged into the general pretraining set.
【Design philosophy】The ordering of the entire funnel embodies "cascaded review with increasing compute cost" — first using lightweight scorers to eliminate the bulk in batch, then having the most expensive multimodal large model perform high-precision final review on the survivors, with deduplication and sharding done last. Compared to Cosmos-Predict1, this is "a far stricter multi-stage filtering pipeline."

### Funnel Quantitative Retention Rate (input/output volume and final retention rate at each tier, e.g., Apollo's 27%) ⚠️

The paper gives a clear end-to-end quantitative funnel, and directly compares it against the previous generation — one of the most quantitatively thorough cases in this survey:
【Cosmos-Predict2.5】Raw material of 35 million hours / over 200 million raw videos → after splitting, cropping, and removing <5-second clips, over 6 billion candidate clips (5–60 seconds) are obtained → after seven-tier filtering + semantic deduplication, "Only about 4% of the initial clips pass all filters" → approximately 200 million trainable clips. That is, the overall clip-level retention rate is approximately 4% (6B → 200M, roughly 1/30).
【Comparison with Cosmos-Predict1】Original 20 million hours, clip-level survival rate 30%. The paper explicitly states: "it achieves improved data quality control through a far stricter multi-stage filtering pipeline, which reduces survival from 30% of clips to only 4%" — that is, while expanding the raw material volume by 1.75× (20M→35M hours), the survival rate was deliberately compressed to about 1/7.5 of its previous value, a deliberate, large-scale strategy shift toward "trading strictness for quality."
【Domain side】Smart Spaces follows "keyword recall → VLM relevance verification → splitting → filtering → approximately 40K clips survive," but the recall-stage input volume is not given, so the retention rate for this domain cannot be calculated.
【Undisclosed】The input/output volume and tier-by-tier pass rate for each of the seven filtering sub-tiers (only the end-to-end 4% total is given), and how much deduplication alone removes. [Uncertain: the tier-by-tier retention rate of each filtering sub-tier]

### Shot-Splitting Method (PySceneDetect/in-house model/shot-aware splitting) ⚠️

The first stage is "shot-aware video splitting": high-accuracy shot-boundary-detection models (plural, implying an ensemble of multiple models) split long videos into shot-level clips, with the core quality requirement being "ensuring that raw shot transitions are excluded" — no residual original transition frames may remain within a cut clip, avoiding abrupt scene changes inside a training sample. The paper does not name a specific model (no mention of PySceneDetect / TransNetV2, etc.), but NVIDIA's independently open-sourced Cosmos Curator provides a corresponding implementation with both a fast pixel-threshold-based splitting mode and a TransNetV2-based neural-network splitting mode, which can serve as engineering corroboration.
Splitting is immediately followed by GPU transcoding and cropping (removing black borders and spatial padding), with a 5-second floor for discarding overly short clips and a 60-second ceiling.
Backstopping of transition quality happens at the filtering stage: the semantic artifacts filter (VTSS-like) specifically removes poor transitions and video-in-video (picture-in-picture), i.e., a secondary remedy for splitting misses.
[Uncertain: the specific model(s) used for shot-boundary detection]

### Quality Filtering (aesthetic scoring, clarity, OCR text filtering, black-bar/watermark/logo detection) ⚠️

Quality filtering is the heaviest link in the seven-tier funnel, consisting of six independent filters run in series, covering the aesthetic, technical, and semantic levels, with the paper explicitly stating the ordering principle (cheap first, expensive last):
(1) Aesthetic scoring filter — scores and tiers input by aesthetic quality; the scorer model and threshold are undisclosed.
(2) OCR filter — "attempts to remove clips with excessive text overlay," removing clips with excessive text overlay (subtitles, pasted-on text, station logos, etc.); the detector and text-area threshold are undisclosed.
(3) Perceptual quality filter — explicitly stated to be "akin to DOVER (Wu et al., 2023)," i.e., adopting a DOVER-style disentangled-view video quality assessment (DOVER splits VQA into technical and aesthetic branches), targeting removal of "technical distortions and perceptual artifacts" such as compression blocking, noise, and blur.
(4) Semantic artifacts filter — explicitly stated to be "akin to VTSS (Wang et al., 2025)," targeting removal of structural/semantic anomalies: video-in-video (picture-in-picture, recursive screen recording), poor transitions, etc. This is a newly added tier relative to Predict1, specifically for handling "samples with normal technical metrics but structurally unsuitable for training."
(5) VLM filter — uses Qwen2.5-VL-series vision-language models "with higher precision" to remove "a set of undesirable issues" as final review; placed last because it is computationally expensive.
(6) Content type classifier + physical-realism pruning — an internally trained content-type classifier assigns a 26-class taxonomy label, and at this stage "physically unrealistic phenomena" are removed: video game footage, synthetic visual patterns, animation, and cartoons, to maintain alignment with the true physical-world distribution. This is a special quality standard under the Physical AI positioning — "non-physical-realism" itself is defined as a quality defect.
Black bars and spatial padding are not handled via filtering but rather directly cropped out in the third (cropping) stage (repair rather than discard). Watermark/logo detection is not separately listed (possibly folded into OCR or the VLM filter).
The threshold values for all filters are uniformly undisclosed; the domain side branches explicitly use "a domain-specific subset of filters with adjusted hyperparameter values," indicating thresholds are domain-tunable. [Uncertain: the specific model types and threshold values of each filter]

### Motion Filtering (optical-flow/motion-score threshold, static and shaky-footage removal) ⚠️

Motion filtering is the second tier of the filtering chain: "we apply a motion filter, which quantifies and removes clips based on their degree of motion" — quantifying each clip's degree of motion and removing accordingly. The paper does not state the measurement method (not specifying whether it is optical flow, frame differencing, or model scoring), and gives no threshold, nor does it state whether it is bilateral filtering (removing both near-static and excessively shaky footage simultaneously) or removes only the low-motion side.
Three pieces of confirmable relevant evidence: (1) robotics-domain preprocessing explicitly "filtered out low-resolution and near-static videos" — removing low-resolution and near-static video, indicating at least a low-motion-side removal exists; (2) the robotics domain also performs motion-rhythm normalization — for videos where the robot arm's action is too slow, playback speed is increased, to ensure consistent action rhythm across datasets, an uncommon approach of "actively rewriting the motion rate" rather than simple filtering; (3) post-training SFT lists high motion as a standalone one of the five major domains, retaining 1.0M clips, indicating high-motion samples are the deliberately preserved and reinforced target, not the object of filtering.
[Uncertain: the motion measurement method and threshold, whether high-frequency shake is filtered]

### Deduplication Method (exact deduplication and embedding-based semantic deduplication recorded separately) ⚠️

Semantic deduplication is an independent sixth stage, with a fairly complete scheme design that specifically accounts for the incremental-expansion scenario:
(1) Clustering: clips are first assigned to clusters using embedding-based similarity, reducing global pairwise comparison to within-cluster comparison, which is key to scalability;
(2) Within-cluster comparison: clips within a cluster are compared pairwise to detect semantically similar content;
(3) Arbitration rule: within a duplicate group, the highest-resolution version is retained; the paper gives the reasoning that higher resolution preserves finer visual detail and provides a richer training signal — this is an explicit "select the best among duplicates" rather than "keep one at random" strategy;
(4) Online incremental deduplication: each newly ingested clip is compared against previously retained clips, with tie-breaking prioritizing earlier and higher-resolution clips. This design allows the dataset to continue growing without needing to rerun deduplication over the full corpus, while maintaining semantic consistency across the entire corpus;
(5) Infrastructure support: integrates the Milvus open-source vector database for embedding retrieval, supporting semantic similarity search and caption-level text embedding retrieval.
The paper only describes semantic/embedding-level deduplication and does not separately describe exact deduplication (MD5/byte-level hashing) — presumably handled upstream by the collection process or the Cosmos Curator engineering implementation, which the paper does not mention. [Uncertain: the embedding model type, similarity threshold, whether separate exact deduplication also exists]

### VLM/LLM as Data Quality Inspector (multimodal LLM quality scoring and mismatch removal; the 2026 trend of shifting from shallow scorers to LLM-based semantic judgment)

This pipeline is a typical representative of the two-stage pattern of "shallow scorers + large-model final review," and the paper rarely explicitly explains why it is ordered this way:
【VLM as final quality reviewer】"Finally, we use a vision language model (VLM) (Bai et al., 2025) to further remove clips with a set of undesirable issues with higher precision. We apply the VLM at the very end of filtering because it is computationally more expensive." — that is, the VLM (Qwen2.5-VL series) does not replace the shallow scorers, but is placed at the end of the chain to perform a high-precision second review of the survivors, with the reasoning being computational cost. This is exactly the economic design of "first use cheap filters to remove the bulk, then let the large model refine the boundary cases," and also represents the transitional form of the industry's shift, around 2026, from pure shallow scoring toward large-model semantic judgment.
【VLM as recall verifier】In the Smart Spaces domain: candidate videos are first recalled via search keywords, and then "used a VLM (Bai et al., 2025) to verify its relevance" — verifying topical relevance clip-by-clip with a VLM, treating the VLM as a semantic-level topic classification/recall purifier.
【VLM as annotator】Captioning is entirely handled by Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with domain data switching to a larger-parameter model of the same family.
【Dedicated classifiers as domain sorters】The content-type classifier (internally trained, 26-class taxonomy) assigns a category to each clip at the end of the filtering chain; on the post-training side, a multi-head classifier on InternVideo2 embeddings splits data into five SFT domains. These two are discriminative small models rather than generative large models, embodying "don't use a large model where a small model will do."
【Model-like scorers】The perceptual quality filter uses a DOVER-style model, and the semantic artifacts filter uses a VTSS-style model — both are learned quality-assessment models rather than rule-based thresholds.
【Model referee on the RL side】Post-training uses VideoAlign (a VLM-based reward model) as the reward model, scoring across three dimensions — text alignment, motion quality, and visual quality — paired with a custom-built Elastic Reward Service (supporting dynamic scaling, latent transfer compression, pipeline parallelism between decode and inference, CUDA IPC zero-copy, and an asynchronous UUID mechanism with Redis for storing results) — a rare case of engineering "model as referee" all the way to the service level.
【Exception on the domain side branches】The five major Physical AI domains explicitly omit the VLM filter, replacing it with a domain-specific filter subset and tuning. This indicates the team judged that where the data source is already trustworthy and the domain is already narrowed, the cost-effectiveness of the expensive VLM final review is insufficient — a valuable engineering judgment.

## Annotation Methods

### Caption Model Used (in-house VLM/open-source model, model scale) ⚠️

Adopts a layered strategy of "medium-scale VLM for general domain, larger VLM for specialized domains":
(1) General pretraining captioning: Qwen2.5-VL-7B (the paper explicitly names the model and parameter count, citing Bai et al., 2025), generating a caption for each 5-second window, with the prompt engineered to produce "factual, context-aware captions."
(2) Domain-specialized captioning: explicitly switches to "a larger VLM model (Bai et al., 2025)" — still in the Qwen2.5-VL family but a larger tier (not specified whether 32B or 72B) — with a customized prompt for each domain. The team's judgment is that domain data is much smaller in volume but demands higher description precision, justifying a larger model.
(3) Captioning for the multi-view driving model: the paper writes "Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct," generated once every 150 frames, at three length tiers (as stated in the original text, differing in phrasing from the "VL" version used on the general side — suspected either to be a typo or to refer to using an LLM for a secondary rewrite of the visual output).
(4) Domain classification models: the content-type classifier is an internally trained model; post-training domain division uses a multi-head classifier trained on InternVideo2 embeddings.
(5) Text encoder (not a caption model, but related): Cosmos-Reason1 replaces Cosmos-Predict1's T5, a Physical-AI-specific decoder-only VLM; rather than taking the output of a single layer, it concatenates the activations of multiple blocks and projects them to 1024 dimensions, to capture both local and global linguistic context simultaneously.

### Caption Density and Structuring Degree (short/long/dense descriptions, structured fields such as camera movement, style tags) ⚠️

Structuring is reflected across three dimensions: "temporal granularity segmentation + three length tiers + domain-customized fields":
【Temporal granularity】Each clip is cut into 5-second windows for window-by-window captioning, rather than one sentence for the whole clip. The paper lists this as one of the core improvements relative to Cosmos-Predict1: "introduces finer content granularity by segmenting clips into shorter temporal windows," aimed at obtaining "richer and more precise supervision signals."
【Three length tiers】Captions are uniformly produced at short / medium / long lengths (Captions are produced at multiple lengths), serving both as training supervision signal and as conditioning prompt, allowing the model to adapt to both brief user input and detailed description. The driving domain likewise uses three tiers. Specific token-length ranges are not given.
【General-side content constraints】Prompt engineering guides the model to describe the primary object, its motion, and key semantic details of the scene, with an emphasis on being factual to suppress hallucination.
【Robotics-domain caption schema】The most heavily structured: the prompt mandates first enumerating the initial scene, then describing the robot's actions in chronological order, and must explicitly annotate motion type (linear / rotational), involved parts (arm / wrist / gripper), camera motion, and fine-grained object attributes. It also normalizes viewpoint and embodiment phrasing (unifying camera-view naming across datasets). To reduce hallucination, the dataset's own metadata is injected into the prompt: GR00T injects task descriptions with human success ratings, Bridge injects step-level instructions, and AgiBot injects initial scene descriptions. This practice of "injecting metadata into the prompt to constrain the VLM" is an effective paradigm for suppressing video-caption hallucination.
【Driving-domain caption schema】Six mandatory categories of elements: ① agents the ego vehicle needs to attend to (vehicles/pedestrians/cyclists) and traffic elements (traffic lights/signs); ② global environmental factors (weather, time of day, road conditions); ③ longitudinal and lateral meta-actions of the ego vehicle and surrounding vehicles; ④ speed of the ego vehicle and surrounding vehicles; ⑤ dynamic behavior or state transitions of other objects; ⑥ interactions between key objects.
【Smart Spaces / Physics / Human Dynamics】Each has a customized prompt: Smart Spaces specifies that the video focuses on manufacturing environments such as factories/warehouses/industrial facilities/vehicles, and adjusts language style accordingly; Physics guides the VLM to accurately and thoroughly describe underlying physical processes and object interactions; Human Dynamics emphasizes fine-grained description of human motion and dynamics.
The average token count of captions and the mixing-ratio sampling of the three length tiers are not disclosed. [Uncertain: caption length figures and the mix ratio across the three tiers]

### Joint Audio-Video Caption Structure (whether visual + auditory tracks are covered simultaneously, whether they are split into separate fields, e.g., LTX-2's full soundscape description, Script-a-Video's factorized streams, Foley-Omni's three-field structure)

Not applicable. The model has no audio modality, and captions are entirely visual/semantic descriptions, containing no auditory-track fields at all, nor any schema splitting visual and auditory streams. A comparable "multi-stream decomposition" structure in this work is reflected along the spatial dimension rather than the modality dimension: the seven camera views in the driving domain are each independently encoded, with captions generated once every 150 frames; and captions are split along the temporal dimension via 5-second windows. This is not comparable to genuine joint audio-video caption paradigms such as LTX-2's full soundscape description, Script-a-Video's factorized streams, or Foley-Omni's three-field structure.

### Dialogue Transcription and Speaker Attribute Annotation (ASR transcription + speaker identity/language/accent/emotion)

Not applicable. There is no audio input, so there is no ASR transcription or speaker identity/language/accent/emotion annotation of any kind. Annotation related to "people" instead follows a purely visual-geometric route: the Human Dynamics domain uses YOLOX for human detection and RTMPose for full-body keypoints and facial landmark estimation, used to filter frame composition (proportion of frames with people, cap on number of people, per-person area proportion), rather than for speech- or expression-semantic annotation.

### Geometric and Structured Annotation (camera parameters, depth, 3D point tracks, action labels, explicit state)

Geometric and structured annotation is a distinguishing feature of this work relative to general-purpose video generation models, given its orientation toward Physical AI and robotics/driving simulation:
【Human keypoints】The Human Dynamics domain uses YOLOX (Ge et al., 2021) for human detection and RTMPose (Jiang et al., 2023) for full-body keypoints and facial landmark estimation; these geometric quantities are used as hard admission criteria for the dataset: proportion of frames containing a person >40%, number of visible people per frame ≤8, at least one person occupying ≥3% of frame area.
【Multi-camera calibration and viewpoint structure】Driving data consists of 7 synchronized cameras (front-wide, front-tele, front-left/left, front-right/right, rear, rear-left, rear-right, with naming varying slightly across sections), 30 FPS, 20 seconds; each view is concatenated in the latent channel dimension with a 7-dimensional per-view learnable embedding, and a separate 3D-factorized RoPE is constructed for each view — i.e., view identity is explicitly encoded as structured information.
【Driving structured control signal "world scenario map"】The control input for Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B/auto/multiview is constructed by projecting HD high-precision map elements and dynamic objects onto the seven camera views, including lane lines, road markings, poles, road boundaries, traffic lights (including light state), and traffic signs, capable of expressing complex road topology including overpasses; dynamic objects are represented as 3D bounding-box cuboids, color-coded by coarse-grained category ontology (truck / vehicle / pedestrian, etc.), with shading distinguishing front from back. This is the heaviest structured annotation system in the entire paper.
【Robotics-domain action and metadata】Action-conditioned variants use action as an input condition; on the captioning side, motion type (linear/rotational), involved parts (arm/wrist/gripper), and camera motion are mandatorily annotated; structured metadata is injected, such as GR00T's human success ratings and Bridge's step-level instructions. Policy-learning experiments additionally include action chunks of end-effector pose and gripper commands (8-step horizon, sampled at 10 FPS) and joint state.
【Segmentation and detection (for downstream augmentation)】In the robot-policy data augmentation, Grounding DINO + SAMv2 is used to detect and segment robot pixels, enabling zone-differentiated control where "edge control applies to the whole image while blur control applies only to robot pixels"; Cosmos-Transfer2.5's general-purpose ControlNet supports four control modalities: edge, blur, segmentation, and depth.
【Not covered】General pretraining corpus does not include depth maps, 3D point tracks, or camera extrinsic trajectories as annotation (these appear only in ControlNet control signals and the proprietary driving data).

### Synthetic Data Construction (controlled perturbation/editing to construct training pairs, e.g., InstructAV2AV)

Presents a "reject synthetic on the training-data side, produce synthetic on the downstream-application side" dual attitude, a notable characteristic of this work:
【Pretraining side: active exclusion of synthetic content】At the end of the filtering chain, video games, synthetic visual patterns, animations, and cartoons are explicitly removed, on the grounds of maintaining alignment with the true physical-world distribution. That is, at the training-corpus level, synthetic/non-physical content is treated as contamination.
【Controlled rewriting of robot data】In the robotics domain, playback speed is increased for videos with overly slow motion, to normalize action rhythm — a controlled temporal perturbation applied to real data.
【Downstream: the model as a synthetic-data factory】This is one of the paper's core application claims — using Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B to visually augment real robot demonstrations to expand policy training sets. Specific method: 100 human teleoperation demonstrations (a bimanual pick-and-place task of grabbing apples and placing them in a bowl) → a VLM generates a detailed caption for the example video → repeatedly using Cosmos-Transfer2.5 to generate and check to refine the caption → parameterizing variable components into slot templates ([TABLE], [COLOR_APPLE], [COLOR_BOWL], [SENTENCE_LIGHT], [SENTENCE_BACKGROUND]) → using an LLM to generate candidate variants for each slot → generating 5 synthetic variants per original demonstration (500 segments total), replacing only the image observations while actions and joint state are kept unchanged. Control parameters: edge threshold medium, blur threshold very low, CFG scale 3.
【Effect】In 10 real-robot test scenarios with 3 trials each: base policy using only 100 real demonstrations achieves 1/30 success; baseline policy using traditional image augmentation (brightness/contrast/saturation/hue perturbation, gamma correction, salt-and-pepper noise, histogram equalization, random blur/sharpening) achieves 5/30; proposed policy using Cosmos-Transfer2.5 augmentation achieves 24/30, significantly stronger across object swaps, tablecloth changes, added spotlights, added distractors, changed background cabinets, opening drawers, and combined hard cases. The paper argues from this that traditional pixel-level augmentation cannot perform semantic edits (changing object color, environmental appearance, lighting), while generative augmentation can.
【VLA synthetic data】Section 6.5 additionally discusses using Cosmos-Predict2.5 to generate synthetic data for training Vision-Language-Action models.

### Degree of Human Involvement (manual annotation, manual quality inspection, model pre-screening + manual review) ⚠️

The data-cleaning stage is fully automated, with human involvement concentrated in two places — evaluation and post-training model selection — a pattern of "humans do not enter the data flow, humans enter the decision loop":
【Data side: almost no human involvement】All seven pipeline stages are driven entirely by models and rules, with no description of manual annotation or manual review. The only indirect traces of human involvement are: manual annotations carried over from the original robotics datasets — GR00T's "human-labeled success ratings" are injected into the caption prompt; and the 100 human teleoperation demonstrations used for policy learning were manually collected.
【Manual selection on the post-training data side】The paper explicitly states in the introduction that it will "manually curate specialized post-training data tailored for Physical AI" — manually curating specialized post-training data for Physical AI, but does not state the rules, workload, or team size behind the manual curation.
【Heavy human involvement on the evaluation and model-selection side】(1) A domain-specific test set is built for each SFT domain, with a human preference study conducted, statistically split into SFT Win / Base Win / Tie; (2) model merging step: quality is first assessed on "a small, hand-picked set of challenging examples" — a manually selected small set of difficult examples — to select the best from 20+ candidate merged models, then verified on a larger evaluation set via human preference voting; (3) human voting comparisons are done before and after RL; (4) the comparison against Wan2.1/2.2 also uses human win ratios, with annotators making pairwise comparisons along four criteria: realism, visual quality, temporal consistency, and alignment with conditioning inputs.
The number of annotators, evaluations per sample, and consistency metrics are all undisclosed. [Uncertain: the scale and specification of the human evaluation annotator pool]

## Training Integration

### Multi-Stage Training Curriculum and Data Curriculum Scheduling (basis for stage division: resolution/duration/quality score/modality; low-res→high-res, image→video, short→long)

Training adopts a progressive curriculum, with the paper explicitly stating that difficulty increases along two axes: pixel resolution and task diversity, while the duration axis remains fixed (always 93 frames / 16fps / approximately 5.8 seconds). The five pretraining stages (Tab. 4):
· Stage 1: Text2Image only, 256p (320×192), 1 frame — first learning to generate high-quality single frames before handling motion and temporal consistency;
· Stage 2: Text2Image | Video2World, 256p, 1 frame | 93 frames — video is introduced, joint image-text training;
· Stage 3: Text2Image | Video2World, 480p (832×480);
· Stage 4: Text2Image | Video2World, 720p (1280×704);
· Stage 5: Text2Image | Video2World | Text2World, 720p — Text2World with no conditioning frames is finally added.
The criterion for advancing resolution is "advancing to the next stage once the model converges and visual quality plateaus," rather than a fixed iteration count.
【Conditioning-frame scheduling】In stages 2–4, either 1 or 5 conditioning frames are randomly sampled, with the model generating the remaining 92 or 88 frames; stage 5 switches to sampling 0 / 1 / 2 conditioning frames by probability, at 0.5 / 0.25 / 0.25 respectively — this is the key scheduling design unifying the three tasks within the same batch (0 conditioning frames = T2W, 1 = I2W, ≥2 = V2W). Conditioning is via frame-replacement (the first several frames of the generated sequence are replaced by the conditioning frames), with a mask token binarily marking which are conditioning input; loss applies only to the frames to be generated.
【Noise curriculum】Timesteps are sampled from a logit-normal distribution, with a shift applied that grows with resolution: β=1 at 256p, progressively increasing to β=5 at 720p, biasing high-resolution training toward higher-noise regions (the paper explains: high-resolution pixels are highly correlated, and insufficient noise means the model fails to learn meaningful structure). There is also an important correction: after observing abrupt, unnatural jump artifacts between frames, the team judged that too few samples were drawn from the high-noise region, and so modified the scheduler so that 5% of training samples are explicitly drawn from the highest 2% of the noise distribution, significantly reducing transition artifacts and improving temporal consistency.
【Subsequent stages】Pretraining → domain SFT (one model trained per domain) → 4K cooldown (learning rate linearly decayed to 0) → model merging → RL (GRPO) → timestep distillation (rCM, 4 steps).
【Optimizer】AdamW, β1=0.9, β2=0.999, weight decay 0.001; learning rate 3e-5 for the 2B model, 1.3e-5 for the 14B model; linear decay schedule + 2000-iteration warmup.

### Data Mixture Changes Across Training Stages (pretraining/annealing/SFT high-quality subset) ⚠️

The most prominent point about the mixture strategy is that the paper explicitly chooses "not to do mixture-ratio tuning," instead using "independent per-domain SFT + post-hoc model merging" to sidestep mixture-ratio tuning:
【Pretraining side】Image and video are jointly trained (Text2Image runs throughout all five stages), but the sampling ratio between images and videos is undisclosed; task mixture is implicitly controlled via the probability distribution over the number of conditioning frames (stage 5: 0/1/2 frames = 0.5/0.25/0.25). At the data level, the four-axis sharding structure (content type / resolution / aspect ratio / length) supports "efficient sampling, curriculum-based training, and fine-grained domain balancing" — i.e., mixture-control capability is built into the data organization form itself, but specific mixture values are unpublished.
【Explicit design tradeoff on the SFT side】"We fine-tune a separate model for each domain rather than training a single model jointly across all domains. This domain-specific strategy enables us to fully leverage the available data without the need to balance mixture ratios across a combined dataset." — a separate model is fine-tuned for each domain rather than trained jointly, with the explicit reasoning being "no need to balance mixture ratios on a combined dataset." The measured result is that domain SFT significantly improves performance in its own domain, causes only slight degradation in the general domain, and that degradation can be further mitigated by subsequent model merging. This effectively transforms the "data mixture problem" into a "parameter-space merging problem," and is the most instructive judgment in this work's data engineering.
【Merging as mixture ratio】Model merging is used to unify the various domain SFT models with the 4K cooldown model, trying four methods — model soup, TIES, DARE-Linear, DARE-TIES — with a hyperparameter sweep for each producing 20+ merged models total; quality is first selected for on a manually chosen small set of challenging examples, then verified on a larger evaluation set via human preference voting. Conclusion: except for DARE-Linear, the methods perform comparably, and model soup was ultimately selected for its simplicity and effectiveness. Another valuable finding is that "simple grid search over hyperparameters consistently outperforms heuristic selection based on individual fine-tuned models' win rates" — hyperparameter grid search consistently outperforms heuristic weighting based on each fine-tuned model's individual win rate.
【Annealing/cooldown】Curated 4K high-definition video is used for cooldown, with the learning rate linearly decayed to zero, aimed at enhancing fine-grained visual detail and producing smoother motion — a typical high-quality small-data annealing.

### Post-Training Data (SFT curated-set scale and selection criteria, number of preference pairs and annotation method, reward-model training data)

Post-training data disclosure is quite specific, divided into domain division, cooldown, and RL:
【SFT domain division method】A multi-head classifier is trained on InternVideo2 embeddings (Wang et al., 2024) to split samples into five domains. This is a reusable paradigm of "using a video representation model + a lightweight classification head for data domain assignment," cheaper than either manual rules or clip-by-clip VLM judgment.
【Five-domain scale】object permanence 10.4M clips, high motion 1.0M clips, complex scenes 1.6M clips, driving 3.1M clips, robotic manipulation 730K clips; plus 4K cooldown data at 388K clips. Note that object permanence dominates by an overwhelming margin (10.4M), reflecting Physical AI's emphasis on the basic physical commonsense principle that "objects do not disappear when occluded."
【SFT training configuration】Each domain model is trained for 30k iterations with batch size 256, with hyperparameters carried over from the final pretraining stage settings. Evaluation shows that each SFT model achieves a significantly higher human win rate over the pretraining baseline in its target domain (e.g., robotic manipulation domain: SFT Win 72.6% vs. Base Win 8.3%; object permanence domain: 50.9% vs. 27.7%; high motion domain: 44.0% vs. 34.7%; complex scenes domain: 42.6% vs. 35.4%; driving domain: 47.9% vs. 28.8%).
【RL】GRPO is used for online reinforcement on the merged model (and the pretrained model), so there is no pre-annotated preference-pair dataset — preferences are given online by the reward model. The reward model is VideoAlign (Liu et al., 2025), a VLM-based reward model that jointly evaluates text alignment, motion quality, and visual quality across three dimensions. Configuration: 8 rollouts are generated per input condition, each using 20 diffusion steps; within-group normalized reward yields the advantage; due to memory constraints, trajectory probability is decomposed into a sum of step-wise conditional probabilities, with gradients computed every two steps and accumulated along the entire trajectory (10 steps total) before a single parameter update; trained for 256 steps at batch size 32; a diffusion loss on the fine-tuning dataset is used as regularization to mitigate reward hacking; the final released post-train checkpoint is the RL-trained EMA weights of the merged model.
【Domain-specialized post-training data】The driving multi-view model additionally uses 1.5M captioned 7-camera 20-second 30FPS clips; the Cosmos-Transfer2.5 driving ControlNet uses a "world scenario map" projected from HD maps and 3D boxes as its control signal.
【Distillation】rCM (Zheng et al., 2025), a hybrid forward-backward joint distillation combining continuous-time consistency distillation and distribution-matching distillation, enables generation in just 4 steps after distillation, with PAI-Bench scores nearly matching the teacher (T2W 0.764 vs. 0.768, I2W 0.816 vs. 0.810 — the distilled I2W version is even slightly higher).

### Data Processing Infrastructure and Throughput (NeMo Curator/Data-Juicer/in-house, GPU acceleration ratio, processing scale, cost) ⚠️

Data infrastructure is one of the most thoroughly disclosed dimensions in this paper, explicitly aimed at petabyte scale:
【Scale and elasticity】The infrastructure has been upgraded to support petabyte-scale data processing; built on highly parallelized workflows, with dynamic auto-scaling of CPU and GPU worker allocation to ensure load balancing across heterogeneous resources.
【Throughput optimization】The inference stage employs video chunking and frame-rate control to reduce redundant computation while preserving semantic fidelity; the transcoding stage is GPU-accelerated (leveraging NVIDIA hardware codecs).
【Data lake and vector database】Integrates the Delta Lake data lakehouse (Databricks, 2019) for large-scale SQL analytics; integrates the Milvus (Zilliz, 2019) open-source vector database for embedding retrieval, supporting semantic-level video content similarity search and caption-level text embedding retrieval. The paper stresses that these analytical capabilities serve not only current training efficiency but also lay the groundwork for large-scale dataset exploration, retrieval-augmented training, and fine-grained knowledge mining.
【Productized counterpart】The engineering form of this pipeline is NVIDIA's independently open-sourced Cosmos Curator (github.com/NVIDIA/cosmos-curator, officially described as "powers the training data generation for Cosmos at NVIDIA"), whose GPU streaming execution framework Cosmos-Xenna is also separately open-sourced; the same capabilities have also entered the video module of NeMo Curator. NVIDIA's publicly stated throughput figure is: 2,000 H100s can process roughly 1 million hours of 720p video in a day (this figure comes from official NeMo/Cosmos Curator materials, not this paper).
【Training-side infrastructure (not data processing)】FSDP2 hybrid sharding (per-parameter sharding, asynchronous distributed checkpointing, meta-device initialization, taken from TorchTitan); Ulysses-style flexible context parallelism (simpler and more communication-efficient than Cosmos-Predict1's ring attention, and better supports NATTEN sparse attention and fused flash attention with JVP; context parallelism is dynamically disabled during image iterations and re-enabled for video batches); fine-grained selective activation recomputation (SAC); the Elastic Reward Service (pipeline parallelism between decode and multiple reward-model inferences, CUDA IPC zero-copy, asynchronous result retrieval via Redis + UUID, supporting batch processing). Efficiency figures: at 4,096 NVIDIA H100s, 720p, 93 frames, MFU is 36.49% for the 2B model (context parallelism degree 2), and 33.08% for the 14B model (context parallelism degree 8, lower due to greater communication overhead).

## Performance Comparison

### Quantified Impact of Data-Strategy Ablations (distinguishing: filtering-strictness ablation / caption-density-style ablation / data-mixture ablation, and corresponding evaluation metrics)

The paper does not perform a strictly controlled "data ablation" (there is no controlled experiment retraining under the same configuration but with a swapped dataset), but it provides three types of quantifiable data-strategy evidence:
【① Filtering strictness: cross-generation comparison rather than same-generation ablation】Cosmos-Predict2.5 compresses the clip survival rate from 30% (Cosmos-Predict1) to 4%, while expanding raw material from 20M to 35M hours. The overall benefit is reflected in PAI-Bench: Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B pretrained model achieves T2W Overall 0.751, I2W Overall 0.799, rising to 0.768 / 0.810 after post-training; the 14B pretrained model achieves 0.757 / 0.806, with post-training at 0.768 / 0.810. However, because architecture (flow matching replacing EDM) and text encoder (Cosmos-Reason1 replacing T5) were changed simultaneously, the gain cannot be attributed solely to data filtering — this is the main limitation of this evidence.
【② Data mixture strategy: human win rates of domain SFT】Human preference win rates (SFT Win / Base Win / Tie) of the five domain SFT models relative to the pretraining baseline: robotic manipulation 72.6% / 8.3% / 19.0%; object permanence 50.9% / 27.7% / 21.4%; driving 47.9% / 28.8% / 23.3%; high motion 44.0% / 34.7% / 21.3%; complex scenes 42.6% / 35.4% / 22.0%. The discernible pattern is: the narrower the domain and the greater its distributional difference from general pretraining (robotic manipulation), the higher the marginal benefit of domain data.
【③ Verification of model merging as an alternative to mixture-ratio tuning】A radar chart compares win rates of Model Soup, TIES, DARE-TIES, and the base model across six dimensions (five specialized domains + General), concluding that all merge methods (except DARE-Linear) perform comparably, and that the merged model achieves each domain's advantages while maintaining general-domain performance ("gets the best of all the worlds"). This indirectly validates the feasibility of the approach of "not tuning mixture ratios, using parameter merging instead."
【④ Rigorous controlled comparison for synthetic data augmentation (downstream task)】The robot policy-learning experiment is the only strictly controlled comparison in the entire paper: for the same task, the same 10 test scenarios, 3 trials each, base policy (only 100 real demonstrations) achieves 1/30, traditional image augmentation baseline achieves 5/30, and Cosmos-Transfer2.5 generative augmentation achieves 24/30. This is the strongest quantitative evidence at the "data augmentation strategy" level.
【⑤ Data/reward-side gains from RL】VideoAlign reward before and after RL: for the 2B pretrained model, T2W total score 1.08→1.69, I2W 0.23→0.42; for the merged model, T2W 1.23→1.74, I2W 0.24→0.45; human voting RL win rate is 40.0% (pretrained+RL) and 46.7% (merged+RL), both outperforming pre-RL.
【Not performed】Ablation of caption density/length (short vs. medium vs. long), ablation of the 5-second window-splitting granularity, ablation of each filter's individual on/off switch, and ablation of the 26-class taxonomy mixture — these are conspicuous gaps in this report's data ablations.

### Evidence for Quality vs. Quantity (cases where small, curated data outperforms large, messy data) ⚠️

There is fairly strong strategic evidence but a lack of rigorous controlled experiments:
(1) The most direct statement is the cross-generation strategy shift — actively compressing clip survival rate from 30% to 4% (a retention volume of roughly 1/7.5 of the previous strategy) while expanding the raw material pool by 1.75×, essentially "trading a larger funnel intake for a smaller, cleaner outlet." The paper's self-assessment is that this yields "a dataset that is larger, cleaner, and semantically richer" as well as "enhanced pre-training efficiency and improved downstream generalization."
(2) Indirect evidence in parameter efficiency: Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B exceeds Wan2.2-27B-A14B (0.806) and Wan2.1-14B (0.797) on PAI-Bench I2W (0.810); in human evaluation, despite the 2B model being 60.0% smaller than Wan2.2-5B and 85.7% smaller than Wan2.1-14B, it still slightly outperforms Wan2.2-5B (30.0% vs. 26.2%) and is comparable to Wan2.1-14B (33.0% vs. 34.8%); the 14B post-trained model achieves a human win rate of 48.6% vs. 31.8% against Wan2.1-14B, and 38.1% vs. 35.9% against the double-parameter-count Wan2.2-27B-A14B. The team attributes this to stricter data and a better text encoder, but does not perform a single-factor decomposition of the data contribution.
(3) The cooldown stage uses only 388K curated 4K clips to improve detail precision and motion smoothness — a typical small-scale, high-quality data finishing step.
(4) In the robot policy experiment, 100 real demonstrations + 500 generatively augmented samples achieve 24/30, while 100 real demonstrations + unlimited traditional pixel augmentation achieves only 5/30 — indicating that the semantic diversity/quality of augmented samples matters far more than quantity.
Still lacking is a direct comparison of "strict vs. loose filtering at the same data volume." [Uncertain: the single-factor gain of 4% retention relative to 30% cannot be quantified]

### Alignment Between Training Data Domain Distribution and Evaluation Benchmark Taxonomy (e.g., VABench's seven major categories) ⚠️

The training-data domain system and the evaluation-benchmark taxonomy show a clear but not explicitly declared alignment relationship, a relatively rare strength for this work:
【Evaluation benchmark】The primary evaluation is PAI-Bench (Zhou et al., 2025, Physical AI Bench, a concurrent NVIDIA work), whose predict task reports two scores: the Domain Score, evaluated via VQA-style assessment covering seven domains — av (autonomous driving), common (general), human (human), industry (industry), misc (miscellaneous), physics (physics), robotics (robotics); and the Quality Score, composed of eight T2V/I2V metrics adapted from VBench; the Overall Score is the average of the two.
【Correspondence with training-data domains】PAI-Bench's seven Domains almost one-to-one correspond to the five major specialized data domains in Section 2.2 of the paper: av ↔ Autonomous Driving, robotics ↔ Robotics, human ↔ Human Dynamics, industry ↔ Smart Spaces (warehouses/factories/construction sites), physics ↔ Physics, common/misc ↔ the general pretraining corpus. This indicates that the domain segmentation of data curation and the evaluation taxonomy were co-designed (both being NVIDIA internal work), a clean example of "training-data taxonomy aligned with benchmark taxonomy." However, the paper does not explicitly declare this correspondence, nor does it give a proportion comparison for the categories on either side.
【Misalignment with the post-training five domains】Note that the SFT five-domain division (object permanence / high motion / complex scenes / driving / robotic manipulation) does not align with the PAI-Bench seven domains — the former mixes "physical-capability dimensions" (object permanence, high motion, complex scenes) with "application-domain dimensions" (driving, robotics), while the latter is divided purely by application domain. The SFT side separately builds domain-specific test sets for human preference evaluation.
【Other evaluations】The multi-view driving model is evaluated on the RDS-HQ-HL dataset via FVD StyleGAN / FVD I3D / FID and the multi-view consistency metrics TSE / CSE, and control-signal adherence is evaluated via downstream perception tasks (lane detection F1, x-coordinate rMSE, category accuracy; cuboid LET-AP/APL/APH) — this "using perception models to verify the structural correctness of generated content" evaluation approach is paired with its structured annotation system.
