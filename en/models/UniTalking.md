## Basic Information

### Name

UniTalking (Unified Audio-Video Talking Portrait Generation Framework)

### Publishing Institution/Company

Led by Huawei's Central Media Technology Institute, in collaboration with the School of Computer Science and Engineering at Beihang University (Beijing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics). Author list: Hebeizi Li (first author, Huawei + Beihang, work completed during internship), Zihao Liang (co-first author), Benyuan Sun (Project Leader), Zihao Yin, Xiao Sha, Chenliang Wang, Yi Yang (corresponding author). Positioned as "an open and reproducible unified audio-video generation framework for talking portraits," explicitly benchmarked against the closed-source inaccessibility of Veo3 and Sora2.

### Publication Date (technical report/paper/open-source release date)

Submitted to arXiv on March 2, 2026 (arXiv:2603.01418v1, primary category cs.CV, cross-listed cs.MM, cs.SD). The paper is annotated as accepted to CVPR 2026 (Findings Track). As of July 2026, no public release record of a code repository, model weights, or project homepage has been found.

### Type (model/dataset/toolchain/evaluation benchmark)

Model (audio-video joint generation framework). The core is UniTalking, a 10B-parameter dual-stream MM-DiT audio-video joint generation model, whose video branch inherits Wan2.2-5B weights while the audio branch is its symmetric twin structure, randomly initialized. No dataset is released (the self-built 2.3-million-sample dataset is not open-sourced), and no evaluation benchmark is released (evaluation reuses Seed-TTS test-en, the MiniMax Multilingual Test Set, and a self-built 50-prompt subjective test set, the latter also not made public).

### Degree of Openness (whether weights/code/data/pipeline are each open-sourced) ⚠️

The paper self-positions as an "open and reproducible framework" and takes "closed-source models hinder academic progress" as its core motivation, but the actual degree of openness as of July 2026 is questionable:
【Weights】No weight release found on HuggingFace or any other platform. [Uncertain]
【Code】No GitHub repository found. Neither the paper body nor the abstract page provides a code link or project homepage URL, which is at odds with its "open-source" narrative. [Uncertain]
【Data】Not open-sourced. Of the 2.3 million aligned samples, the OpenHumanVid portion can be independently obtained by third parties under its original license (requiring submission of user information for review before download authorization); the Huawei-internal-collection portion and the internal TTS data are entirely undisclosed.
【Pipeline】At the methodology level, only the structure of the three-tier filtering and the names of the tools used (PANNs, SentenceASD, LightASD, LipSync) are given — no threshold values, no stage-by-stage retention rates, no original prompt text, and no cleaning scripts are provided, making reproducibility markedly lower than comparable work (compare UniVerse-1, which discloses at minimum the full set of thresholds: 1080p / bitrate ratio 600 / DOVER 0.6 / SyncNet 2.0).
【License】arXiv uses a perpetual non-exclusive license; the model and code license is not stated. [Uncertain]

### Whether Simultaneous Audio-Video Generation Is Supported, and How (native joint / cascaded / MoE fusion)

Supported, via native end-to-end joint generation (a single diffusion inference pass produces both video and speech simultaneously), implemented as "symmetric twin dual-stream + joint attention":
【Architecture】Based on MM-DiT (Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer), with a backbone of N=30 MM-DiT blocks, model dimension dim=3072, 24 attention heads, and a total of 10B parameters. Trained with Flow Matching as a continuous normalizing flow (CNF); inference uses the UniPC solver, with Classifier-Free Guidance applied.
【Asymmetric design of dual-stream initialization】The video stream directly inherits the architecture and weights of Wan2.2-5B as a strong visual prior. The audio stream is designed as an "identical twin" of the video stream — architecturally identical but with randomly initialized parameters — so that the two modalities naturally occupy an isomorphic representation space to facilitate latent-level fusion. This is fundamentally different from UniVerse-1's route of "stitching two heterogeneous experts (Wan2.1 + Ace-step) and then performing layer interpolation alignment": UniTalking trades "structural symmetry + training the audio tower from scratch" for simplicity of fusion, at the cost of the audio tower lacking a pretrained prior (hence the need for a separate audio pretraining stage to compensate).
【Fusion mechanism: Joint Attention】Video and audio latent tokens are concatenated and fed into a single attention operation, allowing the model to learn intra-modal and inter-modal dependencies within one attention pass, explicitly modeling the temporal correspondence between visemes and phonemes. This is "shared self-attention" rather than the cross-modal cross-attention used by UniVerse-1/JavisDiT.
【Condition injection: Cross Attention】Text conditioning (UMT5-encoded) and reference timbre conditioning (MMAudio VAE-encoded) each go through their own key-value projection layers to perform cross-attention, and the two outputs are fused via element-wise summation. Setting up a dedicated KV projection layer for reference audio is a specific design choice of this work in condition injection.
【Anisotropic RoPE】Following OVI's approach, the temporal axis t uses standard RoPE, but the spatial dimensions (h, w) of audio tokens use RoPE from a single fixed position. This "spatial degradation" design forces the model to concentrate its attention budget on the temporal dimension, biasing toward audio-video temporal alignment at the architectural level.
【Supported tasks】T2AV (text → audio-video), TI2AV (text + identity image → audio-video), TR2AV (text + reference timbre → audio-video), TV2A (text + video → audio, used as a unidirectional supervision signal during training).

## Data Scale and Distribution

### Training Data Volume (number of video clips/hours/tokens, pretraining vs. SFT reported separately) ⚠️

【Final output】After the full filtering pipeline, the person-centric dataset comprises a total of 2.3 million (2.3 million) aligned audio-video samples. This is the only data-scale figure given in the paper.
【Characteristics of disclosure】Only the "number of samples" is given — no hour count, no total duration, no token count, and no raw collection volume — so it can neither be converted into hours for cross-work comparison, nor be used to back out a retention rate. If roughly estimated using the typical 5-second clip length of the Wan2.2-5B base model, 2.3 million samples would correspond to roughly 3,200 hours, but the paper gives no clip-duration figure, so this estimate is unsupported. [Uncertain]
【Split between pretraining and SFT】Training is indeed conducted in two stages, but the two stages use different datasets rather than a coarse/fine split of the same dataset:
- Stage 1 (audio-branch pretraining): uses "internal TTS data," with scale, language, and duration entirely undisclosed; batch size 256, learning rate 1e-5, 100,000 steps total. [Uncertain]
- Stage 2 (audio-video joint training): uses the aforementioned 2.3 million audio-video samples; batch size 64, learning rate 1e-5, 100,000 steps total.
【Derived data】Beyond the 2.3 million primary samples, 3 reference audio clips are synthesized for each video using IndexTTS2, producing roughly an additional 6.9 million synthesized reference audio clips (each 3–5 seconds).
【Scale positioning】2.3 million samples is relatively large for the talking-portrait generation space, but in the Future Work section the paper explicitly acknowledges that "model performance is constrained by available training resources and data scale, especially compared with closed-source models" — i.e., the team itself recognizes data volume as still a bottleneck.

### Composition of Data Sources (self-collected/public datasets/web-scraped/licensed procurement/synthetic data) ⚠️

Two sources, a binary composition, with no explicit mention of third-party procurement or web scraping:
【Public dataset: OpenHumanVid】A large-scale person-centric video dataset released by Fudan University at CVPR 2025. Its raw scale is 52.3 million clips / 70.6K hours, which after its own image-quality and person-quality filtering yields 13.2 million high-quality clips; each video comes with short/long/structured text prompts, human skeleton sequences, and a corresponding speech audio track — "comes with its own audio track" is precisely the key reason UniTalking chose it as a foundation (most video datasets lack audio tracks). UniTalking does not specify how many clips were drawn from it, nor whether its native captions and skeleton annotations were reused (judging by its practice of regenerating captions with Qwen3-VL/Qwen3-Omni, the native captions likely were not reused). [Uncertain]
【Internal data: Huawei's large-scale internal collection】The paper only briefly mentions "a large-scale, internal collection," without specifying the collection channel (self-scraped/licensed/self-produced), region, scale, time span, or content type. [Uncertain]
【Ratio between the two】Entirely undisclosed. What proportion of the 2.3 million samples comes from OpenHumanVid versus internal data is one of the most critical gaps in this work's data description. [Uncertain]
【Synthetic data】Used, but only on the audio side: IndexTTS2 zero-shot TTS is used to synthesize 3 reference-timbre audio clips per video (see the synthetic_data_synthesis field for details). There is no synthetic content on the video side.
【Internal TTS data】An independent data source used for Stage-1 audio pretraining, unrelated to the 2.3-million audio-video dataset above; its source and scale are both undisclosed. [Uncertain]

### Data Compliance and Provenance (proportion of licensed data, rights-cleared datasets, C2PA, etc.) ⚠️

The paper does not discuss data compliance and provenance at all: no proportion of licensed data is given, no rights-cleared dataset is declared, no C2PA or any output-side watermarking/provenance marking is mentioned, and there is no discussion of copyright, portrait rights, or deepfake risk. The compliance status that can be indirectly inferred:
- OpenHumanVid portion: this dataset's video samples were collected from "publicly available datasets," and users must comply with the original licenses; also, to prevent misuse, downloading requires submitting user information for review and approval. UniTalking, as a downstream user, does not state whether it fulfilled this review process or whether it complied with the upstream license terms;
- Huawei internal data portion: source unknown, with no compliance statement of any kind;
- IndexTTS2-synthesized reference audio: real human voices from actual videos are used as timbre references for zero-shot cloning, effectively generating a large volume of cloned speech in real people's voices for training — a sensitive point at the level of voiceprint rights and personality rights that the paper does not touch on at all;
- The model's core capability itself (arbitrary identity image + arbitrary voice timbre → talking video) constitutes a high-risk deepfake capability, yet the paper contains no misuse-prevention measures, usage restrictions, or ethical statement.
Overall this is a typical case of "method disclosure present, compliance disclosure absent," and compared with comparable work, the additional compliance issues introduced by its synthetic voice-cloning step are even more pronounced. [Uncertain]

### Clip Duration Distribution and Segmentation Strategy ⚠️

The paper discloses no information on clip duration: no minimum/maximum duration constraint is given, no average duration is given, no duration histogram is given, and it is not stated whether clips are uniformly truncated to a fixed length after segmentation. Indirect clues that can be inferred:
【Video side】The video branch inherits Wan2.2-5B, whose native generation spec is 5 seconds / 24 fps (121 frames), so training clips are very likely regularized to a fixed window of roughly 5 seconds — but the paper offers no textual support for this. [Uncertain]
【The only hard number on the audio side】Reference audio is explicitly constrained to between 3 and 5 seconds — this is the only quantitative duration specification in the entire paper, but it constrains the synthesized conditioning signal, not the duration of the main training samples.
【Latent-length constraints】Sequence lengths on the conditioning side are fixed: the text latent is fixed at 512, and the reference-audio latent is fixed at 257, with truncation for over-length and zero-padding for under-length. A reference-audio latent length of 257, combined with the temporal resolution of the MMAudio VAE, corresponds to a capacity ceiling of roughly 8 seconds — meaning the 3–5 second reference audio leaves considerable zero-padding headroom. The latent length of the main generation stream's audio-video is undisclosed. [Uncertain]
【Segmentation strategy】No segmentation strategy is mentioned at all (see shot_segmentation).

### Resolution/Aspect-Ratio Distribution and Bucketing Strategy ⚠️

The paper discloses no information on resolution or aspect ratio: no resolution admission threshold, no resolution distribution statistics, no aspect-ratio bucketing or padding strategy, and no frame-rate description. It only vaguely mentions, in the video filtering step, removing videos with "low overall visual quality," without stating whether this judgment includes a resolution dimension, which model is used, or what the threshold is.
Indirect clues that can be inferred: the video branch inherits Wan2.2-5B (TI2V-5B), whose base model natively supports 720P (704×1280 / 1280×704) @24fps, and whose 3D causal VAE uses a 16×16×4 spatiotemporal compression ratio (16x spatial, 4x temporal — a higher compression ratio than Wan2.1's 8×8×4); training and inference resolution is therefore very likely around the 720p level, covering both portrait and landscape aspect ratios. But all of this is inferred purely from the base model's specs — the paper itself discloses nothing. [Uncertain]
Comparative reference: UniVerse-1 explicitly gives a dual hard threshold of "remove below 1080p + remove bitrate-to-resolution ratio below 600"; UniTalking's disclosure granularity on this dimension is markedly lower.

### Category/Domain Distribution and Ratio Strategy (proportional control and concept balancing across people, actions, scenes, styles, etc.) ⚠️

UniTalking is a highly vertical, single-domain dataset — the target domain is extremely narrow, so the traditional multi-category-ratio problem does not really apply:
【The single domain: person-centric speaking scenarios】The design goal of the entire pipeline is explicitly stated as to "isolate high-quality, human-centric speech content." Each of the three filtering tiers converges toward this single goal: the video-level tier removes static footage (ensuring the person is moving), the audio-level tier removes samples without speech (ensuring someone is speaking), and the cross-modal tier removes samples with lip-audio desynchronization (ensuring the speaking person is actually in frame). The final product is referred to as a human-centric dataset.
【Comparison of ratio orientation with UniVerse-1】UniVerse-1 is "general soundscape first," with speech accounting for only 15.4% and non-speech soundscapes for 84.6%; UniTalking, by contrast, is nearly 100% speech-and-talking-person content — the opposite extreme. This directly corresponds to the two works' differing capability positioning: UniVerse-1 pursues general audio-video generation, while UniTalking specializes in talking portraits. In its conclusion, the paper acknowledges that "although validated only on talking-person generation, we believe the framework can generalize to general audio-video synthesis, including sound effects and music" — i.e., generality is merely claimed, not supported by data or experiments.
【Undisclosed ratio dimensions】None of the fine-grained statistics within the 2.3 million samples are available: no distribution of person attributes (gender/age/ethnicity), no scene distribution (studio/outdoor/meeting/vlog), no shot-scale distribution (close-up/half-body/full-body), no single-person/multi-person ratio, no frontal/profile ratio, and no source ratio between OpenHumanVid and internal data. Nor is there any description of concept balancing or resampling mechanisms.
【An inferable skew risk】OpenHumanVid's raw source is an aggregation of public datasets, while the source of the Huawei internal data is unknown; the difference in person and scene distribution between the two cannot be assessed, and the paper offers no account of cross-source distribution alignment. [Uncertain]

### Audio Category Distribution and Ratio (proportion and control strategy for speech/sound-effects-foley/music/ambient-sound/silence) — a dimension unique to AV models ⚠️

Audio category ratio in UniTalking is achieved by "filtering down to a single category" rather than "configuring by proportion" — this is its most fundamental data difference from general AV models:
【Target category: speech only】The audio-filtering step explicitly removes three types of samples: silence (muted), lacking speech, and low signal-to-noise ratio (low SNR). PANNs (a large-scale pretrained audio neural network, a 527-class AudioSet event classifier) and SentenceASD are used to perform "speech event detection." As a result, the final 2.3 million samples are, in theory, all guaranteed to contain valid human speech, and sound effects and music are not retained as independent categories.
【Handling of non-speech content: removal rather than separation】The cross-modal filtering stage further "removes videos containing purely diegetic audio without an on-screen sound source," using LightASD (a lightweight Active Speaker Detection model) for the judgment. Note that the paper's original wording is "diegetic" (sound originating within the narrative), but in context this actually means removing samples where "the sound does not originate from the person on screen" — i.e., voice-overs, narration, or music-dominated clips are eliminated. This stands in sharp contrast to UniVerse-1, which "does not remove off-screen sound sources," and is UniTalking's most targeted filtering step on the audio side.
【Background music】No source separation is performed, and no separate threshold is set. However, two pieces of indirect evidence indicate that background music is still partially present in the training data: first, the caption includes an audio-description field capable of describing the background acoustic environment; second, the experimental section demonstrates the ability to control the generated result via the text prompt "without any background music" — such controllability could only be learned if the training data contains both "with background music" and "without background music" samples, with the caption faithfully labeling this difference. This is a "don't filter, instead annotate as a controllable attribute" approach (of the same lineage as MOVA's use of the marker "This video has no subtitles." for subtitles).
【Non-speech sound effects】In the example shown in Figure 4, the prompt "a short laugh" appears and is correctly generated, indicating that paralinguistic sound effects such as laughter are retained in the data and covered by captions.
【Undisclosed ratios】The proportion of speech within total duration, the proportion of background-music samples, the proportion of silent segments, and the distribution of number of speakers are all entirely blank. None of the filtering steps have threshold values disclosed. [Uncertain]

### Narrative Structure Distribution (single-shot vs. multi-shot, average clip duration, shot-count distribution, whether native audio tracks are included) ⚠️

【Single-shot vs. multi-shot】The paper does not discuss shot structure, nor does it describe any shot-segmentation step (see shot_segmentation). However, two constraints indirectly suggest the training data is effectively single-shot: first, the cross-modal lip-sync filtering (LipSync) requires a continuously detectable face in frame that is synchronized with the audio track, and cross-shot cuts would break this continuity and very likely cause a sample to be eliminated; second, LightASD active-speaker detection likewise depends on a continuous face trajectory. Multi-shot samples are therefore very likely to be implicitly excluded by the filtering pipeline, but this is a side effect of filtering rather than an explicit design. [Uncertain]
【Shot properties of upstream data】OpenHumanVid has already completed "decoding, cropping, segmentation" preprocessing in its own pipeline, so the clips UniTalking draws from it are already segmented short-shot units — this may be precisely why UniTalking did not need to build its own shot-segmentation module. Whether the Huawei internal data underwent equivalent processing is not stated. [Uncertain]
【Average clip duration and shot-count distribution】Entirely undisclosed. [Uncertain]
【Whether native audio tracks are included】Mandatory, and the sound source must be on-screen. This is the strictest aspect of this work's audio-track requirements: not only must an audio track exist (excluding silent videos), not only must there be speech (excluding pure ambient audio), it must also be synchronized speech emitted by an on-screen person (LightASD excludes off-screen sound sources + LipSync excludes lip-audio desynchronization). These three conditions chained together are considerably more stringent than UniVerse-1's "an audio track suffices + the speech subset additionally checked with SyncNet."
【Narrative complexity】The data consists of short, single-person speaking clips and does not carry narrative structure. The model also explicitly does not support multi-person reference generation (Future Work acknowledges it lacks Sora2's "Cameo"-style multi-clip input capability).

### Language/Accent Distribution (data basis for multilingual lip-sync capability) ⚠️

The paper gives no statistics on the language or accent distribution of the training data: no language inventory, no per-language proportions, no accent annotations, and no discussion of multilingual phoneme-viseme mapping. Indirect evidence confirms coverage of at least Chinese and English:
【Bilingual evidence on the evaluation side】The TR2AV task's timbre-similarity evaluation uses the MiniMax Multilingual Test Set, with results reported separately for English (0.703) and Chinese (0.662) — the model can produce evaluable cloned speech in both languages, indicating that both Chinese and English are substantially represented in the training data. Notably, the Chinese score is lower than the English score (0.662 vs. 0.703), whereas two of the three comparison methods (ElevenLabs 0.613/0.677, MiniMax 0.756/0.780, Qwen3-Omni 0.773/0.772) score higher in Chinese than English — UniTalking is the only method showing a clear "strong-English, weak-Chinese" tendency, indirectly suggesting its training data may be predominantly English content. This inference is not confirmed by the paper. [Uncertain]
【Monolingual character of the TTS evaluation】The Stage-1 audio model's WER evaluation is conducted only on Seed-TTS test-en (English), with no test-zh results reported, further pointing to English as the primary optimization target.
【Text encoder】UMT5 (multilingual T5) is used, which does not constitute a language limitation at the architecture level.
【Transcription model】Whisper-V3 is a multilingual ASR model, likewise not a constraint.
【Internal TTS data】The language composition of the internal TTS data used in Stage 1 is entirely undisclosed, yet the audio branch's pronunciation ability is primarily established during this stage — the true determining factor of language capability is thus a complete black box. [Uncertain]

## Cleaning Pipeline

### Overall Structure of the Cleaning Funnel (number of filtering tiers, ordering) ⚠️

A three-tier serial funnel + one annotation tier + one derived-data-synthesis tier, with an overall structure of "single-modality first, cross-modal second, annotation last" — the clearest articulation of a contribution in this work's data section (Figure 2 of the paper specifically diagrams this pipeline):
【Tier 1, video filtering (single-modality, visual)】Processes the visual stream, removing three types of content: static video, video with text overlays, and video of low overall visual quality. Neither the model used nor any threshold is disclosed.
【Tier 2, audio filtering (single-modality, auditory)】Processes the audio stream, removing three types of samples: muted, lacking speech, and low SNR. The tools are PANNs + SentenceASD, used to perform speech event detection. No threshold is disclosed.
【Tier 3, audio-video cross-modal filtering】Evaluates cross-modal relationships, removing two types of samples: purely diegetic audio samples whose sound source is not on screen (tool: LightASD, active speaker detection), and samples with poor lip-audio alignment (tool: LipSync). No threshold is disclosed.
【Tier 4, multi-level joint annotation】For audio-video pairs that pass all filtering, generates three granularities/forms of caption (see caption_structure for details), using Qwen3-VL + Whisper-V3 + Qwen3-Omni-Captioner + Qwen3-Omni.
【Tier 5, reference-data synthesis】For each video, synthesizes 3 reference-timbre audio clips of 3–5 seconds each using IndexTTS2, constructing training pairs for the TR2AV task.
【Structural characteristics and comparison】
- The ordering of "single-modality first, cross-modal as a backstop" is a reasonable cost design: cheap single-modality detection eliminates most samples first, then expensive cross-modal detection is applied to the survivors. UniVerse-1's six-tier funnel is essentially the same ordering in nature (hard visual metrics → audio activity → speech detection → SyncNet).
- Quite a few steps are missing: no shot segmentation, no deduplication, no motion-score filtering (only a qualitative statement of "static removal"), no aesthetic scorer, no NSFW/safety filtering, no manual quality inspection, no watermark/logo/letterbox detection.
- The most prominent issue is zero threshold disclosure across the entire pipeline. The paper lists 6 filtering rules and 4 tool names but not a single numeric value — this makes the pipeline understandable at the "know what was done" level but infeasible at the "can it be reproduced" level, in clear tension with its core claim of being "open and reproducible."
【Verification conclusion regarding "SyncNet confidence > 0.9"】This investigation performed a full-text search of the HTML and PDF versions of arXiv:2603.01418v1 (10 pages total); no numeric SyncNet threshold of any form exists in the paper, and the number "0.9" does not appear anywhere either (the only occurrence of "0.9" in the full text is AdamW's β₁=0.9). The paper's lip-sync filtering is described only as "using LipSync to filter out samples with poor lip-audio alignment," without specifying the exact model variant or giving a confidence threshold. Although the body text mentions an Appendix, the v1 PDF does not actually include any appendix. Therefore, the claim of "SyncNet conf > 0.9" cannot be confirmed in the primary literature. [Uncertain]

### Quantitative Funnel Retention Rate (input/output volume at each filtering tier and the final retention rate, e.g., Apollo's 27%) ⚠️

The paper gives no quantitative funnel data at all: it does not disclose the total volume of the raw corpus (in clips or hours), does not give the input/output volumes for each of the three filtering tiers, and does not give per-tier or overall retention rates. The only known figure is the final output of 2.3 million aligned samples — the numerator is known but the denominator is entirely missing, making it impossible to compute a retention rate comparable to Apollo's 27% or MOVA's 26.39%.
An indirect order-of-magnitude estimate that can be made (for reference only, with no basis in the paper): if the 2.3 million samples come primarily from OpenHumanVid's 13.2 million high-quality clips, then the retention rate for that portion would be at most roughly 17.4%; considering that UniTalking additionally layers on three strong constraints — "must contain speech + sound source on-screen + lip-audio synchronized" (OpenHumanVid, while carrying its own speech audio track, was not filtered for talking-person scenarios) — the actual retention rate from the OpenHumanVid side should be significantly lower than this figure, with the remainder made up by Huawei's internal data. But the specific ratio between the two sources is unknown, so this estimate cannot be taken as a conclusion. [Uncertain]
Qualitative judgment: the elimination intensity of this pipeline should be considered fairly strong — each tier in the three-stage series is a hard boolean elimination (no soft weighting, no tiered retention), and the dual condition of LightASD + LipSync at the cross-modal tier naturally has a low pass rate for ordinary web video (a large amount of video contains background music, narration, non-synchronous audio, or faces that are too small).

### Shot Segmentation Method (PySceneDetect/in-house model/shot-aware splitting) ⚠️

The paper does not mention shot segmentation at all: no PySceneDetect, no in-house segmentation model, no shot-aware splitting, no cut/transition detection, and no post-segmentation duration constraint. This is the most conspicuous structural omission in UniTalking's data pipeline relative to comparable work (UniVerse-1, MOVA, Apollo, and others all treat shot segmentation as an independent tier of the funnel).
A plausible reason: its primary data source, OpenHumanVid, has already completed "decoding, cropping, segmentation" preprocessing upstream, delivering already-segmented clip units, so UniTalking can consume them directly without building its own segmentation module. Whether the Huawei internal data is similarly pre-segmented cannot be determined — if the internal data consists of raw long-form video, the lack of a segmentation step would be a substantive problem; if it is already pre-segmented, the omission can be explained as "handled upstream." The paper offers no explanation on this point. [Uncertain]
An indirect safeguard for shot consistency: both LightASD and LipSync in the cross-modal filtering stage depend on a continuous face trajectory, and cross-shot samples very likely cannot pass — so shot consistency is in fact backstopped by cross-modal filtering, but this is a side effect rather than a deliberate design.

### Quality Filtering (aesthetic scoring, sharpness, OCR text filtering, letterbox/watermark/logo detection) ⚠️

Visual quality filtering is concentrated in the first tier of the funnel, summarized by the paper in a single sentence as removing three types of video — static, containing text overlays, and of low overall visual quality — with no model name or threshold value given for any of them:
【Removal of static footage】Removes static video. The determination method (optical flow? frame differencing? a motion-score model?) and threshold are not stated. This is the only motion-related filtering condition in the entire paper. [Uncertain]
【Removal of text overlays】Removes video containing text overlays. This is a fairly targeted rule — burned-in subtitles, danmaku (bullet comments), and station logos are extremely common in talking-person video, and failing to remove them would cause the model to generate frames with garbled text. But the paper does not state whether an OCR detector (PaddleOCR/CRAFT, etc.) or some other method is used, nor whether the rule is "removed if any text is present" or "removed only if the text area exceeds a proportional threshold." Worth contrasting with MOVA's approach: rather than removing such video, it annotates it as a controllable attribute ("This video has no subtitles."), whereas UniTalking opts to eliminate it directly. [Uncertain]
【Removal of low overall visual quality】"Low overall visual quality" — an extremely vague description. It is not stated whether the assessment uses DOVER, LAION-Aesthetic, MUSIQ, or some other evaluator, nor whether sub-dimensions such as sharpness, blur, compression artifacts, or brightness are included. [Uncertain]
【Filtering not performed】No watermark/logo detection, no letterbox detection and cropping, no lower-bound constraint on face size/proportion (despite the task being highly face-dependent, no explicit face-size gate is seen), no exposure/color-cast detection, and no independent aesthetic scoring.
【Quality assurance inherited from upstream】The OpenHumanVid portion has already undergone multi-dimensional quality filtering in its own pipeline based on luminance, blur, aesthetics, motion, and technical metrics, plus additional person-quality filtering and caption-visual consistency filtering (structured captions generated with MiniCPM/CogVLM/Llama and refined via BLIP2 voting). UniTalking's actual quality control on the OpenHumanVid subset is therefore a layering of "meticulous upstream + coarse at this stage," while the Huawei internal data undergoes only this stage's coarse filtering — a potential risk of unequal quality-control intensity between the two sources that the paper does not discuss. [Uncertain]

### Motion Filtering (optical-flow/motion-score thresholds, removal of static and jittery footage) ⚠️

Motion filtering exists only as the single statement "filter out videos that are static," the first of the three rules at the video-filtering tier. The paper discloses none of the following: which motion metric is used (optical-flow magnitude / frame differencing / RAFT / a motion-score model), the threshold value, or whether an upper bound on motion is set (i.e., whether clips with excessively violent motion or camera shake are removed).
【Inference of a lower bound only, no upper bound】From the wording, only "static" footage is removed, with no mention of removing shaky/violently-jittery footage — i.e., there appears to be only a lower bound on motion, with no upper bound. This trade-off is reasonable for talking-person video — the target scenario inherently has limited motion range, and the main risk is "photo-like still frames" (a person not moving at all, or the video actually being a static image + audio track); removing such samples prevents the model from learning a non-moving mouth. But the lack of an upper bound means samples with handheld shake or fast camera motion are not removed.
【Inherited from upstream】OpenHumanVid's filtering dimensions already include a "motion" item, so motion quality for that subset has upstream assurance.
【Relation to evaluation】None of the paper's evaluation metrics (Sync-C / Sync-D / subjective video quality / timbre similarity / WER) relate to motion, and no ablation of motion filtering is performed, so the actual contribution of this step cannot be assessed. [Uncertain]

### Deduplication Method (exact deduplication and embedding-based semantic deduplication recorded separately) ⚠️

The paper does not mention deduplication at all: neither hash/fingerprint-level exact deduplication (pHash, video fingerprinting) nor embedding-based semantic deduplication (CLIP/DINO feature clustering) is discussed, and cross-source duplication issues are not addressed.
【Specific sources of duplication risk】This work's two-source structure raises duplication risk above that of single-source work: OpenHumanVid's video samples are themselves aggregated from "multiple publicly available datasets," so cross-subset duplication may already exist within it; and if the Huawei-internal-collection data also contains public-platform content, the probability of overlap with OpenHumanVid is not low. There is no deduplication mechanism between the two sources at all, which may result in some content being double-counted within the 2.3 million samples and oversampled during training.
【Deduplication at the identity level is also missing】For the talking-person generation task, there is also a duplication dimension unique to this domain — large numbers of clips of the same speaker (e.g., multiple episodes from the same host/actor) may be heavily concentrated in the dataset, causing a long-tailed identity distribution. The paper performs neither identity-level deduplication nor identity-level quota control, and does not report the number of unique speakers in the dataset. This has a direct impact on the model's identity generalization and voice-cloning ability, yet remains a complete information gap. [Uncertain]

### VLM/LLM as Quality Judge (multimodal large-model quality scoring and mismatch removal — the 2026 trend of shifting from shallow scorers to large-model semantic judgment) ⚠️

UniTalking presents an orientation opposite to the mainstream trend of 2026 — large models in its pipeline are responsible only for annotation and are not involved in quality adjudication at all; quality control is handled entirely by traditional specialized discriminative models:
【Role of large models: annotation only】Qwen3-VL (video captioning), Whisper-V3 (speech transcription), Qwen3-Omni-Captioner (audio captioning), and Qwen3-Omni (fused audio-video description) — all four models sit in the annotation step, downstream of the three-tier filtering. The paper describes no quality scoring, cross-modal semantic-consistency verification, caption-content mismatch detection, or hallucination self-audit mechanism performed by any VLM/LLM. This is a generation behind MOVA's approach of using GPT-OSS-120B for cross-modal consistency adjudication with a built-in hallucination_check.
【Quality control handled entirely by specialized discriminative models】
- PANNs: a CNN model for 527-class AudioSet audio-event classification, used for speech event detection (determining whether speech is present in the audio track);
- SentenceASD: works alongside PANNs to perform speech event detection;
- LightASD: a lightweight Active Speaker Detection model, used to determine whether the speaker is on screen — this is the most targeted tool choice in the pipeline, as ASD is precisely the standard solution for "audio-visual co-origin" determination;
- LipSync: a lip-audio synchronization evaluation model, used to remove samples with poor lip-audio alignment. The paper does not specify whether this is SyncNet, Syncformer, or some other implementation variant. [Uncertain]
【The model behind video-quality determination is entirely undisclosed】The paper says not a word about what model underlies the three rules of "static/text overlay/low visual quality." [Uncertain]
【Quality-assurance mechanisms are missing at the annotation step】The three caption streams are independently produced by three different models and then concatenated, and the fourth stream is produced by Qwen3-Omni through fusion, but there is no cross-stream consistency check, no hallucination filtering, no manual spot-checking, and no caption quality scoring. Given that Qwen3-VL, when describing video, is prone to cross-modal hallucination if it can hear the audio, and the paper neither states whether modality isolation was applied nor whether any safeguard exists, this is an uncontrolled risk point on the annotation side. [Uncertain]
【One point worth crediting】The tool selection itself is well-matched to the task: the combination of ASD + LipSync precisely addresses the two core failure modes of the "talking-person video" domain (sound source not on screen / sound source on screen but not synchronized), which is more targeted than general AV work that relies on a single SyncNet threshold.

### Safety and Compliance Filtering (NSFW, copyright, face/privacy) ⚠️

The paper does not touch on safety and compliance filtering at all: no NSFW/violent-content detection, no copyright filtering, no face-privacy protection measures, no minor-content identification, no voiceprint-privacy protection, and no model-card-level safety statement or usage restriction.
【The accountability gap is especially notable】Compared with comparable work, UniTalking's absence in this dimension deserves particular attention, for three reasons:
1) The model's core capability is itself a high-risk deepfake capability — given any person's image (TI2AV) + any voice reference (TR2AV) + any text, one can generate a video of that person saying anything, which is precisely the complete technical path for forging celebrity-statement videos;
2) On the data side, timbre-cloning training pairs were actively constructed — real human voices from real videos are cloned zero-shot with IndexTTS2, batch-producing 6.9 million clips of cloned speech for training, a step that itself requires compliance scrutiny at the level of voiceprint rights, which the paper does not discuss at all;
3) The paper's core motivation is "open accessibility"; if the weights were actually released, the capabilities above would proliferate without any barrier, and yet the entire text contains not a single sentence about misuse prevention, watermarking, content provenance, or terms of use.
【Partial backstop from upstream】To prevent misuse of its dataset, OpenHumanVid requires downloaders to submit user information for review and approval — the upstream data provider has a clear awareness of misuse prevention, while the downstream model provider (UniTalking) fails to carry that awareness forward. [Uncertain]

## Annotation Methods

### Caption Models Used (in-house VLM/open-source models, model scale) ⚠️

Entirely open-source models are used (primarily Alibaba's Qwen series), with no in-house captioner; the four models have clearly divided roles:
【Qwen3-VL】Responsible for generating purely visual video captions, producing two granularities: detailed and short. The parameter scale tier used is undisclosed (Qwen3-VL has multiple scale variants). [Uncertain]
【Whisper-V3】OpenAI's multilingual ASR model, responsible for speech transcription. Used to provide the audio's textual content (spoken lines).
【Qwen3-Omni-Captioner】The captioner variant within the Qwen3-Omni series dedicated to audio description, responsible for generating audio captions (describing acoustic content and environment).
【Qwen3-Omni】An omni-modal large model that receives both video and audio streams simultaneously, producing a single fused audio-visual unified description (the third caption format). This is the only one of the four models that performs cross-modal fusion. Its scale tier is undisclosed. [Uncertain]
【IndexTTS2】Not a captioner but part of the annotation/derived-data step; a zero-shot TTS model responsible for synthesizing reference-timbre audio.
【Text encoder】A frozen UMT5 is used at both training and inference time to encode text conditioning, with the text latent sequence length fixed at 512 (truncated if too long, zero-padded if too short).
【Design feature: the same content annotated via two parallel paths】There is both "separate annotation then concatenation" (Qwen3-VL + Qwen3-Omni-Captioner/Whisper-V3) and "unified model fused annotation" (Qwen3-Omni), and the outputs of the two paths are randomly sampled during training — a way of handing the "choice of annotation strategy" over to random sampling, letting the model adapt simultaneously to two text distributions (see caption_structure).
【Scale comparison with comparable work】The total scale of annotation models is noticeably smaller than MOVA's (MiMo-VL-7B + Qwen3-Omni×2 + GPT-OSS-120B), but larger than UniVerse-1's (QWen2.5-Omni + Whisper); and UniTalking's annotation is completed offline as a one-time process, unlike UniVerse-1, which generates captions online in real time during training.
【Annotation prompts】None are made public; there is no caption example whatsoever (Figure 4 shows a prompt, but it is only a test prompt used at inference time, not the original training caption text). [Uncertain]

### Caption Density and Degree of Structuring (short/long/dense descriptions, structured fields such as camera motion, style tags) ⚠️

Adopts a design of "three parallel formats + random sampling during training," the most distinctive aspect of this work on the annotation side:
【Format a: detailed video caption + audio caption (concatenated)】A detailed video description generated by Qwen3-VL, concatenated with an audio description produced by Qwen3-Omni-Captioner/Whisper-V3, into a single piece of text.
【Format b: short video caption + audio caption (concatenated)】Same as above, but the video description is replaced with a "short and concise" version.
【Format c: fused description】Video and audio streams are fed into Qwen3-Omni simultaneously, directly producing a single unified audio-visual description, with no concatenation.
【Usage: random sampling】During training, one of the three prompt formats is randomly sampled per sample as the text conditioning input. This approach serves multiple purposes:
1) Caption length/density augmentation — the model sees both long and short descriptions, so at inference time it can respond to user prompts of varying detail, mitigating the classic problem of "training captions being too dense, causing short prompts to fail";
2) Caption structure augmentation — the model sees both "concatenated (visual segment and auditory segment clearly delineated)" and "fused (visual and auditory interwoven)" text distributions, avoiding overfitting to a single caption style;
3) Amounts to a cheap form of prompt-robustness regularization, at no additional data cost.
This is a third route distinct from both MOVA's "unified fusion into a single caption" and UniVerse-1's "three fields always kept separate": UniTalking wants "both forms, randomly switched during training."
【Degree of structuring】All three formats internally consist of free-form natural-language text, with no enumerated fields (no camera-motion tags, no shot-scale tags, no style tags, no emotion enumeration). The so-called "multi-level" refers to levels of detail granularity and fusion form, not levels of field structure.
【Density and length】No length statistics are given at all, no caption example is provided, and no annotation prompt is disclosed. The exact length boundaries between "detailed" and "short and concise" are undefined. [Uncertain]
【Indirect evidence of a controllable attribute】The experimental section shows that the prompt "without any background music" can effectively control the generated background sound — indicating the captions do indeed contain descriptions of the presence or absence of background music, and that the descriptions are consistent enough to support controllable generation. But the paper does not formalize such controllable attributes as an explicit annotation standard.
【Temporality】It is not stated whether captions contain an ordering of events along a timeline, nor whether a caption corresponds to the entire clip or to a sub-window. [Uncertain]

### Joint Audio-Video Caption Structure (whether both visual and auditory tracks are covered simultaneously, whether split into independent fields — e.g., LTX-2's full-soundscape description, Script-a-Video's factorized streams, Foley-Omni's three fields) ⚠️

UniTalking's joint audio-video caption schema is the only one in this investigation's sample set to simultaneously offer both a "factorized" scheme and a "fused" scheme, letting the model be exposed to both at random — a kind of compromise answer to this problem:
【Two routes coexist】
- Factorized route (formats a, b): visual description and auditory description are generated separately by dedicated models and then concatenated into a single piece of text. Although physically concatenated into a single string fed to UMT5, the two segments remain semantically clearly delineated, close to Script-a-Video's factorized streams and Foley-Omni's multi-field parallel approach;
- Fused route (format c): Qwen3-Omni consumes the video and audio streams simultaneously, producing a single, visually-and-auditorily-interwoven unified description. This is closer to LTX-2's single full-soundscape description and MOVA's approach of fusing via a large LLM.
【Positioning relative to the two comparison works】UniVerse-1 is "split at annotation time, also split at training time" (three fields always kept independent and fed to the model in parallel); MOVA is "split at annotation time, fused at training time" (annotated separately, then synthesized into a single paragraph by a 120B LLM); UniTalking is "both finished products retained, randomly chosen one-of-two per sample during training." The three form three distinct points in this design space.
【Form of condition injection】Regardless of format, the result is ultimately flattened into a single text sequence (fixed length 512), encoded by a frozen UMT5, and fed to cross-attention — the model side does not distinguish which format the text came from, nor does it retain independent conditioning channels for visual versus auditory description. This means factorization exists only at the annotation stage; the model architecture itself is not factorized. The truly factorized conditioning channel is a different one: the reference-timbre audio (c_audio) has its own independent KV projection layer, separated from the text condition and then fused via summation. UniTalking's dimension of condition separation is therefore "semantics (text) vs. timbre (reference audio)," not "visual vs. auditory."
【Attribution of transcribed content】Whisper-V3's ASR transcription is folded into the audio-caption side, meaning the spoken-line content and the acoustic-environment description co-exist in the same text segment, not listed as a separate field (compare UniVerse-1, which lists verified speech content as its own separate field). This requires the model, during training, to separate "what is being said" from "how it sounds" from a mixed text on its own. [Uncertain]
【Missing mechanisms】There is no consistency check among the three caption types; no cross-modal hallucination guard; no caption quality scoring or filtering; and no verification of alignment between caption and audio-video content. Nor was an ablation of "which caption format is more effective" performed.

### Dialogue Transcription and Speaker Attribute Annotation (ASR transcription + speaker identity/language/accent/emotion) ⚠️

【Transcription】ASR is performed using Whisper-V3, producing the text content of the speech. The transcription result participates in constructing text conditioning as part of the audio caption. The paper does not state: whether the original language is preserved (or uniformly translated into English), whether punctuation and numerals are normalized, whether samples with low transcription confidence are filtered, whether failed transcription clips are handled, or whether timestamps (word-level) are preserved. [Uncertain]
【Speaker attribute annotation: explicit annotation entirely absent】No annotation of speaker identity, gender, age, ethnicity, emotion, speech rate, or volume; no speaker diarization; no speaker clustering or identity deduplication. The number of unique speakers in the dataset is also not reported.
【Handling of timbre: not annotated, instead treated as an audio condition】This is the key divergence between UniTalking and MOVA's route. MOVA uses natural language to describe timbre, accent, tone, and recording environment in detail, textualizing speaker attributes explicitly; UniTalking, by contrast, performs no textual description at all, instead injecting timbre as an independent audio condition (c_audio) directly — 3–5 seconds of reference audio is encoded by a frozen MMAudio VAE (latent length fixed at 257), and cross-attention is applied via a dedicated KV projection layer. This is "representing audio with audio" rather than "describing audio with text," sidestepping the inherent difficulty of precisely describing timbre in language.
【The data cost paid for this】Because the source data does not itself contain a "reference audio" pairing signal, the team had to manually construct one (see synthetic_data_synthesis) — the costliest and riskiest step in the entire pipeline.
【Measured level of timbre capability】TR2AV timbre similarity: English 0.703, Chinese 0.662. Compared with professional TTS, this is at a lower-middle level — below Qwen3-Omni (0.773/0.772) and MiniMax (0.756/0.780), and roughly on par with ElevenLabs (0.613/0.677) only. The paper candidly admits it is "difficult to surpass the performance of SOTA large audio models," claiming only to have achieved "a certain degree of voice cloning capability."
【Multiple speakers】Not supported. Future Work explicitly acknowledges the framework does not yet support multi-person reference generation (similar to Sora2's Cameo feature). No construction of multi-speaker dialogue samples is seen in the training data either.
【Evaluation metrics】WER (Seed-TTS test-en, 0.038) measures textual fidelity, Speaker Similarity measures voice cloning, and Sync-C/Sync-D measure lip synchronization. No cpCER-type metric for multiple speakers.

### Geometric and Structured Annotation (camera parameters, depth, 3D point tracks, action annotation, explicit state) ⚠️

The paper uses no geometric or structured annotation of any kind: no camera parameters, no depth maps, no 3D point tracks, no optical flow fields, no facial landmarks, no expression coefficients (3DMM/FLAME/blendshape), no head pose, no explicit physical-state annotation. Information such as camera movement, shot scale, and composition is also not parameterized or tagged, and may only be scattered as natural language within Qwen3-VL's video captions, though no caption example is available to confirm this. [Uncertain]
【Only two models in the pipeline involve spatial structure, and neither retains its output】
- LightASD: active speaker detection, which internally needs to localize and track the face region, but its output in the pipeline is used only as a boolean criterion (is the speaker on screen); face bounding boxes and trajectories are not retained as annotations;
- LipSync: lip-sync evaluation, which likewise needs to localize the mouth region, but only outputs a single alignment score used for filtering; the mouth-region information is not retained.
That is, spatial information about the face is used during the filtering stage and then discarded, never converted into a condition usable for training.
【A ready-made structured annotation that was abandoned】Its upstream data source, OpenHumanVid, natively comes with human skeleton sequences as a motion condition — a ready-made, high-value structured annotation. UniTalking makes no use of it whatsoever (the paper does not mention skeletons at all); its conditioning system consists only of three channels: text, identity image, and reference audio. For the talking-person generation task, skeletons could plausibly offer gains in controllability of upper-body and hand motion, and abandoning them is an unexplained trade-off. [Uncertain]
【Alternative structural modeling is architectural rather than data-side】UniTalking's handling of spatiotemporal structure is reflected in its anisotropic RoPE: the spatial dimensions (h, w) of audio tokens use RoPE from a single fixed position, degrading the spatial dimension to force the model to concentrate its modeling capacity on the temporal axis — this injects a structural prior via positional encoding rather than via annotation.

### Synthetic Data Construction (controlled perturbation/editing to construct training pairs, e.g., InstructAV2AV) ⚠️

UniTalking constructs a clearly-defined type of synthetic training data, and at no small scale — this is the most engineering-distinctive and also most worth scrutinizing part of its data pipeline:
【Motivation for construction】The model needs to support the TR2AV task (text + reference timbre → audio-video), which requires "reference audio + target audio-video" paired training data. But within the source dataset, each video only has its own audio track — there is no "another audio clip of the same speaker" available as a reference. The paper's original text: "our source dataset lacks corresponding reference audio." Synthesis is therefore required.
【Construction method】IndexTTS2 (a zero-shot, emotion-controllable, duration-controllable autoregressive TTS model) is used. For each video:
1) Input one: the video's original audio track, used as the target timbre reference;
2) Input two: a randomly generated text prompt — constructed as "randomly sampling sequences of words and numbers," i.e., a semantically meaningless random word string;
3) Output: a synthesized speech clip matching the original video's speaker's timbre but with content entirely unrelated;
4) Duration constraint: each synthesized reference audio clip is strictly controlled to between 3 and 5 seconds;
5) Data augmentation: this process is repeated 3 times per video, generating 3 different reference-audio clips. Across the 2.3 million videos, this yields roughly 6.9 million synthesized reference-audio clips in total.
【Analysis of design intent: why use random word strings rather than meaningful text】This is the most critical and cleverest point of this design. If a reference audio were synthesized from meaningful text, the model might learn to extract semantic content from the reference audio and leak it into the output, creating a "reference-audio content contaminates generated content" shortcut. Using semantically meaningless random word strings ensures the only stable, usable information in the reference audio is the timbre itself, thereby forcibly achieving "decoupling of timbre and content" at the data level. This is a typical case of "eliminating a shortcut through data construction," more direct than decoupling via a loss function or an architectural choice.
【Analysis of design intent: why synthesize via TTS rather than slice a segment from the original audio track】A more naive approach would be to cut another segment out of the same video's audio track to use as the reference. But in that case, the reference audio and target audio come from the same recording session, sharing the recording environment, background noise, and even some content, making it very easy for the model to "cheat" via acoustic-fingerprint matching rather than actual timbre understanding. Re-synthesizing via TTS cuts off these side channels, retaining only the timbre features that were modeled and reconstructed by the TTS model.
【Potential risk】The reference timbre comes from IndexTTS2's reconstruction rather than a genuine recording, so its timbre fidelity ceiling is bounded by IndexTTS2's cloning capability ceiling; at the same time, the acoustic statistical characteristics unique to synthesized speech (TTS artifacts) become an inherent part of the reference condition's distribution, leading to a train-inference mismatch — at inference time, users provide genuine recordings, which differ in domain from the TTS-synthesized audio seen during training. This may well be one of the reasons TR2AV timbre similarity is only 0.66–0.70, lagging behind professional TTS methods. The paper does not discuss this domain gap, nor does it perform an ablation of "synthetic reference vs. real reference." [Uncertain]
【No synthesis on the video side】No synthetic or edited-and-constructed video data is used; there are no controlled-perturbation training pairs (not of the InstructAV2AV type).
【Another broadly synthetic component】The nature of the "internal TTS data" used for Stage-1 audio pretraining is unclear — if it is TTS training corpus, it is genuine recordings; if it is TTS-generated corpus, it is synthetic data; the paper does not specify. [Uncertain]

### Degree of Human Involvement (manual annotation, manual quality inspection, model pre-screening + manual review) ⚠️

Human involvement on the training-data side is zero, while on the evaluation side it is the sole and central evaluation method — an extreme allocation of "fully automated data, fully manual evaluation":
【Training data side: entirely no human involvement】The three tiers of filtering (video/audio/audio-video) are all determined by automated models, all annotation is automatically generated by Qwen3-VL/Whisper-V3/Qwen3-Omni, and reference audio is automatically synthesized by IndexTTS2. The paper does not mention any manual annotation, manual spot-checking, or manual review, nor does it state whether filtering thresholds were calibrated with human verification (compare MOVA, which explicitly states that "human spot-checking of videos retained under different cutoffs was used to set thresholds"). Given that no threshold value is disclosed anywhere in the paper, the calibration process is even more of a complete black box.
【Evaluation side: human evaluation is the primary method】The core evaluation of the T2AV joint-generation task uses a blind pairwise preference study:
- Number of participants: 20 participants per model comparison;
- Test set: 50 talking-person generation test prompts;
- Three evaluation dimensions: video quality, audio quality, audio-video synchronization;
- Scoring method: OVI is used as the baseline (normalized to 100), with the win rate computed as rate = (win + tie) / (lose + tie);
- Results: relative to OVI, UniTalking scores 116% on audio quality and 107% on audio-video synchronization, while video quality is roughly tied (the paper explains this by noting that both share the Wan2.2 base model, so the video side is inherently homologous).
【Methodological limitations of the human evaluation】A scale of 20 people × 50 prompts is relatively small; no confidence interval or statistical significance test is reported; participant background and selection criteria are not stated; and it is not stated whether sample randomization and order-balancing were performed. Compared with MOVA's construction of a 732-item bilingual Chinese-English Arena evaluation set, both scale and rigor fall short. [Uncertain]
【Supporting role of objective metrics】Sync-C/Sync-D, Speaker Similarity, and WER — three objective metrics — are used as supplements, but the paper raises doubts about the credibility of Sync-C (suggesting the metric favors exaggerated lip movement, so OVI's high score of 6.56 does not necessarily represent genuinely better sync quality), which itself indicates the team regards manual subjective evaluation as the more reliable criterion.

## Audio-Video Alignment

### Audio-Video Synchronization Detection Method (lip-sync, event alignment)

Audio-video synchronization in UniTalking is decomposed into two independent sub-problems — "sound-source co-origin" and "lip-audio temporal alignment" — each gated in series by a dedicated model at the cross-modal filtering tier; this decomposition is the most valuable design in this work's synchronization-detection approach:
【First gate: sound-source co-origin detection (LightASD)】Used to "filter out videos containing purely diegetic audio." LightASD is a lightweight Active Speaker Detection model, whose task is precisely to determine "is the person currently on screen actually speaking / does the speech in the audio track come from the person on screen." This step removes samples where "sound is present but its source is not on screen" — voice-overs, narration, post-production dubbing, background dialogue, etc. Such samples' audio track and visual footage may bear no temporal relationship whatsoever, and are the primary contamination source for talking-person datasets. UniVerse-1 has no corresponding facility at all (it explicitly does not remove off-screen sound sources) — this is a point where UniTalking is relatively ahead.
【Second gate: lip-audio temporal alignment (LipSync)】After confirming sound-source co-origin, frame-level temporal alignment between lip movement and speech is then evaluated, and poorly-aligned samples are removed. The samples removed here are those where "the sound source is indeed on screen, but there is audio-visual offset" — common causes include timestamp drift from transcoding/re-muxing, post-editing misalignment, and dubbing replacement.
【Logical relationship between the two gates】The former is a semantic/source-level judgment (is the sound actually produced by this person), and the latter is a temporal-level judgment (is the timing of what was produced correct). The serial ordering is logically sound: exclude fundamentally non-co-originating samples first, then verify temporal precision for the co-originating ones. This effectively constitutes a separated treatment of the temporal and semantic dimensions (see temporal_vs_semantic_sync).
【Event-level alignment: not applicable】UniTalking's data domain is pure speech / talking-person content, and does not involve event alignment for foley sound effects, so there is no AV-align and no Synchformer-style general event-alignment detection. In Related Work, the paper explicitly criticizes existing unified generation work for "mainly focusing on the synchronization of foley sounds with video, which does not reach the precision required for speech" — i.e., it deliberately chose a different alignment target.
【Architectural safeguards for alignment】Beyond data filtering, alignment is primarily carried by three architectural designs: (1) Joint Attention performs a single attention operation over the concatenated audio-video tokens, explicitly modeling viseme-phoneme correspondence; (2) anisotropic RoPE degrades the audio spatial dimension while reinforcing the temporal dimension; (3) the TV2A task during training — by applying an attention mask in the "audio token to video token" direction, influence of audio on the video branch is blocked, forcing the audio branch to predict audio purely from the real video's latent features, thereby learning a precise mapping from frame-by-frame visual motion to acoustic outcome. This is a design that uses an attention mask to construct a unidirectional supervision signal, the most direct alignment supervision on the training side.
【Empirical validation of the effect】Figure 5's attention-map visualization shows: in audio-to-video attention, the face and body regions receive higher weight; in video-to-audio attention, audio tokens attend "exclusively" to the lip region — this provides intuitive evidence that Joint Attention has indeed learned lip-audio correspondence. The paper also candidly acknowledges the presence of mismatched highlighted regions in the attention maps that vary randomly across training iterations, speculating this stems from "flaws in the training strategy or data noise" — the only place in the entire paper that acknowledges an effect from data noise.

### Specific Synchronization Detection Metrics and Thresholds (SyncNet/Synchformer/LSE/in-house, threshold values — e.g., MOVA's LSE-D≤9.5 and LSE-C≥4.5, SkyReels-V4's SyncNet |offset|≤3 ∧ conf>1.5) ⚠️

【Verification conclusion regarding "SyncNet confidence > 0.9" (important)】This investigation performed a full-text keyword search of both the HTML and PDF (10 pages) versions of arXiv:2603.01418v1, with the conclusion that: the paper contains no numeric threshold for synchronization detection of any kind, the term "SyncNet" does not appear, and the threshold number "0.9" does not appear either (the only occurrence of "0.9" in the full text is the AdamW optimizer's β₁=0.9). The complete original wording for lip-sync filtering is only "samples exhibiting poor lip-synced alignment (using LipSync)," which neither specifies the exact model implementation of LipSync nor gives a confidence or distance threshold. Section 4.4 of the body text does mention "Further details are provided in the Appendix," but the arXiv v1 PDF does not actually include any appendix content. Therefore the claim of "SyncNet conf > 0.9" cannot be confirmed in the primary literature, and may originate from a secondhand paraphrase, a different version, or cross-talk with information unrelated to this paper. [Uncertain]
【Synchronization-related information actually given in the paper】
- Filtering tools: LightASD (sound-source co-origin) + LipSync (lip-audio temporal alignment), both without thresholds;
- Evaluation metrics: Sync-C (higher is better) and Sync-D (lower is better), i.e., SyncNet-family confidence and distance metrics.
【Evaluation results (Table 1, T2AV task lip synchronization)】
- UniVerse-1: Sync-C 1.85 / Sync-D 11.97
- OVI: Sync-C 6.56 / Sync-D 8.6
- Sora2: Sync-C 5.35 / Sync-D 7.78
- UniTalking: Sync-C 4.87 / Sync-D 8.05
That is, UniTalking leads UniVerse-1 by 3.02 points on Sync-C and 3.92 points on Sync-D; relative to OVI, it leads by 0.55 on Sync-D but trails on Sync-C.
【The paper's own doubt about the Sync-C metric】The authors explicitly point out that OVI's unusually high Sync-C (6.56, even higher than Sora2's 5.35) may reflect metric bias rather than genuine synchronization quality — speculating that Sync-C favors "exaggerated lip movement" over "natural articulation." This is a valuable metric critique: it implies that using SyncNet confidence directly as a filtering threshold would introduce the same bias, potentially systematically eliminating samples with natural, subtle lip movement while retaining samples with exaggerated lip movement. UniTalking gives no threshold, so it cannot be determined whether it is affected by this.
【Comparison with other works】UniVerse-1 uses SyncNet conf > 2.0 (relatively lenient); MOVA uses the dual constraint LSE-D ≤ 9.5 and LSE-C ≥ 4.5 (relatively strict). UniTalking discloses no threshold at all, making its transparency on this dimension the lowest of the three.

### Separate Treatment of Temporal vs. Semantic Synchronization (temporal alignment and content-semantic matching as two independent filtering conditions) ⚠️

UniTalking is one of the few works in this investigation that genuinely separates "semantic/source alignment" from "temporal alignment" into two independent conditions at the data-filtering level, even though the paper itself does not use this terminology:
【Semantic/source dimension: LightASD】Determines "whether the speech in the audio track is produced by the person on screen." This is fundamentally a content-level co-origin judgment, answering the question "are the sound and the picture even the same event." It removes voice-overs, narration, post-production dubbing, background human voices, and the like — samples whose audio and visuals may be entirely unrelated temporally, constituting semantic-level mismatch.
【Temporal dimension: LipSync】After co-origin is confirmed, determines whether lip movement and speech are aligned at the frame level. This answers the question "is the timing of the match correct." It removes audio-visual offset — timestamp drift, editing misalignment, dubbing replacement.
【The serial ordering of the two carries methodological significance】Performing semantic co-origin determination first, then temporal precision determination, is logically necessary — computing a lip-sync score for a sample whose sound source is not even on screen is meaningless (a low score there would not reflect a temporal-offset problem). If ASD were skipped and a SyncNet threshold applied directly, voice-over samples would also be eliminated for a low score, but the reason for elimination would be conflated with that of genuine temporal-misalignment samples, and the threshold would need to compromise between two different problems. Separating the two makes the criterion at each tier purer.
【Comparison with other works】
- UniVerse-1: only has the temporal dimension (SyncNet conf > 2.0), with the semantic dimension entirely blank, and explicitly does not remove off-screen sound sources — its data source contains large amounts of vlog and tutorial content (post-production music and narration are extremely common), a substantive data risk;
- MOVA: splits Stage-2 alignment evaluation into two orthogonal dimensions — temporal (SynchFormer) and semantic (ImageBind) — each with its own threshold, an explicit two-dimensional design;
- UniTalking: the two-dimensional separation genuinely exists (ASD + LipSync), but because its domain is extremely narrow (pure talking-person speech), its "semantic alignment" is instantiated as the specific form of "sound-source co-origin," rather than MOVA's general-purpose audio-visual semantic matching. For the talking-person domain, this specific instantiation is appropriate and more targeted.
【Missing】No ImageBind-style general cross-modal embedding similarity is used for semantic matching; no consistency check between caption and audio-video content is performed; neither tier discloses a threshold. [Uncertain]

### Audio Quality Filtering (SNR, silence detection and silence-proportion threshold, no-audio-track removal, off-screen-source removal, background-music separation) ⚠️

Audio quality filtering is concentrated in the second tier of the funnel, comprising three rules, with the tools being PANNs + SentenceASD, none of which have a disclosed threshold:
【Silence removal】Removes muted samples, i.e., videos without a valid audio signal. The determination method (energy threshold? all-zero detection?) and a silence-proportion threshold are not stated — only a binary "is it silent" removal is performed, with no sign of a proportional criterion such as "removed if the silent proportion is too high." [Uncertain]
【No-speech removal】Removes samples that lack speech. This is the core rule of this tier, performed by PANNs + SentenceASD for speech event detection. PANNs is a CNN pretrained on AudioSet for 527-class audio-event classification, capable of giving the presence probability of the "Speech" class; SentenceASD works alongside it. Compared with UniVerse-1's use of Whisper ASR as a binary gate for the presence of speech, using a dedicated audio-event classifier is lighter-weight and more robust (not dependent on whether the ASR can successfully transcribe). Note that the elimination here is thorough — UniVerse-1 downgrades no-speech samples to be retained as "general audio-video data," whereas UniTalking discards them outright, which is precisely what determines the two works' starkly different domain positioning.
【Low-SNR removal】Removes low-SNR samples. No SNR threshold is given, and the method for estimating SNR (a dedicated reference-free SNR estimation model, or signal statistics) is not stated. [Uncertain]
【Audio filtering not performed】
- No audio aesthetic scoring (compare MOVA's use of Audiobox-aesthetics' PQ/CU/CE three scores);
- No lower bound on bandwidth, i.e., audio tracks with heavy compression and high-frequency loss are not removed;
- No clipping, distortion, or pop/click recording-defect detection;
- No background-music source separation — background music is not stripped out and enters training together with the human voice (as noted above, this is instead turned into an attribute controllable via prompt);
- No reverberation/room-acoustics filtering;
- No lower bound on sample rate.
【Sample rate and audio representation】The raw waveform is converted to a Mel spectrogram via STFT and then encoded by MMAudio's 1D VAE; at inference time the latent is decoded back into a Mel spectrogram, then synthesized into a 44.1 kHz waveform by the BigVGAN vocoder. The 44.1 kHz output sample rate is higher than UniVerse-1's, which was lowered to 25.6 kHz to accommodate its 25 fps video — because UniTalking uses architectural RoPE alignment rather than sample-rate alignment, it is able to retain the full audio bandwidth, an incidental advantage from its architectural choice.
【Overall assessment】The coverage of audio quality control (silence/no-speech/low SNR/off-screen source) is adequate for the talking-person domain, and the tool selection (PANNs + ASD) is well-suited to the task, but the complete absence of disclosed thresholds makes it unreproducible; there remains a clear gap in granularity compared with MOVA's audio aesthetic scoring system.

### Classification and Differentiated Handling Strategy for Speech/Sound-Effects/Music

UniTalking adopts an extreme "keep only speech, discard everything else" strategy for the three audio categories, with no categorical retention and no differentiated handling:
【Speech】The only category retained, and it passes through a quadruple-condition screen: not silent → contains speech (PANNs + SentenceASD) → SNR passes → sound source on screen (LightASD) → lip-audio synchronized (LipSync). The final 2.3 million samples are, in theory, entirely human speech content.
【Sound effects (foley) and ambient sound】Not retained as an independent category. Samples containing only ambient sound with no speech are eliminated at the second tier by the "lack speech" rule. This is the complete opposite of UniVerse-1's approach of "downgrading no-speech samples to be retained as general data — 3,074 hours — plus additionally introducing 3,422 hours of VGGSound/AudioSet to reinforce sound effects." Paralinguistic sound effects (such as laughter) are retained as an accompanying component of speech samples (Figure 4 shows controllable generation of "a short laugh").
【Music】Not treated as an independent category — no music dataset is introduced, and no dedicated capability-building for music is done. Background music, if it accompanies speech, is passively retained without source separation, and is instead described faithfully by the caption, becoming an attribute controllable via prompt ("without any background music" is shown to be effective).
【Classification mechanism】There is only a single binary gate (speech present or not), with no multi-class audio classifier (compare MOVA's use of the EAT self-supervised audio Transformer for speech/non-speech classification). No further subdivision is performed within the non-speech category.
【Differentiated handling】There is no differentiated loss or sampling weight by audio category (compare UniVerse-1's application of LQLS, a source-differentiated loss, to VGGSound/AudioSet). All data is treated identically.
【Direct consequence for capability boundaries】The model's audio capability is entirely concentrated on speech synthesis and voice cloning, with no sound-effect or music generation capability. The paper's conclusion claims the "framework can generalize to general audio-video synthesis, including sound effects and music," but this claim has neither data support (the data contains no such content) nor experimental verification, and is purely aspirational.
【Relation to Stage-1 data】The audio branch's foundational articulation ability comes from Stage-1 pretraining on "internal TTS data," which is likewise pure speech data — i.e., the model sees only speech throughout, from pretraining through joint training; the singularity of its audio capability is a consistent design carried through both stages.

## Training Coordination

### Multi-Stage Training Curriculum and Data-Curriculum Scheduling (basis for stage division: resolution/duration/quality score/modality; low-res→high-res, image→video, short→long) ⚠️

A two-stage progressive training curriculum is adopted, but the basis for dividing the stages is "modality maturity" rather than the more common resolution/duration/quality dimensions — this is a direct consequence of its asymmetric initialization of "pretrained video tower, randomly initialized audio tower":
【Motivation for the curriculum】The paper explicitly identifies the problem: the video branch inherits the large-scale pretrained weights of Wan2.2-5B, while the audio branch is randomly initialized — the two are severely imbalanced in initialization. If trained jointly from the start, the mismatch in modality convergence rates would cause training instability, and audio quality would degrade significantly.
【Stage 1: Audio Branch Pre-training】
- Task: pure TTS (text-to-speech), and a compound task — simultaneously optimizing both "with timbre reference" and "without timbre reference" synthesis modes;
- Data: internal TTS data (scale and language undisclosed);
- Parameter-efficient strategy: all parameters related to the text branch and video branch (including their FFN and attention projection layers) are frozen; only the audio input projection layer, the audio-branch FFN, and the audio-branch attention projection layers are trained. The paper states that "fine-tuning only these parameters is sufficient to generate good speech";
- Hyperparameters: batch size 256, learning rate 1e-5, 100,000 steps;
- Objective function: L_total = L_CFM^a (audio-side Flow Matching loss only);
- Purpose: to force the audio branch to learn a meaningful mapping from semantics to acoustics, aligning its representational capacity with the text branch.
【Stage 2: Multi-Task Joint Audio-Video Training】
- End-to-end training of the full framework;
- Data: 2.3 million audio-video samples;
- Hyperparameters: batch size 64 (substantially lower than Stage 1's 256, reflecting the memory overhead of joint audio-video samples), learning rate 1e-5, 100,000 steps;
- Objective function: L_total = L_CFM^a + L_CFM^v (audio and video losses summed with equal weighting, no weighting coefficient seen);
- Task scheduling: alternates among the four tasks T2AV, TV2A, TI2AV, and TR2AV. The paper does not disclose the specific ratio, frequency, or scheduling strategy of the alternation (whether rotated by step, randomly sampled by batch, or scheduled in phases). [Uncertain]
【No curriculum along other dimensions】No resolution curriculum (low-res→high-res), no duration curriculum (short→long), no quality curriculum (coarse-filter→fine-filter), no image→video modality curriculum. A single resolution and duration configuration is used throughout (specific values undisclosed).
【Empirical evidence of curriculum effectiveness】The paper reports one relevant ablation: omitting Stage-1 training causes a significant degradation in the audio quality generated by the final audio-video model. The authors attribute this to an imbalance in modality convergence rates: the video branch benefits from large-scale pretraining, while the audio branch is relatively undertrained. This is the only ablation in the entire paper with a clear conclusion, but no quantitative figure is given. [Uncertain]
【Comparison with other works】UniVerse-1 has no curriculum at all (a flat single-stage training), because both of its experts are already pretrained; MOVA uses three stages to achieve a "decreasing scale, increasing quality" pyramid curriculum; UniTalking's two-stage curriculum is fundamentally about "compensating for an audio tower trained from scratch," a different nature from MOVA's quality pyramid.

### Changes in Data Mixture Across Training Stages (pretraining/annealing/SFT high-quality subset) ⚠️

Very little information on data mixture is disclosed; the only thing confirmed is the structure of "two completely different datasets used across the two stages":
【Stage-1 data】Internal TTS data, pure audio (text-speech pairs), containing no video. Scale, language, source, and whether it is synthetic speech are all undisclosed. [Uncertain]
【Stage-2 data】2.3 million audio-video samples, all containing both video and audio simultaneously. Whether Stage-1's TTS data continues to be mixed in during Stage 2 (to prevent forgetting of audio capability) is not stated — given that Stage 2's objective function includes a video-side loss, pure-audio samples cannot directly participate, so it is presumed not to be mixed in, but the paper offers no statement on this. [Uncertain]
【Task mixture within Stage 2】The four tasks (T2AV, TV2A, TI2AV, TR2AV) alternate, but the sampling ratio, alternation granularity, and scheduling curve for each task are entirely undisclosed. This is a substantive gap — the four tasks shape the model in very different directions (T2AV establishes coarse-grained alignment, TV2A provides strong temporal supervision, TI2AV/TR2AV confer personalization capability), and a different mixture ratio would directly change the model's capability emphasis. [Uncertain]
【Data-source mixture】The proportion of OpenHumanVid versus Huawei internal data within the 2.3 million samples is undisclosed; whether training applies up/down-sampling weighting to either source is not stated. [Uncertain]
【Caption-format mixture】The three caption formats (detailed+audio, short+audio, fused) are "randomly sampled" during training, but it is not stated whether the sampling is uniform (each 1/3) or weighted. This is the only explicit sample-level random-mixture mechanism in the entire paper. [Uncertain]
【Reference-audio mixture】Each video has 3 synthesized reference-audio clips; how one is selected during TR2AV task training (randomly chosen? rotated?) is not stated. [Uncertain]
【No annealing and no SFT】No annealing operation of switching to a high-quality subset toward the end of training is described, and there is no separate SFT stage — completion of Stage-2 training constitutes the final model.
【No source-differentiated loss】There is no UniVerse-1-style mechanism such as LQLS (a source-differentiated noise interval restricting loss application); all data is treated identically in loss computation.

### Post-Training Data (scale and selection criteria of the SFT curated set, number of preference pairs and annotation method, reward-model training data)

No post-training data. UniTalking performs no post-training whatsoever after its two training stages (100,000 steps each):
- No SFT curated set: no step of filtering a high-quality subset from the 2.3 million samples for a second round of fine-tuning is described;
- No RLHF / DPO: no preference pairs are constructed, no reward model is trained, and no human-preference alignment is performed;
- No distillation or inference-acceleration post-training: no mention of step distillation, CFG distillation, etc.;
- No reward-model training data.
【Human preference data is used only for evaluation, not for training】The blind pairwise preference study of 20 participants × 50 prompts produced preference-judgment data, but this data is used only to report win rates and is not fed back into training a preference model or used for alignment fine-tuning. This is also small in scale compared with MOVA's construction of a 732-item bilingual Chinese-English Arena evaluation set.
【All evaluation data is external or a small self-built set】TR2AV timbre similarity uses the MiniMax Multilingual Test Set; the WER for TTS uses Seed-TTS test-en; T2AV uses a self-built set of 50 talking-person prompts (not made public). All are for evaluation purposes and do not constitute post-training data.
【Relation to compute constraints】Future Work explicitly acknowledges that "model performance is constrained by available training resources and data scale," consistent with the overall resource positioning implied by the absence of any post-training step.

### Data-Processing Infrastructure and Throughput (NeMo Curator/Data-Juicer/in-house, GPU acceleration ratio, processing scale, cost) ⚠️

The paper's disclosure of data-processing infrastructure is close to nil:
【Cleaning infrastructure】No mention of using NeMo Curator, Data-Juicer, Ray, or any distributed data-processing framework; no processing scale, throughput, elapsed time, GPU acceleration ratio, or cost data is given for the cleaning steps; it is not stated whether the three-tier filtering is done as offline batch processing or streaming processing. Given the scale of raw corpus that must be processed (if 2.3 million samples are filtered down from OpenHumanVid's tens-of-millions of clips, at least tens-of-millions of clips' worth of four-model inference would be required), the engineering workload of this step is likely substantial yet entirely unrecorded. [Uncertain]
【Annotation infrastructure】The four annotation models (Qwen3-VL, Whisper-V3, Qwen3-Omni-Captioner, Qwen3-Omni) each need to run inference over all 2.3 million samples, with Qwen3-Omni needing to process both video and audio streams simultaneously, at especially high cost; in addition, IndexTTS2 needs to generate 6.9 million reference-audio clips. All of this is offline, one-time processing (in contrast with UniVerse-1's online annotation architecture — UniTalking uses traditional offline pre-annotation instead). The inference cluster scale, total elapsed time, and whether a latent precomputation cache was used are all undisclosed. [Uncertain]
【Training infrastructure】Only the following are disclosed: bf16 precision, FSDP (Fully Sharded Data Parallel) distributed parallelism, AdamW (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999, ε=1e-8), and the UniPC scheduler. Undisclosed: GPU model and count, total training duration, GPU-days, whether Huawei Ascend NPUs were used (given the Huawei background, this point is especially worth noting yet is not mentioned at all), and whether sequence parallelism or context parallelism was used. [Uncertain]
【An indirect efficiency clue】Stage 1 adopts a parameter-efficient strategy (freezing all parameters of the text and video branches, training only audio-branch-related layers), a choice made for stability rather than pure efficiency, but it objectively reduces Stage 1's memory and compute overhead.
【Latent precomputation】The video VAE, audio VAE, and UMT5 encoder are all frozen during training (explicitly marked as frozen in the paper), so in principle their latents could be precomputed offline and cached to save encoding overhead during training, but the paper does not state whether this was done. [Uncertain]

## Effect Comparison

### Quantitative Impact of Data Strategy Ablations (distinguishing: filtering-strictness ablation / caption-density-style ablation / data-mixture ablation, and corresponding evaluation metrics) ⚠️

The paper's ablation experiments are extremely thin — the entire paper contains only one ablation, and it concerns training strategy rather than data composition; there is no ablation in the strict sense of data strategy:
【The sole ablation: necessity of Stage-1 audio pretraining】Omitting Stage-1 audio-branch pretraining causes "significant degradation" in the audio quality generated by the final audio-video model. The authors' explanation is an imbalance in modality convergence rate: the video branch benefits from large-scale pretraining, while the audio branch is relatively undertrained.
- This ablation is a training-curriculum ablation, not a data ablation;
- And it gives no quantitative figure at all (no WER, audio quality score, or subjective rating change after removing Stage 1 is reported), only the qualitative statement "significant degradation," making it impossible to assess the effect size. This is the most obvious weak point of the paper's experimental section. [Uncertain]
【A complete list of missing data ablations】Each of the following is a core data-design choice of this work, yet none is validated:
1) The contribution of each of the three filtering tiers — how much degradation would result from removing video filtering / audio filtering / cross-modal filtering respectively? In particular, what is the value of LightASD's off-screen-source removal (a differentiating design relative to comparable work)?
2) The contribution of random sampling among the three caption formats — how would results differ using only the fused format, only the concatenated format, or without random switching? This is the most distinctive design on the annotation side, yet it is unvalidated;
3) The contribution of random-word-string reference audio — how would voice leakage and similarity change if meaningful text were used to synthesize reference audio, or if a slice from the original audio track were used directly as the reference? This is the most critical decision on the data-synthesis side, yet it is unvalidated;
4) The choice of synthesizing 3 reference-audio clips per video — the marginal benefit of 1 clip vs. 3 vs. more;
5) Scaling effects of data scale — the effect curve of training on subsets of the 2.3 million samples;
6) The respective contributions of OpenHumanVid and internal data, and sensitivity to their mixture ratio;
7) An ablation of the mixture ratio of the four training tasks (T2AV/TV2A/TI2AV/TR2AV), in particular the unidirectional-supervision design of TV2A's attention mask (which the paper claims "directly teaches the model a fine-grained mapping from viseme to phoneme") — this core claim likewise has only qualitative discussion, no ablation support.
【A substitute for quantitative evidence】The paper uses Figure 5's attention-map visualization as a substitute for some quantitative verification: in video-to-audio attention, audio tokens attend "exclusively" to the lip region, used as evidence that Joint Attention has learned lip-audio correspondence. This is a persuasive visualization, but it cannot substitute for an ablation.
【Conclusion】The overall absence of data ablations means this work can only provide information on "what was done" along the data-processing dimension, not on "which practices are effective and how effective." For the core concern of this investigation — the quantitative impact of data strategy — this represents a substantive information gap. [Uncertain]

### Evidence for Quality vs. Quantity (cases of small-but-refined data surpassing large-but-messy data) ⚠️

The paper provides no direct evidence for a quality-quantity trade-off and performs no related controlled experiment: no data-scale scaling curve, no "small-but-refined vs. large-but-messy" comparison, and no analysis of the relationship between filtering strictness and effectiveness.
【An observable orientation: a clear quality-first preference】The entire pipeline consists of six hard boolean elimination rules chained in series (static/text overlay/low visual quality/silent/no speech/low SNR/off-screen source/lip desync), none of which are soft-weighted or tiered retention — i.e., all substandard samples are discarded outright, with no downgraded utilization. This stands in sharp contrast to UniVerse-1, which downgrades no-speech samples to retain them as "general data" and isolates low-quality VGGSound/AudioSet with an LQLS loss for continued use — a typical "don't waste anything" orientation; UniTalking, by contrast, is purely "better to have too little than too much impurity."
【But the team itself does not consider the data refined enough already】Future Work explicitly states: "the performance of our model is constrained by available training resources and data scale, especially compared with closed-source models" — i.e., the team lists data scale as a performance bottleneck, implying a need for more rather than more-refined data. This self-assessment is in tension with the "quality-first" pipeline design, and can be read as: under its own self-built filtering standard, the amount of qualifying data that could be found was only 2.3 million samples — scale has become a constraint rather than an actively chosen "small but refined" strategy.
【An indirect piece of "quality-first" evidence】The sole ablation (omitting Stage 1 causes significant audio-quality degradation) speaks to "the quality of the training curriculum" rather than "the quality of the data," and does not constitute evidence for this field.
【A piece of countervailing evidence】The paper acknowledges the presence of "mismatched highlighted regions that vary randomly across training iterations" in its attention maps, speculating one cause is "data noise" — the only place in the entire paper acknowledging residual data noise, indicating that even after six layers of filtering, the 2.3 million samples still contain noise with an observable effect. The paper does not further analyze the type of noise or propose a countermeasure, however.
【Conclusion】UniTalking's data strategy is "high threshold, pure elimination, single domain," an orientation that leans quality-first, but there is no evidence supporting that this orientation outperforms alternatives, and the team itself considers scale to still be the primary bottleneck. [Uncertain]

### Alignment Between Training-Data Domain Distribution and Evaluation-Benchmark Taxonomy (e.g., VABench's seven major categories) ⚠️

UniTalking does not release an accompanying evaluation benchmark; evaluation primarily reuses external test sets plus one small, unreleased self-built set, so there is no self-consistent design aligning "training-data distribution ↔ evaluation taxonomy":
【Three evaluation setups and their relationship to the training data】
1) T2AV joint generation — a self-built set of 50 talking-person generation test prompts (not made public), used for a 20-person blind pairwise preference study (three dimensions: video quality/audio quality/audio-video synchronization) as well as Sync-C/Sync-D objective lip-sync evaluation. This is the only evaluation fully aligned in domain with the training data (both are talking-person scenarios), but a scale of only 50 items cannot constitute a taxonomy, nor is any stratified design done along any dimension (person attributes/scene/language/duration);
2) TR2AV voice cloning — adopts Qwen3-Omni's experimental setup, using the MiniMax Multilingual Test Set to evaluate timbre similarity, reported separately for English/Chinese. This test set is a purely audio benchmark designed for TTS voice cloning, and its language and speaker distribution bear no correspondence to UniTalking's training-data distribution — it is a purely external borrowing;
3) TTS generation — follows OVI's evaluation protocol, evaluating WER on Seed-TTS test-en (purely English). Again a purely audio external benchmark, covering only English, not aligned with the training data (whose language distribution is unknown and may contain substantial Chinese).
【Absence of alignment and its impact】
- The training data has never been classified or statistically analyzed under any taxonomy (no dimension of person attributes, scene, language, single/multi-person, etc.), so even if the evaluation side had a category breakdown, there would be nothing to align it against;
- Both external benchmarks (MiniMax Multilingual, Seed-TTS test-en) are purely audio TTS benchmarks that can only assess the model's speech quality and timbre capability, not the core capability of joint audio-video generation — meaning UniTalking's core capability (joint generation + lip sync) is in fact covered only by the subjective evaluation of the 50 self-built prompts and the two objective metrics Sync-C/Sync-D, a noticeably insufficient evaluation coverage;
- No public benchmark for joint audio-video generation is adopted (e.g., UniVerse-1's released Verse-Bench is not used, even though UniVerse-1 is one of its main comparison baselines) — meaning comparison with UniVerse-1 can only be conducted on the self-built 50-prompt set, limiting comparability.
【One methodological contribution to evaluation】The paper raises a valuable doubt about the Sync-C metric itself: OVI achieves an unusually high Sync-C (6.56, higher than Sora2's 5.35), and the authors speculate this metric favors "exaggerated lip movement" over "natural articulation." This observation carries a cautionary implication for evaluation across the entire talking-person generation field (and for the practice of using SyncNet scores as data-filtering thresholds) — if a filtering threshold is based on the same metric, it would systematically favor training samples with exaggerated lip movement.
【Conclusion】Between the training-data domain and the evaluation system, there is "domain consistency without taxonomy alignment": both focus on talking-person scenarios, but the training side has no taxonomy statistics and the evaluation side has no taxonomy design, so there is no analyzable correspondence between the two. [Uncertain]
