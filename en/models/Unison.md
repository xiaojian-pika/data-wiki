### Type (model/dataset/toolchain/evaluation benchmark)

Model (an audio-video joint generation framework). This is the point in this entry that most needs clarification: Unison is not a dataset release, nor is it an evaluation benchmark.
【Is a model】The main deliverable is a dual-branch audio-video joint generation framework (a Wan2.2-5B video branch + an MMAudio/Zipformer audio branch), with three core methodological contributions (Semantic-Guided Harmonization Strategy, SGHS; Bidirectional Audio Cross-Attention, Bi-ACA; and Cross-Modal Forcing Strategy, CMFS).
【Is not a dataset】The corpus of roughly 2 million synchronized audio-video clips / 3,000+ hours is a product of "aggregating existing open-source datasets + automated pipeline refinement." It is neither named, nor released with statistics, nor open-sourced — it is only described in two sentences in the experimental setup section. The task description's phrase "an automated pipeline aggregating multiple large-scale datasets" is accurate, but note that this pipeline receives almost no elaboration in the paper.
【Self-built evaluation set】The authors also built an internal test set of 1,000 held-out samples (with annotations generated by Gemini), but it is neither named nor open-sourced and does not constitute a public evaluation benchmark.
【Task forms】The primary task is TI2AV (text+image → audio+video), while also supporting T2AV (text-only → audio-video), A2V (audio → video), and V2A (video → audio) — four task types in total.

### Degree of Open-Sourcing (whether weights/code/data/pipeline are each open-sourced)

As of July 2026, the project is entirely closed-source; only the paper itself is open (CC BY 4.0).
【Weights】Not open-sourced. Paper's exact wording: "The code and models will be made publicly available upon acceptance" — a conditional promise contingent on conference acceptance, not yet fulfilled.
【Code】Not open-sourced. No GitHub repository, project homepage, or demo page has been observed.
【Data】Not open, and in fact there is no "releasable dataset" to speak of — the training corpus is a re-curated and reprocessed version of existing open-source datasets such as OpenHumanVid, HDTF, VFHQ, CelebV-Text, and VGGSound; the filtered sample list (roughly 2 million entries) has not been released. The audio side additionally includes undisclosed "internal speech data."
【Pipeline】Disclosure is extremely limited, and this is the biggest information gap in this entry. The paper devotes only a single sentence to the "automated processing pipeline"; the only stage described in any detail is the lip-filtering operator (face detection + in-box SyncNet verification), and no threshold values, stage-by-stage retention rates, or pseudocode are given anywhere. Compared with MOVA (stage-by-stage thresholds + a retention-rate table) and UniVerse-1 (a full six-stage funnel with all thresholds disclosed), Unison is clearly a tier behind in the reproducibility of its data processing — a typical characteristic of a "methodological-innovation paper" rather than a "data-engineering paper."
【Evaluation set】Neither the 1,000 test samples nor the Gemini-generated ground-truth annotations have been released, nor has the raw data from the user study (40 samples × 25 participants).
【Reused open-source assets】All backbones and tools used are open-source: Wan2.2-5B, MMAudio, Zipformer, Mel-RoFormer, SyncNet, Whisper-large-v3, Synchformer, ImageBind, CLAP, LAION-Aesthetic V2.5, DINOv3, Audiobox-Aesthetics.

### Whether Simultaneous Audio-Video Generation Is Supported, and the Implementation Approach (native joint / cascaded / MoE fusion)

Supports simultaneous audio-video generation, implemented via "dual-branch native joint generation" — two expert models coupled through frame-level bidirectional cross-attention. This is a continuation and deepening of UniVerse-1's "Stitching of Experts" paradigm, rather than a single-tower unified model trained from scratch, and it is not cascaded.
【Branch composition】
- Video branch: based on Wan2.2-5B, 29 layers (L_v = 29). The video backbone is fully frozen during the joint-training stage.
- Audio branch: based on MMAudio, 23 layers (L_a = 23), with Zipformer (from the ZipVoice family) introduced to add speech-generation capability — MMAudio was originally a foley/sound-effect generation model with no speech-synthesis capability, and the introduction of Zipformer is precisely meant to fill this gap.
【Coupling mechanism】Frame-level bidirectional cross-attention, in which the video latent and audio latent serve as queries for each other, enabling bidirectional information exchange. This contrasts with the unidirectional-conditioning schemes of UniAVGen, Harmony, and similar works; the paper lists "bidirectionality" as a core differentiator.
【Three-layer safeguard mechanism for lip sync】The paper explicitly decomposes word-level lip-sync constraints across three layers simultaneously, which is key to understanding Unison's data-side design:
1) Architecture layer: cross-attention aligns features within a three-frame window with stride=1, retaining only the middle frame's representation upon recovery — an extremely short-horizon local alignment design;
2) Data layer: the lip-filtering operator (first detecting the number and location of faces, then running SyncNet verification only within the face bounding boxes);
3) Training layer: the bidirectional Cross-Modal Forcing Strategy (CMFS).
【Dual-stream decoupling on the audio side — the most distinctive aspect of this work】The audio latent is not a single tensor but a [speech, sfx] dual-stream tensor. Source audio is separated by Mel-RoFormer into speech and sound-effect streams, each encoded as a temporally aligned independent sequence; both streams share the same RoPE time index to guarantee strict temporal alignment, and "modality-specific learnable biases" are used to distinguish the two within a shared self-attention layer. Each Transformer block performs an "interact-merge-split" cycle, re-separating into two independent generation trajectories at the output, ultimately supervised by two separate flow-matching losses. This means Unison physically isolates speech and sound effects at the generation stage, eliminating the "speech being drowned out by ambient sound" problem at the source — a fundamental difference from single-mixed-audio-track schemes such as Ovi/LTX-2/MOVA.
【Cross-modal forcing】The diffusion timesteps for video and audio are sampled independently (the audio branch is mapped to a restricted interval); the modality with lower noise (the "cleaner" one) guides the "dirtier" modality, with a directional indicator dynamically designating the "student modality" and upweighting its loss. A three-stage curriculum (synchronous warmup → incremental decoupling → full independence, with stage proportions of 0.3/0.4/0.3) ensures training stability.
【Inference configuration】A 50-step flow-matching sampler, CFG scale = 6.0, output at 25 FPS video.

## Data Scale and Distribution

### Training Data Volume (number of video clips/hours/token count, pretraining vs. SFT separated)

The paper's entire disclosure of data scale is concentrated in a single sentence in Section 4.1, covering both the audio-video and audio-only corpora, explicitly labeled as the final output "After refinement through our automated processing pipeline":
【Audio-video joint training data】Approximately 2 million synchronized audio-video clips, totaling over 3,000 hours.
【Audio-only training data】50 million high-quality audio segments, with a total duration exceeding 130,000 hours.
【Inferable average clip duration】
- Audio-video side: 3,000 h × 3600 s ÷ 2,000,000 ≈ 5.4 seconds/clip, a short-clip-dense corpus, on the same order of magnitude as UniVerse-1's roughly 5-second window and MOVA's fixed 8.05-second length;
- Audio-only side: 130,000 h × 3600 s ÷ 50,000,000 ≈ 9.4 seconds/segment.
(The above are estimates made by this entry based on the disclosed figures; the paper itself does not give an average duration.)
【Scale positioning】3,000+ hours of audio-video is a small scale among comparable works — roughly 0.4× UniVerse-1's 7,685 hours and 1/20 of MOVA Phase 1's 61.5k hours, and 2-3 orders of magnitude smaller than the million-hour-scale industrial corpora of works like Sora 2/Veo 3. However, the 130,000-hour scale on the audio side is quite substantial, approaching the corpus scale of dedicated TTS/audio-generation foundation models — this extreme "audio-heavy, video-light" ratio is a direct consequence of the training strategy: the video backbone is completely frozen during joint training, so Unison in effect only trains the audio branch and the fusion module, with all visual capability inherited from Wan2.2-5B, hence there is no need for a large-scale video corpus.
【Pretraining/SFT split】Not applicable. The paper's two-stage division is "Stage 1: single-modality audio-branch training" and "Stage 2: joint fine-tuning" — the former consumes 130,000 hours of audio-only data, the latter consumes 3,000+ hours of audio-video data, a split by modality rather than by pretraining/SFT. There is no separate SFT stage and no post-training data.
【Undisclosed】The total volume of raw collection/aggregation (the denominator), the hour and clip contributions of each source dataset, token counts, the number of training steps and epochs for Stage 2, and any duration histogram of the corpus.

### Data Source Composition (proprietary/public datasets/web scraping/licensed procurement/synthetic data) ⚠️

Entirely an aggregation of public open-source datasets, with only a small amount of proprietary data on the audio side; no web scraping, no licensed procurement, no synthetic data. This is the most notable difference between Unison and industrial works — it is an academic project that "stands on the shoulders of existing public datasets."
【Audio-video joint training corpus (approximately 2 million clips / 3,000+ hours), aggregated from five open-source datasets】
1. OpenHumanVid — a large-scale, high-quality human-centric video dataset, and the main data support for this entry's "human-centric" positioning, providing captioned videos of human activity;
2. HDTF (High-Definition Talking Face) — high-definition talking-head videos, mostly frontal faces from speeches/news broadcasts, contributing high-quality lip-sync samples;
3. VFHQ (Video Face High-Quality) — high-fidelity face video sourced from interview scenarios, contributing face clarity and identity diversity;
4. CelebV-Text — an in-the-wild face-video dataset with text descriptions, contributing text annotations of facial attributes and actions;
5. VGGSound — an audio-visual event dataset (approximately 310 sound-event categories), the only non-face data source among the five, contributing ambient sound/sound effects and their visual correspondences.
Notably, the first four data sources are all face/human-centric datasets, with VGGSound as the sole general sound-effect supplement — this composition determines Unison's capability bias: strong at speakers and human motion, while the visual diversity of ambient sound depends on a single data source.
【Audio-only training corpus (50 million segments / 130,000+ hours), collected by sound-type division of labor】
- Sound effects: the datasets YouTube-8M, AudioSet, and WavCaps;
- Music: VidMuse;
- Singing: drawn mainly from the YuE collection (the corpus of an open-source singing/music generation foundation model);
- Speech: includes "internal speech data," explicitly stated in the paper as used to "further enrich the diversity and coverage of the training corpus." This is the only non-public source among the entire corpus — its scale, languages, collection method, and licensing status are all undisclosed. [Uncertain]
【Licensed procurement】No paid licensed procurement is mentioned anywhere.
【Synthetic data】No synthetic video or synthetic audio was used as training data.
【Self-collected scraping】No self-built crawler or web scraping is mentioned — all web-sourced data was obtained via existing open-source datasets as intermediaries.
【Design intent】The audio side's four sound types (speech/sound effects/music/singing), each equipped with a dedicated data source, directly serves its speech-sound-effect dual-stream decoupled architecture: the model needs to acquire high-quality priors separately on the two streams, so the corpus is likewise partitioned by type.

### Data Compliance and Provenance (proportion of licensed data, rights-cleared datasets, C2PA, etc.) ⚠️

The paper does not discuss data compliance, licensing, or provenance at all: no proportion of licensed data is given, no rights-cleared datasets are declared, no C2PA or any output-side watermarking/provenance marking is mentioned, no discussion of copyright, portrait rights, or deepfake risk, and no statement of model-usage restrictions.
【Indirectly assessable compliance status — overall better than purely scraped works】
- The audio-video corpus is entirely drawn from published academic open-source datasets (OpenHumanVid, HDTF, VFHQ, CelebV-Text, VGGSound), each of which has explicit academic-use license terms, typically restricted to non-commercial research use. Unison's reuse of them means the compliance responsibility for its training data is, to a considerable degree, transferred to the licensing frameworks of the upstream datasets, posing lower risk than UniVerse-1 (which self-collected YouTube videos and movie clips);
- However, note that VGGSound, AudioSet, and YouTube-8M are all essentially collections of YouTube video links, whose content copyright belongs to the original uploaders — academic dataset terms are not equivalent to copyright clearance;
- HDTF, VFHQ, and CelebV-Text are all real-face datasets (including celebrities and public figures); Unison is trained on top of them to acquire "arbitrary person talking-video generation" capability, so portrait-rights and deepfake-abuse risks objectively exist, yet the paper discusses this not at all — given the participation of two industry parties, ByteDance and China Telecom, this omission is especially noteworthy;
- The source and licensing status of the "internal speech data" is entirely unclear, making it the least transparent link in the compliance chain.
【A point of tension with licensing】The paper states that code and models will be released upon acceptance, but its training corpus is mostly datasets restricted to academic use; the commercial usability of the model weights is therefore questionable, and the paper offers no clarification on this. 【Duration configuration during training】Not disclosed: the frame-count/second configuration per sample during Stage 2 joint training is unknown; only the output frame rate of 25 FPS is known. If estimated at roughly 5 seconds, this corresponds to about 125 frames. [Uncertain]

### Resolution/Aspect-Ratio Distribution and Bucketing Strategy ⚠️

The paper does not discuss resolution and aspect ratio at all: no resolution admission threshold, no aspect-ratio distribution, no description of bucketing strategy, no mention of padding or cropping, and no disclosed training resolution.
【The only certain piece of information】The output video frame rate is 25 FPS (inference configuration).
【Why this may genuinely be unnecessary】This gap has a structural explanation: during Stage 2 joint training, the video backbone Wan2.2-5B is completely frozen, and only the audio branch and fusion module (bidirectional cross-attention and layer normalization) are trained. This means Unison does not train visual-generation capability at all, so resolution-related data curation naturally becomes unnecessary — resolution handling and bucketing on the visual side are effectively inherited from Wan2.2's pretraining, outside the scope of this paper. This is fundamentally different from works such as MOVA (a three-stage 360p→720p resolution curriculum) or UniVerse-1 (a hard ≥1080p threshold), which genuinely need to train a visual branch.
【Resolution status indirectly inferable from upstream datasets】HDTF is 720p-1080p high-definition talking-head video; VFHQ explicitly uses "high-fidelity" as its selection criterion; OpenHumanVid also self-describes as "high-quality" — all three have relatively high visual quality; whereas VGGSound is sourced from ordinary YouTube video and has notably lower visual quality (UniVerse-1 specifically designed an LQLS loss isolation strategy for it). Unison discloses no special handling for VGGSound's lower visual quality — but because the video backbone is frozen, low-quality visual data will not contaminate visual-generation capability, and the risk is naturally sidestepped by the architectural choice. This is an inference made by this entry. [Uncertain]

### Category/Domain Distribution and Mixture Strategy (proportional control and concept balancing across people, actions, scenes, styles, etc.) ⚠️

The paper gives no domain-mixture figures and describes no concept-balancing mechanism. The following is a qualitative reconstruction based on the composition of the data sources:
【Positioning: human-centric】This is the positioning declared in the paper's very title. Four of the five audio-video data sources are face/human-centric datasets (OpenHumanVid, HDTF, VFHQ, CelebV-Text); only VGGSound is a general audio-visual event dataset, so the absolute majority of the corpus consists of footage containing people.
【Subject-matter coverage inferable from each source dataset】
- OpenHumanVid → human actions, everyday activities, human scenes from film/TV clips; the main source of motion diversity;
- HDTF → speeches, news-broadcast-style frontal high-definition talking heads, contributing standardized lip-sync samples;
- VFHQ → interview-scene faces, contributing identity and expression diversity;
- CelebV-Text → in-the-wild face video with attribute/action/emotion text annotations, contributing facial-attribute diversity;
- VGGSound → 310 categories of audio-visual events (instrument playing, animals, traffic, machinery, nature, etc.), the only non-human domain source, and also the visual anchor for the model's ambient-sound capability.
【The "domain" dimension the paper actually cares about is not subject matter but acoustic-scene type】From the qualitative discussion, it is clear that the domain division Unison truly cares about is split by "the relative relationship between speech and sound effects" into three scene types:
1) Narration-dominant scenes (e.g., dense voice-over) — SCG gating suppresses the sound-effect stream to protect speech purity;
2) Complex-soundscape scenes (e.g., musical performance) — SCG gating amplifies cross-stream influence to enrich non-speech acoustics;
3) Difficult scenes where both matter equally — the paper repeatedly cites examples such as "singing while playing an instrument," sports commentary (the commentator's voice should not drown out crowd cheers and impact sounds), a beach scene (the human voice should not overpower the waves), piano performance (note onsets must correspond to finger movements), and a moving locomotive (the engine noise should not be drowned out by voice).
This scene classification "by speech-to-sound-effect ratio rather than by visual subject matter" is a distinctive feature of Unison, and it directly corresponds to the instance-level analysis of its SCG gating (Fig. 8c gives average gate values across semantic categories, but the paper does not list the category taxonomy or their proportions).
【Entirely missing information】The hour/clip proportion of each data source, a subject-matter taxonomy and its proportions, distribution of person attributes (gender/age/ethnicity), distribution of motion types, distribution of scene types, and any form of concept balancing or reweighting mechanism. The paper provides neither a domain-mixture table nor a statement on whether certain subsets were up-/down-sampled during training. [Uncertain]

### Audio Category Distribution and Mixture (proportion and control strategy for speech/sound-effect foley/music/ambient sound/silence) — a dimension unique to AV models ⚠️

Audio category is the only distribution dimension in Unison's entire data design that is treated seriously — because the whole architecture is built around the binary division of "speech vs. sound effects." Even so, the paper still gives no proportion figures.
【Architecture-level forced binary classification: speech / sound effects】All audio (whether from audio-video data or audio-only data) is uniformly separated by Mel-RoFormer into speech and sound-effect (SFX) streams, each encoded as a ground-truth latent, then supervised separately by two independent flow-matching losses. The paper's exact wording: "we leverage Mel-Roformer to disentangle mixed audio into high-fidelity speech and sound-effect (SFX) components." This is not "classify, then process separately" but rather "every sample is split into two streams and processed simultaneously" — even if a given sample has no speech, its speech stream still exists (with empty/near-silent content). This is a completely different processing paradigm from UniVerse-1 (Whisper-based binary classification as a routing gate, with speech/non-speech taking different paths) and MOVA (an EAT classifier making a speech/non-speech determination): Unison does not route — it processes in parallel dual tracks.
【The four-way division of labor for the audio-only corpus (category attribution can be determined by data source, but no proportion figures)】
- Speech: internal speech data;
- Sound effects: YouTube-8M, AudioSet, WavCaps;
- Music: VidMuse;
- Singing: the YuE collection.
The paper explicitly lists singing as an independent category, alongside speech and music — this is relatively rare among comparable works, and directly corresponds to the failure mode called out in its qualitative results ("Universe-1 and UniAVGen struggle to distinguish singing from ordinary speech"). Unison specifically introduces the YuE singing corpus to address this problem.
【Silence handling】No mention of silence detection, silence-proportion thresholds, or removal of tracks with no audio. [Uncertain]
【Proportion-control strategy】Entirely undisclosed. The hours, clip counts, sampling weights, and whether reweighting is applied during training for each of the four audio categories are all unstated. How the total 50 million segments / 130,000 hours is allocated among the four categories (speech/sound effects/music/singing) is the most critical information gap in this field. [Uncertain]
【A runtime dynamic-balancing mechanism (not data-side, but functionally equivalent)】Worth noting is that Unison converts "controlling the ratio of speech to sound effects" from a data-mixture problem into a learnable, inference-time gating problem — SCG predicts two gating coefficients (sigmoid-constrained) based on the global semantic vectors of the caption and the transcription, suppressing sound-effect intrusion into the speech stream in narration-dominant scenes, and amplifying cross-stream influence in complex-soundscape scenes. The analysis in Fig. 8 shows this gating exhibits dynamics along three dimensions: layer (polarization intensifies in deeper layers), timestep (gate divergence intensifies as denoising progresses), and instance (adaptive across semantic categories). This is a technical route where "the mixture ratio is not controlled by data but adapted by the model" — an interesting contrast with traditional data-mixture control.

### Narrative Structure Distribution (single-shot vs. multi-shot, average clip duration, shot-count distribution, presence of native audio tracks) ⚠️

The paper does not treat narrative structure as an independent dimension for discussion, and there are no relevant statistics. The following is indirect inference:
【Single-shot vs. multi-shot】Almost certainly pure single-shot. Among the five audio-video data sources, HDTF, VFHQ, and CelebV-Text are all already-cropped single-face single-shot clips; VGGSound consists of fixed-length short clips; OpenHumanVid is also released in already-segmented clip form. The roughly 5.4-second average duration also essentially rules out the presence of multi-shot samples. 【Average clip duration】Estimated at roughly 5.4 seconds (3,000 hours ÷ 2 million clips); not given directly by the paper.

## Filtering Pipeline

### Overall Structure of the Filtering Funnel (number of stages, order of each stage) ⚠️

This is the most weakly disclosed field in this entry. The paper's entire description of the data-filtering process consists of only two passages, and both are extremely brief:
【Passage 1: a one-sentence summary】At the end of Section 4.1, "Training Corpora": "After refinement through our automated processing pipeline, the final dataset encompasses approximately 2 million synchronized audio-visual clips totaling over 3,000 hours, alongside 50 million high-quality audio segments exceeding 130,000 hours" — this only informs us that an "automated processing pipeline" exists and states its final output volume; the number of stages, their order, methods, and thresholds are all undisclosed.
【Passage 2: the only stage described in any detail — the lip-filtering operator】Located at the start of Section 3 (Method), described as the data-layer measure within the "three-layer safeguard for word-level lip-sync":
Step a) Face detection — "first detects the number and location of faces," determining both the number and location of faces. Note that detecting "number" implies the pipeline has the ability to recognize multi-face scenes (which could be used to exclude multi-speaker ambiguity or select a primary speaker), but the paper does not state how cases with multiple detected faces are handled;
Step b) In-box SyncNet verification — "SyncNet is then applied exclusively within these bounding boxes to verify alignment"; the key design choice is that SyncNet runs only within the face bounding boxes (exclusively within these bounding boxes), rather than on the full frame. This is a practically meaningful engineering detail: running SyncNet on the full frame tends to fail when faces occupy a small portion of the frame or when multiple people are present, whereas restricting it to the detected box significantly improves the reliability of the sync judgment;
Step c) Dual removal targets — this operator "naturally filters out" two types of samples: (i) clips where lip movement and speech are unsynchronized, and (ii) off-screen voice-over clips. The removal of the second type is particularly important — post-production voice-overs are extremely common in vlogs, documentaries, and tutorial content, where the frame shows no speaker but the audio track has speech; such samples would severely contaminate lip-sync learning. UniVerse-1 achieves a similar effect indirectly via the Boolean condition "a face is detected by RetinaFace," whereas Unison explicitly lists this as a design goal.
【Passage 3: audio decoupling (not a filtering step, but a preprocessing step)】All audio is separated by Mel-RoFormer into speech and sound-effect streams, serving as ground truth for dual-stream supervision. This is the only explicitly named audio preprocessing step in the entire pipeline, applicable to both audio-video and audio-only data.
【Reconstructable minimal pipeline skeleton】Aggregate five open-source datasets → automated refinement (details undisclosed) → lip-filtering (face detection + in-box SyncNet) → Mel-RoFormer speech/sound-effect decoupling → dual-stream latent encoding → training.
【Completely missing stages】No description of shot segmentation, no aesthetic/clarity filtering, no OCR/watermark/letterbox detection, no motion filtering, no deduplication, no safety filtering, no VLM quality inspection, no stage-by-stage retention rates, and no threshold values whatsoever. Compared with contemporaneous works like MOVA, UniVerse-1, and LTX-2, Unison's data-pipeline disclosure sits at the lowest tier — though it should be fairly noted that this is a consequence of its positioning: with the video backbone frozen, the marginal impact of visual-side data quality is minimal, so the team's attention is reasonably concentrated on the two dimensions that are actually trained — audio and alignment. [Uncertain]

### Quality Filtering (aesthetic score, clarity, OCR text filtering, letterbox/watermark/logo detection) ⚠️

All related information is a blank.
【Why it's blank — a structural explanation, not an oversight】During Stage 2 joint training, the video backbone Wan2.2-5B is completely frozen (keeping the video backbone frozen), with only the audio branch and fusion module (bidirectional cross-attention and layer normalization) being trained. ### Caption Density and Degree of Structuring (short/long/dense descriptions, structured fields such as camera motion, style tags) ⚠️

【The one certain structure inferable from the model's input interface — a dual-channel text condition】This is the only part of this field with textual support. Unison's generation process is formalized as a function taking caption text and transcription text as parallel inputs, two independent text channels:
1) caption text (visual/scene description text);
2) transcription text (speech transcription text, i.e., "what the character said").
Each is separately average-pooled into a global semantic vector, jointly fed into SCG to predict two gating coefficients. This constitutes an explicit two-field structured schema: descriptive text and dialogue text are strictly separated, not merged into a single paragraph.
【The functional significance of this structure】The separation of caption and transcription is not merely an organizational form for annotation but directly carries functionality — SCG compares the relative strength of the two semantic vectors to determine whether the current scene is "narration-dominant" or "complex-soundscape," and dynamically adjusts the mutual influence between the speech stream and the sound-effect stream accordingly. That is, the design of the text schema is coupled with the gating mechanism of the audio architecture: without distinguishing caption from transcription, SCG would have no way to judge the scene type. This is a clear case in this entry of "annotation structure serving architectural design."

### Joint Audio-Video Caption Structure (whether both visual and auditory tracks are covered, whether split into independent fields, e.g., LTX-2's full soundscape description, Script-a-Video's factorized streams, Foley-Omni's three fields) ⚠️

Unison's joint audio-video annotation schema is a two-field factorized structure, but its real distinctiveness lies not in the annotation layer but in the "one-to-one correspondence between the annotation schema and the audio generation streams":
【Annotation layer: caption + transcription, two parallel fields】Visual/scene description and speech transcription are input as two independent text conditions, never merged throughout. Compared with UniVerse-1's three fields (video caption / verified speech content / ambient audio caption), Unison lacks one field — it has no independent "ambient sound description" field; ambient-sound information is implicit within the caption text. This differs in approach from MOVA's practice of "annotating in separate streams, then fusing into a single paragraph via a 120B LLM" — Unison annotates in separate streams and uses them in separate streams.
【Generation layer: speech stream + SFX stream, two parallel streams】The audio latent is physically split into speech and sound-effect streams, each with an independent ground-truth latent (produced by Mel-RoFormer separation) and an independent flow-matching loss.
【The correspondence between the two layers — a structural distinctive feature of this work】The transcription text naturally corresponds to the speech stream (what is said), while the caption text naturally corresponds to the SFX stream and the visual stream (what is in the frame, and hence what sound should accompany it). SCG works precisely by exploiting this correspondence: the global semantic vector of the transcription is used to modulate the speech stream's gating over the sfx stream, and the global semantic vector of the caption is used to modulate the sfx stream's gating over the speech stream. Thus, "the field division of the annotation schema" and "the stream division of audio generation" are isomorphic in Unison — a more thorough decoupling than a simple factorized caption: not only is annotation split into streams, but the generation targets and supervision signals are split as well.
【Positioning relative to comparable schemes】
- LTX-2's "single full-soundscape description": annotation not split, generation not split;
- MOVA: annotation split, then fused into a single caption; generation not split;
- UniVerse-1: annotation split three ways, generation not split (a single audio latent);
- Foley-Omni/Script-a-Video: annotation split into multiple fields, generation varies;
- Unison: annotation split two ways + generation split into two streams + one-to-one correspondence between the two + dynamic rebalancing via gating. In terms of thoroughness of decoupling, this is the highest tier among the works surveyed here.
【Costs and limitations】The lack of an independent ambient-sound description field means users cannot specify ambient-sound content independently as they could with UniVerse-1 — ambient sound can only be controlled indirectly through the scene caption; also, neither of the two annotation streams undergoes cross-modal consistency verification, so if there is a semantic conflict between caption and transcription (e.g., the caption describes a quiet library while the transcription is shouting), there is no detection mechanism. The paper does not discuss this. [Uncertain]
【Ablation support】Table 2 shows that removing SGHS (the semantic-guided harmonization strategy, i.e., canceling the dual-stream decoupling) causes PQ to drop from 6.34 to 6.12, the largest decline among all audio-side ablations, which the paper attributes to "spectral interference"; removing SCG (i.e., keeping the dual streams but removing the semantic gating) drops PQ to 6.21 and LSE-C from 3.30 to 3.22. Together these two results demonstrate the independent contributions of both "decoupling" and "text-semantics-based rebalancing."

【The two operations closest to "construction," though neither is data synthesis】
1) Mel-RoFormer source separation — decomposing real mixed audio tracks into speech and sound-effect streams as ground truth for dual-stream supervision. This is a decomposition of real data, not a synthesis — both output signals are genuine components of the real audio and contain no generated content. However, it does create a form of supervision signal absent in the original data — the original data has only a mixed track; a "clean speech track" and a "clean sound-effect track" are products of the separation model, so the quality ceiling of these two ground-truth streams is bounded by Mel-RoFormer's separation ability, and separation residue (such as sound-effect leakage into the speech track) becomes training noise. The paper does not discuss the impact of separation errors on dual-stream supervision, nor does it give any quantitative assessment of separation quality — this is a potential weak point of the dual-stream architecture. [Uncertain]
2) Asynchronous noise sampling for cross-modal forcing — independently sampling timesteps for video and audio, constructing a training configuration where "one modality is clean, the other noisy," and dynamically designating the student modality. This is a noise-scheduling construction on the training side, not content synthesis, but it does artificially create a "noise-mismatch" training scenario absent in the original data, functionally similar to a form of data augmentation. Ablation proves its value clearly: removing CMFS worsens DS from 0.08 to 0.19 (the worst among all ablations), LSE-C drops from 3.30 to 3.02, and VA drops from 4.02 to 3.91.
【A notable phenomenon worth recording】Removing CMFS damages not only alignment but also video quality (VA 4.02→3.91); the paper interprets this as "the video branch benefits from guidance by the audio stream to reinforce visual coherence" — i.e., even with the video backbone frozen, the audio signal can still improve visual output coherence indirectly through the fusion module. This is direct evidence of the value of cross-modal mutual training.

### Degree of Human Involvement (human annotation, human quality inspection, model pre-screening + human review) ⚠️

Human involvement on the training-data side is zero: the entire pipeline (dataset aggregation → automated refinement → face detection + in-box SyncNet lip filtering → Mel-RoFormer source decoupling) is fully automated, explicitly called an "automated processing pipeline" by the paper. There is no mention of any human annotation, human spot-checking, or human review, nor any statement of whether the thresholds for stages such as SyncNet were calibrated by humans.
【Indirectly inherited human effort】It should be noted that although Unison's own corpus involves no direct human intervention, each of the five upstream open-source datasets included substantial human work during their own construction — CelebV-Text's text descriptions, VGGSound's sound-event category labels, and VFHQ/HDTF's quality screening are all human or semi-human products. A hidden benefit of aggregation-based data construction is precisely "free-riding" on the human labor already invested upstream — which is also the precondition for Unison being able to obtain a usable corpus via a purely automated pipeline.
【Human involvement is concentrated entirely on the evaluation side】
1) The test set is "curated" — the 1,000 held-out samples are described as a "curated test set," implying some human selection step, though the annotations themselves are Gemini-generated and the exact human role is unstated; [Uncertain]
2) User study — 25 participants "from diverse backgrounds" ranked 40 sample groups, producing 1,000 average ranking votes in total (40 × 25). Three evaluation dimensions: lip-sync, speech-sound harmony, and motion-audio alignment (considering both speech and ambient sound). Participants ranked shuffled-order videos from multiple methods (lower is better). Results: Unison ranks first in both speech-sound harmony (1.55) and motion-audio alignment (1.92), and second in lip-sync (1.86), behind LTX-2's 1.74 — this ranking distribution precisely mirrors the paper's stated contribution focus: its innovation lies in "acoustic-level balancing" and "motion-audio alignment" rather than lip sync itself.
【Resource-allocation orientation】Consistent with UniVerse-1, both follow "training data fully automated, evaluation data semi-human"; but Unison's human-evaluation design is more complete (three-dimensional ranking + LLM judge cross-validation), whereas UniVerse-1 has no subjective human evaluation at all.

## Audio-Video Alignment

### Audio-Video Synchronization Detection Method (lip sync, event alignment)

Audio-video synchronization detection is the only explicitly described filtering mechanism on the data side in Unison, but it covers only lip sync, not general event alignment:
【Lip-sync detection — the lip-filtering operator, two steps in series】
1) Face detection: detecting the number and location of faces in the frame, yielding bounding boxes. The detector model is undisclosed. The "number" is also captured, indicating an ability to recognize multi-face scenes;
2) In-box SyncNet verification: SyncNet runs "exclusively within these bounding boxes" to verify alignment. This "restricted region" design is the most engineering-valuable detail in this work's synchronization detection — when SyncNet is run on the full frame, if the face occupies a small portion of the frame, or if the frame contains multiple people, the synchronization confidence is diluted and distorted by a large amount of irrelevant pixels; restricting it to the face box significantly improves the signal-to-noise ratio of the judgment. UniVerse-1 uses a looser serial combination of "RetinaFace detection → full-frame SyncNet," whereas Unison truly couples the two together.
【Dual removal targets】The paper explicitly states this operator "naturally filters out" two types of samples: (i) clips where speech and lip movement are unsynchronized; (ii) off-screen voice-overs/narration. The explicit statement of the second type deserves emphasis — off-screen voice-over is the norm in documentary, tutorial, and vlog content, where the audio track has speech but the frame has no corresponding speaker; if not removed, this would teach the model that "speech doesn't require lip movement," the most destructive kind of noise for lip-sync learning. Unison is among the few works surveyed here that explicitly states "removing off-screen voice-over" as a design goal.
【Non-speech event alignment: no data-side detection】The paper performs no temporal event-alignment verification on non-face data such as VGGSound — no AV-align, no Synchformer-type scoring as a filtering condition, and no onset-alignment detection. Alignment between ambient sound and on-screen events relies entirely on the natural assumption of "native synchronized audio track."
【The weight of alignment assurance lies at the training layer rather than the data layer — Unison's core methodological choice】On the data side, only lip filtering is performed; assurance of event-level alignment is entirely carried by three mechanisms:
1) Architecture layer: frame-level bidirectional cross-attention, aligning within a three-frame window, stride=1, retaining only the middle frame's representation — a deliberately designed, extremely short-horizon local alignment;
2) Training layer: bidirectional cross-modal forcing, decoupling the denoising timesteps so the cleaner modality guides the noisier one, forcing the model to rely on cross-modal information rather than within-modality priors alone;
3) Within audio: the speech stream and the sound-effect stream share the same RoPE time index, ensuring strict temporal alignment between the two audio streams.
The paper's position is clear — alignment is "trained in," not "filtered in," in contrast with MOVA's data-driven route (dual-threshold filtering via Synchformer + ImageBind). The ablations in Table 2 support this position: removing CMFS worsens DS from 0.08 to 0.19 (a 2.4× degradation), the most severe single-item decline among all ablations, proving that the training strategy's contribution to alignment is indeed dominant.
【Evaluation-side synchronization metrics】SyncNet's LSE-C (3.30) and LSE-D (7.88) measure lip sync; Synchformer's DeSync (DS) score measures the absolute temporal offset of modal onsets — Unison's 0.08 is the best overall under TI2AV (better than LTX-2's 0.10 and MOVA's 0.13), and 0.06 under T2AV is likewise the best. Note that Synchformer is used only for evaluation, not as a data filter — unlike MOVA, which uses Synchformer as a filtering threshold.

### Specific Synchronization Metrics and Thresholds (SyncNet/Synchformer/LSE/in-house, threshold values, e.g., MOVA's LSE-D≤9.5 and LSE-C≥4.5, SkyReels-V4's SyncNet |offset|≤3∧conf>1.5) ⚠️

【The metric used is clear, but all thresholds are missing】The data-filtering stage uses SyncNet computed within the face bounding box for lip-audio alignment, but the paper gives no threshold value whatsoever — no confidence lower bound (compare UniVerse-1's conf > 2.0, UniTalking's conf > 0.9), no LSE-D distance upper bound (compare MOVA's dual constraint of LSE-D ≤ 9.5 and LSE-C ≥ 4.5), and it does not even state whether a confidence or distance convention is used, nor the specific SyncNet implementation version (the original Chung & Zisserman version vs. the Wav2Lip team's LSE variant). The paper cites [Chung16a] (the original SyncNet paper) in the Method section, but cites [Prajwal_2020] (Wav2Lip) in the evaluation section for LSE-C/LSE-D, hinting that training filtering and evaluation may use different implementations, but the paper does not clarify this. [Uncertain]
【Threshold calibration method】Unstated. There is neither a statement of human spot-checking nor any threshold-ablation experiment. [Uncertain]
【No sync threshold for non-speech data】Non-face data such as VGGSound is not screened by any temporal-alignment threshold.
【Full list and values of evaluation metrics (TI2AV setting)】
- LSE-C (higher is better): 3.30, second to LTX-2's 3.45, better than MOVA's 3.24, UniAVGen's 2.89, Ovi's 2.81, and UniVerse-1's 2.32;
- LSE-D (lower is better): 7.88, second to LTX-2's 7.62, better than MOVA's 7.92, UniAVGen's 9.49, and Ovi's 9.12;
- DeSync / DS (lower is better; Synchformer measures the absolute temporal offset of modal onset): 0.08, best overall, better than LTX-2's 0.10, Ovi's 0.12, MOVA's 0.13, UniAVGen's 0.15, and UniVerse-1's 0.50.
【A notable metric divergence】Unison ranks first on DS (general event-level temporal alignment) but slightly trails LTX-2 on LSE-C/LSE-D (lip-sync-specific); the user study shows the same pattern (lip sync 1.86, second to LTX-2's 1.74, while motion-audio alignment at 1.92 and speech-sound harmony at 1.55 both rank first). This divergence quite coherently reflects the two works' differing routes: LTX-2 wins on lip-sync detail via a 19B video backbone and vastly larger-scale data, while Unison wins on global event alignment and acoustic-level harmony via a 5B backbone (nearly 4× smaller) plus cross-modal forcing. The paper itself emphasizes "achieving superior cross-modal synchronization with roughly a quarter of the video backbone parameters."
【The practical impact of missing thresholds】Because the data-filtering thresholds are entirely unknown, Unison's lip-sync data quality cannot be horizontally compared with other works, nor can it be determined whether its slightly-behind LSE-C relative to LTX-2 stems from looser filtering or from backbone scale — a substantive gap in this work's reproducibility.

### Separate Treatment of Temporal vs. Semantic Synchronization (temporal alignment and content-semantic matching as two independent filtering conditions) ⚠️

Unison does not treat temporal synchronization and semantic synchronization as two independent data-filtering conditions, but it does draw a relatively clear distinction between the two at the training and evaluation levels:
【Data-filtering layer: only temporal, no semantic】The only filtering mechanism, lip-filtering, is purely temporal in nature (whether lip movement corresponds to speech in time). There is no semantic-matching filtering of any kind — no ImageBind-type cross-modal embedding similarity threshold, no determination of "whether the frame content and the sound content are semantically consistent," and no VLM adjudication of audio-visual semantic self-consistency. Consequently, samples with semantic mismatch — e.g., "the frame shows a kitchen but the audio track is post-production background music" — cannot be identified and removed. However, note that off-screen voice-over removal partially covers speech-category semantic mismatch functionally — it excludes the most common case of "audio and video not sharing the same source."
【Training layer: semantics handed to SCG gating】Unison's approach is to convert semantic consistency from a "filtering problem" into a "conditional-modeling problem" — SCG dynamically determines the mutual influence strength between the speech stream and the sound-effect stream based on the global semantic vectors of the caption and transcription. That is, the model does not pre-remove semantically mismatched samples but instead learns to judge, from text semantics, what acoustic mixture ratio a given scene should generate. This is a completely different approach from MOVA (Synchformer handles the temporal dimension, ImageBind handles the semantic dimension, two orthogonal thresholds filtering separately).
【Evaluation layer: temporal and semantic metrics are clearly separated】This is a relatively well-designed aspect of Unison's evaluation system:
- Temporal-alignment metrics: LSE-C / LSE-D (SyncNet, lip temporal), DS (Synchformer, absolute temporal offset of modal onset);
- Semantic-consistency metrics: TA (CLAP, audio-text semantic consistency), TV (VideoCLIP-XL-V2, video-text semantic alignment), AV (ImageBind, audio-video semantic similarity).
Unison's AV reaches 0.91, the best overall under TI2AV (LTX-2 0.89, MOVA 0.88, Ovi 0.87, UniAVGen 0.81, UniVerse-1 0.62), indicating its audio-visual semantic matching is indeed leading — but this is a result of the training strategy, not of data filtering.
【Core tension】Unison draws a strict distinction between temporal and semantic on the evaluation side, yet is nearly defenseless against both on the data side (temporal coverage limited to lips, semantic coverage absent entirely), relying entirely on the training mechanism to compensate. The results — DS 0.08 and AV 0.91 — show this approach works, but it also implies the effectiveness depends on the semantic cleanliness of the upstream open-source datasets themselves; the same method might not hold if applied to noisy, self-collected web data instead. The paper does not discuss this boundary condition. [Uncertain]

### Audio Quality Filtering (SNR, silence detection and silence-proportion thresholds, removal of tracks with no audio, removal of off-screen audio sources, background-music separation) ⚠️

The paper describes no audio quality-filtering stage of any kind: no SNR threshold, no silence detection or silence-proportion threshold, no statement of removing tracks with no audio, no audio-aesthetic score threshold, no lower bandwidth bound, no clipping/distortion/pop detection, and no statement of sample-rate unification.
【The only explicitly performed audio processing is separation, not filtering】Mel-RoFormer decouples mixed audio into "high-fidelity speech and sound-effect components." This is preprocessing, not screening — every sample is processed, and no sample is removed due to audio quality.
【An important distinction from "background-music separation"】It must be specifically clarified: when works like MOVA and UniVerse-1 mention "background-music separation," they typically mean "stripping out and discarding background music, keeping only the human voice"; whereas Unison's Mel-RoFormer separation has the exact opposite purpose — both separated streams are retained, both serving as ground truth in training. The speech stream learns speech, the sound-effect stream learns sound effects; neither is discarded. This is "decoupling in order to model separately" rather than "separation in order to purify" — a fundamental difference in data-processing philosophy from comparable works.
【The phrasing "high-quality audio segments"】The paper uses this phrase to describe the 50-million-segment audio corpus, implying some quality screening exists, but gives no screening basis, metric, or threshold. Most likely inherited from the upstream datasets' own quality standards (WavCaps, AudioSet, YuE, etc.) rather than built by Unison itself. [Uncertain]
【Audio quality models used on the evaluation side but not for filtering】Audiobox-Aesthetics (following the Audiobox protocol) computes perceptual quality (PQ) and content usefulness (CU). Unison ranks best overall under TI2AV on both (PQ 6.34, CU 5.61, both better than LTX-2's 6.30/5.58). As on the visual side, the team has access to audio quality-scoring tools but does not use them as data filters — in contrast with MOVA, which explicitly uses Audiobox-Aesthetics's three thresholds PQ>5.0 / CU>4.5 / CE>2.5 to filter training data; Unison is a clear blank in this respect.
【Hazard of separation residue】Mel-RoFormer's separation is not perfect — residual sound effects in the speech track and residual voice in the sound-effect track are common. These residues enter dual-stream supervision as "incorrect ground truth," which in theory would weaken the decoupling effect. The paper neither assesses separation quality nor discusses the impact of residue, nor does it set any separation-quality threshold to remove hard-to-separate samples. This is the most worth-questioning aspect of the dual-stream architecture on the data side. [Uncertain]

### Classification and Separate Treatment Strategy for Speech/Sound Effects/Music ⚠️

The processing strategy for the four audio categories — speech, sound effects, music, and singing — is the most systematic part of Unison's entire data design:
【Classification mechanism: no classification decision, but forced decomposition】All audio is uniformly decomposed by Mel-RoFormer into speech and sfx streams; there is no "determine which category this audio belongs to" classification step at all. This is fundamentally different from UniVerse-1 (Whisper-based speech/non-speech binary classification used as a routing gate) and MOVA (an EAT self-supervised audio Transformer making the speech/non-speech determination): the former two are "classify, then route down different paths," whereas Unison is "every sample goes down both paths." The benefit is no classification-error risk and no path-imbalance problem; the cost is that a speech-free sample still must maintain an empty speech stream, and the approach relies entirely on the quality of the separation model.
【The audio-only corpus is collected by four-way source division, each with its own role】
- Speech: internal speech data → trains the speech-generation capability of the Zipformer-enhanced audio branch;
- Sound effects: YouTube-8M + AudioSet + WavCaps → three datasets stacked together, the most heavily covered part on the sound-effect side;
- Music: VidMuse → instrumental/soundtrack capability;
- Singing: the YuE collection → specifically reinforcing singing capability.
【The significance of listing singing as an independent category】This is a differentiated investment relative to comparable works. In its qualitative comparison, the paper explicitly points out that UniVerse-1 and UniAVGen "fail to synthesize musical accompaniments and struggle to distinguish singing from standard speech," and MOVA "can generate credible vocals in music scenes but shows notable speech artifacts in complex environments." Introducing the YuE singing corpus is specifically meant to overcome this named failure mode. The paper also repeatedly uses "singing while playing an instrument" as a representative example of the most difficult scenario — structured vocal content (singing) and structured non-vocal content (instrumental playing) occurring simultaneously is the hardest boundary case for the speech-sound-effect binary division.
【Type division of labor on the generation side】The speech stream carries speech and singing (both vocal), while the sfx stream carries sound effects, music, and ambient sound. Note that singing and music fall onto two different streams respectively — this is a natural consequence of Mel-RoFormer's (a vocal-separation model's) separation logic: it splits by "vocal vs. non-vocal" rather than by "semantic category." This division happens to fit the "singing while playing" scenario well (voice into the speech stream, accompaniment into the sfx stream) — a coincidental fit between the architectural choice and the tool choice.
【Runtime type adaptation】SCG gating dynamically adjusts the two-stream mixture ratio at inference time based on caption/transcription semantics: in narration-dominant scenes it suppresses the sound-effect stream's injection into the speech stream to protect "phonetic purity"; in complex-soundscape scenes it amplifies cross-stream influence to enrich non-speech acoustics. The instance-level analysis in Fig. 8c gives a concrete example from sports commentary — the gating constrains the commentary stream to prevent it from masking the high-frequency transients of the stadium atmosphere (impact sounds and crowd cheers).
【Undisclosed】The hours and proportions of each of the four audio categories, the sampling weights during training, and the curriculum or mixture-ratio arrangement of the four data types within Stage 1. [Uncertain]

## Training Coordination

### Multi-Stage Training Curriculum and Data-Curriculum Scheduling (basis for stage division: resolution/duration/quality score/modality; low-res→high-res, image→video, short→long) ⚠️

Unison has two nested levels of stage division that must be understood separately — an outer two-stage training scheme divided by modality, and an inner three-stage curriculum for cross-modal forcing.
【Outer level: two-stage training (by modality)】
- Stage 1 (single-modality training of the audio branch): 4× NVIDIA H100, batch size 96, learning rate with a 1k-step linear warmup and step decay at 240k and 270k steps (the decay coefficient is given in the paper via a formula). The decay points at 240k/270k imply this stage's training runs to over 270k steps, the bulk of the training volume, consuming the 130,000 hours of audio-only corpus, with the goal of giving the MMAudio + Zipformer audio branch the ability to generate all four types — speech, sound effects, music, and singing;
- Stage 2 (joint fine-tuning): 16× H100, bf16 precision, ZeRO-2 optimization, batch size 32, fine-tuning the coupled system toward the TI2AV objective, consuming the 3,000+ hours of audio-video corpus. The key constraint is that only the audio branch and the fusion module (bidirectional cross-attention and layer normalization) are trained — the video backbone Wan2.2-5B is frozen throughout. The paper does not disclose the number of training steps for this stage. [Uncertain]
【The nature of the outer curriculum: a modality curriculum, not a resolution/duration/quality curriculum】This is a classic "single-modality first, cross-modality second" two-stage scheme, different from both MOVA's three-stage resolution curriculum (360p→360p→720p, 61.5k→37.6k→11k hours) and UniVerse-1's single-stage flat training. Unison has no resolution curriculum (moot, given the frozen video backbone), no duration curriculum, no quality curriculum (no quality-score tiers), and no image→video curriculum. The only dimension along which the data curriculum is scheduled is "modality."
【Inner level: the three-stage progressive curriculum for cross-modal forcing (Progressive Training Strategy)】This is the true curriculum-learning innovation of this work, operating within Stage 2, scheduling "the degree of decoupling between the two modalities' denoising timesteps" rather than data:
- Phase I, Synchronous Warmup: forces the video and audio timesteps to be equal (t_v = t_a), establishing basic alignment;
- Phase II, Incremental Decoupling: activates independent sampling with probability p, while constraining the upper bound on the timestep difference between the two; from this phase onward, direction-aware loss reweighting is enabled — upweighting the video loss when audio is cleaner, prioritizing the audio loss when video is cleaner;
- Phase III, Full Independence: unconstrained decoupled updates.
The stage proportions are 0.3 / 0.4 / 0.3. The design motivation is that directly optimizing fully independent timesteps from the start would cause training instability due to excessive cross-modal noise disparity and random oscillation of the guidance direction.
【Quantitative evidence of curriculum effectiveness (Table 3)】Three scheduling schemes compared:
- SyncOnly (fully synchronous timesteps throughout): VA 3.90, PQ 6.10, LSE-C 3.12, DS 0.17 — worst across the board, proving that uniform noise prevents the model from exploiting denoising asymmetry;
- IndepOnly (fully decoupled from the start, no warmup): VA 3.95, PQ 6.18, LSE-C 3.28, DS 0.14 — alignment improves but optimization is unstable, LSE-C capped at 3.28;
- ProgForcing (this paper's three-stage curriculum): VA 4.02, PQ 6.34, LSE-C 3.30, DS 0.08 — best on all metrics.
This ablation set clearly demonstrates that the progressive transition is indispensable for both "stable optimization" and "precise temporal alignment," directly supporting the value of this work's curriculum design.

### Change in Data Mixture Across Training Stages (pretraining/annealing/high-quality SFT subset) ⚠️

The paper discloses no data-mixture figures and does not state whether the mixture changes across stages.
【Determinable stage-to-corpus correspondence】
- Stage 1 consumes the audio-only corpus: 50 million segments / 130,000+ hours, covering speech (internal data), sound effects (YouTube-8M/AudioSet/WavCaps), music (VidMuse), and singing (YuE) — four categories. The mixture ratio, sampling weights, and whether the stage is further sub-staged internally (e.g., sound effects before speech) among the four categories are all unstated; [Uncertain]
- Stage 2 consumes the audio-video corpus: approximately 2 million clips / 3,000+ hours, from five datasets. The mixture ratio among the five sources and whether any subset was up-/down-sampled are both unstated. [Uncertain]
【The data-volume ratio between the two stages】By hour count, 130,000 : 3,000 ≈ 43 : 1 — the audio corpus is more than forty times the audio-video corpus. This stark ratio is key to understanding Unison's data strategy: it is fundamentally a work "trained primarily on audio, with a small amount of audio-video data for alignment fine-tuning." Visual capability is entirely outsourced to the frozen Wan2.2-5B, while audio-visual alignment capability is acquired quickly on the fusion module using 3,000 hours of curated human-centric data.
【No annealing stage】No operation of switching to a high-quality subset at the end of training is described. Stage 1 does have a learning-rate step decay (at 240k and 270k steps), but this is an optimizer schedule, not a data-mixture schedule.
【No independent SFT stage】Stage 2 is itself the fine-tuning stage; there is no second round of fine-tuning afterward.
【What actually changes across stages is not the data mixture but the noise-coupling degree】Unison's three-stage curriculum schedules the degree of decoupling between the video and audio timesteps (synchronous → partially independent → fully independent, at proportions 0.3/0.4/0.3) and the direction of loss weighting; the data composition remains unchanged throughout Stage 2. This is a route where "the curriculum acts on the training mechanism rather than on the data distribution," in sharp contrast with MOVA's "decreasing scale, increasing quality" data-pyramid curriculum.

### Post-Training Data (SFT curated-set scale and screening criteria, number of preference pairs and annotation method, reward-model training data)

No post-training data. Unison performs no post-training whatsoever after its two training stages:
- No SFT curated set (Stage 2 is itself the fine-tuning stage, with no second round of fine-tuning afterward, and no screening criteria for any high-quality subset are described);
- No RLHF/DPO preference alignment; no preference pairs constructed; no reward model trained;
- No human-preference-annotated data used for training — the 1,000 ranking votes from the user study (40 samples × 25 people) are used only for final evaluation, not fed back into training;
- No distillation or accelerated post-training (no step distillation or CFG distillation mentioned; inference still uses the full 50-step flow-matching + CFG 6.0 sampling, with no acceleration applied).
【Composition of the evaluation system (not training data, but worth recording)】
1) Objective metrics: 1,000 held-out test samples, ground-truth annotations generated by Gemini, covering both T2AV and TI2AV tasks, with 11 metrics in total (VA/ID/PQ/CU/WER/TA/TV/AV/LSE-C/LSE-D/DS);
2) Subjective evaluation: 40 samples × 25 participants from diverse backgrounds = 1,000 average ranking votes, across three dimensions (lip sync, speech-sound-effect harmony, motion-audio alignment);
3) LLM judge: an additional Gemini Score column scoring motion-speech-sfx coherence, cross-validated against human rankings (the two are consistent).
This "objective metrics + human ranking + LLM judge" triple evaluation is relatively complete among comparable works, but none of its outputs are open-sourced.

### Data-Processing Infrastructure and Throughput (NeMo Curator/Data-Juicer/in-house, GPU acceleration ratio, processing scale, cost) ⚠️

The paper's disclosure of data-processing infrastructure is zero: no mention of NeMo Curator, Data-Juicer, Ray, or any distributed data-processing framework; no processing scale, throughput, duration, or cost given for the cleaning stage; no statement of GPU acceleration for the pipeline; no discussion of the storage/IO scheme for 2 million clips / 50 million audio segments; and no statement of whether latents are precomputed offline and cached or encoded online.
【The disclosed training infrastructure (a fairly modest scale)】
- Stage 1 (audio branch): 4× NVIDIA H100, batch size 96, learning rate with a 1k-step linear warmup, with step decay at 240k and 270k steps;
- Stage 2 (joint training): 16× H100, bf16 precision, ZeRO-2 optimization (not FSDP, nor the more aggressive ZeRO-3), batch size 32; only the audio branch and fusion module are trained, with the video backbone frozen;
- Inference: a 50-step flow-matching sampler, CFG scale 6.0, output at 25 FPS.
- Undisclosed: total training duration, GPU-days, Stage 2 step count, and sequence-parallel/context-parallelism strategy (given the frozen video backbone and clips of only about 5 seconds, long-sequence pressure is limited).
【Scale positioning and the economics of the "frozen backbone" strategy】16 H100s is a notably small joint-training configuration — MOVA uses a mixed NVIDIA GPU and Ascend NPU compute cluster, UniVerse-1 uses multi-node FSDP, and industrial models are on the order of thousands of GPUs. Unison's ability to complete joint training with 16 cards directly benefits from its design of "freezing the 5B video backbone, training only the audio branch and fusion module" — the trainable parameter count is greatly reduced, correspondingly lowering memory and compute requirements. This, together with the paper's emphasis on "achieving superior cross-modal synchronization with a 5B backbone (nearly 4× smaller than LTX-2's 19B)," forms two sides of its efficiency narrative.
【The blank in data-side infrastructure is understandable】Because the corpus is aggregated from existing open-source datasets (already segmented, already downloaded, and partly already annotated), Unison has no need to build heavy infrastructure for crawling, transcoding, or large-scale segmentation, and its processing volume is far smaller than works starting from raw long videos. Even so, lip-filtering across 2 million clips (each requiring face detection + SyncNet inference) and Mel-RoFormer separation across 50 million audio segments still represent a considerable amount of computation, and the paper's complete silence on this remains a disclosure gap. ## Comparative Results

### Quantitative Impact of Data-Strategy Ablations (distinguishing filtering-strictness ablation / caption-density-and-style ablation / data-mixture ablation, and corresponding evaluation metrics) ⚠️

All of Unison's ablation experiments target architectural components and training strategies — none is a data-strategy ablation: no filtering-strictness ablation, no caption-density/style ablation, no data-mixture ablation, and no data-scale scaling experiment were conducted. This is the fact most needing clarification in this entry under the "Comparative Results" category.
【Ablation 1: core components (Table 2, four items, metrics VA/PQ/LSE-C/DS, full-model baseline 4.02/6.34/3.30/0.08)】
- w/o SGHS (removing the semantic-guided harmonization strategy, i.e., canceling the speech-sound-effect dual-stream decoupling): 3.99 / 6.12 / 3.08 / 0.15. The PQ decline of 0.22 is the largest among all audio-side ablations, which the paper attributes to spectral interference. This is the item most relevant to data processing — it indirectly validates the value of the data-processing decision to "use Mel-RoFormer to split the training data's audio track into two streams for separate supervision";
- w/o Bi-ACA (removing bidirectional audio cross-attention): 4.00 / 6.20 / 3.18 / 0.11. Both PQ and lip-sync precision decline, indicating that mutual context between the two audio streams is indispensable;
- w/o SCG (removing semantic conditional gating): 4.01 / 6.21 / 3.22 / 0.10. This is the item most relevant to the annotation schema — SCG's input is precisely the global semantic vectors of the caption and transcription text channels, and removing it means giving up the ability to "dynamically adjust the two-stream mixture ratio using text semantics";
- w/o CMFS (removing the cross-modal forcing strategy): 3.91 / 6.24 / 3.02 / 0.19. This exhibits a completely different degradation pattern from the audio-side ablations — DS spikes to 0.19 (the worst across all settings), LSE-C drops to 3.02 (the worst), and VA drops to 3.91.
【Two conclusions worth recording】
1) Functional complementarity: the three audio-side ablations have minimal impact on VA (3.99-4.01, a fluctuation of only 0.03), proving that the audio components and visual quality are decoupled (modularity); whereas removing CMFS damages both alignment and visuals (VA 4.02→3.91), indicating the video branch benefits from guidance by the audio stream — this effect appearing even with the video backbone completely frozen is strong evidence of the value of cross-modal mutual training;
2) Clear division of labor: audio-side modules are responsible for acoustic fidelity (PQ), while CMFS is responsible for audio-visual synchronization (DS/LSE-C), with the failure modes of the two categories of modules not overlapping.
【Ablation 2: training schedule (Table 3, three variants)】SyncOnly (3.90/6.10/3.12/0.17) < IndepOnly (3.95/6.18/3.28/0.14) < ProgForcing, this paper's method (4.02/6.34/3.30/0.08). See the multi_stage_curriculum field for details.
【The missing data-ablation checklist】None of the following was validated: the impact of loosening or tightening the SyncNet filtering threshold; the individual contribution of each of the five audio-video data sources (e.g., what happens if VGGSound is removed); whether the mixture ratio of the four audio categories is optimal; the scale effect of 130,000 hours of audio vs. less audio; the scale effect of 3,000 hours of audio-video vs. more audio-video; the impact of Mel-RoFormer separation quality on dual-stream supervision; and the individual necessity of the caption and transcription text conditions (though w/o SCG touches on this indirectly, the text inputs are not directly ablated). For a paper that claims to have an "automated processing pipeline," zero ablation of the pipeline itself is a clear argumentative shortfall. [Uncertain]

### Evidence for Quality vs. Quantity (cases where small, curated data outperforms large, messy data) ⚠️

Unison provides no direct experimental evidence for "small, curated data outperforming large, messy data" — it conducted no controlled experiment on data scale or data quality. But it does provide a different kind of evidence, worth discussing separately:
【What the paper actually argues is "architectural efficiency outperforms parameter and data scale," not "data quality over quantity"】The paper's core efficiency argument is: with a 5B video backbone (nearly 4× smaller than LTX-2's 19B), it achieves superior cross-modal synchronization (DS 0.08 vs. 0.10, AV 0.91 vs. 0.89, PQ 6.34 vs. 6.30, CU 5.61 vs. 5.58, WER 0.22 vs. 0.25), explicitly summarized as "our architectural innovations effectively capture high-fidelity audio-visual correlations without the need for massive parameter scaling." This is a parameter-efficiency argument, not a data-efficiency argument — the paper never compares its own 3,000 hours against LTX-2's corpus scale (the latter's scale is undisclosed in this paper), so no data-side quality-priority conclusion can be drawn from this.
【An observation indirectly supporting "curated data is sufficient"】The 3,000+ hours of audio-video corpus is on the small end among comparable works (0.4× UniVerse-1's, 1/20 of MOVA Phase 1's), yet achieves SOTA or near-SOTA results on multiple metrics. But the attribution of this result is highly conflated: with the video backbone frozen, visual capability comes from Wan2.2's massive pretraining, the 130,000 hours of audio ensures acoustic capability, and the 3,000 hours of audio-video need only handle the relatively lightweight task of "learning alignment." So the real meaning of "small data also works well" here is "the task of alignment itself does not require much data," not "curated data can substitute for large-scale data."
【Evidence pointing in the opposite direction: the audio side takes the scale route】Worth noting is an internal tension — Unison piles up 130,000 hours / 50 million segments on the audio side, a thoroughly scale-oriented route, with no quality-screening description whatsoever (covered only by the single word "high-quality"). If the team truly believed in quality priority, the audio side should have been the part with the most conditions for fine screening (audio quality-assessment tools are mature and cheap, and Audiobox-Aesthetics is already in their hands for evaluation). The actual approach is a three-part scheme — "quantity for audio, alignment for audio-video, inheritance for vision" — each following a different data philosophy.
【Conclusion】This field has no reliable evidence in Unison. The paper conducted neither a data-scale scaling curve nor a fine-screening vs. coarse-screening comparison; any inference of "quality over quantity" lacks support. [Uncertain]

### Alignment Between Training-Data Domain Distribution and Evaluation-Benchmark Taxonomy (e.g., VABench's seven major categories) ⚠️

Unison has published no public evaluation benchmark and has built no taxonomy of any kind, so there is no strict-sense "alignment between training-data distribution and evaluation taxonomy" relationship.
【Composition of the evaluation set】1,000 held-out samples, described as "curated," with ground-truth annotations generated by Gemini, covering the T2AV and TI2AV tasks. The paper does not disclose: which data sources these 1,000 samples came from, how they were sampled, whether they were stratified by category, the proportion of each category, or whether they are strictly isolated from the training data (the term "held-out" implies a same-source holdout, in which case the evaluation set would share the same distribution as the training set, and generalization ability could not be assessed). This is a gap in the evaluation design worth questioning. [Uncertain]
【Comparison with works like VABench/Verse-Bench】UniVerse-1's Verse-Bench gives explicit proportions for nine audio categories (natural environment 36.1%, music and instruments 19.3%, etc.) and a three-subset structure; MOVA built a bilingual Chinese-English Arena evaluation set. Unison has neither a taxonomy nor subset division, nor is its evaluation set open-sourced — clearly weaker than both in benchmark construction.
【The one place with any taxonomy awareness: the instance-level analysis of SCG gating】Fig. 8c shows "mean gate values across semantic categories," indicating the team did internally divide samples by semantic category and compute gating behavior by category. But the paper neither lists these categories' taxonomy nor gives each category's sample proportion, nor does it state the correspondence between these categories and the training-data composition — an opportunity to fill the taxonomy gap that was left unexplored. [Uncertain]
【The implied three-way scene division (the only reconstructable "taxonomy")】From the paper's qualitative discussion and the design intent of SCG, a three-way classification by acoustic-scene nature can be reconstructed: narration-dominant scenes (SCG suppresses the sound-effect stream to protect speech purity), complex-soundscape scenes (SCG amplifies cross-stream influence to enrich non-speech acoustics), and difficult scenes where speech and sound effects matter equally (the paper's examples: singing while playing an instrument, sports commentary, beach conversation, piano performance, moving locomotive). This division is the true organizing principle of Unison's data and method design — it explains why the YuE singing corpus and VGGSound were introduced, explains the logic of SCG's gating, and corresponds to the original "speech-sound-effect harmony" evaluation dimension in the user study (where Unison ranks first at 1.55, with the largest margin over the runner-up LTX-2's 1.95). But it is a reconstruction made by this entry from the paper's discussion — the paper itself never formalizes it as a taxonomy, nor gives a corresponding data mixture.
【The actual state of training-evaluation alignment】The three evaluation dimensions of the user study (lip sync, speech-sound-effect harmony, motion-audio alignment) correspond one-to-one with the paper's three technical contributions (lip-filtering + three-frame cross-attention, SGHS/Bi-ACA/SCG, CMFS) — this is a genuine structural alignment between the evaluation design and the method design; but there is no verifiable correspondence with the training data's category distribution.
