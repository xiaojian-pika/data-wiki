## Multi-Stage Training Curriculum and Data Curriculum Scheduling (Stage-Division Basis: Resolution/Duration/Quality Score/Modality; Low-Res→High-Res, Image→Video, Short→Long)

`multi_stage_curriculum` · Detail level: brief

### [Allegro](../models/Allegro.md)

Four stages (five including T2I) of progressive curriculum, whose scheduling basis jointly spans four axes — modality, resolution, duration/frame count, and quality score — forming the core of this work's data-stratification design:
· Stage 0 — T2I pre-training: 368×640, single frame, 700M training samples (107M images in the dataset), batch 4096, 170K steps, 128× H100. Uses images to establish visual priors and text-image alignment.
· Stage 1 — T2V pre-training 360p: 368×640, 40 frames, 87M training samples (48M videos in the dataset), batch 1024, 85K steps, 256× H100. Modality transfer from image to video, low resolution with large data volume.
· Stage 2 — T2V pre-training 720p: 720×1280, 40 frames, 21M training samples (18M in the dataset), batch 512, 41K steps, 256× H100. Low-res → high-res.
· Stage 3 — T2V pre-training long frames: 720×1280, 88 frames, 8M training samples, batch 256, 31K steps, 256× H100. Short → long (40 frames → 88 frames).
· Stage 4 — T2V fine-tuning: 720×1280, 88 frames, 2.6M training samples (about 2M in the dataset), batch 256, 10K steps, 256× H100. Uses only the curated set under the strictest thresholds.
It can be seen that resolution, duration, and the quality threshold tighten strictly in lockstep, while data volume drops by orders of magnitude stage by stage (107M→48M→18M→about 2M), with batch size correspondingly falling from 4096 to 256. Fig. 9 of the paper qualitatively visualizes the progressive gains of the multi-stage training.

### [Apollo](../models/Apollo.md) ⚠️

Adopts a three-stage "Progressive Multi-Task Training" curriculum, but the basis for dividing stages is **task and quality**, rather than the common resolution/duration dimensions:
【Stage I — Pre-training】Trained on large-scale, multi-scenario corpora, with the goal to "acquire atomic generation capabilities across all tasks" — mastering the atomic capabilities for all tasks, including cross-modal semantic alignment, temporal synchronization, high-fidelity audio synthesis, and precise visual feature construction.
【Stage II — Specialized Post-training】"Guided by evaluation metrics, we adaptively rebalance data distributions across scenarios and tasks to strengthen underperforming capabilities while preserving overall competency" — driven by evaluation metrics, adaptively rebalancing the data mixture across scenarios and tasks to shore up weak capabilities without sacrificing overall competence. This is a metric-feedback-driven dynamic data curriculum, closer to industrial practice than fixed mixture ratios.
【Stage III — Quality-Refined Post-training】Fine-tuned on a manually curated high-quality dataset to improve generation fidelity and robustness in complex scenarios.
【Orthogonal task curriculum】Running in parallel with the three stages is a multi-task masking mechanism: through random modality masking, the model jointly optimizes five task types — T2A, T2V, T2AV, I2V, I2AV — within a single model, which the paper terms "from random modality masking to joint optimization across tasks."
【Notable omissions】No resolution curriculum (low-res→high-res), duration curriculum (short→long), or conventional image→video progressive strategy is used or disclosed; the training steps, epoch counts, data volumes, and resolution configurations for each stage are entirely unreported. This is the largest disclosure gap relative to open-source work such as MOVA (360p→360p→720p, 61.5k→37.6k→11k hours, with per-stage day counts fully listed). [Uncertain]

### [CineDance / CineDance-1M](../models/CineDance.md) ⚠️

The CineDance model (an LTX-2.3 adaptation) adopts a dual curriculum design — a data curriculum and a reference-frame curriculum advancing in parallel:
【Data-Driven Curriculum, two stages】
  · Stage 1: 2–3 adjacent shots, 10–12 seconds, with the training objective being local shot-switching capability;
  · Stage 2: up to 8 shots, about 30 seconds, with the training objective being cross-shot consistency.
  The curriculum is divided along the axis of "shot count + duration" — a narrative-complexity axis — rather than the traditional resolution or quality-score axis. This is a curriculum-design orientation that differs from most video generation models (low-res→high-res, image→video), reflecting that this work's core tension lies in narrative length rather than image quality.
【Dual-Axis Reference Curriculum (DARC)】As training progress u advances, the reference-frame scaffolding is progressively removed:
  · Visual scaffolding: continuous noise is added to the reference frames via ρ(rₖ; ηᵥ(u)) = ηᵥ(u)·rₖ + (1−ηᵥ(u))·εₖ, with the noise ratio increasing over training;
  · Temporal scaffolding: stochastic index switching is performed with probability q(ηₜ);
  · Reference-frame dropping: reference tokens are wholesale removed with probability p_drop(u).
  Together, the three mechanisms achieve a smooth transition from "strong reference guidance" to "autonomous generation."
【Resolution curriculum】All source material is unified at 1080p and above; the paper does not describe a low-to-high resolution progression strategy.
【Undisclosed】Training steps, sample counts, batch size, learning rate, and total compute for each stage. [Uncertain]

### [CogVideoX](../models/CogVideoX.md)

The training curriculum follows the main line of "progressive resolution and duration + final high-quality fine-tuning," with the hyperparameters of the four stages given in full in Table 5 of the paper (shared between CogVideoX-2B and 5B):
· stage1: max resolution 256×384, max duration 6s, batch size 2000, sequence length 25k, 400k training steps — learning semantics and low-frequency knowledge;
· stage2: 480×720, 6s, batch 1000, sequence 75k, 220k steps;
· stage3: 768×1360, 10s, batch 250, sequence 700k, 120k steps — learning high-frequency detail;
· stage4 (FT): 768×1360, 10s, batch 100, sequence 700k, 10k steps — high-quality fine-tuning.
As resolution rises, batch size falls from 2000 to 100, sequence length climbs from 25k to 700k, and step count falls from 400k to 10k — compute is heavily weighted toward the low-resolution stages.
Other curriculum elements:
· Mixed image-video training runs throughout (each image is treated as a single-frame video), resolving the problem where fixed-frame-count joint training would otherwise split the model by token count into two distinct generation modes;
· Mixed-duration training + Multi-Resolution Frame Pack, allowing samples of different durations/resolutions to share a batch, avoiding the need to discard short videos or truncate long ones;
· Data-filtering thresholds (optical flow score, aesthetic score) are dynamically adjusted over the course of training, i.e., the data curriculum is coupled to the training process;
· The 3D VAE itself has a two-stage curriculum: first trained at 17 frames, then fine-tuned to 161 frames with context parallelism;
· The diffusion setup uses v-prediction + zero SNR, and proposes Explicit Uniform Sampling (dividing 1..T into n intervals, with each rank sampling uniformly within its own interval) to stabilize the loss curve and speed convergence;
· RoPE adaptation for high resolution opts for extrapolation rather than interpolation.

### [Cosmos-Predict2.5](../models/Cosmos-Predict25.md)

Training uses a progressive curriculum, with the paper explicitly stating that difficulty increases along two axes: pixel resolution and task diversity, while the duration axis stays fixed throughout (always 93 frames / 16fps / about 5.8 seconds). Five pre-training stages (Tab. 4):
· Stage 1: Text2Image only, 256p (320×192), 1 frame — first learn to generate high-quality single frames before handling motion and temporal consistency;
· Stage 2: Text2Image | Video2World, 256p, 1 frame | 93 frames — video is introduced, joint image-text training;
· Stage 3: Text2Image | Video2World, 480p (832×480);
· Stage 4: Text2Image | Video2World, 720p (1280×704);
· Stage 5: Text2Image | Video2World | Text2World, 720p — finally adding the unconditional-frame Text2World task.
The criterion for advancing resolution is "advancing to the next stage once the model converges and visual quality plateaus," rather than a fixed iteration count.
【Conditioning-frame schedule】In stages 2–4, 1 or 5 conditioning frames are randomly sampled, and the model generates the remaining 92 or 88 frames; in stage 5 this changes to sampling 0 / 1 / 2 conditioning frames with probabilities 0.5 / 0.25 / 0.25 respectively — this is the key scheduling design that unifies the three tasks within the same batch (0 conditioning frames = T2W, 1 = I2W, ≥2 = V2W). Conditioning is done via frame-replacement (the first several frames of the generated sequence are replaced by the conditioning frames), with mask tokens binarily marking which are conditioning inputs; the loss applies only to the frames to be generated.
【Noise curriculum】Timesteps are sampled from a logit-normal distribution, with a shift applied that grows with resolution: β=1 at 256p, increasing step by step to β=5 at 720p, biasing high-resolution training toward higher-noise regions (the paper explains: at high resolution pixels are highly correlated, and insufficient noise means the model fails to learn meaningful structure). There is also a notable correction: after observing abrupt, unnatural jump artifacts between frames, the team judged that there were too few samples in the high-noise region, and so modified the scheduler so that 5% of training samples are explicitly drawn from the top 2% of the noise distribution, which markedly reduced transition artifacts and improved temporal consistency.
【Subsequent stages】Pre-training → domain SFT (one model trained per each of the five domains) → 4K cooldown (learning rate linearly decayed to 0) → model merging → RL (GRPO) → timestep distillation (rCM, 4 steps).
【Optimizer】AdamW, β1=0.9, β2=0.999, weight decay 0.001; learning rate 3e-5 for the 2B model, 1.3e-5 for the 14B model; linear decay schedule + 2000-iteration warmup.

### [Data-Juicer 2.0](../models/Data-Juicer.md) ⚠️

Data-Juicer does not train models, but it provides two kinds of support for "data curriculum scheduling," and its T2V case study itself embeds an implicit two-stage curriculum.
【Curriculum-learning recipe in the DJ-Cookbook】The official maintainers provide a "curriculum learning based on data difficulty" YAML recipe — operators first tag samples with a difficulty score (any statistic can serve as a difficulty proxy, e.g. aesthetic score, motion score, CLIP similarity, text complexity), and the dataset is then output stratified by difficulty for staged feeding. This is a ready-made template for productizing curriculum scheduling.
【The Sandbox pyramid data pool = a natural curriculum structure】The 2^n−1 cross data pools constructed in the Refine stage form a pyramid: the top layer satisfies the strict conditions of all operators simultaneously (highest quality, fewest samples), and each layer down relaxes the conditions (declining quality, increasing sample count). This structure maps directly onto a training curriculum — the large-sample bottom layer for early pre-training to lay the foundation, and the small, high-quality top layer for later-stage annealing/fine-tuning. In its comparison of scale-up routes, the paper actually tests a path of "progressively drilling down to incorporate suboptimal data + deduplication," which is equivalent to a reverse curriculum.
【Two-stage practice in the T2V case study】The optimal data pool (147k) is first used for distillation training, then expanded to a 228k data pool (corresponding to a 640k training-sample volume, including about 2.8× repetition) for the final model — this constitutes a two-stage "small and refined → scale up" pipeline.
【Engineering-level stage support】v1.5.0 introduces OP-level environment management, allowing different operator combinations with different dependencies to be used at different stages; the partitioned Ray executor and repartition pipeline (v1.5.3) make it convenient to produce data shards of different specifications for different stages.
[Uncertain] DJ does not provide an officially recommended multi-stage curriculum scheme for video generation (e.g., the specific division basis and switching timing for "low-res→high-res" or "image→video"), nor does it report curriculum experiments along the resolution or duration dimensions in its public case studies. This falls under the responsibility of the training framework; DJ only supplies the raw material for data stratification.

### [Foley-Omni](../models/Foley-Omni.md) ⚠️

Adopts a three-stage progressive curriculum whose division basis is 【modality complexity + task compositeness】, rather than the resolution/duration dimensions common in text-to-video work. The overall route is "text conditioning → adding visual conditioning → joint multi-component."
【Stage 1: Text-driven foundation】Tasks TTA + TTS + TTM, 5 epochs, about 0.7M text-audio pairs. The goal is to first let the model master the generation of each of the three audio types under purely textual conditioning, establishing an audio prior. No video input at this stage. The most epochs (5 rounds) are used here, indicating that establishing the basic audio-generation capability is given the largest training budget.
【Stage 2: Introducing visual conditioning】Task V2A + VisualTTS, 3 epochs. Adds a video-conditioning pathway (CLIP semantic features + Synchformer sync features Z_sync), learning the "visual→audio" semantic mapping and temporal alignment. This stage is still a single-type audio task; only the conditioning modality becomes more complex.
【Stage 3: Joint scoring fine-tuning】Task V2ST, 2 epochs, using 216 hours of V2ST data. Learns to jointly generate multiple audio tracks within the same clip while keeping them mutually coordinated. The fewest epochs are used, being a lightweight fine-tune, consistent with the common practice of "small-scale, high-quality data for final alignment."
【Quantitative evidence of curriculum effectiveness】The Table 6 ablation shows that skipping the curriculum and going straight to single-stage joint training causes performance to collapse across the board: V2ST WER worsens from 7.59 to 29.29 (nearly a 4× degradation), GRID WER from 15.3 to 27.4, IB drops from 0.26 to 0.24, and FD_VGG rises from 1.57 to 1.73. Speech intelligibility suffers the most damage, indicating that speech — a modality that is highly structural and has the least tolerance for error — depends especially on prior single-task pre-training; when trained directly in a mixture, speech capability gets drowned out by the gradients of the sound-effect/music tasks. This is the paper's strongest empirical evidence regarding the data/training curriculum.
[Uncertain] The learning rate, batch size, total training steps, hardware configuration, and training duration for each stage are not disclosed in the accessible content.

### [Goku](../models/Goku.md)

★ A three-stage training curriculum whose stage-division basis is "task modality + resolution," a typical progressive image→video, low-res→high-res curriculum:
【Stage 1: Text-Semantic Pairing】
  Pure text-to-image pre-training, using 100 million public text-image pairs from LAION to establish a basic correspondence between text and visual semantics. This step provides a semantic prior for subsequent video training, avoiding the inefficiency of learning semantics from scratch directly on video.
【Stage 2: Image-Video Joint Learning】
  Mixed image and video training, with a resolution cascade: 288×512 → 480×864 → 720×1280. This curriculum corresponds strictly to the resolution-bucketing on the data side — 36M videos are available at the 480p stage, shrinking to 24M at the 720p stage, and only 7M at the 1080p stage; data volume decreases as resolution increases, forming a pyramid-shaped curriculum. Image data continues to participate throughout this stage, ensuring that image quality and semantic capability do not degrade (this is the core claim of Goku's joint modeling).
【Stage 3: Modality-Specific Fine-tuning】
  Specialized optimization is performed separately for T2I and T2V; T2I fine-tuning uses 60 million in-house high-quality images, while I2V is fine-tuned from a T2V initialization using 4.5 million text-image-video triplets.
【Related design】The data pipeline's resolution-tiered filtering thresholds (Table 4) are tailor-made for this curriculum — every training resolution tier has a batch of data already filtered to the corresponding stringency standing by; the data side and training side are tightly coupled.

### [HunyuanVideo-Foley](../models/HunyuanVideo-Foley.md)

【No multi-stage curriculum — single-stage flat training】The full 100,000 hours of data for the TV2A main model is mixed and trained in a single stage in one shot, totaling 200k steps, on 128 H20 GPUs, with an effective batch size of 2048, the AdamW optimizer, a learning rate of 1e-4, and an independent CFG dropout of 0.1 applied to each modality. There is no resolution curriculum, duration curriculum, quality curriculum, or modality curriculum.
【Why this work can forgo a curriculum】Curriculum learning's main motivation in video generation is cost optimization under compute constraints (low resolution to quickly establish semantics, high resolution to refine detail) and training stability. This work sidesteps that need for three reasons:
1) The data format itself is already normalized — every sample is strictly 8 seconds fixed-length, audio is uniformly 48 kHz, and the latent frame rate is fixed at 50 Hz, so there is no variable dimension along which to grade difficulty;
2) The visual side is not generated, so there is no need for resolution ramp-up;
3) The sequence length is short (8 seconds × 50 Hz = 400 audio latent tokens), so training cost is inherently manageable and does not need to be amortized via a curriculum.
【The only two-stage structure lies between the audio VAE and the main model】The DAC-VAE is first independently trained for 700k steps on about 100,000 hours of audio data (32 H20 GPUs, batch size 256); once trained it is frozen, and the TV2A main model is then trained for 200k steps. This is the standard "train the VAE first, then train the diffusion model" two-step process, which is architecturally necessary rather than a data curriculum.
【A stability technique that substitutes for curriculum: REPA representation alignment】The paper explicitly lists REPA (Representation Alignment) as one of three main contributions, whose function is precisely "efficiently improving audio quality and generation stability." This works by aligning the internal hidden representations of the diffusion model with representations extracted by the pretrained self-supervised audio encoder ATST-Frame via cosine similarity (the loss is negative cosine similarity), equivalent to providing an extra representation-level supervisory signal for intermediate layers that accelerates the establishment of semantic structure. This functionally substitutes, in part, for the role of curriculum learning — curriculum learning reduces optimization difficulty by going "easy first, hard later," whereas REPA directly supplies the optimization direction via "external representation guidance." This idea of substituting representation supervision for a data curriculum is a mainstream approach in the diffusion-model field in 2025 (originating from the SiT/REPA line of work).
【Ablation of REPA configuration】In encoder choice, ATST-Frame (PQ 6.28) outperforms EAT (PQ 6.24), and combining the two actually degrades performance (PQ 6.00) — indicating that the supervisory signals from multiple self-supervised representations can interfere with each other; in terms of application layer position, applying it at the shallower layer 8 (PQ 6.34, DeSync 0.74) outperforms layer 12 (6.28 / 0.81) and layer 16 (6.32 / 0.78), i.e., representation guidance should be applied at shallow layers where features are not yet highly specialized.

### [HunyuanVideo](../models/HunyuanVideo.md)

Both generations are textbook examples of a progressive "image→video, low-res→high-res, low-frame-rate→high-frame-rate" curriculum, with the dataset stratification corresponding one-to-one with the training stages (the data tiers are the curriculum steps):
【Original version】
· Two-stage image pre-training: Stage 1 — 256px multi-aspect-ratio training; Stage 2 — mixed-scale training at 256px and 512px (mix-scale);
· Three-stage video-image joint training: low-resolution short video (256×256×65 frames) → low-resolution long video → high-resolution long video (720×1280×129 frames); video and image are trained jointly throughout to prevent image capability from degrading; the video:image ratio in VAE training is 4:1;
· Finally, SFT is layered on top (about 1 million manually curated samples, multi-scale).
【1.5】Eight stages in total (Table 2), with the curriculum axis simultaneously spanning resolution, frame rate, and task type:
· Stage I: 256p images, 5 billion, T2I;
· Stage II: 512p images, 1 billion, T2I;
· Stage III: 256p / 16fps / 2–10s, 800 million, mixed T2V+I2V+T2I training, task ratio 1:6:3 (I2V has the highest share, at 60%);
· Stage IV: 480p / 16fps / 2–10s, 200 million;
· Stage V: 720p / 16fps / 2–10s, 100 million;
· Stage VI: 720p / 24fps / 2–10s, 100 million (this stage only raises the frame rate, not the resolution);
· Stage VII: CT continued training, 480p/720p / 24fps, 1 million, focused on T2V;
· Stage VIII: CT continued training, 480p/720p / 24fps, 1 million, focused on I2V;
· Followed by SFT → RLHF/DPO. A separate super-resolution module is trained independently on 1K–4K data.
A notable difference: the original version treats long duration as a curriculum axis (65 frames→129 frames), whereas 1.5 keeps a fixed 2–10 seconds throughout with no duration progression, instead using frame rate (16fps→24fps) as a new axis, and offloads the task of increasing resolution to a separate super-resolution module — this is the key trade-off 1.5 makes to keep training costs down for the 8.3B model.

### [InstructAV2AV](../models/InstructAV2AV.md) ⚠️

Adopts a Two-Stage Training Strategy (TSTS), whose division basis is 【modality coupling degree】— first adapting each modality separately to the editing task, then jointly fine-tuning — with the goal of smoothly transferring the pre-training prior to the editing task.

【Stage 1: Modality-specific training】The video branch and the audio branch are optimized independently, without interfering with each other. Training steps are explicitly given: 320K steps for the video branch, 960K steps for the audio branch. The audio branch's step count is 3× that of the video branch, a striking ratio worth noting — combined with the loss weighting in the joint stage, λ_v=0.85 / λ_a=0.15 (the visual weight is about 5.7× the audio weight), one can infer the authors' judgment: the audio branch needs more steps to converge (hence 3× more training in Stage 1), but in the joint stage the audio gradients need to be suppressed so as not to interfere with the visual side (hence a weight of only 0.15). That is, a strategy of "train audio thoroughly on its own first, then let it 'speak less' during the joint stage."

【Stage 2: Joint audio-video training】The unified architecture is fine-tuned as a whole for 400K steps, with the two branches coupled via Ovi's dual-tower interaction mechanism; the loss is λ_v·L_v + λ_a·L_a, with λ_v=0.85, λ_a=0.15 (which the paper calls empirical modality-balancing weights).

【Quantitative evidence of curriculum effectiveness】In the ablation "W/o Two-Stage Training Strategy" (going straight to single-stage joint training): FVD worsens from 180.38 to 291.55 (a 61.6% degradation), TV-A falls from 25.23 to 24.87, and FAD rises from 2.75 to 5.18 (an 88.4% degradation). The paper describes its failure mode as visual distortion and inconsistency. Note that the degradation on the audio metric FAD (88%) is larger than on the visual metric FVD (62%), indicating that the audio branch is more sensitive to curriculum design — consistent with Foley-Omni's finding (in that work, removing the three-stage curriculum caused WER to worsen by nearly 4×, with speech suffering the most). This may reflect a cross-work regularity: in joint audio-video training, modalities that are highly structural and have low error tolerance, such as audio/speech, depend more heavily on prior single-modality pre-training; when trained directly in a mixture, their gradients are easily drowned out by the visual task.

【How the division basis differs from common paradigms】This work's stage division is not based on resolution, duration, or data-quality tiering (there is no "low-res→high-res," "short→long," or "coarse-filter→curated" curriculum), because the data spec is uniformly fixed at 5s/720p/24FPS/16kHz, leaving no gradable dimension. This is a side effect of a synthetic dataset: uniform specs bring engineering simplicity but also foreclose the possibility of a resolution/duration curriculum.

【Hyperparameters and hardware】8× NVIDIA H100, the AdamW optimizer, learning rate 1×10⁻⁵. [Uncertain] Batch size, total training duration, warmup/scheduling strategy, and EMA settings are not disclosed. The three stages total 1.68M steps on 8× H100 — a fairly substantial training volume that should take on the order of weeks in wall-clock time, but the paper does not report it.

### [2026 Other Joint Audio-Video Generation Work](../models/JAVG_2026_misc.md) ⚠️

【ALIVE — a seven-stage curriculum (the longest and most granular in this batch, with audio and audio-video threads intertwined)】
Per the stage table given in the paper:
① T2A Stage I (audio-tower pre-training): 700k hours of transcribed speech / 384M audio samples, 1 epoch, lr 5e-5 — pure speech foundation
② T2A Stage II (audio-tower mixed fine-tuning): 19M samples (5k hours of high-quality speech + 111k hours of video audio tracks), 10 epochs, lr 5e-5 — introduces real soundscapes; note the epoch count jumps from 1 to 10, with the high-quality data reused repeatedly
③ T2VA Joint-Train (joint audio-video training): 11M samples @480p/24fps, 1.2 epochs, lr 1e-4 — the stage with the highest learning rate, where cross-modal connections are established from scratch
④ T2VA+I2VA (adding image conditioning): same 11M samples, 0.3 epochs, lr 5e-5 — a brief capability extension
⑤ Continue-Training (continued training on balanced data): 4.3M balanced samples, 3 epochs, lr 5e-5 — retraining for 3 rounds on a balanced subset adjusted for domain mixture ratio, a key stage for correcting distributional skew
⑥ SFT (supervised fine-tuning): 5M samples, high-aesthetic data and realistic data mixed at 3:1, 0.5 epochs, asymmetric learning rates (video 1e-5 / audio 1e-6, with the audio side an order of magnitude lower to protect the already well-trained audio capability)
⑦ 1080p Refiner (high-definition refinement): 0.7M high-clarity samples, 1 epoch, lr 5e-5
There is also a parallel Character-driven training track (0.8M reference-paired samples).
The curriculum-division basis spans: modality (audio first, then audio-video) → conditioning type (T2VA→+I2VA) → data distribution (raw→balanced) → quality (→high-aesthetic SFT) → resolution (480p→1080p). This is the curriculum design with the most dimensions in this batch. The asymmetric learning rate (video 1e-5 / audio 1e-6) is a detail worth noting: it reflects that the two modalities have different levels of maturity in joint training and require differentiated protection.
【NAVA — three stages, divided by modality mixture ratio】
① Stage 1: audio-only : audio-visual = 3:1 — audio-side dominant, first training the audio-generation capability to be strong
② Stage 2: the ratio flips to 1:2, "train on high-quality audio data together with the full audio-visual dataset" — high-quality audio + the full audio-visual dataset
③ Stage 3: fine-tuned on a curated 160K high-quality audio-visual dataset
Total compute: "Approximately 107,520 H100 GPU-hours" across three stages — the only work in this batch to publicly disclose total compute, which is highly valuable as a reference. At the scale of a 6.3B model + 15M clips, this figure corresponds to roughly 1,120 H100s training for 4 days, a medium industrial scale.
The division basis is purely "modality mixture ratio + data quality," with no resolution or duration curriculum involved.
【CCL — two stages, minimalist】
① Audio-diffusion pre-training: 160,000 steps, batch 3200, lr 1e-4
② Joint audio-video training: cross-modal attention parameters use lr 1e-4, all other parameters use lr 1e-5 (differentiated learning rates — newly added cross-modal modules learn quickly, already-pretrained parts learn slowly); the final setup is batch 320, 12,000 steps; the ablation stage uses batch 128, 6,000 steps
The video stream is initialized from Wan2.1-14B pretrained weights, while the audio stream is built from scratch (architecture similar but with reduced channel dimensions). The entire joint training phase is only 12,000 steps — this is the core support for its "compute-saving" claim: compared with peer works that often use tens of thousands of steps, CCL achieves SOTA with an order of magnitude less training volume. GPU count and total GPU-hours are undisclosed [Uncertain].
【StreamChar — two-stage pre-training + two-stage distillation】
① Pretraining Stage 1 (orchestrator): the Emilia dataset, 80k steps, batch 640, lr 6e-5
② Pretraining Stage 2 (joint training): 100k steps, batch 128, lr 1e-5
③ Distillation Stage I: 600 steps, compressing to a 4-step generator
④ Distillation Stage II: 400 steps, doing consistency fine-tuning under online chunk rollout, student lr 2e-6, fake score network lr 4e-7
The distillation stages total only 1,000 steps, almost negligible relative to the 180,000 steps of pre-training, indicating that distillation is a low-cost final-mile engineering step.
【Baton — a three-stage progressive curriculum built around decoupling the planner and the DiT】
① Stage 1 (VA-Planner pre-training): target continuous features are extracted from the second-to-last layer of a frozen SigLip2 and WavTokenizer, and the planner is trained with L2 regression
② Stage 2 (DiT adaptation): the DiT receives ground-truth features projected through Latent-MLPs, and is trained with a flow-matching loss — deliberately using clean encoder features "to avoid confounding planner prediction noise"
③ Stage 3 (joint fine-tuning): the VA-Planner is frozen while the DiT remains trainable, and the DiT instead receives the planner's predicted features — "bridging the gap between clean features and imperfect predictions"
Totals: 10 epochs, batch 1 per GPU, lr 1e-5. This three-step approach of "train first on clean labels, then switch to model predictions" is essentially a curriculum design for solving exposure bias (analogous to scheduled sampling), and is the most sophisticated piece of curriculum-design logic in this batch.
【OmniCustom】The training-stage division is not detailed [Uncertain]; it employs a combination of LoRA fine-tuning (identity LoRA + timbre LoRA) + a contrastive-learning objective + flow matching, representing lightweight adaptation on an existing base rather than multi-stage pre-training.
【ITS-JAVG】No training curriculum (training-free).

### [Collection of Joint Audio-Video Generation Baselines](../models/JavisDiT_baselines.md) ⚠️

This collection shows a clear convergent trend in training-curriculum design — the three post-2025 works all independently adopt a three-stage paradigm of "audio-only pre-training → joint audio-video training → reinforcement/multi-task":
【MM-Diffusion (no curriculum, but a two-part resolution scheme)】Single-stage joint training, no multi-stage curriculum. The only division is along the spatial scale: the base model is trained at 64×64, with a separate super-resolution model trained independently to upsample to 256×256 (this SR model is initialized from the upsampling model of guided-diffusion). This is the primordial form of a "low-res → high-res" curriculum, but the two models are independent rather than forming a progressive curriculum.
【AV-DiT (no curriculum)】Single-stage training for 100,000 iterations, batch 16, lr 5e-4, the AdamW optimizer, completed on NVIDIA RTX A6000; only the newly inserted adapter layers are trainable, with the DiT backbone frozen. The parameter-efficient route itself substitutes for the role of a curriculum.
【JavisDiT (three stages, divided by "modality → alignment capability → joint generation")】
- Stage 1: audio pre-training — 780,000 audio-text pairs, 55 epochs, establishing the audio branch's T2A capability firmly (the video tower uses Open-Sora, the audio tower uses AudioLDM2, and the VAE is frozen throughout).
- Stage 2: ST-Prior training — 610,000 synchronized triplets + synthesized asynchronous negative samples, training the HiST-Sypo spatiotemporal synchronization prior estimator with contrastive learning. This is a stage dedicated specifically to "learning to judge what counts as synchronized," unique within this collection.
- Stage 3: JAVG training — 610,000 triplets, fine-tuning the cross-attention and bidirectional attention modules.
【JavisDiT++ (three stages, with parameter-count allocation worth recording)】
- Stage 1 audio pre-training: 780,000 audio-text pairs, 50 epochs, training the audio FFN (794M parameters).
- Stage 2 audio-video SFT: 330,000 triplets, 2 epochs, LoRA training (121M parameters).
- Stage 3 AV-DPO: 25,000 preference pairs, 1 epoch, LoRA training (121M parameters).
Note the disparity in epoch counts (50 : 2 : 1) alongside the decline in trainable parameter count (794M → 121M → 121M): audio capability is learned from scratch over many epochs, joint capability is adapted via LoRA over a small number of epochs, and preference alignment needs only 1 epoch — this configuration is highly informative for "how to do joint generation on small data."
【Harmony (three stages, divided by "audio foundation → timbre disentanglement → cross-task joint")】
- Stage 1 base audio pre-training: 100,000 iterations, global batch 1536, maximum clip length 10 seconds, speech and ambient sound mixed 1:1, reference audio a random 1–3 second clip.
- Stage 2 timbre-disentanglement fine-tuning: 20,000 iterations, reference-target pairs mismatched across utterances.
- Stage 3 cross-task audio-video joint training: 10,000 iterations, batch 128, still maintaining a 1:1 data mixture ratio, λv=0.1 (video-driven loss), λa=0.3 (audio-driven loss).
- The learning rate is held constant at 1e-5 throughout. The video branch is initialized from Wan2.2-5B.
【UniAVGen (three stages, divided by "audio → joint → multi-task")】
- Stage 1 audio pre-training: the English subset of Emilia, 160,000 steps, batch 256, lr 2e-5.
- Stage 2 joint training: in-house real human audio-video data, 30,000 steps, batch 32, lr 5e-6.
- Stage 3 multi-task learning: 10,000 steps, five task types mixed at a 4:1:1:2:2 ratio.
【Common regularity (an important conclusion)】JavisDiT++, Harmony, UniAVGen, and the contemporaneous Ovi all adopt the strategy of "first train the audio branch well using large amounts of pure audio data, then use a small amount of paired audio-video data for joint adaptation," and in each case the audio stage's steps/epochs far exceed those of the joint stage. The reason is that high-quality paired audio-video data is scarce while pure audio data is abundant — this has become the de facto standard curriculum in the joint audio-video generation field.
【Common omission】None of the five adopt a progressive curriculum of "low-res→high-res," "short→long," or "image→video"; resolution and duration remain fixed throughout training.

### [LTX-2](../models/LTX-2.md) ⚠️

The training curriculum is partially disclosed, but details of stage division along data dimensions are lacking.
(1) Modality progression: the core idea is to "extend a pre-trained video DiT with an audio stream" — stated in the conclusion as "by extending a pretrained 13B video diffusion transformer with a lightweight 3B audio stream" (the body text and abstract give the spec as 14B video + 5B audio, a discrepancy in figures), connected via bidirectional cross-attention, 1D temporal RoPE, and cross-modality AdaLN, thereby avoiding the need to duplicate the visual backbone; this is followed by "progressive joint training." This is a clear "video first, then audio-video" modality curriculum.
(2) The two-step process for the text projection: the projection matrix W for multi-layer feature extraction is jointly optimized with LTX-2 during a brief initial training stage (with the LLM weights frozen throughout, using a standard diffusion MSE loss), which brings an overall quality improvement; thereafter W is frozen and reused for all subsequent training. The text connector (including thinking tokens) is trained together with the audio-video DiT blocks.
(3) Resolution/duration: rather than a strict low-res→high-res staged progression, multiple resolutions and durations are trained simultaneously + token-count alignment + random token dropping; high resolution is achieved via a multi-scale, multi-tile strategy at inference time (0.5MP base → latent upsampling → tile refinement) rather than a training curriculum.
(4) Image participation: images are mixed into training as a "resolution-duration combination" alongside video.
(5) Predecessor LTX-Video: after pre-training, fine-tuned on a high-aesthetic subset.

### [LongCat-Video](../models/LongCat-Video.md)

Pre-training follows a strict five-stage progressive curriculum, whose scheduling basis simultaneously spans three axes — "modality (image→video), task (single-task→multi-task), resolution (low-res→high-res)" — while the duration axis stays fixed at 93 frames:
· Stage 1: T2I only, 256p, lr 1e-4, 285k iterations — using pure images to first establish visual priors and text alignment, accounting for about 42% of total iterations, the heaviest investment;
· Stage 2: T2I + T2V, 256p × 93 frames, lr 1e-4, 140k iterations — introducing video, with images still mixed in to keep image quality stable;
· Stage 3: T2I + T2V + I2V + VC, all tasks, 256p × 93 frames, lr 5e-5, 164k iterations — filling out all four task types at low resolution first, with the learning rate halved;
· Stage 4: all tasks, 480p × 93 frames, lr 5e-5, 36k iterations — raising resolution, with iteration count shrinking sharply;
· Stage 5: all tasks, mixed 480p + 720p × 93 frames, lr 2e-5, 53k iterations — mixed dual-resolution training, with the learning rate lowered further.
This is followed by SFT (480p+720p × 93 frames, lr 1e-5, 7.5k iterations) and RLHF/GRPO (480p+720p × 93 frames, about 0.5k iterations).
Overall this presents a pyramid-shaped allocation of iterations: "a large volume of low-res images to lay the foundation → full-task rollout at low resolution → a small amount of high-res refinement → a very small amount of SFT/RL alignment." Task unification is achieved via "conditioning-frame count": T2V has no conditioning frames, I2V takes 1 reference frame as input, and VC takes multiple preceding frames as input, allowing the four task types to be trained mixed within the same batch. In addition, the generation side uses a spatiotemporal dual-axis coarse-to-fine strategy, combined with Block Sparse Attention and distillation, speeding up inference by about 10.1×.

### [MOVA](../models/MOVA.md)

Two major stages with three joint-training phases, whose curriculum division basis simultaneously spans three axes — **modality, data quality, and resolution**:
【Stage A: Audio-tower pre-training】An 1.3B text-to-audio DiT is trained separately (Wan2.1-1.3B backbone, with 3D positional encoding replaced by a 1D temporal axis), on data covering music, general sound effects, and TTS, using fixed-length clips, with each clip paired with a text prompt containing an explicit duration token to control the target length. The audio VAE is frozen.
【Stage B: Progressive joint training (three phases)】The video tower is initialized from Wan2.2 A14B, the audio tower from Stage A, and the Bridge is randomly initialized; all three are jointly optimized end-to-end from the very first step (the paper notes that in early experiments, the two-step alternative of "first freeze both towers and train the Bridge, then fully fine-tune" plateaued very early, hence the switch to end-to-end).
- Phase 1 (360×640, diverse data, ~61,500 hours, 1 epoch, 15 days): asymmetric sigma shift (video 5.0 aggressive denoising / audio 1.0 gentle), with aggressive text dropout p=0.5 forcing the model to rely on the Bridge to learn cross-modal alignment rather than take the text shortcut. Goal: rapidly establish basic synchronization capability.
- Phase 2 (360×640, quality-filtered data, ~37,600 hours / 16.8M clips, 1 epoch, 7 days): the audio sigma shift is aligned to 5.0 to strengthen audio denoising and improve timbre fidelity; text dropout is reduced to 0.2 to allow text-guided semantic refinement; LUFS loudness normalization is introduced. Goal: refine consistency and timbre under stable cross-modal attention.
- Phase 3 (720×1280, highest-quality subset, ~11,000 hours, 1 epoch, 20 days): with cross-modal alignment already stabilized, the model can safely allocate capacity to higher resolution and finer spatial detail without disrupting the already-learned synchronization structure. CP increases from 8 to 16, batch size drops from 128 to 64, and the checkpoint interval shortens from every 5000 steps to every 2000 steps.
【Quantitative validation of curriculum effectiveness】Figure 9 gives the full curve of LSE-C / LSE-D against training steps (0→400K), colored by stage: in Stage 1, LSE-D drops rapidly while LSE-C rises (rapidly learning the basic synchronization pattern); in Stage 2, LSE-D continues to drop while LSE-C shows a marked jump (improvement in consistency and confidence); in Stage 3, LSE-D drops further and plateaus while LSE-C stabilizes at a high level (converging to high-quality lip sync). This is direct evidence that "progressively tightening the data curriculum → monotonic improvement in synchronization metrics."
【Three axes of curriculum design】Resolution (360p→360p→720p), data quality (diverse→quality-filtered→highest-quality), and noise scheduling and text dropout (exploratory→refinement-oriented) — the three co-evolve, rather than only resolution being tuned.

### [Merged entry: ① Mochi 1](../models/Mochi_MAGI_Motif.md) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: the training curriculum is not disclosed. Only known that the model is "trained from scratch," with full 3D attention covering a context window of 44,520 video tokens, outputting 480p; the official party stated that a 720p HD version would follow (it was in fact never fully open-sourced on schedule). [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1 (three stages, scheduling axis = resolution + duration + data quality + caption type, Table 5):
· stage-1: resolution 256p/360p, video duration ≤8 seconds, image:video = 4:1, AR caption share 0%.
· stage-2: resolution 480p, duration ≤8 seconds, image:video = 4:1, AR caption share 10%.
· stage-3: resolution 720p, duration ≤16 seconds, image:video = 4:1, AR caption share 10%.
The paper explicitly states that "as resolution increases, data volume gradually decreases, and a stricter filtering strategy is adopted to ensure higher data quality"; in the final stage the duration cap is relaxed from 8 seconds to 16 seconds, letting the model capture richer temporal dynamics. Notably, MAGI-1's image:video ratio stays constant at 4:1 across all three stages (not adjusted by stage), while AR captions are introduced only from the second stage onward and held fixed at 10% — i.e., "autoregressive controllability" is threaded through the middle and later stages as a constant small-proportion auxiliary task, rather than being progressively ramped up.
③ Motif-Video 2B (ten stages, scheduling axis = modality + resolution + frame count + text encoder + REPA teacher + architectural changes, Table 1 — the most complex and most "non-linear" curriculum in this group):
· Stage 1: T2I, 144p, 1 frame, sentence-embedding encoder, REPA teacher DINOv2 — spatial bootstrap, initializing the spatial generation pathway before introducing temporal complexity.
· Stage 2: T2IV, 144p, 33 frames, sentence embedding, REPA teacher switched to V-JEPA.
· Stage 3: T2IV, 144p, 65 frames, sentence embedding, V-JEPA.
· Stage 4: T2IV, 360p, 65 frames, text encoder switched to T5Gemma2, REPA turned off (rationale: after early convergence, representation alignment becomes counterproductive).
· Stage 5: T2IV, 480p, 65 frames — first taking a "resolution bridge": before introducing 480p video, first jointly train on 360p video and 480p images, letting the model acquire high-resolution spatial features from the cheaper images before the temporal pathway adapts to the increased token count.
· Stage 6: T2IV, 480p, 121 frames.
· Stage 7: T2IV, 480p, 121 frames — the first SFT.
· Stage 8: T2IV, 720p, 121 frames — a key unconventional decision: starting from the 480p SFT checkpoint (rather than the 480p pre-training checkpoint).
· Stage 9: T2IV, 360p, 121 frames — resolution falls back, introducing Shared Cross-Attention for cross-attention refinement (because semantic alignment was observed to regress in the 720p stage).
· Stage 10: T2IV, 720p, 121 frames — final pre-training + second SFT.
Two design elements run throughout: all video stages are jointly trained with image-text data (image samples stabilize per-frame visual quality and reinforce semantic grounding, while video samples drive temporal modeling); the prompt dropout probability for classifier-free guidance training is fixed at p=0.1, with no modifications made to the noise schedule or loss weighting.
The most notable aspect of the Motif curriculum is that it openly acknowledges it is not a single straight run — "treating training as a diagnostic loop rather than a one-time pass through a predefined curriculum," with the resolution fallback and mid-course architectural insertion at Stage 9 serving as evidence; as does the assumption behind starting Stage 8 from the SFT checkpoint (SFT has already concentrated density onto the high-quality manifold, so the 720p stage thereby inherits a cleaner starting distribution and can allocate capacity to resolution adaptation rather than simultaneously having to remedy pre-training quality deficits); the paper honestly notes that this decision "was not validated against a control ablation starting from the pre-training checkpoint," and is reported only as a pragmatic recipe decision.

### [Movie Gen](../models/Movie_Gen.md)

· Movie Gen Video's three-step curriculum (with 3 progressively stricter data subsets on the data side): ① first train pure text-to-image (T2I) as a warmup, then jointly train T2I + T2V (training jointly from scratch directly converges markedly more slowly, with both visual and temporal quality worse at equal GPU-hours); ② progressive resolution, training from 256px low resolution up to 768px high resolution, with the data switching from "the low-res set with a minimum side of 720px" to "the high-res set with a minimum side of 768px"; ③ continued training with an improved dataset and optimization recipe (continuous training under compute- and time-constrained conditions). · PT2V three stages: Stage-I identity injection (truncating the TAE embedding to 8 latent frames, i.e. 64 RGB frames, training only the backbone with the visual encoder frozen, using paired samples for fast identity injection) → Stage-II expanding to long video → Stage-III using cross-paired (real + synthetic) samples to eliminate copy-paste effects and improve the naturalness of expressions and motion.
· Movie Gen Edit three stages: single-frame editing multi-task → synthetic multi-frame editing → backtranslation video editing.
· Movie Gen Audio two stages: pre-training (sequence cap of 30 seconds/750 tokens, batch of 1536 sequences, 500K steps, 384 GPUs × 14 days, constant lr 1e-4 + 5K-step warmup) → fine-tuning (batch 256, 50K steps, 64 GPUs × 1 day, lr linearly ramped up to 1e-4 then linearly decayed to 1e-8, EMA decay 0.999).

### [NVIDIA NeMo Curator](../models/NeMo_Curator.md)

As data infrastructure, it does not define a training curriculum, but its output format is built to serve curriculum-based training, which is its most tightly coupled design point with the training side:
【Bucketing as the curriculum interface】In the sharding stage, clips are packed into different WebDataset shards along three dimensions — "resolution × aspect ratio × duration" — with the official documentation explicitly stating the purpose is to "align with the training curriculum": downstream training frameworks use this to implement progressive low-res→high-res, short→long curricula simply by switching which shard buckets are read, with no need to reprocess the data.
【Quality scores stored alongside the data】Aesthetic scores, motion scores, genre tags, and other discriminative results are saved together with the clip in the metadata, allowing the training side to dynamically filter subsets by quality score (e.g., taking only high-aesthetic samples during an annealing stage) without rerunning the pipeline.
【Coordination with NeMo on the training side】NeMo Framework's video foundation model training stack (arXiv:2503.12964) interfaces directly with the WebDatasets output by Curator, supporting sequential multi-GPU reading of petabyte-scale data.
The specific division basis and switching criteria for each stage are determined by the user's training configuration; Curator does not prescribe them.

### [OmniHuman Dataset + OHBench](../models/OmniHuman.md) ⚠️

This entry is a dataset and evaluation benchmark rather than a model, and does not include its own training-curriculum design. Relevant information can only be indirectly observed from its sole validation experiment and its data structure:
【Training setup of the validation experiment】A 20% subset of OmniHuman (180,000 single-person + 20,000 two-person = 200,000 samples) is used to fine-tune LTX-2, a single-stage SFT fine-tune with no multi-stage curriculum, no resolution progression, and no duration progression. The paper does not disclose the specific hyperparameters, training steps, or compute resources for this fine-tune. [Uncertain]
【The data structure's capacity to support curriculum design】Although the dataset itself does not prescribe a curriculum, its annotation system provides a rich basis for users to build their own curricula:
- Quality-based stratification: CLIP aesthetic score, DOVER score, face-clarity score Cs, and speech-quality annotations are all queryable fields, from which high-quality subsets can be carved out for annealing or SFT;
- Complexity-based stratification: single-person → two-person → human-object interaction forms a natural difficulty progression, the most natural curriculum dimension in this dataset;
- Resolution/duration-based stratification: the metadata contains fps, duration, and resolution, supporting the classic low-res→high-res, short→long curriculum;
- Modality-based stratification: the vocal track and background track are separated, supporting a modality progression of "learn speech sync first, then the full soundscape";
- Shot-language-based stratification: shot-scale and camera-movement labels support progression from simple fixed shots to complex camera movement.
This pattern — "the dataset provides stratified metadata, and the curriculum is assembled by the user" — is a reasonable positioning for dataset-type work.
【A curriculum clue discovered via evaluation】The evaluation shows that "the model's performance drops on shot transitions from close-up to wide shot" and that "identity distortion is more severe in two-person scenes" — these two findings directly point to where curriculum design should focus effort (shot-scale diversity and multi-person complexity), demonstrating the dataset's diagnostic capability.

### [Open-Sora Series](../models/Open-Sora.md)

Both projects adopt a clear multi-stage progressive curriculum, with **data stratification tightly bound to the training stages** — the core of their low-cost strategy:
【Open-Sora 2.0 (three stages, corresponding one-to-one with the data pyramid)】Stage 1: 70M videos, 256px, pure T2V, 85k iterations, 224 GPUs; Stage 2: 10M videos, 256px, mixed T2V+I2V, 13k iterations, 192 GPUs; Stage 3: 5M videos, 768px, T2V+I2V, 13k iterations, 192 GPUs. 【Open-Sora 1.2】Stage 1, 30k steps: Webvid-10M, 240p/360p, 2–16 seconds; Stage 2, 23k steps: the Panda-70M subset with aesthetic score >4.5 (41k hours), 360p/480p; Stage 3, 15k steps: about 2M high-quality clips (5k hours), 720p/1080p, with 25% masking training. 68k steps in total.

### [Ovi](../models/Ovi.md) ⚠️

A three-stage curriculum, whose division basis is "modality → duration → modality fusion," rather than the common resolution or quality-score axes:
【Stage 1 | Audio pre-training (Ovi-Aud pretraining)】The audio tower is trained from scratch (architecture copied from Wan2.2 5B), on hundreds of thousands of hours of variable-length raw audio up to 12 seconds long, dominated by human speech. The goal is to firmly establish long-range modeling of speaker identity, pitch, emotion, and prosody. 50k steps, batch 2880, lr 1e-4, AdamW(β1=0.9, β2=0.999, ε=1e-8). A key engineering detail: scaled RoPE is applied to all attention layers from the start of pre-training, thereby avoiding re-adaptation when entering the audio-video stage and avoiding the need to maintain multiple audio RoPE scales.
【Stage 2 | Audio fine-tuning】Switches to waveforms padded to an exact 5.04 seconds (aligned to 121 frames @24fps), mixing in sound-effect data from VGGSound/AudioSet/WavCaps and audio tracks extracted from in-house audio-video data, so that the audio distribution matches downstream fusion training while also supplementing sound-effect capability and keeping TTS capability alive.
【Stage 3 | Audio-video fusion fine-tuning】The pre-trained audio tower is spliced together with the Wan2.2 5B video tower, with the cross-modal attention layers initialized from scratch and all FFNs frozen to save memory (5.7B of the 11B total parameters are trainable — training only the single-modality self-attention and two types of cross-attention: text-to-modality and modality-to-modality). 40k steps, batch 768, lr 5e-5, AdamW(β1=0.9, β2=0.95, ε=1e-8). The loss is a weighted sum of the flow-matching losses of both modalities, λ_v=0.85, λ_a=0.15, with shared timesteps but independent noise.
【Subsequent (Ovi 1.1)】The README Todo list has already checked off "Finetune model with higher resolution data, and RL for performance improvement" and "Longer video generation (10s)" — i.e., higher-resolution (960×960 native) fine-tuning, RL optimization, and duration-extension stages have been added, constituting a de facto "low-res short clips → high-res long clips" curriculum; the data and training details for this stage are undisclosed [Uncertain].
【Not adopted】An image→video progressive curriculum, a low-res→high-res multi-resolution curriculum (the original version stays at a fixed 720×720-equivalent area throughout), and a quality-score-based tiered curriculum schedule all do not appear in the paper.

### [Script-a-Video](../models/Script-a-Video.md) ⚠️

The training curricula for the captioning side and the generation side differ enormously:
【Captioning side: single stage, no curriculum】Qwen3-Omni-MTSS-FT is obtained by simply doing one round of supervised fine-tuning on the open-source Qwen3-Omni-Instruct using 500K MTSS data. No multi-stage, no resolution/duration/quality-tiered curriculum design. The paper uses the phrasing "we simply performed supervised fine-tuning" to emphasize its simplicity — this itself is part of the argument: no complicated training tricks are needed; a change in the data representation format alone brings a large improvement.
【Generation side: a four-stage progressive curriculum, divided by "capability dimension" rather than "resolution/duration"】This is the most distinctive aspect of this work's training-curriculum design — the stage division is not based on the traditional low-res→high-res, short→long, or image→video progressions, but on the capabilities corresponding to each of the four MTSS streams:
Stage 1 · ID Customization: a 400K identity-centric dataset, 3 epochs, 128 GPUs. The goal is to anchor persistent character and environment features, corresponding to the capability of the Reference stream.
Stage 2 · Multi-shot Control: 250K multi-shot sequences, 1.5 epochs, 32 GPUs. The goal is to fine-tune the shot-aware cross-attention mechanism, corresponding to the capability of the Shot stream. Note that this stage has the fewest GPUs (32) and the fewest epochs (1.5), the lightest investment among the four stages.
Stage 3 · Audio-Visual Synergy: 870K cinematic pairs, 3 epochs, a 400-GPU cluster. The goal is explicitly stated as sub-frame alignment for the Gemma Connectors, corresponding to the capability of the Event stream. This stage has both the largest data volume (870K) and the largest compute investment (400 GPUs), indicating that audio-video alignment is regarded as the hardest capability.
Stage 4 · Joint Fine-tuning: 15K steps, 64 GPUs, using an interleaved mix of 60K high-fidelity cinematic-grade alignment pairs + 250K multi-shot sequences. The goal is to simultaneously coordinate identity stability, narrative coherence, and temporal synchronization within a single generation pass, ensuring that the multiple constraints do not interfere with each other ("without mutual interference").
【Summary of the curriculum logic】The first three stages are "staged optimization for individual tasks," while the fourth stage is "joint coordination of multiple capabilities." This is a training design isomorphic to MTSS's stream-decomposition idea — first mastering the capability corresponding to each stream separately, then re-coordinating them, echoing the representational design of Stream Factorization + Relational Grounding.
【Undisclosed】The learning rate, batch size, optimizer, GPU model, resolution/duration configuration, and total training duration/GPU-hours for each stage are all not given. [Uncertain]

### [SpeakerVid-5M](../models/SpeakerVid-5M.md)

### [Step-Video-T2V](../models/Step-Video-T2V.md)

### [UniTalking](../models/UniTalking.md) ⚠️

【Stage 2: Multi-task joint audio-video training】
- End-to-end training of the entire framework;
- Data: 2.3 million audio-video samples;

The authors attribute this to the imbalance in modality convergence rates — the video branch benefits from large-scale pre-training while the audio branch is relatively undertrained. This is the only ablation in the paper with a clear conclusion, though no quantitative figures are given. [Uncertain]
【Comparison with control works】UniVerse-1 has no curriculum at all (single-stage flat training), because both of its experts are already pre-trained; MOVA uses three stages to realize a "decreasing scale, increasing quality" pyramid curriculum; UniTalking's two-stage curriculum is essentially about "filling in an audio tower trained from scratch," a different nature from MOVA's quality pyramid.

### [UniVerse-1](../models/UniVerse-1.md)

UniVerse-1 has no multi-stage training curriculum — one of its most notable structural differences from comparable work:
【Single training stage】All 7,685 hours of data are mixed and trained in a single stage, totaling 50k steps, with an effective batch size of 128, gradient accumulation of 4 steps, the AdamW optimizer, a learning rate of 5e-6 (constant, with no mention of warmup or decay schedule), and FSDP multi-node distributed training. There is no resolution curriculum (no low-res→high-res), no duration curriculum (no short→long), no quality curriculum (no coarse-filter→fine-filter), and no modality curriculum (no image→video).
【The curriculum is replaced by "expert inheritance"】The fundamental reason no curriculum is used is the SoE paradigm itself: the unimodal capabilities of video and audio have already been fully established through the respective pre-training of Wan2.1 and Ace-step, so UniVerse-1 only needs to learn the single thing of "cross-modal alignment," and therefore does not need to build up visual capability progressively starting from low resolution. This can be viewed as shifting the upfront cost of curriculum learning onto the two base models — SoE's core selling point is precisely "bypassing training from scratch" to improve training efficiency.
【The only "grading" is not on the time axis but on the noise axis】The LQLS strategy applies differentiated losses by diffusion timestep for data from different sources: VGGSound/AudioSet participate in the Flow Matching loss computation only when t>800 (out of 1000 total steps). This is effectively a kind of "noise-level curriculum" — low-quality data contributes supervision only at the coarse-structure stage, while high-quality data covers the full noise range. This is an interesting design that substitutes the noise dimension for the time dimension in grading data, but it holds constant throughout the entire training process and does not change as training progresses, so it does not constitute a curriculum schedule in the traditional sense.
【Comparison with MOVA】MOVA uses three stages (61.5k→37.6k→11k hours, 360p→360p→720p) to realize a "decreasing scale, increasing quality" pyramid curriculum; UniVerse-1, by contrast, is single-stage flat training + data grading on the noise axis. The former is suited to gradually climbing up from a weaker base model, while the latter is suited to scenarios that already have a strong base model and only need alignment.

### [Unison](../models/Unison.md) ⚠️

Unison has two nested layers of stage division that must be understood separately — the outer layer is a two-stage training scheme divided by modality, while the inner layer is a three-stage curriculum of cross-modal forcing.
【Outer layer: two-stage training (by modality)】
- Stage 1 (audio-branch unimodal training): 4× NVIDIA H100, batch size 96, learning rate with a 1k-step linear warmup, and step decay at 240k and 270k steps (the decay coefficient is given by formula in the paper). Since the decay points are at 240k/270k, one can infer this stage's training steps exceed 270,000, the bulk of the training volume, consuming 130,000 hours of pure-audio corpus, with the goal of giving the MMAudio + Zipformer audio branch the ability to generate all four types — speech, sound effects, music, and singing;
- Stage 2 (joint fine-tuning): 16× H100, bf16 precision, ZeRO-2 optimization, batch size 32, fine-tuning the coupled system with the TI2AV objective, consuming 3,000+ hours of audio-video corpus. The key constraint is that only the audio branch and the fusion module (bidirectional cross-attention and layer normalization) are trained, while the video backbone Wan2.2-5B is frozen throughout. The paper does not disclose the number of training steps for this stage. [Uncertain]
【Nature of the outer-layer curriculum: a modality curriculum, not a resolution/duration/quality curriculum】This is a classic "unimodal first, then cross-modal" two-step process, different from both MOVA's three-stage resolution curriculum (360p→360p→720p, 61.5k→37.6k→11k hours) and UniVerse-1's single-stage flat training. Unison has no resolution curriculum (the video backbone is frozen, so it's moot), no duration curriculum, and no quality curriculum (no tiering by quality score), and no image→video curriculum. The data-curriculum scheduling basis has only a single dimension: "modality."
【Inner layer: a three-stage progressive curriculum of cross-modal forcing (Progressive Training Strategy)】This is the work's true curriculum-learning innovation, operating inside Stage 2, with the scheduling target being "the degree of decoupling between the two modalities' denoising timesteps" rather than data:
- Phase I Synchronous Warmup: forces video and audio to share the same timestep (t_v = t_a), establishing basic alignment;
- Phase II Incremental Decoupling: independent sampling is activated with probability p, while an upper bound is placed on the timestep difference between the two; from this phase onward, a direction-aware loss reweighting is enabled — the video loss is up-weighted when audio is cleaner, and the audio loss is prioritized when video is cleaner;
- Phase III Full Independence: unconstrained decoupled updates.
The phase proportions are 0.3 / 0.4 / 0.3. The design motivation is that directly optimizing fully independent timesteps causes training instability due to excessively large cross-modal noise differences and random oscillation in guidance direction.
【Quantitative evidence of curriculum effectiveness (Table 3)】Comparison of three scheduling schemes:
- SyncOnly (synchronous timesteps throughout): VA 3.90, PQ 6.10, LSE-C 3.12, DS 0.17 — worst across the board, proving that unified noise prevents the model from exploiting denoising asymmetry;
- IndepOnly (fully decoupled from the start, no warmup): VA 3.95, PQ 6.18, LSE-C 3.28, DS 0.14 — alignment improves but optimization is unstable, with LSE-C capped at 3.28;
- ProgForcing (this paper's three-stage curriculum): VA 4.02, PQ 6.34, LSE-C 3.30, DS 0.08 — best across all metrics.
This set of ablations clearly demonstrates that a progressive transition is indispensable for both "stable optimization" and "precise temporal alignment," directly supporting the value of this work's curriculum design.

### [Post-Training Data Strategy for Video Generation](../models/post_training_data.md)

【The anchor paper's four-stage curriculum is its core contribution】Phase 1 SFT → Phase 2 RLHF(GRPO) → Phase 3 PE → Phase 4 AD. The dependency relationships among the four stages are argued fairly clearly: SFT provides a stable reference policy for RLHF and expands the exploration space; PE shares the same reward loop as RLHF but acts on the input side, the two being complementary; AD transfers the capabilities from the first three stages into a causal architecture. AD is further subdivided into three substages: ① DMD distillation — first distilling the original pre-trained model into a bidirectional student model that needs only a few denoising steps, retaining the full global-attention receptive field, providing a high-quality, easily regressed teacher trajectory for the subsequent transfer to the causal architecture; ② Causal ODE regression — directly training the causal student with the DMD loss would be unstable due to architectural differences, so an efficient initialization strategy is introduced along with a block-causal mask, training the model to perform effective denoising prediction based solely on causal history; ③ Self-Forcing distillation — each frame is generated via autoregressive rollout with a KV cache based on the model's own previously self-generated outputs, thereby applying the DMD loss at the video level, directly assessing the quality of the entire sequence.
【The specific curriculum design of GRPO (the anchor paper's technical highlight)】Following DanceGRPO, flow-matching sampling is formulated as an MDP, with sparse rewards given only at the terminal step. For video generation specifically, the authors point out that MixGRPO experiences reward collapse when the random subset is small, and so, inspired by Flash-GRPO, adopt "isotemporal grouping" — each prompt is assigned a different timestep t_i, and during denoising each prompt group performs an ODE→SDE conversion only at its assigned timestep, using SDE sampling at that step to support exploration and gradient computation, while all other timesteps use deterministic ODE updates to produce higher-quality generations and more reliable reward signals. They also introduce "Temporal Gradient Rectification," which explicitly normalizes the time-dependent scaling factor λ(t)=√Δt/σ_t + σ_t√Δt(1−t)/(2t). Following DanceGRPO, the KL regularization term is omitted.
【Cross-work curriculum comparison】HunyuanVideo 1.5's three-segment CT→SFT→RLHF, with T2V/I2V kept fully separate throughout; SkyReels-V2's 540p concept-balanced SFT→720p high-quality SFT→three-stage DPO; Cosmos-Predict 2.5's five-domain parallel SFT→model merging→GRPO→rCM distillation; Motif-Video 2B inserting SFT into the middle of the resolution ladder (the 720p pre-training starts from the 480p SFT checkpoint); Seedance 1.0's multi-submodel SFT→model merging→RLHF; LongCat Avatar 1.5 extending GRPO from video-level rewards to per-frame rewards, with multi-segment rollout supporting up to 5 clips where only the final segment participates in optimization, distilled to 8 steps with DMD2.

### [Survey Merging Mainstream Video Pre-training Datasets: Panda-70M, InternVid, Koala…](../models/pretraining_datasets.md) ⚠️

**The seven are datasets rather than training frameworks, so they do not themselves define a training curriculum**; multi-stage scheduling is left to downstream users to implement. But three data-side designs relevant to curricula are worth recording:
1. **MiraData's four nested subsets (330K/93K/42K/9K) are essentially a ready-made data curriculum** — tiered by increasing stringency of thresholds on color/aesthetics/motion, letting users directly implement a progressive curriculum of "large and loose at the bottom to lay the foundation → small and strict at the top for the finishing touches," with no need to build their own stratification. 2. **UltraVideo's short/long split** (3–10 seconds, 42K clips / >10 seconds, 17K clips) naturally supports a "short→long" duration curriculum.
3. **Panda-70M's 10M/2M subsets and InternVid's FLT/DIV/AES subsets** likewise provide quality tiering, but at a coarser granularity (2–3 tiers each).
【The training curriculum of the accompanying models (as corroborating evidence)】UltraWan is the model, among the seven's accompanying models, with the most complete curriculum information: LoRA fine-tuning of Wan-T2V-1.3B, with UltraWan-1K at 1088×1920×81 frames (rank 64) and UltraWan-4K at 2160×3840×29 frames (rank 16); the LoRA modules = self-attention QKV + output linear layer + the 1st and 3rd linear layers of the FFN; AdamW betas(0.9, 0.999), weight_decay 1e-2, **lr 1e-4, only 1 epoch**, batch 1 per GPU, no tensor parallelism, trained on **H20**, with 1K taking **3.4K GPU-hours** and 4K taking **7.6K GPU-hours**. The authors admit that UltraWan-4K is noticeably undertrained because it is **trained for only 1 epoch and limited to 29 frames by memory constraints**, with more artifacts in the main subject than the 1K version. The LVD-2M experiments use a two-step process of "pre-train on short clips first, then fine-tune on LVD-2M for frame-count extension" (32 frames→65 frames).
**Shared gaps**: none of the seven **define a "low-res→high-res" resolution curriculum** (since each has a single resolution), nor do any **provide an official data-mixture-ratio scheme organized by training stage**. [Uncertain]
## Changes in data mixture ratios across training stages (pretraining / annealing / SFT high-quality subsets)

`stage_data_mixture` · Detail level: brief

### [Allegro](../models/Allegro.md)

Allegro's "mixture ratio" is not a weighted blend of multiple sources, but a nested quality stratification of a single corpus: the same batch of data, filtered through 7 levels, is cut into 4 nested subsets ranging from loose to strict thresholds, each containing the next; each training stage uses only the full data of its corresponding subset, with no cross-subset ratio mixing, and the paper does not describe the mixing ratio of image and video data during the T2V stage.
Full comparison of thresholds across stages (Table 1):
· Duration: — / [2s,16s] / [2s,16s] and [6s,16s] / [6s,16s]
· Resolution: W≥640,H≥368 / same as left / W≥1280,H≥720 / same as 720p
· Brightness: [20,180], constant across all four stages
· DOVER clarity: — / — / — / ≥0.07 (fine-tuning only)
· LPIPS: — / — / — / ≥0.05 (fine-tuning only)
· UniMatch motion: — / — / — / [1.0,100] (fine-tuning only)
· Aesthetic score: ≥4.8 / ≥4.8 / ≥5.0 / ≥5.3
· Text area ratio: ≤0.05%, constant across all four stages
· CLIP similarity: ≥0.17 / ≥0.17 / ≥0.20 / ≥0.20
That is, the pretraining stage mainly relies on three yardsticks — "aesthetics + resolution + image-text consistency" — while the fine-tuning stage additionally stacks on three more expensive yardsticks: "clarity + temporal consistency + motion magnitude." This is a clear cost–quality trade-off design: the expensive metrics are computed only after the data volume has already been reduced by two orders of magnitude.

### [Apollo](../models/Apollo.md) ⚠️

【Mechanism level】There is clearly a stage-wise data mixture adjustment, and it is **metric-driven adaptive rebalancing**: the core action of Stage II is to "adaptively rebalance data distributions across scenarios and tasks" — dynamically adjusting the mixture along two axes, scenario (single speaker / multi-speaker / singing / natural sound) and task (T2A/T2V/T2AV/I2V/I2AV), based on evaluation metrics: whichever capability is weak gets more of that type of data added, while constraining against damaging existing capabilities. This "closed-loop mixture adjustment driven by evaluation feedback" is the most valuable methodological statement in Apollo's training design.
【Annealing/curation level】Stage III is essentially the annealing stage, switching the data entirely to a manually curated high-quality subset.
【Numbers level】All missing: the data volume at each stage, the mixture numbers of the four audio scenarios, the sampling weights of the five task types, the probability distribution of the masking strategy, and the magnitude/frequency of rebalancing adjustments are all undisclosed. Figure 5 "Ablations of different training stages" graphically shows the stage-by-stage improvement across four metric groups (video/audio/TTS/audio-video consistency), but does not annotate the corresponding data mixture ratios. [Uncertain] (mechanism is clear, numbers are entirely missing)

### [CineDance / CineDance-1M](../models/CineDance.md) ⚠️

【Known mixture changes】The change in data mixture is mainly reflected in the progression along the "narrative complexity" dimension rather than in modality or quality mixture: Stage 1 uses a subset of short sequences with 2–3 shots / 10–12 seconds, Stage 2 switches to a subset of longer sequences with up to 8 shots / about 30 seconds. Since CineDance-1M averages 24.2 shots / 92.8 seconds, both training stages in fact use sub-clips truncated from the full sequences, never using the full length.
【Supporting infrastructure for subset construction】The dataset stores all quality scores (DOVER aesthetic/technical, AMT motion, DNSMOS, CLAP variance, ImageBind, SyncNet) and structured fields in full as metadata; the paper emphasizes that this allows users to "flexibly construct task-specific subsets," meaning the infrastructure for mixture-ratio adjustment is fully in place.
【Undisclosed】The specific sample counts and proportions at each stage, whether there is an annealing stage, whether there is an SFT stage using a high-quality subset, the mixing ratio of image-text data to video data, and the supplementation of audio-only data. The paper provides almost no quantitative description of the training data mixture. [Uncertain]

### [CogVideoX](../models/CogVideoX.md) ⚠️

· Resolution dimension: as stages progress, training data switches step by step from 256px to 512px to 768px (aspect ratio preserved, only the short side is resized); low-resolution data dominates absolutely in the early stages.
· Duration dimension: stage1/2 are capped at ≤6s, stage3/4 are relaxed to ≤10s — longer clips only enter in the later stages.
· Quality dimension: the thresholds for optical-flow score and aesthetic score are dynamically tightened during training (dynamically adjust their threshold during training), forming an implicit data curriculum in which "quality monotonically improves with stage."
· Annealing/fine-tuning stage: the final stage (stage4 FT, 10k steps) uses only the top 20% highest-quality subset, specifically targeting the removal of subtitles, watermarks, and low-bitrate data. The paper honestly reports the cost: this step "effectively removed subtitles and watermarks in the generated results, and slightly improved visual quality, but at the same time a slight degradation in the model's semantic ability was observed" — this is a rare piece of negative evidence regarding "fine-tuning on a small high-quality dataset sacrifices semantic coverage."
· Image-text mixing ratio: 2B images and 35M video clips are trained jointly, but the specific batch-level mixture ratio is undisclosed [uncertain].

### [Cosmos-Predict2.5](../models/Cosmos-Predict25.md) ⚠️

The most notable point of the mixture strategy is that the paper explicitly chooses "no mixture-ratio tuning," instead using "independent SFT per domain + post-hoc model merging" to avoid mixture-ratio hyperparameter tuning:
【Pretraining side】Images and video are jointly trained (the Text2Image task runs through all five stages), but the sampling ratio between images and video is undisclosed; task mixture is implicitly controlled via the probability distribution over the number of conditioning frames (Stage 5: 0/1/2 frames = 0.5/0.25/0.25). At the data level, the four-axis sharding structure (content type / resolution / aspect ratio / length) supports "efficient sampling, curriculum-based training, and fine-grained domain balancing," meaning the ability to control the mixture is built into the data organization itself, but specific mixture numbers are not published.
【Explicit design trade-off on the SFT side】"We fine-tune a separate model for each domain rather than training a single model jointly across all domains. This domain-specific strategy enables us to fully leverage the available data without the need to balance mixture ratios across a combined dataset." — Fine-tuning a separate model for each domain rather than joint training, with the explicit rationale being "no need to balance mixture ratios on a combined dataset." The measured result is that domain-specific SFT significantly improves in-domain performance while causing only slight degradation on the general domain, and this degradation can be further compensated by subsequent model merging. This effectively transforms the "data mixture problem" into a "parameter-space merging problem," and is the most inspiring judgment in this work's data engineering.
【Merging as mixture ratio】Model merging is used to unify the domain-specific SFT models with the 4K cooldown model; four methods were tried — model soup, TIES, DARE-Linear, DARE-TIES — with hyperparameter sweeps for each producing 20+ merged models in total; the best is first selected on a small, manually curated set of hard samples, then verified via human preference voting on a larger evaluation set. Conclusion: apart from DARE-Linear, the methods perform comparably, and model soup was ultimately chosen for its simplicity and effectiveness. Another valuable finding is that "simple grid search over hyperparameters consistently outperforms heuristic selection based on individual fine-tuned models' win rates" — hyperparameter grid search is consistently superior to heuristic weighting based on each fine-tuned model's win rate.
【Annealing/cooldown】Curated 4K high-definition video is used for cooldown, with the learning rate linearly decayed to zero, which enhances fine-grained visual detail and produces smoother motion — this is a typical annealing with a small high-quality dataset.
[Uncertain: the image/video sampling ratio, and the specific weight numbers of each domain in the merge]

### [Data-Juicer 2.0](../models/Data-Juicer.md) ⚠️

【DJ's role definition】Provides tools for measuring, splitting, and searching data mixtures, but does not itself provide a mixture-ratio scheme.
【Mixture-search mechanism】The Sandbox's Refine stage directly answers the question of "how should data at different quality tiers be combined?": the top-ranked n operators are cross-combined into 2^n−1 data pools, each pool corresponding to one mixture scheme; a small reference model is trained and evaluated on each in turn, and the best combination is selected. This is a concrete implementation of turning data mixture ratio from manual design into a searchable problem.
【Two contrasting scale-up routes — the most valuable mixture-ratio conclusion in this case】The paper explicitly compares two routes for scaling from a small pool to a large pool:
  Route A: repeating the high-quality data (duplicating the top small pool several times).
  Route B: digging down layer by layer to include suboptimal data, deduplicating (expanding sample diversity).
  The experimental conclusion supports Route A as superior for text-to-video: "the performance obtained by repeating the highest-quality data 6× is higher than that obtained by using 8× the compute together with suboptimal data." This means that in the T2V setting, the marginal value of data quality exceeds that of data diversity and compute investment.
【Related mixture-ratio research】The same team's BiMix (arXiv:2405.14908) proposes a bivariate data mixing law for language model pretraining, modeling the effect of "data mixture ratio × data volume" on loss as an extrapolable law; Diversity as Reward (arXiv:2502.04380, NeurIPS 2025) studies diversity-driven fine-tuning data selection on domain-uncertain data. These two works represent the DJ team's exploration of mixture-ratio theory, but both target language/multimodal understanding rather than video generation.
[Uncertain] DJ does not provide an official mixture-ratio template for the pretraining/annealing/SFT three stages; the T2V case study does not report the specific mixing method between the 147k and 228k pools, nor the changing proportions of each source dataset (InternVid/Panda-70M/MSR-VTT) in the final pool.

### [Foley-Omni](../models/Foley-Omni.md) ⚠️

The core change in the mixture ratio across stages is "expansion of the task set + introduction of a replay mechanism."
【Stage 1 → Stage 2】Data switches from purely text-audio pairs (TTA 912h + TTS 1,253h + TTM 139h, about 0.7M pairs) to video-audio pairs (VisualTTS 1,980h + V2A 403h). The task set is completely replaced rather than stacked.
【Stage 2 → Stage 3】Switches to V2ST data (216 hours, internal + SpeakerVid), but critically, an explicit experience replay mechanism is introduced to mitigate catastrophic forgetting: in addition to the V2ST fine-tuning data, 100 hours of data from each of the preceding single-task domains are mixed in. By this calculation, Stage 3's mixture is roughly 216 hours of V2ST + 100 hours each from five preceding task domains (about 500 hours total), i.e., the target task accounts for about 30% and replay data about 70% — a fairly high replay proportion, reflecting how seriously the authors take the forgetting problem.
【Argument for the necessity of replay】This corroborates the single-stage training ablation in Table 6: in multi-task audio generation, different tasks interfere with each other severely (especially speech vs. non-speech), so training cannot start with everything mixed together from the beginning (as the stage ablation proves), nor can it fully abandon prior-stage data later on (hence the need for replay); balance can only be achieved via "separate first, then combine + high-ratio replay."
【Annealing】The paper does not use the term "annealing," but Stage 3 uses a small-scale (216h) high-quality V2ST dataset + a decreasing number of epochs (5→3→2) for final fine-tuning, which is functionally equivalent to an annealing stage.
[Uncertain] The sampling weights among different datasets within each stage (whether by natural hour-based proportion or weighted sampling) are undisclosed; whether the replay data is randomly sampled 100 hours or selected by quality score is also unspecified.

### [Goku](../models/Goku.md) ⚠️

The qualitative pattern of mixture-ratio change across stages is clear, but quantitative proportions are undisclosed:
(1) **Public → internal quality leap**: The paper explicitly states "We use public data for pre-training and internal data for fine-tuning" — 100 million LAION public images are used for pretraining, and 60 million internal high-quality images are used for fine-tuning. This is a standard "large and messy base, small and refined finish" recipe.
(2) **Image proportion gradually decreasing, video proportion increasing**: Stage-1 pure image → Stage-2 joint image and video → Stage-3 T2V-specific. Images are continuously retained rather than fully removed during the joint stage, to prevent video data (whose picture quality is generally lower than curated images) from dragging down single-frame image quality.
(3) **Resolution-driven data pool shrinkage**: 480p stage 36M → 720p stage 24M → 1080p stage 7M — the later the stage, the less data and the higher the quality bar, which in itself constitutes an implicit annealing — the late high-resolution training is in effect training on a high-quality subset.
(4) **Timing of distribution balancing**: The paper does not specify whether distribution balancing (Stage 5) is performed once over the full dataset, or with different mixture ratios across different training stages.
【Undisclosed】The specific image:video mixing ratio at each stage, the number of training steps and tokens seen per stage, and whether there is an explicit high-quality annealing stage. [Partially uncertain]

### [HunyuanVideo-Foley](../models/HunyuanVideo-Foley.md) ⚠️

Since there is only a single training stage, the dimension of "mixture ratio changing with training stage" does not apply. The data mixture is static and constant throughout:
【The only adjustment mechanism for the static mixture is category labels】Level 7 of the pipeline uses a speech-music detection and audio classification model to tag each clip with a category label, which is used to "manage category distribution" and "ensure balanced representation." This is the only data-mixture lever in this work, but the paper gives neither a target mixture ratio nor the actual mixture ratio, nor does it specify whether this is achieved via sampling weights or via truncation during dataset construction. [Uncertain]
【No annealing】The paper does not describe any annealing stage that switches to a high-quality subset near the end of training — this is fairly common in comparable works (e.g., using a high-quality small subset for the final k steps), but it is absent here.
【No SFT stage】Training ends at 200k steps as the final model, with no subsequent fine-tuning.
【An alternative "quality-mixture" lever: the high-quality tag】Although there is no stage-wise switch in data mixture, a form of "inference-time quality selection" is achieved via the high-quality label in captions: high- and low-quality data are mixed during training (labeled truthfully), while at inference time the high-quality tag is always appended, pulling the generation distribution toward the high-quality end. In effect this approximates "annealing with a high-quality subset," but at zero cost and without losing any training data volume. This is the cleverest design in this work along the data-mixture dimension: it defers the mixture-ratio decision from training time to inference time, and makes it continuously controllable rather than a one-off choice.
【Comparison with UniVerse-1's LQLS】UniVerse-1 isolates the influence of low-quality data by "restricting low-quality data to contribute to the loss only in the high-noise segment (t>800)"; HunyuanVideo-Foley achieves a similar goal via "quality tags + forced tagging at inference time." Both belong to the philosophy of "not discarding low-quality data, but controlling how it exerts influence," but at different levels: the former operates at the loss-function level (effective during training, not adjustable), the latter at the conditioning-input level (effective at inference time, adjustable). The latter is more flexible, the former more thorough.

### [HunyuanVideo](../models/HunyuanVideo.md)

【Original version】Video and image are jointly trained throughout (joint image-video training) to prevent degradation of the image prior; VAE training explicitly uses video:image = 4:1; the specific video/image mixture at each DiT stage is not published. The SFT stage switches to a manually curated high-quality subset of about 1 million samples.
【1.5】Mixture disclosure is more specific: pretraining Stages III–VI adopt a task mixture ratio of T2V : I2V : T2I = 1 : 6 : 3 (I2V dominates absolutely, reflecting that image-to-video is the main use case on the product side, and also explaining why I2V instruction-style captions are a matching design); data volume shrinks monotonically across stages, 800M→200M→100M→100M→1M, i.e., a typical "volume decreases, quality increases" annealing-style schedule: the early stages use large, messy, low-definition data to build a world prior, while the later stages use small, refined, high-definition data to polish. The CT stage is split into two independent branches by task (T2V and I2V, each with 1 million samples), and SFT and RLHF are also carried out separately by task.

### [InstructAV2AV](../models/InstructAV2AV.md) ⚠️

[Uncertain] The paper discloses no data mixture information whatsoever — this is the main gap in this work along the training-data-coordination dimension.

【Known structural information】
  · The two branches of Stage 1 process different modality slices of the same batch of InsAVE-80K data — the video branch uses only video pairs, the audio branch only audio pairs — so this is not a cross-source mixture issue, just a split along the modality dimension.
  · Stage 2 uses the complete audio-video paired data.
  · Throughout, there is only one data source, InsAVE-80K (79K/87K pairs); there is no design involving multiple dataset mixing, annealing, or switching data sources across stages.

【Five subsets revealed by the HF dataset card, but no mixture ratio】The paper and the dataset card do not state the sample counts of the five categories — add_and_remove, clone_id, clone_id_voice, clone_voice, general_editing — how they were sampled during training (natural proportion or weighted balancing), or whether scarce categories were upsampled. This is a rather regrettable omission: the difficulty and data volume of the five task types are likely very different (e.g., clone_id_voice requires simultaneous face-swap and voice-swap, has the highest synthesis difficulty and lowest success rate, and likely has far fewer samples than general_editing); without mixture-ratio control, the model's capability on scarce tasks would be noticeably weaker than on advantaged tasks — yet the paper's evaluation is done on an aggregate metric over a mixed evaluation set, which cannot reveal per-task capability differences.

【Modality weights in place of data mixture】The only clearly stated "mixture ratio" is the modality-balance weight at the loss level, λ_v=0.85 / λ_a=0.15, along with the step allocation of Stage 1's video 320K / audio 960K (1:3). Together these two constitute this work's actual "mixture strategy" — not adjusting proportions at the data level, but adjusting weights at the gradient level and budget at the step level. This is a reasonable substitute for a scenario with only a single data source.

【No annealing/SFT curation stage】No design for a final annealing or SFT stage using a small, high-quality curated subset is observed. The 1K manually curated set is explicitly used for evaluation, not training.

### [Other 2026 joint audio-video generation works](../models/JAVG_2026_misc.md) ⚠️

The explicitness of mixture-ratio scheduling varies enormously across this batch of works; ALIVE and NAVA provide directly usable numbers:
【ALIVE — three explicit mixture ratios】
(1) Speech/soundscape switch in the audio stage: Stage I is 700k hours of pure transcribed speech (384M samples); Stage II drops sharply to 5k hours of high-quality speech + 111k hours of video native audio tracks (19M samples). Speech duration is compressed from 700,000 hours to 5,000 hours (1/140), while 111,000 hours of video-native audio tracks are introduced — this is a drastic distribution shift, moving from "massive speech data to learn pronunciation" toward "curated speech to preserve ability + massive real soundscapes to learn scenes." Training Stage II for 10 epochs indicates this mixed batch of data is treated with great importance.
(2) Aesthetic mixture at the SFT stage: "high-aesthetics data mixed at 3:1 ratio with realistic data" — high-aesthetic data and realistic data mixed 3:1. This is the only explicitly public SFT data mixture number in this batch, meaning: the SFT stage deliberately lets high-aesthetic content dominate (75%), while still retaining 25% realistic content to prevent the model from becoming overly "Instagram-filtered" and losing realism.
(3) Balanced mixture for continued training: 11M → 4.3M "balanced samples," based on two priors — a nine-domain, three-tier taxonomy and "concept frequency + expected application scenario." The specific proportion of each category is undisclosed [uncertain].
(4) Asymmetric learning rate as an implicit mixture ratio: at the SFT stage, video lr 1e-5 / audio lr 1e-6 — audio-side learning rate is an order of magnitude lower — beyond loss weighting, a learning-rate difference is used to control the "update ratio" between modalities.
【NAVA — explicit reversal of the modality mixture】Stage 1 audio-only : audio-visual = 3:1 → Stage 2 reversed to 1:2 → Stage 3 purely high-quality audio-video (160K). The logic of this curve is clear: first use a large amount of pure audio data to build solid audio generation ability (audio data is cheap and plentiful: the raw pool has 20M audio clips), then gradually shift focus to the more expensive paired audio-video data, and finally finish up on a boutique subset amounting to one-thousandth of the total. The absolute data volumes of the three stages and the proportion of each source (Koala-36M / TED / film & TV) at each stage are not broken down [uncertain].
【CCL — multi-task probabilistic mixture (the only work in this batch to give task-level sampling probabilities)】
During training, task types are randomly sampled according to probability:
- text/image-to-video: 0.1
- text-to-audio: 0.1
- joint generation (joint): 0.6
- audio-to-video: 0.15
- video-to-audio: 0.15
Total 1.0. Joint generation accounts for 60% and is the overwhelming majority; the two cross-modal conversion tasks (A2V, V2A) each account for 15% — the role of these two tasks is to reinforce cross-modal conditional dependence (the model must truly "understand" one modality to generate the other), which is key regularization preventing the two streams from degenerating into two independent generators. The single-modality tasks each account for 10%, used to keep single-modality ability alive. This mixture scheme is one important reason CCL can reach SOTA with only 4M data: through multi-task mixing, the same batch of data is reused in five different ways, greatly increasing effective data efficiency.
【Baton】The data mixture across the three stages is not broken down [uncertain]; the proportions of the four sources within the 1.5M clips — OpenHuman-Vid / AudioCaps / WavCaps / internet video — are undisclosed [uncertain].
【StreamChar】Emilia (pure speech) is used for Stage 1, an audio-video dataset for Stage 2 — the two stages use entirely different sources with no mixing [the mixture ratio among the three audio-video datasets is uncertain].
【OmniCustom】A single dataset, so no mixture-ratio issue; but its fixed split of "first 4 seconds as reference + last 5 seconds for training" is a form of mixture design along the temporal dimension.
【ITS-JAVG】No training mixture ratio; but its ARW algorithm addresses exactly "how to weight and aggregate multiple reward signals" — which is structurally isomorphic to data mixture ratio in mathematical terms (both are weighted-combination optimization over multiple sources of signal). Its idea of adaptively weighting by variance could in theory transfer to data mixture scheduling (adaptively adjusting sampling weights by the gradient variance of each domain), though the paper does not make this extension.

### [Baseline collection for joint audio-video generation](../models/JavisDiT_baselines.md) ⚠️

(5) Quality threshold tightens with stage: the SFT stage switches to a high-quality curated set (filtered by multiple metrics including aesthetic score, video quality, and motion quality), and further layers on a "sampling by the inverse of caption-embedding-space density" long-tail balancing strategy, while also introducing two specialized datasets for camera motion and visual style.
The report does not describe an independent annealing stage; Stage5's dual-resolution mixed training + progressively decreasing learning rate (1e-4→5e-5→2e-5→1e-5) functionally fills a similar role. [Uncertain: specific numerical values for all mixture ratios]

### [MOVA](../models/MOVA.md)

The mechanism by which the data mixture changes across stages is "decreasing scale + increasing quality threshold," and the specific numbers are fully disclosed:
【Phase 1 (exploration period)】~61,500 hours, from diverse sources: SpeakerVid5M, Chinese TV dramas, cartoons, films, YouTube, OpenHumanVid. This phase pursues broad distribution coverage, with no additional quality narrowing.
【Phase 2 (quality convergence period)】Three orthogonal filters are layered on top of the Phase 1 corpus:
- No burned-in subtitles via OCR: retains ~9.5M clips, and appends "This video has no subtitles." to their prompts (a filtering + conditioning mixture strategy)
- Lip-sync correspondence: LSE-D ≤ 9.5 and LSE-C ≥ 4.5, retaining ~2.5M clips
- Visual fidelity: DOVER technical score > 0.15, retaining ~2.4M clips
Combined, the Phase 2 dataset = 16.8M clips ≈ 37,600 hours, which the paper describes as "balancing scale and quality." This is about 61% retention relative to Phase 1.
【Phase 3 (refinement period)】Uses only the highest-quality 720p subset, ~11,000 hours, DOVER technical score > 0.14 (recalibrated at the 720p scale). This is only about 18% relative to Phase 1.
【No annealing/SFT stage】MOVA has no independent annealing or SFT stage; Phase 3's small, high-quality dataset in effect serves as the annealing/refinement stage.
【Accompanying hyperparameter mixture changes (Table 7)】Visual sigma shift is held at 5.0 across all three stages; audio sigma shift changes from 1.0 to 5.0 (starting from Phase 2); text dropout drops from 0.5 to 0.2 (starting from Phase 2); audio loss weight is held at 0.2 across all three stages (i.e., the velocity regression losses for video and audio are weighted 1:0.2, with video dominant); weight decay is held at 0.001; backbone LR is held at 1e-5, Bridge LR at 2e-5; LUFS normalization is turned on starting from Phase 2.

The category mixture of the SFT corpus is instead determined iteratively based on intermediate evaluation feedback (Fig.8).

### [Movie Gen](../models/Movie_Gen.md)

· Video pretraining: the low-definition stage uses a 720px-threshold set (aspect ratio 60% landscape/40% portrait); the high-definition stage switches to a stricter 768px set and adds new data augmentation, with the mixture adjusted to 80% landscape/20% portrait and ≥60% containing people — that is, as the stages progress, the resolution threshold, aesthetic/motion thresholds, and the proportion of people-containing content all tighten simultaneously, a textbook example of a "quality monotonically increasing with stage" data curriculum.
· Caption mixture: across the entire training set, 70% of captions come from LLaMa3-Video 8B and 30% from the 70B model.
· Video SFT: data is completely replaced with a manually curated set, duration limited to 10.6–16 seconds (50% at exactly 16 seconds), and the training frame rate switches by duration (16 seconds uses 16FPS, 10.6–16 seconds uses 24FPS), allowing the model to support both 10-second and 16-second outputs; multiple SFT models are trained (different fine-tuning data versions, different hyperparameters, different pretraining checkpoints) and then combined via model averaging (following LLaMa3) to fuse the different strengths of each model in motion, consistency, and camera control.
· PT2V SFT: paired and real cross-paired reference images are mixed at a 1:1 ratio.
· Audio fine-tuning: cinematic-quality audio-video (Cin-AV) and high-quality pure audio (HQ-A) are mixed at a ratio of 10 batches : 1 batch; 10-second chunks and 30-second chunks are mixed at 5 batches : 1 batch.
· The conditioning dropout in audio training also constitutes a form of "modality mixture": the overall condition (video + text + audio context) is dropped entirely with probability 0.2; text and video are each independently dropped with probability 0.1 to reduce dependence on any single modality; masked audio is fully masked (pure generation) with probability 0.5, otherwise 75%–100% is masked (audio extension).

### [Script-a-Video](../models/Script-a-Video.md) ⚠️

### [SkyReels series](../models/SkyReels.md)

Both generations exhibit the mixture-ratio pattern of "stage progression = decreasing data volume + increasing data quality":

· UniTalking: outputs from two paths — "annotate separately then concatenate" and "unify via a single fused-annotation model" — are randomly sampled during training, i.e., the choice of annotation strategy is left to random sampling, letting the model adapt to both text distributions simultaneously.
· SkyReels-V4: three tiers of description — short/long/structured — coexist for mixed training.
· Movie Gen: the 70/30 mix of 8B/70B captions runs through the entire training set, and is not stage-dependent.
· NAVA (JAVG batch): modality mixture scheduled from 3:1 to 1:2, with a 160K SFT subset, but no controlled experiment validating the benefit.
【Ecosystem gap】Almost no work reports the ablation-optimal point for "mixture ratio of different caption styles/lengths"; Motif's candid disclosure (0.5/0.3/0.2, unvalidated) represents the true state of the field. [Uncertain]

### [Collection of geometric/structured annotation datasets](../models/geometric_datasets.md) ⚠️

SpatialVID's HQ subset is an explicit product of mixture-ratio design — tightening the distribution on three metrics (aesthetics, brightness, motion) and balancing the scene-category distribution and motion diversity; compared with Panda-70M, it shows a more concentrated quality distribution and can directly serve as a high-quality mixture source for an annealing stage; Action100M's k-means semantic resampling is itself a mixture tool for combating long-tail distribution, used together with deduplication of 7.58M duplicate groups; SceneScribe-1M's downstream experiments uniformly adopt an additive mixture of "original recipe vs. original recipe + SceneScribe-1M," without disclosing the specific mixing ratio [uncertain]; WildWorld does not address multi-stage mixture scheduling [uncertain]

### [Video-generation post-training data strategy](../models/post_training_data.md) ⚠️

【Anchor paper】The data mixture at every stage is entirely undisclosed [uncertain]. The structural facts that can be confirmed are: SFT uses "text-video paired data," while the four post-training steps RLHF/DMD/ODE regression/self-forcing share a single "curated prompt set" (requiring only prompts, no paired video) — this is the key structural divide in post-training data: SFT requires paired data, while RL and distillation require only prompts. This substantially lowers the data-acquisition cost of the latter three stages, and also explains why the construction of the post-training prompt set (diversity, category balance, deduplication, and non-overlap with the SFT set) is given more emphasis than raw material collection.
【Cross-work stage-mixture numbers】
· ALIVE is the most complete: Continue-Training 4.3M balanced samples (3 epochs) → SFT 5M (high-aesthetic and realistic data mixed 3:1, only 0.5 epoch, asymmetric learning rate video 1e-5 / audio 1e-6) → 1080p Refiner 0.7M (1 epoch) → Character-driven 0.8M. SFT is deliberately trained for only 0.5 epoch to avoid overfitting to the high-aesthetic distribution and losing diversity — an important trick beyond the mixture ratio itself;
· SkyReels-V4: 5 million (3 epochs) → 1 million manually curated (3 epochs);
· Cosmos-Predict 2.5: 30k iterations, batch 256 for each of the five domains separately, plus 388K 4K cooldown samples;
· Movie Gen: model averaging after SFT; Seedance 1.0: model merging after SFT of multiple sub-models — weight averaging is an alternative implementation of "data mixture";
· Foley-Omni: Stage 3 uses 216 hours of target-domain data + 100 hours of replay per domain to prevent forgetting, a rare case of explicit experience replay in the post-training stage;
· HunyuanVideo 1.5: CT 1 million per task → SFT with a stricter subset → RLHF with O(10K) prompts; the super-resolution module is trained independently on 1 million 1K–4K clips.
【Pattern】The SFT stage is generally trained "shallowly" (low epoch count, small learning rate, early stopping) — Seedance 1.0 uses a smaller learning rate than pretraining, a limited GPU count, and early stopping to prevent overfitting and preserve text controllability; ALIVE trains for only 0.5 epoch; CogVideoX trains for only 10k steps. This stands in sharp contrast to pretraining's "train to convergence," and is fundamentally about balancing "fitting the high-quality distribution" against "preserving the diversity and controllability learned during pretraining."

### [Survey of mainstream video pretraining dataset collections: Panda-70M, InternVid, Koala…](../models/pretraining_datasets.md) ⚠️

**None of the seven provide a scheme for "data mixture changing across training stages"** — this is the essential difference between datasets and model technical reports. Three categories of substitute information are available:
1. **The natural tiered proportions within each dataset**: MiraData 788K unfiltered→330K(41.9%)→93K(11.8%)→42K(5.3%)→9K(1.1%); Koala-36M 48M unfiltered→36M(75%); UltraVideo 62K→46K→42K; Panda-70M 70.7M→10M (high-quality subset)→2M; InternVid 234M→18M(AES)→10M(FLT/DIV). Users can directly treat these tiers as the data sources for the "pretraining/annealing/SFT" three segments.
2. **Mixing ratio among source datasets (LVD-2M is the clearest)**: within its 2M output, **the WebVid family accounts for about 1.2 million (nearly 60%), the YouTube family (Panda-70M+InternVid) for about 600,000, and HD-VG for about 300,000** — this is the only cross-source mixture ratio among the seven that can be inferred from the release files. Note that the paper's main text does not give this breakdown.
3. **OpenVid-1M's differing filter strength by source**: it takes **the top 20% by aesthetic score from the Panda-50M source, and the top 90% from each of the other three sources** — this is an implicit form of mixture control, applying the strictest filtering to the noisiest source, thereby suppressing its proportion in the final set. This practice of "setting different filter strength by source" is very practical and worth reusing.
**Annealing and SFT high-quality subsets**: none of the seven give an explicit definition. If one must be chosen as an "SFT curated set," the most reasonable candidates are **MiraData-9K** (1.1% retention rate), **UltraVideo-42K** (only 2.3% bad-video rate after manual verification), or **InternVid-18M-AES** (aesthetic ≥4, widely used by the open-source community as a T2V fine-tuning set). [Uncertain]

### [Cosmos-Predict2.5](../models/Cosmos-Predict25.md)

Post-training data disclosure is quite specific, covering three parts: domain partitioning, cooldown, and RL:
[SFT domain partitioning method] A multi-head classifier is trained on InternVideo2 embeddings (Wang et al., 2024) to sort samples into five domains. This is a reusable paradigm of "using a video representation model plus a lightweight classification head to partition data by domain," which is cheaper than either manual rules or item-by-item VLM judgment.
[Five-domain scale] object permanence: 10.4M clips, high motion: 1.0M clips, complex scenes: 1.6M clips, driving: 3.1M clips, robotic manipulation: 730K clips; plus 388K clips of 4K cooldown data. Note that object permanence accounts for the overwhelming majority (10.4M), reflecting Physical AI's emphasis on the basic physical common sense that "objects do not vanish when occluded."
[SFT training configuration] Each domain model is trained for 30k iterations with batch size 256, with hyperparameters carried over from the final stage of pretraining. Evaluation shows that every SFT model achieves a significantly higher human win rate over the pretraining baseline in its target domain (e.g., robotic manipulation domain: SFT Win 72.6% vs. Base Win 8.3%; object permanence domain: 50.9% vs. 27.7%; high motion domain: 44.0% vs. 34.7%; complex scenes domain: 42.6% vs. 35.4%; driving domain: 47.9% vs. 28.8%).
[RL] GRPO is used to perform online reinforcement on the merged model (and the pretrained model), so there is no pre-annotated preference pair dataset — preferences are given online by the reward model. The reward model is VideoAlign (Liu et al., 2025), a VLM-based reward model that jointly evaluates three dimensions: text alignment, motion quality, and visual quality. Configuration: 8 rollouts are generated per input condition, each using 20 diffusion steps; rewards are normalized within the group to obtain the advantage; due to memory constraints, the trajectory probability is decomposed into a sum of step-wise conditional probabilities, with a gradient computed every two steps and accumulated along the entire trajectory (10 steps total) before a single parameter update; trained for 256 steps with batch size 32; the diffusion loss on the fine-tuning dataset is used as regularization to mitigate reward hacking; the EMA weights of the merged model after RL are finally released as the official post-train checkpoint.
[Domain-specialized post-training data] The driving multi-view model additionally uses 1.5M captioned 7-camera, 20-second, 30FPS clips; the Cosmos-Transfer2.5 driving ControlNet uses a "world scenario map" projected from HD maps and 3D boxes as the control signal.
[Distillation] rCM (Zheng et al., 2025) is a hybrid forward-backward joint distillation, combining continuous-time consistency distillation with distribution matching distillation; after distillation, generation is possible in 4 steps, with PAI-Bench scores nearly matching the teacher (T2W 0.764 vs 0.768, I2W 0.816 vs 0.810, with the distilled I2W score even slightly higher).

### [Data-Juicer 2.0](../models/Data-Juicer.md) ⚠️

### [Foley-Omni](../models/Foley-Omni.md) ⚠️

[The stage closest to SFT] Stage 3 can be regarded as SFT-like: it uses the smallest-scale data (216 hours), most closely matched to the final target (V2ST full soundtrack scoring), for 2 epochs of light fine-tuning, paired with 100 hours of replay per domain to prevent forgetting. Compared with the preference-alignment stage that is common in industrial-grade models (Veo 3, Sora 2, etc.), Foley-Omni, as an academic work, is blank on the post-training dimension — which also means there remains room for improvement on subjective dimensions such as audio aesthetics and fidelity to creative intent (A-MOS 3.92 vs. GT 4.33 is the largest gap among the three subjective scores).

### [Goku](../models/Goku.md)

### [HunyuanVideo](../models/HunyuanVideo.md)

[Original version] SFT only: about 1 million manually curated samples, with selection criteria of four aesthetic dimensions (color harmony, lighting, subject prominence, spatial composition) plus three motion dimensions (motion speed, action completeness, motion blur). The original version did not do RLHF/DPO.
[1.5] Post-training is a three-stage CT → SFT → RLHF pipeline, with T2V and I2V kept fully separate throughout:
· CT (continued training): 1 million high-quality clips per task, at 480p/720p, 24fps;
· SFT: a further, stricter selection on top of the CT data, with criteria of aesthetic appeal, clarity, and motion smoothness; the final dataset is constructed via manual annotation; the specific sample count and thresholds are not published;
· RLHF/DPO: on the I2V side, a prompt set spanning 100+ categories is built, with accompanying images curated from high-aesthetic images and manually verified for text-image consistency; on the T2V side, a balanced prompt set of O(10K) scale (tens-of-thousands level) is built, sourced from a mix of LLM-generated prompts and training captions, category-balanced across the three dimensions of motion/scene/subject; for each prompt, N candidate videos are sampled to form non-duplicate preference pairs, and preference data is obtained via manual GSB annotation;
· Super-resolution module: trained independently using 1 million 1K–4K high-quality video clips plus high-resolution images.

### [InstructAV2AV](../models/InstructAV2AV.md) ⚠️

This work's training pipeline stops at two-stage supervised training (the Stage 2 joint fine-tuning being the final stage), and includes no independent preference-alignment post-training step.

[Overall positioning clarification] It's worth noting that InstructAV2AV itself is, in nature, closer to "post-training": its generation capability comes from the upstream pretrained prior of Ovi, and the 79K editing pairs in InsAVE-80K play precisely the role of an instruction-tuning dataset — teaching an existing audio-video generation model to "perform localized edits according to instructions." ### [Other 2026 Joint Audio-Video Generation Work](../models/JAVG_2026_misc.md) ⚠️

The post-training forms in this batch of work are dominated by "high-quality subset SFT," and none of them adopt preference learning/RLHF:
[NAVA — one-in-a-thousand curated SFT (the most typical example in this batch)]
From a 15M-sample training corpus, "multi-operator collaborative filtering" is used to filter out 160K high-quality samples, with selection criteria of "accurate captions and strong audio-visual alignment." The SFT retention rate is about 1.07% — i.e., ten out of every thousand samples. Moreover, the captions of this subset are regenerated by the more expensive Gemini-3-Pro ("to produce more accurate, structured, and temporally grounded audio-visual captions") rather than reusing the original captions produced by Flash. This double purification of "curated subset + upgraded captions" is a noteworthy practice: the SFT stage swaps not only the data but also the annotation quality. The specific thresholds are not disclosed [Uncertain]. No preference pairs, no reward model, no RLHF [Uncertain].
[ALIVE — multi-layer post-training but no preference learning]
(1) Continue-Training: 4.3M balanced samples, 3 epochs — using a domain-balanced subset to correct distribution skew, a transitional stage between pretraining and SFT.
(2) SFT: 5M samples, a 3:1 mix of high-aesthetic and realistic data, 0.5 epochs, asymmetric learning rates (video 1e-5 / audio 1e-6). Note that only 0.5 epoch is trained — the SFT stage is deliberately trained shallowly to avoid overfitting to the high-aesthetic distribution at the cost of diversity.
(3) 1080p Refiner: 0.7M high-clarity samples, 1 epoch — an independent high-definition refinement module, which is also essentially a form of targeted post-training.
(4) Character-driven: 0.8M reference-paired samples (including data constructed via cross-pair and in-pair augmentation) — specialized post-training targeting reference-character capability.
No description of DPO/RLHF/preference pairs/reward model [Uncertain]. Human evaluation (blind tests vs. Veo 3.1 / Kling 2.6 / Wan 2.6 / Sora 2 / LTX-2) is used only for evaluation and is not recycled as a training signal [Uncertain].
[StreamChar — distillation as post-training]
Two-stage distillation: Stage I, 600 steps, for step-count compression (→ a 4-step generator); Stage II, 400 steps, for rollout consistency (fine-tuning the student under online chunk rollout), with student lr 2e-6 and fake score network lr 4e-7. The training data for Stage II is the student model's own online rollout — this is on-policy self-generated data, and is the only post-training form in this batch that involves "feeding model output back in." Ablation shows single-stage distillation performs worse than the two-stage approach.
[OmniCustom — LoRA adaptation as post-training]
Identity LoRA and timbre LoRA are trained on the joint audio-video backbone, paired with a contrastive-learning objective (the predicted flow with reference conditioning as the positive example, without reference conditioning as the negative example) and flow matching. The training data is the paired samples of OmniCustom-1M. No independent preference data or curated subset [Uncertain].
[CCL / Baton] No post-training stage [Uncertain]. CCL's 12,000-step joint training is itself the final stage; Baton's Stage 3 joint fine-tuning (freezing the planner, fine-tuning the DiT) can be regarded as post-training in a broad sense, but there is no preference data.
[ITS-JAVG — replacing post-training with inference-time search (the most instructive point in this batch)]
Its core claim is precisely that quality can be improved without post-training at all — using multiple verifiers plus Best-of-N/EvoSearch to select the best output at inference time (JavisDiT 5 samples, MMDisCo 10 samples), achieving results comparable to training-side optimization. This effectively proposes an alternative path: rather than spending resources on SFT/RLHF, one can sample more at inference time and then filter with verifiers. Conversely, the high-scoring samples it filters out are a ready-made source for constructing SFT/preference data (high-scoring samples as positives, low-scoring as negatives), but the paper does not take this step.
[Common gap] Zero of the seven works use preference pairs/RLHF/DPO — this stands in sharp contrast to contemporaneous closed-source industrial models (Sora 2, Seedance, etc., which commonly include an RLHF step), reflecting that preference alignment remains blank territory on the open-source/academic side of JAVG. The verifier-hacking problem revealed by ITS-JAVG also foreshadows a risk: if automatic verifiers are used carelessly to construct preference data for RLHF, the result is very likely to be a model that merely learns to please the judge.

### [Collection of Joint Audio-Video Generation Baselines](../models/JavisDiT_baselines.md) ⚠️

Only JavisDiT++ has a complete post-training data system; it is the sole contributor on this dimension within this collection, and also the part most worth learning from:
[JavisDiT++'s AV-DPO (Audio-Video Direct Preference Optimization) — fully disclosed]
- Preference data scale: about 25,000 audio-video preference pairs (~25k audio-video preference pairs), trained for 1 epoch, with 121M trainable LoRA parameters.
- Prompt pool: 30,000 text captions, explicitly non-overlapping with the SFT training data (apart from the SFT training data), avoiding data leakage.
- Candidate construction: for each prompt, the reference model generates N=3 audio-video pairs, plus 1 ground-truth sample, giving 4 candidates in total per candidate group.
- Reward model ensemble (six models divided by role, the most complete multi-dimensional reward design in this collection):
  · Audio quality → AudioBox (AudioBox-Aesthetics)
  · Text-audio alignment → ImageBind
  · Video quality → VideoAlign
  · Text-video alignment → ImageBind
  · Audio-video cross-modal similarity → ImageBind
  · Temporal synchronization → Synchformer
- Pairing strategy: "normalized modality-aware ranking" is used to select winning/losing pairs; the authors explicitly state the purpose is "to ensure consistency within each modality, rather than pairing high-quality audio with low-quality video" — this is the core engineering lesson for constructing preference pairs under multi-dimensional rewards, directly addressing the problem that contradictory samples such as "high-quality audio + low-quality video" would pollute the preference signal.
- A quantitative observation of reference value: about 30% of the winning samples in the final preference data come from model generation rather than ground truth, from which the authors judge that "the baseline model itself already possesses fairly strong generation capability." This ratio can serve as an empirical signal for judging whether a model has reached the stage where "preference optimization can be done with self-generated data."
- Open-source status: the DPO preference data is "in preparation for release" and is not yet public [Uncertain].
[JavisDiT (v1)] No DPO/RLHF; all three stages are supervised training.
[Harmony] No preference-optimization data. Its "Cross-Task Synergy" is functionally somewhat similar — using bidirectional generation tasks to provide additional supervisory signal that suppresses alignment drift — but this is multi-task supervised learning, not preference learning.
[UniAVGen] The multi-task learning in Stage 3 (five tasks in a 4:1:1:2:2 ratio) is likewise multi-task supervision rather than preference optimization; no SFT curated set, no preference pairs, no reward model [Uncertain].

### [LongCat-Video](../models/LongCat-Video.md) ⚠️

Split into two stages, SFT and RLHF:

· Text alignment TA: also based on VideoAlign, fine-tuned on internal annotated data, with color video as input.

### [Wan 2.5 / 2.6 / 2.7](../models/Wan.md) ⚠️

Wan 2.1's post-training (Section 3.2) is disclosed in relatively fine detail, but covers only SFT-style high-quality data curation, with no preference-learning step.
[General principle] The core goal of post-training is to simultaneously improve visual fidelity and motion dynamics through high-quality data; different strategies are used for static and dynamic data — image data optimizes visual quality, while video data specifically polishes motion quality.
[Image side (two construction paths)]
1) Expert-model-driven: from the pool of high-scoring images, the top 20% by expert-model predicted score is taken, and within this subset, style and category factors are additionally considered to ensure distributional diversity;
2) Manual collection: top images are manually collected from every category and data source, and concepts missing from the dataset are actively filled in to enhance model generalization.
Together these yield "millions" of curated images, with selection criteria explicitly being excellence in three aspects: quality, composition, and details.
[Video side] Top-ranked videos are first selected using a visual-quality classifier, and then a motion-quality classifier separately selects "millions of simple-motion videos" and "millions of complex-motion videos"; all selection follows a strategy emphasizing category balance and high diversity, drawing from 12 major categories (technology, animals, art, people, vehicles, etc.) to strengthen generation capability for commonly used categories.
[Task-specific fine-tuning data (Section 5.1.2, also part of post-training)]
- I2V data: it was found that an excessive difference between the first frame and the video content causes instability in image-driven generation, so SigLIP features are used to compute the cosine similarity between the "first-frame feature" and the "mean of the remaining frames' features," and only samples above a preset threshold are kept (the threshold is not published);
- Video-continuation data: the same method is used to compute the SigLIP-feature cosine similarity between the first 1.5-second segment and the following 3.5-second segment, filtering for temporally consistent videos accordingly;
- First/last-frame transition data: this goes the opposite way, raising the proportion of samples with a significant difference between the first and last frames, to cater to the community's demand for transition smoothness.
[Gaps] No RLHF/DPO section, no preference-pair count or annotation method, no description of reward model training data (Wan-Bench uses human-feedback weighting, but that is an evaluation weight rather than a training signal). ### [Video Captioning Model Ecosystem](../models/caption_models.md) ⚠️

[Captioner post-training data (the core point of competition in this ecosystem in 2025–2026)]
· AVoCaDO's GRPO: the reward consists of three terms — ℛ_C (five-dimensional checklist coverage, judged by GPT-4.1), ℛ_D (dialogue F1, speaker accuracy plus content edit-distance DP-alignment similarity, threshold 0.6), and ℛ_L (length regularization, penalizing >4096 tokens). The preference signal is generated online; there is no fixed-scale preference-pair dataset.
· AVSCap's GRPO: hybrid reward = length control + speech preservation + audio-visual consistency. The paper's key claim — "RL gains exceed those from expanding SFT data volume" — is a direct basis for prioritizing post-training investment over expanding annotation volume.
· Tarsier2's DPO: uses model-based sampling to automatically construct preference data (no need for manual preference-pair annotation); the scale is undisclosed [Uncertain].
· video-SALMONN 2's MrDPO: multi-round DPO, periodically resetting the reference policy with a newly trained lightweight adapter; the caption-quality objective simultaneously rewards completeness and factual accuracy, reducing the 7B model's caption error rate by 28% relative to the baseline.
· video-SALMONN-o1's pDPO: process-level DPO, using contrastive step selection for step-level reward, paired with RivaBench (4000+ expert-annotated QA items, covering talk shows, academic talks, and synthetic-video detection).
· Tencent Hunyuan 1.5's caption model: uses OPA-DPO (preference optimization targeting multimodal hallucination) for RL post-training to suppress hallucination — the only case among generation-side teams to publicly disclose a captioner post-training method.
[SFT curated-set selection criteria] AVoCaDO keeps only samples scored ≥4/5 by GPT-4.1; SkyCaptioner curates 2 million out of 10 million; ALIVE's caption data is MLLM-generated, then manually revised, followed by two rounds of SFT.
[Ecosystem trend] Before 2025, captioner post-training was almost exclusively SFT; from the second half of 2025 onward, DPO/GRPO became standard, and reward design (especially dialogue accuracy and cross-modal consistency) replaced data volume as the primary competitive dimension.

### [Video Generation Post-Training Data Strategy](../models/post_training_data.md)

This entry has this field itself as its topic; here we give a directly reusable summary of conclusions.
[I. Scale and selection criteria of SFT curated sets] Scale is on the order of 10^6–10^7 items, 0.4%–20% of the pretraining pool (NAVA 1.07%, Allegro 0.4%, CogVideoX 20%, Goku 12.5%, Cosmos ~10%, SkyReels-V4's second stage 20%). The general structure of the selection criteria stacks four layers: ① a stricter version of the pretraining thresholds (aesthetic score, clarity, motion score, text area, CLIP similarity — Allegro provides the only fully reproducible complete threshold combination); ② concept/domain balancing (LongCat's inverse-density sampling over the caption embedding space, Movie Gen's k-NN concept balancing, Cosmos's classifier-based five-domain split, Seedance's targeted collection across hundreds of categories, Motif's style/subject-tag-driven domain-balancing); ③ caption recaptioning upgrade (NAVA recaptions with Gemini-3-Pro, Open-Sora 2.0 recaptions with Qwen2.5-Max, Movie Gen does manual rewriting, Step-Video does manual refinement); ④ manual final review (HunyuanVideo's seven-dimensional rubric of four aesthetic items plus three motion items, Step-Video's four items of clarity/aesthetics/motion appropriateness/transition smoothness). Training configurations are generally "shallow-trained" to prevent overfitting.
[II. Number and annotation method of preference pairs] Scale is on the order of 10^4–10^5 pairs, 2–3 orders of magnitude smaller than the SFT set. Four construction methods:
① Manually annotated real preferences (the most expensive, most reliable): Step-Video-T2V (multi-seed generation + annotator scoring + QC-supervised consistency), Kling 3.0 Omni (human comparison of multiple variants under the same MVL), HunyuanVideo 1.5 (GSB annotation + non-duplicate pairing), SkyReels-V2 (30,000 manually annotated sample pairs);
② Automatically constructed negative samples: SkyReels-V2's controlled-distortion corrupted samples (covering V2V/I2V/T2V);
③ Automatic ranking by multiple reward models: JavisDiT++ (six RMs plus normalized modality-aware ranking, 25,000 pairs);
④ Online reward replacing offline preference pairs (currently mainstream): the anchor paper's GRPO route, LongCat-Video (group size 4, 64 prompts per step, about 0.5k iterations), Cosmos-Predict 2.5 (8 rollouts × 20 steps, within-group normalization, 256 steps, batch 32) — there is no pre-annotated preference-pair dataset; preferences are given online by the RM during rollout.
Two key pieces of engineering experience regarding annotation protocol: Seedance 1.0's "select the best/worst on a specified dimension, with the best not underperforming the worst on other dimensions"; JavisDiT++'s "normalized modality-aware ranking" to avoid pairing high-quality audio with low-quality video. On the evaluation-protocol side, GSB (Good–Same–Bad) has become the de facto standard for industrial reports (used by the anchor paper, Kling, Seedance, and HunyuanVideo 1.5), with the advantage of allowing annotators to express indifference, reducing edge-case noise.
[III. Reward model training data] Two public assets: HPDv3 (1.08 million text-image pairs, 1.17 million pairwise comparisons) and the VideoReward preference set (16,000 prompts, 108,000 videos generated by 12 T2V models, 182,000 triplets, with professional annotators recording A-wins/tie/B-wins separately across the three dimensions VQ/MQ/TA, using a Bradley-Terry model with ties, BTT, plus a held-out validation set of 13,000 non-overlapping-prompt triplets). The anchor paper's RM training paradigm: a Qwen3.5 VLM backbone plus an MLP head, an uncertainty-aware ranking loss, and two-stage training (Stage 1 uses data-aware orthogonal gradient projection to fuse HPDv3++'s diverse aesthetic preferences while retaining HPSv3's original knowledge; Stage 2 uses unlabeled data produced by models of different capability levels and different RL iterations). Multi-reward fusion is explicitly identified by the paper as the system's key difficulty, requiring carefully designed aggregation strategies and weighting coefficients.
[IV. Industry-stratification conclusion] Closed-source industrial models (Seedance, Kling, HunyuanVideo 1.5, Step-Video, SkyReels, LongCat) have generally completed the two-stage post-training pipeline of SFT plus preference alignment; the open-source and academic side (Movie Gen, CogVideoX, Allegro, Goku, Motif, MAGI-1, Open-Sora, and almost all AV academic work) mostly stops at SFT, with preference alignment being the biggest gap — in the JAVG joint audio-video generation field in particular, this is a complete blank except for JavisDiT++.

### [Combined Survey of Mainstream Video Pretraining Datasets: Panda-70M, InternVid, Koala…](../models/pretraining_datasets.md) ⚠️

**None of the seven have post-training data in the modern sense** — no preference pairs, no reward model training set, no RLHF/DPO data. This follows from their positioning as "pretraining datasets," but it is also a notable gap relative to industrial data systems. Specifically:
- **Preference data used only for evaluation, not fed back into training**: Panda-70M's 200-video study with 5 human raters comparing teacher vs. student; UltraVideo's 10-person preference comparison of UltraWan vs. the official Wan (video quality aesthetics **81.10% vs. 18.90%**, temporal stability 55.70% vs. 44.30%, text consistency 54.50% vs. 45.50%); LVD-2M's user study with 200 valid responses. **None of this human preference data was constructed into preference pairs for training**.
- **The only human annotation actually used for training is a "quality score," not a "preference pair"**: Koala-36M's 200,000 items × 8 experts × 1–5 scoring trains a data filter (the TSA network), not a reward model; Panda-70M's 100,000 caption-selection annotations train a caption selector (UMT), not a reward model. **Both are cases of "using human annotation to train a data-pipeline component," not "using human annotation to align a generative model"** — this distinction is key to understanding the positioning of open-source datasets in 2024–2025.
- **Subsets usable as SFT curated sets**: see the stage_data_mixture field (MiraData-9K, UltraVideo, InternVid-18M-AES). Their selection criteria are simply the threshold combination at the strictest tier of each dataset's own funnel.
- The accompanying generative models (ViCLIP, MVDiT, UltraWan, MiraDiT) **all only do pretraining or LoRA fine-tuning; none has undergone preference alignment**.

### [Data-Juicer 2.0](../models/Data-Juicer.md)

This is a core contribution dimension of Data-Juicer 2.0, and also the object with the most thorough data-infrastructure disclosure in this survey — most model teams stay tight-lipped on this, whereas DJ's entire paper is devoted to answering this question.
【Scale capability】Processes data at the TB scale, scheduling 10k+ CPU cores; experiments cover dataset scales from 560K to 70 billion samples; one production deployment ran stably for over 5 months, cumulatively processing more than TB-scale data.
【Multi-backend adaptive execution】The runtime layer provides hardware-agnostic adapters that automatically or manually select a backend based on scale:
  · Small scale (~560K samples): the standalone single-machine mode is the most cost-effective.
  · Medium scale (5.6M–56M samples): Ray mode is recommended, with a 4-node acceleration ratio of 148%–226%.
  · Large scale (70 billion samples): for pure text, Alibaba Cloud MaxCompute is recommended (at 10k+-core scale it takes about 1/4 the time of Ray); for multimodal data, Ray-DLC (PAI's containerized deep-computing cluster) is recommended.
  · Distributed measurements: 3,200 Ray-DLC cores took 1,780 seconds to process a 500x dataset and 7,083 seconds for a 2,500x dataset (near-linear scaling).
【Key optimization techniques and quantified gains】
  · Adaptive splitting: 2–3x speedup on large datasets.
  · Workload-aware OP reordering: reorders lightweight operators earlier wherever operator commutativity permits, cutting processing time by up to 70.22%.
  · Adaptive GPU resource allocation: model-based operators automatically use GPU and quantization (integrating vLLM), saving up to 99% of processing time.
  · Batched processing: cuts time by up to 84%.
  · Operator fusion (FusedOP): batch-level operator fusion using the Strategy/Decorator pattern, significantly lowering network I/O — peak throughput dropped from about 160MB/s to 60MB/s (i.e., about a two-thirds reduction in unnecessary data movement).
  · I/O-intensive operators use hierarchical parallelism combining batching, multiprocessing, and multithreading.
  · v1.5.4 adds batch-local stage fusion for a further speedup.
【Deduplication performance benchmarked against NeMo Curator】MinHash deduplication uses a load-balanced union-find, achieving a 3.3x speedup over a native Ray implementation; 8 Ray nodes process 5TB in 2.8 hours. The paper uses NVIDIA NeMo Curator's processing of 1.1TB in 1.8 hours (on 64 A100s) as a comparison point, highlighting the cost advantage of DJ's pure-CPU approach. Scalability: a 5x increase in data volume increases time by 4.02–5.62x; doubling core count reduces time to 58.9%–67.1%.
【Empirical insights on cost and storage】
  · Alibaba Cloud's dedicated cluster-network optimization saves 24.8% cost compared with standard ECS.
  · Storage choice has a significant impact: using NAS/OSS instead of CPFS raises cost by 20–30%.
  · A 3x increase in network bandwidth yields a 2.7x processing speedup — the paper explicitly states that large-scale multimodal data processing is I/O-bound rather than compute-bound, an engineering conclusion of considerable value to this survey.
【Ecosystem and adoption】Natively compatible with HuggingFace Datasets and the Ray compute engine; integrates with ModelScope, NVIDIA NeMo, Alibaba Cloud PAI (including a visual Designer component), PAI-DLC, and 20+ other frameworks and platforms. The list of adopters covers enterprises such as Alibaba Group, Ant Group, BYD, ByteDance, DTSTACK, JD.com, NVIDIA, OPPO, Xiaohongshu, Xiaomi, and Ximalaya, as well as academic institutions such as the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Nanjing University, Peking University, Renmin University of China, Tsinghua University, UCAS, and Zhejiang University. The paper specifically notes that it underpins Alibaba Tongyi's enterprise-grade foundation model training, particularly the high-cost video/image processing for TB-token-scale pretraining and the "spatial intelligence" direction.

【Inferable cost structure (this work's cost profile differs fundamentally from pure-cleaning pipelines)】

【Self-positioning of "scalability" versus evidence gap】The paper claims a "scalable data synthesis pipeline" as one of its core selling points, but provides no engineering data to support that scalability claim — no throughput, no per-sample cost, no parallelization scheme, no failure/retry strategy, and no synthesis success rate. For a pipeline that calls itself "scalable," these are precisely the metrics that should have been reported. 【NAVA — the only publicly stated total compute and segmentation infrastructure】
(1) Total compute: "Approximately 107,520 H100 GPU-hours" across three training stages. This is the only compute figure in this batch, and a rare instance of transparent disclosure among contemporaneous JAVG papers. Converted: about 1,120 H100s training continuously for 4 days, or 512 H100s training for about 8.75 days. For a 6.3B model + 15M-clip configuration, this is a mid-scale industrial effort — by comparison, contemporaneous large-vendor video models routinely run into hundreds of thousands or even millions of GPU-hours, making NAVA relatively frugal.
(2) Data-segmentation infrastructure: "raw videos are first segmented at scale with a Hadoop-based pipeline" — a Hadoop distributed pipeline is used to segment 100M videos. Choosing Hadoop (rather than Spark, Ray, or a GPU-accelerated framework) indicates that its data processing is dominated by CPU-intensive I/O and transcoding.
(3) Deduplication infrastructure: VideoCLIP embedding extraction + "large-scale k-means clustering" — performing embedding clustering over a hundred-million-scale video corpus is itself a substantial engineering challenge, but the specific implementation (whether FAISS is used, the distributed scheme, the time taken) is not disclosed [Uncertain].
(4) Inference side: an approximately 1-minute video is generated under 8-way sequence parallelism; weights are 24GB (bf16) / about 7GB (fp8 quantized).
【ALIVE — FAISS retrieval infrastructure (inference side)】
Constructs a dense vector index using FAISS: "We first construct a dense vector index ... using FAISS ... extract sound events from the user's prompt and query this database for the nearest neighbors based on cosine similarity," with a retrieval similarity threshold τ>0.85. This is prompt retrieval augmentation at inference time rather than training-data processing, but it still qualifies as data infrastructure. The training-side cluster scale, card model, card-hours, data throughput, and processing cost are all undisclosed [Uncertain]. Given its scale of 384M audio samples + 11M video samples, its compute investment should far exceed NAVA's, but no figures are given.
【CCL — "savings" implied indirectly through training scale】
Although GPU count and card-hours are not disclosed [Uncertain], its core selling point is resource efficiency: training data of 4M (vs. Ovi's 30.7M, MOVA's 50M), with joint training of only 12,000 steps @ batch 320. The paper states it "achieves state-of-the-art performance with substantially reduced computational resources compared to recent academic approaches." Computing batch 320 × 12,000 steps ≈ 3.84 million sample-passes, this training volume is indeed extremely small. Initializing the video stream from Wan2.1-14B is the precondition for this savings — standing on a strong base model, only the cross-modal connector needs training.
【StreamChar — inference-side real-time performance is its core engineering metric】
Runs in real time on a single H100 GPU, at 33 frames per chunk @24fps. This is the only work in this batch whose primary engineering goal is "real-time performance," and its two-stage distillation (4-step generator) exists precisely for this purpose. No infrastructure disclosure on the data-processing side [Uncertain].
【OmniCustom / Baton】No data-infrastructure disclosure [Uncertain]. Baton gives only batch size 1 per GPU, lr 1e-5, 10 epochs — batch 1 per GPU indicates tight per-card VRAM (Qwen3-8B planner + DiT resident in memory simultaneously), but the total GPU count is not stated [Uncertain].

The essence of ITS is trading inference compute for quality: JavisDiT generates 5 candidates per prompt, MMDisCo generates 10 per prompt, plus the scoring overhead of 6 verifiers — inference cost is amplified by 5–10x or more compared to a single generation. The paper's value proposition is that this expense is far below the cost of retraining the model. Specific latency/throughput figures were not extracted [Uncertain].
【Commonality】None of the seven works uses ready-made data processing frameworks such as NeMo Curator or Data-Juicer; all either build their own pipelines or directly reuse public datasets. Only NAVA names general-purpose infrastructure such as Hadoop and k-means.

### [MOVA](../models/MOVA.md)

【Data-processing infrastructure】An in-house video preprocessing pipeline built on the Ray distributed framework, which the paper describes as "balancing data quality and processing efficiency." Neither NeMo Curator nor Data-Juicer nor any other off-the-shelf framework is used. The annotation stage mixes NVIDIA GPUs with Huawei Ascend NPUs. The paper gives no throughput, GPU acceleration ratio, or processing cost figures for the data-processing stage.
【Training infrastructure】
- Scale: 1,024 GPUs (128 nodes × 8 cards), DP replicate size 64.
- Parallelism strategy: FSDP shards model parameters + USP sequence parallelism; 360p uses CP=8 (effective batch 128), 720p uses CP=16 (effective batch 64).
- MFU: about 35%.
- VAE redundancy elimination: following Wan's approach, within each CP group input preprocessing (mainly VAE encoding) is performed only once, and the designated rank broadcasts the preprocessed features to the other ranks in the same group, avoiding repeated VAE computation caused by sequence parallelism.
- Memory management: manual memory management is used to avoid the Python GC overhead issue reported by OpenSora2.
- MoE adaptation: because FSDP requires a consistent computation graph, the A14B MoE video tower uses alternating optimization — on odd steps, high-noise timesteps are sampled across all samples and the high-noise DiT is optimized; on even steps, low-noise timesteps are sampled and the low-noise DiT is optimized; the shared Bridge and audio tower are optimized on every step.
- Domestic-hardware adaptation: the training stack has been ported to Ascend NPUs, with operator fusion applied to attention kernels, tensor-layout transformations, and rotary positional encoding computation to reduce framework overhead. An 8-card Ascend 910A2 micro-benchmark (CP=4, DP-shard=2): 34.1 seconds per step, 376 TFLOPs in FP16, about 40GB per-card memory usage, and ≥128GB host memory. The paper cautions that this figure depends on the software-stack version and should not be extrapolated to large-scale training cost.
- Total cost: 42 days × 1,024 GPUs ≈ 43,000 GPU-days.
【Sequence-length bottleneck】A 720p, 8-second, 24fps clip produces about 1.6×10^5 tokens, which the paper identifies as the primary bottleneck for training throughput and inference latency (especially under the most common guidance setting of NFE=3), and proposes future directions: more aggressive spatiotemporal compression, hierarchical or chunked generation, and system-level optimization targeting long-context video tokens.

### [Merged entry: ① Mochi 1](../models/Mochi_MAGI_Motif.md) ⚠️

① Mochi 1: No disclosure of data-processing infrastructure. Training-side scale is also undisclosed; only the inference side is known to require about 60GB of VRAM per card. [Uncertain]
② MAGI-1: The report states it built "a scalable data processing system" to construct a training set from tens of petabytes of raw material, but does not name the framework (no mention of NeMo Curator/Data-Juicer, etc.), and gives no GPU acceleration ratio, processing machine-hours, or cost. The inferable cost-scale clue is: over 10 categories of model inference — PySceneDetect, DOVER, LAION aesthetics, RAFT (already downsampled to 8 FPS to save compute), saliency detection, Hough border detection, text detection, Florence-2, face detection, CLIP, and DINOv2 — must be run across petabyte-scale material, and then an MLLM must be run on the survivors — merging MLLM filtering and annotation into a single call is precisely intended to control this cost.
By contrast, the training-side infrastructure disclosure is very thorough (occupying a large portion of Section 4 of the technical report): DP + CP + TP three-dimensional parallelism is used; to address the DP load imbalance and low GPU utilization on short sequences caused by variable-length video sequences, an online distributed Packing and Padding (PnP) scheme was designed — reformulating the bin-packing problem as "packing M candidate samples into N bins of capacity max_length, where M ≫ N, N must be divisible by DP_SIZE, and max_length must be divisible by TP_SIZE × CP_SIZE," solved online with an extended First-Fit Decreasing greedy algorithm plus custom heuristics, achieving 99% capacity utilization with negligible variance across DP groups. The reason it must be online rather than offline packing is precisely because "the data composition is adjusted frequently during training" (echoing the dynamic distribution adjustment in Section 3.5) — a typical case of data strategy in turn constraining infrastructure design. It also contributes MagiAttention distributed attention (Flex-Flash-Attention + a dispatch solver + Group-Cast/Group-Reduce communication primitives + multi-level overlap), achieving linear scalability for ultra-long sequences and heterogeneous masks. [Partially uncertain: the framework, throughput, and cost on the data-cleaning side]
③ Motif-Video 2B (the most complete data-infrastructure disclosure in this group, with quantified gains given):
· Cleaning framework: "We made extensive use of NeMo Curator," stating that its scalable data-governance toolkit and support for large-scale video processing pipelines significantly simplified the preprocessing workflow.
· Deduplication acceleration: NVIDIA cuVS multi-GPU IVF-PQ index (cosine distance, k=64, nprobe=16).
· OCR servitization: PaddleOCR-VL deployed via vLLM.
· Preprocessing implementation: black-bar cropping, OCR-region exclusion, resolution scaling, and frame-rate limiting are all done in a single ffmpeg re-encoding pass, avoiding repeated transcoding.
· Storage and sampling (an independent contribution, Section 5.3 + Table 2): the corpus is stored as WebDataset tar shards. The baseline approach (global clip index + shuffle_block_size=1 random shuffling + round-robin assignment by index mod W), while preserving randomness, destroys archival locality, sacrifices WebDataset's core advantage of sequential reads, and causes severe cross-rank bucket-composition imbalance — under FSDP2/HSDP synchronous training, the global step count for a given bucket is determined by the slowest rank in that bucket, causing the baseline to achieve only N steps per epoch, about 20% utilization, and a dataloader latency of 0.05 s/step. The team's offline bucket-balanced sampler moves filtering, bucketing, and rank assignment entirely to an offline planning stage: filtering rules are first applied from clip-level Parquet metadata and joint "frame-count × resolution" buckets are assigned, then each tar shard is greedily placed on the rank that most reduces the current cross-rank bucket imbalance, followed by 30,000 iterations of simulated annealing (each iteration proposing a swap of two tar shards) to optimize the shard-to-rank mapping, with the objective of minimizing the coefficient of variation of clip counts across ranks for each of the 1f/33f/65f/121f buckets; the final plan is serialized as a rank{r}.npz plan file per rank. At runtime, each rank reads only its assigned tar shards in shard order, combined with a rolling shuffle over a 4,096-sample window to preserve intra-bucket randomness without breaking read locality.
· Quantified gains (Table 2): baseline is N steps/epoch, about 20% utilization, 0.05 s/step; offline sampler + greedy achieves about 4.6N steps, about 76% utilization, <0.001 s/step; offline sampler + simulated annealing (the final scheme) achieves about 5.4N steps, utilization close to 90%, <0.001 s/step. That is, throughput improves by about 5.4x relative to the baseline, utilization rises from 20% to about 90%, and dataloader latency drops by more than 50x; SA improves a further ~18% over greedy.
· Training hardware: 8 Azure nodes × 8 H200s = 64 cards total, orchestrated with Kubernetes, launched via SkyPilot (handling scheduling, failure recovery, and cloud resource provisioning), using FSDP2 via Accelerate; at the 2B scale, a single node's sharding group alone can hold the full model state for the 720p/121-frame configuration, so tensor parallelism or sequence parallelism is not needed, simplifying the communication pattern and reducing synchronization overhead. Mixed precision uses bfloat16 for compute and activations, with float32 reserved for reduction-sensitive communication and optimizer state.
This section is the only one among the three models in this group that fully connects "data-infrastructure improvement → quantifiable training-throughput gain," making it the most practically valuable reference for "building video models on a small budget."

### [NVIDIA NeMo Curator](../models/NeMo_Curator.md) ⚠️

This is the core field of this entry, and the basis on which this toolchain is called "the de facto industrial-grade standard for video data infrastructure."
【Overall acceleration ratio】Official figure: using 1,000 GPUs, at ISO power (equal power draw), achieves an 89x speedup over an unoptimized CPU pipeline, compressing processing time from "years" to "days." Specifically, for 20 million hours of video: about 40 days on a Hopper cluster, about 14 days on Blackwell, versus about 3.4 years for an unoptimized CPU pipeline.
【Breakdown of acceleration sources】(1) NVDEC hardware decoding + NVENC hardware encoding — the decode and transcode stages gain about a 3x speedup (per the NeMo paper's figures); the Cosmos paper gives a transcoding throughput of 0.3702 videos/second on L40S, a 6.5x improvement over the baseline; (2) FP8-quantized TensorRT-LLM engines for VLM captioning — about a 10x speedup; (3) Ray distributed scheduling and auto-scaling eliminates GPU idle time; (4) streaming execution avoids fully persisting intermediate outputs to disk.
【Auto-scaling mechanism (the core of Cosmos-Xenna)】In streaming mode, the throughput (samples/second) of each stage is continuously measured, and the number of workers for each stage is dynamically adjusted to balance the entire pipeline: workers are scaled down for fast stages and scaled up for bottleneck stages. A sophisticated bin-packing algorithm packs workers optimally onto cluster nodes to maximize utilization. Default parameters: autoscale_interval_s = 180 seconds (the auto-scaling check interval), logging_interval = 60 seconds, cpu_allocation_percentage = 0.95, and execution_mode can be streaming (recommended) or batch. A separate serving mode supports queue-based real-time input/output.
【Key engineering conclusion】Captioning is explicitly identified as the rate-limiting bottleneck stage (its throughput is far below that of embedding generation), so auto-scaling will allocate the most resources to it — any self-built video data pipeline should expect the same bottleneck distribution.
【Other mechanisms】Backpressure control prevents memory overflow; CPU-intensive stages do not block GPU stages; stateful actors ensure model weights are loaded only once per worker; SPMD mode supports distributed inference of large models requiring tensor parallelism across GPUs/nodes; P2P artifact distribution accelerates multi-node model-file downloads.
【Heterogeneous clusters】Supports heterogeneous clusters mixing GPU types such as L40S, H100, and GB200, and can specifically leverage a given GPU's NVENC capability.
【Ray object store】Leverages the Ray Object Store and object references to minimize data movement.
【Text-modality comparison figure】Fuzzy dedup on RedPajama v2 achieves a 16x speedup over the CPU approach with a 40% reduction in TCO, and scales near-linearly from 1 to 4 nodes — note this is a text-side figure and should not be conflated with the 89x figure on the video side.
【Operations】Since version 26.07, LMDB-recorded completed-shard tracking supports resumable runs and SLURM job-array support; since version 26.02, automated per-modality stage and pipeline benchmarking is provided.
【Cost】The dollar cost per unit of data is not published. [Uncertain]

### [UniVerse-1](../models/UniVerse-1.md) ⚠️

The paper discloses very little about data infrastructure, but its online-annotation architecture itself is an infrastructure design worth recording:
【Online annotation service】A dedicated server process that runs concurrently with training, responsible for completing, on the fly at every sampling step: fixed-length windowed time sampling → QWen2.5-Omni multimodal annotation + Whisper ASR → text encoding (umT5) → video 3D VAE encoding → audio mel-spectrogram + Music-DCAE latent encoding, ultimately feeding the training process aligned "data–annotation pairs." This effectively merges annotation, encoding, and data loading into a single online pipeline, with the training side directly consuming latents.
【Engineering implications】This architecture requires the throughput of annotation and encoding to match the training consumption rate, or it becomes a bottleneck; it also eliminates the storage overhead of offline latent precomputation (the latent cache volume for 7,685 hours of video would be considerable), but it must repeatedly pay the annotation and encoding cost every epoch. The paper does not disclose this service's deployment scale, GPU count, per-sample latency, throughput ceiling, or whether there is a caching mechanism to reuse already-annotated windows. [Uncertain]
【Training infrastructure】FSDP distributed training across multiple nodes; GPU model, card count, total training duration, and GPU-days are undisclosed; long-sequence parallelism strategies such as context parallelism are not mentioned (constrained by the 1.3B base model and short clips, sequence-length pressure is lower than MOVA's).
【Cleaning infrastructure】No mention of using any data processing framework such as NeMo Curator, Data-Juicer, or Ray; no processing scale, throughput, or cost data is given for the cleaning stage. In contrast to MOVA, which explicitly states it is based on the Ray distributed framework and mixed NVIDIA GPU + Ascend NPU compute, UniVerse-1 is essentially blank on this dimension. [Uncertain]

### [Unison](../models/Unison.md) ⚠️

The paper's disclosure of data-processing infrastructure is zero: no mention of NeMo Curator, Data-Juicer, Ray, or any distributed data processing framework; no processing scale, throughput, duration, or cost given for the cleaning stage; no description of the pipeline's GPU acceleration; no discussion of the storage and I/O scheme for 2 million clips / 50 million audio segments; and no statement of whether latents are precomputed offline and cached or encoded online.
【Disclosed training infrastructure (a fairly restrained scale)】
- Stage 1 (audio branch): 4 NVIDIA H100s, batch size 96, learning rate with a 1k-step linear warmup, step decay at 240k and 270k steps;
- Stage 2 (joint training): 16 H100s, bf16 precision, ZeRO-2 optimization (not FSDP, nor the more aggressive ZeRO-3), batch size 32; only the audio branch and fusion module are trained, with the video backbone frozen;
- Inference: a 50-step flow-matching sampler, CFG scale 6.0, output at 25 FPS.
- Undisclosed: total training duration, GPU-days, Stage 2 step count, and sequence-parallel/context-parallelism strategy (since the video backbone is frozen and clips are only about 5 seconds long, long-sequence pressure is limited).
【Scale positioning and the economics of the "frozen backbone" strategy】16 H100s is a noticeably small joint-training configuration — MOVA uses a mixed NVIDIA GPU + Ascend NPU compute cluster, UniVerse-1 uses multi-node FSDP, and industrial models are on the order of thousands of cards. Unison's ability to complete joint training with 16 cards benefits directly from its design of "freezing the 5B video backbone and training only the audio branch and fusion module": the trainable parameter count is drastically reduced, and VRAM and compute requirements drop accordingly. Together with the paper's emphasis that "a 5B backbone (nearly 4x smaller than LTX-2's 19B) achieves superior cross-modal synchronization," this forms two sides of its efficiency narrative.
【The gap in data-side infrastructure disclosure is understandable】Since the corpus aggregates existing open-source datasets (data already segmented, already downloaded, partly already annotated), Unison did not need to build heavyweight infrastructure such as its own crawling, transcoding, or large-scale segmentation, and its processing scale is far smaller than works starting from raw long-form video. Even so, lip-filtering over 2 million clips (each requiring face detection + SyncNet inference) and Mel-RoFormer separation over 50 million audio segments still represent a considerable amount of computation, and the paper's complete silence on this remains a disclosure gap. [Uncertain]

### [Video Captioning Model Ecosystem](../models/caption_models.md) ⚠️

[Partially uncertain] This dimension has sparse disclosure, but throughput considerations profoundly shaped captioner model selection:
【Concrete compute figures given】
· SkyCaptioner-V1: 32 A800s, global batch size 512, about 2 million training samples.
· Panda-70M's UMT selector model: 8×A100-80G, 12 frames at 224×224, AdamW lr 2e-5, batch 32, 10 epochs.
· Open-Sora Plan v1.3's prompt refiner: LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct LoRA (rank 64, batch 32, 1 epoch), trained on a single H100 in 30 minutes.
· MOVA: annotation performed with a mix of NVIDIA GPUs and Huawei Ascend NPUs — a rare public record of domestic chips participating in large-scale annotation.
· Motif-Video 2B: PaddleOCR-VL served via vLLM; CineDance's inference framework acknowledgments credit vLLM. vLLM is the de facto standard for batch annotation inference.
【Direct evidence that throughput drives model selection (the most valuable engineering record in this ecosystem)】
· Open-Sora's official documentation: "GPT-4V works better, but 20 seconds/sample is too slow for us" — hence the switch to PLLaVA-13B. This is the most candid record of an open-source project's trade-off on annotation cost.
· Allegro's key reason for choosing Aria is the official claim that it can generate a caption for a 256-frame video within 10 seconds.
· The convergence of parameter counts across the entire ecosystem to 7B (ShareCaptioner, Tarsier2, SkyCaptioner, AVoCaDO, AVSCap, MiMo-VL) is itself a product of throughput constraints; placing a 120B-class model (GPT-OSS-120B) only at the fusion-adjudication step rather than the per-sample perception step follows the same logic.
· UniVerse-1's online annotation architecture mandates lightweight models (Qwen2.5-Omni + Whisper), unable to bear the per-sample inference cost of a 120B-class model — a coupling between "annotation timing" and "annotation model capacity."
· MAGI-1 distills the enhanced prompts of a large MLLM into a roughly 7B small model (using about 2 million samples of corpus) to reduce inference latency, with human evaluation showing comparable quality while latency and compute overhead drop substantially.
【Cost】No work publicly discloses the dollar cost or GPU-hours of annotation [Uncertain]. Apollo/Klear's calls to Gemini-2.5-Pro across 81 million samples is the largest known commercial-API annotation investment, but the amount is undisclosed.
【Dedicated frameworks】No public record of the captioner ecosystem using data-engineering frameworks such as NeMo Curator or Data-Juicer; annotation is generally handled by in-house scripts plus batch inference via vLLM.

### [Video-Generation Post-Training Data Strategy](../models/post_training_data.md) ⚠️

The anchor paper discloses no infrastructure or throughput data whatsoever (no GPU count, no training duration, no cost) [Uncertain], but the main text twice emphasizes that compute constraints during post-training are the core tension: "post-training must operate under strict constraints on sampling cost, evaluation quality, and system efficiency" and "rollout generation is expensive," citing DanceGRPO as evidence. Its countermeasures are threefold: ① isochronal grouping — each prompt performs SDE sampling and gradient computation at only one assigned timestep, with ODE used for the remaining steps, compressing GRPO's gradient-computation cost to close to a single step; ② a strategy that freezes the generation backbone and trains only the PE LLM, allowing the PE to be paired with any off-the-shelf generator without retraining the video model; ③ autoregressive distillation compresses a bidirectional model into a causal, few-step model to lower deployment inference cost.
【Cross-model post-training compute figures】Movie Gen's video SFT uses only 512 H100s (an extremely small figure relative to its pretraining scale); Allegro's SFT consumes 2.6M samples, batch 256, 10K steps, on 256× H100s; Cosmos-Predict 2.5's GRPO, due to memory constraints, decomposes trajectory probability into a sum of per-step conditional probabilities, computing a gradient every two steps and accumulating along the full 10-step trajectory before a single parameter update, training for 256 steps at batch 32; Seedance 1.5 pro states that infrastructure optimization targeting the RLHF pipeline brought nearly a 3x training speedup; BranchGRPO organizes rollouts into a branching tree, sharing prefixes to reduce overhead and pruning low-reward paths; MixGRPO uses a hybrid ODE-SDE approach to improve training efficiency. On the data-processing-tooling side: neither NeMo Curator nor Data-Juicer provides the capability to construct preference pairs for video generation (Data-Juicer 2.0 states support for foundation-model post-training, but on the video side this covers only the supervised-distillation route) — post-training data construction currently depends entirely on each team's in-house work. [Uncertain]

### [Merged Survey of Mainstream Video Pretraining Datasets: Panda-70M, InternVid, Koala…](../models/pretraining_datasets.md) ⚠️

**All seven works disclose very little about data-processing infrastructure and throughput — the field with the scarcest information in this entire survey**:
- **Framework**: all seven **use no mature data processing framework such as NeMo Curator or Data-Juicer**, relying entirely on in-house scripts. The only one with engineered packaging is **Panda-70M — it forked and modified `video2dataset`** (the upstream iejMac version could not parse its CSV format), providing a `panda70m.yaml` configuration, making it the most engineered download-and-segmentation pipeline among the seven. Two easy-to-miss default settings in its configuration: `download_size: 360` (**by default only downloads at 360p — you won't get 720p without changing the config**), and `download_audio: True` (audio track included by default; turning it off speeds things up).
- **Explicitly given compute figures (only three, and all for model training rather than data processing)**: Panda-70M's UMT selector fine-tuning used **8×A100-80G**, and its student captioning model was trained on **48×A100-80G for 300,000 steps**; InternVid's ViCLIP was trained on **64×A100 for about 3 days** (over 50 million video-text pairs, using DeepSpeed + FlashAttention, with MAE-style random-patch masking for acceleration, followed by a final 0.5 epochs of unmasked training at lr 4e-6 to bridge the pretrain/deployment gap); UltraVideo's UltraWan used **3.4K GPU-hours for the 1K version / 7.6K GPU-hours for the 4K version (H20)**.
- **The only data-processing-side performance figures (Koala-36M, of high reference value)**: per-frame-pair transition-detection time (ms) on CPU — 256 resolution: 1.42, 512: 2.45, 720p: 6.15, 1080p: 12.26, 4K: 41.98; compared with PySceneDetect(HSL) at 0.68/2.63/10.73/26.16/102.55 and PySceneDetect(HSL+edge) at 2.50/8.82/30.57/70.11/267.18. **The conclusion is that its method is actually slower at 256 resolution, but 2.4x faster at 1080p and 2.4x faster at 4K (6.4x faster than HSL+edge at 4K)**, due to feature extraction being fixed-downsampled to 256/128, decoupling it from the source resolution. This is the **only public material among the seven that gives a concrete data-processing throughput figure**.
- **Cost**: **none of the seven discloses the compute cost of the data-processing stage**. Given the need to run optical flow, aesthetics, OCR, and VLM captioning across tens of millions of videos (Panda-70M runs 9 model-inference passes per clip; UltraVideo uses a 72B VLM to judge 16 attributes per item), this cost is very likely the hidden bulk of the expense.
- **Storage scale (useful for capacity-planning reference)**: Panda-70M's full set is about **36TB** (the 10M subset about 8.0TB, the 2M subset about 1.6TB); OpenVid-1M is about **12.4TB** (the HD subset about 4.5TB); InternVid metadata 40.9GB; Koala-36M metadata 48.9GB; LVD-2M metadata about 5.08GB.
